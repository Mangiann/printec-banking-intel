What the board would act on

IRIS acceptance is now the law for every Greek business, both a national upgrade wave and a threat to our card rails
From 1 December 2025 every business in Greece must accept IRIS instant payments, forcing every till and terminal estate in the country to be upgraded and wired to the tax authority on a fixed clock, which is exactly the field integration and managed rollout Printec sells, now at national scale. The same rail is being engineered to compete with cards: a bank charges just 0.55% on an IRIS QR payment (F08), ceilings are rising into everyday basket sizes in Jan 2026 (F22), and it now runs cross-border on EuroPA (F26) with UPI accepted at Greek merchants (F43). The opportunity and the threat to our card and terminal revenue sit in one line.
CEO, CTO, Chief Commercial Officer, CFO F06 F08 F22 F26 F43
Mandatory POS to cash-register to tax interconnection rewrites the spec of every terminal in Greece
A country-wide rule that every payment terminal must talk to the electronic cash register and to the tax authority is the single largest forced change to the Greek terminal estate, squarely our integration and terminal-services territory, with recurring certification revenue and a re-qualification risk if our kit does not comply. The finance minister dated it to its final stage of implementation in 2024 (F40), so this is live delivery now, not a roadmap, and any non-compliant estate is an open opportunity.
CEO, CTO, Chief Commercial Officer, CFO F31 F40
A systemic bank now sells the phone as the terminal (NBG Pay softPOS), shrinking our hardware base
NBG Pay turns any Android phone or iPhone into a card terminal on Global Payments' rails, with links into cash registers and ERP, which means the smallest merchants may never buy a physical terminal again. This directly erodes the addressable base for the physical terminal estates we supply and is the clearest downside-if-ignored item in the sample for our largest product line; it forces a build-versus-partner decision and a shift from boxes to software and managed acceptance at the low end. Quality caveat: the finding carries no date, worth firming up before the board leans on it.
CEO, CTO, Chief Commercial Officer, CFO F24
The digital euro's rules for ATMs and terminals are being written now, and our biggest rival is already ahead
The ECB rulebook group has opened a dedicated workstream (G5) on building the digital euro into Europe's existing ATM and terminal estate, is defining the offline artefacts providers must implement (F30), leaves the build cost with providers (F56), and is basing the scheme on existing card and API standards so our deployed estate is the starting point not scrap (F46). The ECB has opened unpaid private-sector expert seats for exactly ATM and terminal specialists (F34): a seat is the cheapest way to shape a spec we will otherwise inherit and later have to build to. Meanwhile Nexi was chosen to support the ECB on offline digital-euro payments and reached investment grade in 2025 (F25, F37), pulling ahead on the terrain closest to our terminals.
CEO, CTO, Chief Commercial Officer, CFO F02 F30 F34 F46 F56 F25 F37
DORA puts EU supervisors directly onto banks' critical IT suppliers, which can mean Printec itself
For the first time a European supervisor looks straight at technology suppliers, not only the banks, and the detailed rulebook is complete and binding (F41). This is the one item in the sample about exposure to Printec itself: as a core tech supplier to banks we may have to evidence resilience testing, incident reporting and concentration controls, a real compliance cost and a gate on our ability to sell to banks if unmet, but also a selling point to bank clients who now need that evidence in tenders.
CTO, CFO, CEO, Chief Commercial Officer F28 F41
Cash 2030 keeps the cash cycle a funded obligation, protecting a core Printec revenue base
The Eurosystem has committed to keeping banknotes widely available and accepted, which keeps ATMs, recyclers and cash logistics a mandated line rather than one drifting into managed decline, and underpins the durability of one of our core franchises for long-range forecasts. Dated 2020, so worth refreshing with the current version and tracking whether Greek banks' actual ATM and recycler plans follow the policy or quietly retreat from cash.
CEO, CFO F16
Named, time-boxed Greek account openings: Alpha's 2026 rebrand and the state's connect-the-banks platforms
A systemic bank changing identity, head office and branch formats in 2026 pulls through ATM and self-service refresh, signage and channel work across a national network, and Alpha is in an active buying phase (Click to Pay F33, Samsung Wallet F59). Separately, EU recovery money is funding a state credit bureau every lender must connect to (about EUR 2.24m, F11), plus an out-of-court debt-settlement platform (F45) and capital-market systems (F21), a repeatable connect-the-banks-to-the-state integration opportunity across our whole bank client base.

Where the board disagreed

The four split on how much of this sample actually matters. The CTO and Chief Commercial Officer scored significance 4, reading roughly half the set as landing on Printec's build and deal agenda; the CEO and CFO scored 3, seeing the same market-shaping core but sitting inside a heavier tail of noise, so the same facts read as 'about half signal' to the operators and 'a core inside a heavy tail' to the CEO and CFO. They also split on the cash franchise: the CEO and CFO made Cash 2030 (F16) a top priority as an existential backstop for ATMs and cash management, while the CTO and CCO left it out entirely, focused on the payments build and named deals. On DORA (F28), the CTO and CFO treated it as one of the most material items, both a compliance bill and a sales lever, whereas the CEO and CCO did not elevate it and instead flagged its thin treatment as a gap. The CCO alone pushed named, time-boxed account openings (Alpha's 2026 rebrand F15, the state credit bureau F11) that no other member top-picked, a commercial lens the more risk- and strategy-focused members did not weight. Finally, the CEO alone caveated that F24 (softPOS) carries no date and should be firmed up before the board relies on it, and uniquely salvaged F54 for the one useful crumb it holds, naming UP Hellas as a competitor, even while discarding the finding.

What the board would collect next — the gaps

  • No sizing anywhere. Nothing gives the installed base of terminals, ATMs, cash registers, recyclers and kiosks in Greece, how many units each mandate (IRIS F06, POS-tax F31, digital euro F02) forces us to touch, over what timeline, or the unit economics. The top picks are directional, not budgetable.
  • No Printec position or incumbency map. We cannot see who holds each bank's and retail chain's terminal, ATM, cash-cycle and self-service estate today, when those contracts renew, or our win/loss by segment. Competitor intel is all payments-processor side (Nexi F25/F37, Verifone/FreedomPay F48, Global Payments via NBG Pay F24, UP Hellas F54) with nothing on the ATM and self-service rivals we actually face in Greece (Diebold Nixdorf, NCR/Atos).
  • No pace-of-shift data. There is no read on how fast IRIS, softPOS and account-to-account rails (EuroPA F26, UPI F43, Samsung Wallet F59) are taking share from cards and hardware. One price point (0.55%, F08) with no card-MDR baseline beside it is not a trend.
  • No Greek cash-cycle trajectory. There is no ATM count, branch or ATM closure rate, pooling deals, or cash-in-circulation trend to test Cash 2030 (F16) against reality and size the cash business.
  • No digital-euro build detail. No binding 'terminals must support by date X', no view of which standard wins (Nexo, Berlin Group, or card rails), no build cost or timing, and no read on whether Greek procurement lets local integrators in versus incumbents like Nexi and G&D.
  • Printec's own DORA exposure is unquantified (F28/F41). We do not know whether we could be designated a critical ICT third party or what evidencing resilience would cost us.
  • Almost nothing on public-sector self-service and kiosk demand, which we serve, beyond one tiny prepaid award (F54). No e-government or citizen-service kiosk programmes.
  • No read on customers' spending capacity or buying windows. Greek bank IT and capex budgets and procurement calendars are missing; F13 (macro slowdown) and F36 (fee pressure) hint at tightening but do not quantify it.

What the board would bin as noise

  • F07 Origin-story background for regimes already in force (MiCA/DORA and related). Dated 2014 to 2022, with no build or sales action attached today.
  • F01 Lithuania/ECB reclassification of five non-Greek banks, Tanzania mobile money, UK MREL and leverage thresholds, and Alpha's insurance deal in Cyprus. No Greek demand and no bill for us.
  • F12 A tiny cooperative bank's 0.12-point capital add-on (2020), Optima's COVID moratoria disclosure (2022), Eurobank unwinding its hive-down (weak union source), NBG's 2014 JESSICA urban funds, and the 2014 origin of the NPL framework. Dated, granular, no forward technology demand.
  • F51 Investment-firm wind-down rules (2017, quoted from a hoteliers' federation), the IFD/IFR prudential regime, a single government portal's privacy assessment, and who enforces the interchange cap. Not our client segment, or administrative rather than a demand signal.
  • F35 The EUR 50 unauthorised-payment liability cap (undated, well-known) and Revolut's global fraud-rate and headcount stats. A principle and a cost benchmark, with no Greek timing or account to act on.
  • F54 A tiny municipal prepaid meal-card award is immaterial as a finding, but it usefully names UP Hellas as a niche public-sector prepaid competitor, worth logging even as the finding is dropped.
