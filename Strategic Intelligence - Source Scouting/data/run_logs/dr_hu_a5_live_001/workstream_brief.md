The issuer-origin layer for Hungarian leadership and org-change facts is well developed and almost entirely Hungarian-language: every bank in the cell runs a corporate press room (OTP /portal/hu/hirek, MBH /sajtoszoba with date-slugged permalinks, K&H /csoport/kozlemenyek, Erste /hu/sajto/sajtokozlemenyek, Raiffeisen /raiffeisen-csoport/sajtoszoba, CIB /sajtoszoba plus a year-indexed legacy archive on net.cib.hu, UniCredit HU as dated PDFs under a /content/dam path), and each also maintains a diffable governance roster page that catches changes never separately announced. Only OTP, Gránit and MBH Jelzálogbank are listed, so BÉT 'Kibocsátói hírek' and the issuers' own IR pages give a legally timestamped spine for those three, while for the five foreign-owned subsidiaries the binding disclosure sits with the parent group — a structural boundary gap this cell cannot close alone. The 'jobs/hiring' half of the topic set is much thinner at the origin layer: only OTP's own vacancy portal (karrier.otpbank.hu/viewalljobs) was located, leaving eight issuers' employer-owned ATS endpoints unfound and hiring signal at risk of defaulting to third-party job boards. Nothing here was liveness-verified beyond one successful fetch of OTP Group IR, and several paths (Erste's misspelled 'hivatalalos-kozzetetelek' segment, K&H's Liferay portlet URLs, MBH's 2023-dated organigram PDF) are structurally fragile and should be treated as unverified rather than dead if they fail.

Hungary has an unusually strong legally-forced spine for A5 officeholder intelligence, but it is split across three separate legal regimes rather than one register: MNB prior approval of 'vezető állású személyek' (published as numbered H-EN-I-style decisions via the Határozatok és végzések keresése database), Tpt. §55 issuer disclosures filed to BÉT's Kibocsátói hírek and to the MNB's OAM at kozzetetelek.mnb.hu (where 'Közlemény vezető állású személyek körében bekövetkező változásról' is a standard document type), and Ctv.-mandated company-register entries surfacing in the Cégközlöny and e-cégjegyzék within 30 days of any officer change. Crucially, no regulator-side person-level officeholder register appears to exist — the MNB Intézménykereső is institution-level only — so person-level coverage must be assembled by diffing register officer blocks and parsing decision/disclosure PDFs, with historical (deleted-officer) data behind the paid OCCSZ channel or commercial aggregators such as OPTEN. The cell's pointer value is high: Intézménykereső plus the Közvetítők register define an entity universe of banks, financial enterprises, payment/e-money institutions and intermediaries that press-driven cells never enumerate, and per-bank 'felügyeleti határozatok' pages (e.g. MBH Bank) plus Magyar Közlöny appointments for state banks (MFB, EXIM, MEHIB) close the remaining segments. Local-language collection is mandatory — English searching surfaced only law-firm summaries and the thin MNB EN mirror, and zero primary named-appointment events.

For Hungary's public-sector and state-controlled financial route, the authoritative layer is a small set of statutory publication venues — Magyar Közlöny and its Hivatalos Értesítő annex (ministerial SZMSZ instructions, mirrored in NJT), Cégközlöny Online for legally registered executive-officer changes at MFB, Eximbank/MEHIB, Magyar Posta, Diákhitel Központ, and the Közbeszerzési Értesítő plus EKR and TED for procurement. Cégközlöny and the Hivatalos Értesítő are the highest-value, least-exploited sources: they carry appointments and org-structure changes as legal fact rather than as press narrative. Hiring signal comes from KÖZIGÁLLÁS (note the apparent migration from kozigallas.gov.hu to kozszolgallas.ksz.gov.hu) and from HR/consultancy/IT tender notices, which double as a pointer into supplier names. The main constraint is machine access: only TED offers a documented API and bulk XML, EKR gates full documentation behind registration, and the Hungarian portals will require HTML/PDF scraping with snapshot-and-diff on entity leadership pages.

Hungary's private-bank supplier ecosystem is genuinely the only visible half of the transaction, and it splits into three very different source layers. Layer one is statutorily reliable: BÉT and kozzetetelek.mnb.hu disclosures for listed HU vendors (4iG, ANY Biztonsági Nyomda) and the company registries (nemzeticegtar, ceginfo) for officer-level changes at every vendor's Hungarian entity — this is the only route that works for foreign vendors, which publish nothing in Hungarian. Layer two is the HU trade and business press (Bitport, ITBusiness with its EPA PDF backfile, Portfolio tag streams) plus consultancy commentary, above all MINDSPIRE's core-banking transformation blog, which is the single densest pointer into which bank runs which programme with which vendor. Layer three is vendor self-publication — Tietoevry, valantic, Shiwaforce, Finshape, Nexi Central Europe case studies and newsrooms — high-signal but marketing-timed and best snapshot-diffed. The cell's named seed vendors are partly mis-specified for this market: no Temenos/Backbase/Finastra footprint surfaced at OTP, Erste or MBH, the real stack is Flexcube, Intellect iGCB, Tietoevry and Finshape, and the ATM vendors have no Hungarian publication channel at all.

Hungary's banking/payments event layer is concentrated in three publishers whose pages function as continuously refreshed titleholder registries: Portfolio.hu's conference series (Hitelezés, Financial IT, Banking Technology) with per-event /eloadok pages, FinTechZone's FinTechShow/BankTechShow/PayTechShow family (merging into TechShow X on 14-15 Oct 2026), and the MNB's own conference tree led by the Lámfalussy Lectures, which uniquely maintains a multi-year 'previous speakers' archive. On the awards side, the Mastercard – Év Bankja programme (20th edition, 9 categories, 13 competing institutions) publishes year-stamped jury pages under stable URL patterns plus winner coverage that names the executives who collected each prize. Specialist and adjacent events extend reach into layers no directory covers — Financial Fraud Summit and the businessevents.hu/IIR bank verticals expose bank security, AML and fraud-prevention unit heads, while e-commerce, AI and marketing conferences surface card-scheme and acquirer commercial roles. The binding constraints are not discovery but preservation and normalisation: past-edition pages get rewritten, current-year jury lists are often unpublished, and the same role appears under several Hungarian title strings, so snapshotting and a hu→en title dictionary should precede any drift-detection logic.

The negative-signal half of A5 in Hungary rests on three distinct layers: a statutory layer (NFSZ/ESTAT collective-redundancy notifications and the underlying Mt. rules) that is legally compelled but not published case-by-case, a quantitative layer (MNB Aranykönyv per-institution branch and headcount series, KSH NACE-K employment, and the recurring mfor/Privátbankár bank-by-bank survey) that is fully open and machine-collectable, and a contested layer (Munkástanácsok, BBDSZ, UNI Europa, and press) where disputes surface as narrative rather than documents. The most important finding is that branch closures and workforce cuts are currently decoupled in Hungary — branches fell from 1401 to 1268 between early 2025 and Q1 2026 while sector employment rose above 40,500 — so network contraction must not be used as a layoff proxy. Eurofound's European Restructuring Monitor is the only structured, case-level, cross-checkable database found for named Hungarian bank restructuring events, and the newly unionised UniCredit Bank Hungary workforce (with its president dismissed in April 2026 and litigation pending) is the live focal conflict to watch. Main gaps: no open case-level redundancy filings, no per-bank union map beyond UniCredit, and unprobed official registries (birosag.hu, e-cegjegyzék, e-beszámoló) that would yield audited headcount and entity-level structural change.

Hungary's corporate-filing layer for banking and payments is unusually strong and almost entirely state-operated: e-beszámoló holds every entity's statutory accounts (with the átlagos statisztikai állományi létszám note that is the only per-institution headcount ground truth), while cegportal.im.gov.hu and Cégközlöny record officer appointments and terminations with legal effect dates. For listed banks a triple-publication rule means every annual report, corporate-governance report and extraordinary leadership disclosure exists in three archivable places at once — the issuer site, bet.hu, and kozzetetelek.mnb.hu — and MNB's Aranykönyv plus the personal-authorisation regime supply the institution universe and a potential leading indicator of senior appointments. The genuinely unserved capability is archival diffing: nothing published tracks Hungarian bank 'vezetőség' pages over time, so the Wayback CDX index must be driven in-house with digest-collapsed queries to catch silent, unannounced departures. Main gaps are actor breadth beyond the four banks reached here, the whole payment-institution and fintech tier, and the unread commercial terms on the two bulk data routes.

The A5 regional/indirect landscape for Hungary is anchored by two reliable, dateable publishers: HIPA's bilingual press room, which announces every subsidised business-service-centre investment with explicit headcount figures, and the HOA sector association, whose annual benchmark report maps the ~200 centres and their growth intentions (mirrored via BBJ and Issuu when gated). Local depth is real but uneven: Debrecen has a dense municipal/press stack (EDC Debrecen, haon.hu, hajdupress.hu) that reliably surfaces centre openings before national press, while Szeged produced no bank-specific service-centre evidence in this run and looks closer to an empty geography than an under-covered one. The talent-pipeline weak signal is best read from Corvinus and BME press rooms (MBH Bank strategic partnership feeding internship/Start+; OTP cybersecurity tie-up; MNB's higher-education network register), plus the Műegyetemi Állásbörze exhibitor roster and bank career portals — with Erste's ErStep pipeline visible only behind LinkedIn's authwall. Biggest remaining gaps are the EKD state-aid decisions in Magyar Közlöny (the most authoritative early record of subsidised job creation) and regional universities outside Budapest.

Hungarian payments employers split cleanly into two source regimes. Domestic players (SimplePay/ex-OTP Mobil, Barion) publish in Hungarian on their own sites — SimplePay maintains a vezetőség page and a dated press stream that carried both its Kft.-to-Zrt. conversion and its CEO appointment, while Barion offers a careers portal but no discoverable press room, so its officer changes must be read from the company registry. Foreign players (Revolut, Wise, PayU) have almost no Hungarian-language first-party surface: their signals arrive via English careers sites, HU business media (Forbes.hu broke the Revolut country-lead appointment; Portfolio and FinTechZone tag streams aggregate the rest), and press wires like Prim. The reliable backbone for this cell is therefore regulatory and registry data — the MNB Intézménykereső for the true employer population and Nemzeti Cégtár/OPTEN for PR-independent officer changes — with media and career sites layered on for timeliness; the main constraints are ToS-blocked hiring-volume sources (LinkedIn, Glassdoor) and JS-rendered ATS pages.

Hungary's payment-rails layer has almost no dedicated media watchdog: appointment and reorganisation signals for schemes, acquirers, processors and ATM/POS deployers are split between one specialist trade outlet (FinTechZone, which carries GIRO CEO interviews, Mastercard market-lead news and conference speaker rosters), one cross-sector appointment aggregator (HR Portál's vezetőváltás tag), and the statutory record. Because PR coverage is thin, the highest-yield sources are structural rather than editorial: the e-cégjegyzék/Cégközlöny company register (executive-officer changes must be filed within 30 days), its commercial mirrors (Nemzeti Cégtár, OPTEN, Céginfo), the MNB Intézménykereső for the licensed-entity universe, and foreign-parent disclosure channels such as Euronet's IR/SEC filings from its Budapest-headquartered European operations. Visa's Hungarian organisation is the clearest blind spot - the term itself is search-toxic in both languages due to travel-visa noise - and no HU-language trace of Euronet or Nexi local org changes was found, so register-diffing plus careers/ATS monitoring (GIRO's karrierportal, multinational career sites) must substitute for press coverage.

Hungarian-language business press is a dense, high-recall channel for A5 signals, anchored by Portfolio.hu's Bank section (with publisher RSS), Privátbankár's dedicated Karrier/karrierhír column, and vg.hu for state-owned financial institutions where appointments are politically driven. Telex and HVG supply the counter-signal that corporate sources never publish — layoffs, post-merger restructuring and forced exits — while Forbes.hu and FinTechRadar/FinTechZone are the only reliable routes to fintech and neobank leadership. BBJ's English 'Appointments' column is the most structured single feed in the cell and is the natural machine-readable spine, with mfor.hu and ProfitLine acting as archive depth and redundancy respectively. Main weaknesses: the press channel covers executives but not ordinary hiring, under-covers payments infrastructure versus banks, and lacks the upstream MTI/OS wire that several of these outlets simply republish.

English-language coverage of Hungarian banking and payments personnel is anchored by one genuinely strong source — the Budapest Business Journal's appointments column (plus its legacy 'Who's News' archive) — supported by issuer and scheme newsrooms (OTP Group's English IR site, Citi Hungary, Mastercard Europe, Visa CEMEA) and the MNB's English pressroom as the authoritative confirmation layer. The international wires (Bloomberg, Reuters) break the biggest moves first but are paywalled and bot-defended, so in practice they are reachable only through licensing or syndication mirrors such as MarketScreener and Investing.com; bne IntelliNews adds CEE strategic context behind a subscription. Global payments trade press (Finextra, The Paypers, FinTech Futures, PaymentsSource) proved structurally right but empirically almost empty of Hungary-specific personnel items, so it should be run as a keyword tripwire rather than a primary feed. A Hungarian-language control query confirmed a systematic language asymmetry — Portfolio.hu and the HU press carry Raiffeisen/Erste/UniCredit Hungary appointments that never appear in English — so this cell must be treated as a partial, lagging mirror of the hu-press cell, not a substitute.

Hungary's professional-network layer is overwhelmingly LinkedIn-centric: the major banks (OTP, MBH, K&H, Erste, Raiffeisen) and the leading PSP Barion all maintain active company pages, and Hungarian executives reliably self-announce moves using a small set of local idioms, with 'örömmel osztom meg' empirically the highest-yield anchor phrase — a search on it surfaced real appointment posts across OTP, MBH, MNB and Gránit. X and Facebook are thin by comparison and function as confirmation rather than discovery channels, though MNB's Facebook page does carry genuine leadership-transition posts. The binding constraint in this cell is access, not availability: nearly every high-value source is auth-walled and ToS-restricted, so the only login-free window found is search-engine indexing of public linkedin.com/posts and /in/ URLs. Priority next steps are resolving the missing payments-sector company slugs and taking a first headcount-curve baseline so the employee-growth signal becomes a time series.

Hungary's vacancy-signal landscape splits into three collectable layers: two dominant general boards (Profession.hu, CVonline.hu) whose per-employer profile pages give clean company-level time series; bank-owned ATS portals, most of which sit either on OTP's own SuccessFactors-style site (karrier.otpbank.hu, with a full view-all-jobs index and stable category folders) or on the Nexum *.karrierportal.hu tenant family that also hosts GIRO and legacy MKB - making one parser reusable across several institutions; and international ATS feeds (Greenhouse, Lever, SmartRecruiters) where Budapest hubs of foreign fintechs such as Wise and Revolut publish. The richest payment-infrastructure signal is the smallest source: GIRO Zrt.'s career portal plus MNB's careers and subsidiary-offers pages, where instant-payment and supervisory roles appear directly. Main weaknesses are the absent Erste HU and CIB feeds, an unmapped Workday/SuccessFactors tenant layer, thin coverage of PSP/acquirer-side employers, and the fact that the two highest-volume aggregators (Indeed.hu, LinkedIn) are anti-bot or auth-walled and usable only as manual spot-checks.

Hungary's recruiter/HR-analyst layer produces exactly two kinds of tractable evidence for banking and payments hiring: annual cross-sector salary guides with a finance chapter (Hays Hungary is the anchor, with a full 2026 PDF mirrored ungated on Pénzcentrum; Enloyd, Grafton and Randstad's benchmarking products are secondary), and a quarterly quantified hiring-intent series (ManpowerGroup's Munkaerőpiaci Előrejelzés, which breaks out 'pénzügy és biztosítás' as its own net-employment reading). No HU recruiter publishes a banking- or payments-specific report, so the finance signal must always be extracted as a sub-slice. The executive-search layer — Amrop Kohlmann & Young, HumanField, Beck & Partners, Stanton Chase, Horton International — is the indirect channel that reveals which institutions are staffing senior roles, and it is best enumerated through the pointer sources HR Portál, hrpwr.hu and the paid BBJ Book of Lists ranking rather than firm by firm. Two named actors in the cell brief did not hold up: Michael Page has no Hungarian office (CEE is run from Vienna) and Boyden produced no Hungarian footprint at all, while Trenkwalder's recurring product turns out to be a blue-collar hourly-wage barometer and belongs in macro context rather than financial-services coverage.

Hungary's banking/payments association layer is small, highly concentrated and unusually generous with named-person data: Magyar Bankszövetség is the anchor, publishing both a ~67-entry member roster (banks, branches, plus observers such as GIRO, the State Treasury and OBA) and a governance section in which every presidency and supervisory-board seat is tied to a named executive at a named bank on a three-year tisztújítás cycle. Around it sit complementary rosters that no bank-centric list captures — MAFISZ/hunfintech.com for fintechs and PSPs, IVSZ for IT and payments vendors, Magyar Lízingszövetség for bank-owned asset-finance arms, MABISZ for bancassurance partners, MAKISZ for NPL servicers — making this cell the cheapest entity-universe expansion available to the whole run. The chamber layer (AmCham, BCCH, DUIHK, HBLF) adds annual, English-language board elections that double as executive-move signals, with BBJ's Chamber of Commerce Corner acting as a single aggregating monitor. Practical constraints: no RSS or APIs were observed, several sites use fragile legacy query-string URLs, the committee/working-group layer where the most operationally relevant names live is not search-engine visible and needs direct crawling, and one seed assumption — a Hungarian SZÉP-card issuers' association — could not be substantiated at all.
