Regulator personnel intelligence for North Macedonia rests on a clean, legally mandated three-stage chain: a Government/Presidential public call or proposal (vlada.mk 'Јавен оглас за именување', pretsedatel), an Assembly committee and plenary decision (sobranie.mk, Комисија за прашања на изборите и именувањата), and final publication as an act in Службен весник — with NBRNM, KHV, ASO, MAPAS and the Deposit Insurance Fund all sharing that pipeline, which makes cross-checking straightforward. The most valuable diffable primaries are the NBRNM Council roster, Governor page, org chart and Конкурси vacancy page, plus the Ministry of Finance Financial System Sector page, which uniquely names the banking-and-payments unit head and deputy. Two caveats shape collection design: nbrm.mk and sobranie.mk returned HTTP 403 to plain fetches (bot defence suspected, not dead), so browser-context collection is likely required; and no structured feed or appointment register exists anywhere in the chain, so everything is snapshot-and-diff over HTML and gazette PDFs. The clearest blind spot is the sub-Council layer — no primary channel publishes directorate heads such as a payment-systems director, and Albanian-language sources were not covered in this pass.

For North Macedonia the compelled organisation-change record sits in three stacked layers: the Централен регистар на РСМ (Trade Register) holds every registered change of управител/овластено лице and of управен/надзорен одбор members, the Службен весник publishes state appointment decrees (legally binding in its electronic edition since July 2021), and NBRSM's registers of banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions define the exact entity population plus the Governor's prior-consent step that must precede any bank board appointment. The practical constraint is delivery, not existence: CRM offers a free per-entity basic profile and paid current-status/history certificates but no discoverable change bulletin or open API, so monitoring means polling a known EMBS watchlist or contracting the Интернет дистрибутивен систем, while nbrm.mk returned 403 to direct fetch and needs a browser-grade client. The fastest-signal complement is the Macedonian Stock Exchange's 'Промени во органите на управување и раководење' issuer announcements, which are dated, categorised and typically precede the CRM registration. Main open gaps: the licensed-entity list is not yet enumerated, NBRSM consent decisions appear unpublished, and Albanian-language coverage was out of scope this pass.

North Macedonia's association layer for banking and payments is bifurcated: the standalone Македонска банкарска асоцијација (mba.mk) has absorbed the banking-governance role that once sat inside the Стопанска комора, publishing named officers (President Maja Stevkova Shterieva of Komercijalna banka, VP Bojan Stojanoski of Stopanska banka, Executive Secretary Milena Percinkova) but defining membership by rule rather than by list — so the bank population must still be drawn from NBRM registers. The richest enumerable directories sit elsewhere: MASIT's per-company member pages (including KIBS, the interbank clearing house, and fintech members such as Finelevate) and the Chamber's numbered здруженија/групации URL space, notably the Групација на небанкарски финансиски друштва which captures leasing, pension, insurance and card-processing actors like Casys. AmCham (130+ members, five tiers) and the Foreign Investors Council add the foreign-capital slice, while OEMVP and the German AHK chamber remain partly unmapped. The key unresolved item is MBA's Комисии/SKIBOR subtree: the cell's central hypothesis — that the payments-committee chair reveals institutional weight — could not be tested because no committee-chair page was reached in this pass.

The MK public-procurement route is highly centralised: ESJN (e-nabavki.gov.mk), operated by the Биро за јавни набавки under the Ministry of Finance, is the mandatory publication point for notices, award decisions and 'известување за склучен договор' from every contracting authority, with an institution-level concluded/realised-contracts grid and CSV/open-data extracts alongside it. The named-people signal this cell targets — buying-unit contact officers, winning economic operators, and key-expert requirements — sits mostly inside PDF tender dossiers, DKZJN appeal decisions and donor ToRs (TED/IPA, UNDP-MKD individual-consultant notices such as the National Sustainable Finance & Institutional Specialist role) rather than in search-indexed HTML. Two practical constraints shape collection: the portal returned HTTP 403 to a plain fetch (bot-defended, presumed live) so a headless/session-aware crawler is required, and general web search does not index individual MK notices, so discovery must run through the portal's own search plus open-data exports. Independent corroboration and prioritisation come from the Center for Civil Communications monitoring reports, the State Audit Office, Open Finance transaction data and international aggregators (paywalled, do not scrape).

For North Macedonia's private banks, vendor relationships surface almost exclusively on the supplier side, and this pass confirmed it empirically: bank-side queries for 'добавувач/систем интегратор' returned nothing, while supplier-side sources yielded named engagements (ASEE's Sparkasse/Ohridska banka merger integration; CaSys as the sole MK card processor certified by Mastercard/Visa/Diners and approved by UnionPay; KIBS as digital-certificate and open-banking supplier to MK banks; Interspace's explicit 'Клиенти и препораки' page). The strongest recurring hiring signal is the country-tagged G+D Netcetera Skopje job feed, backed by MK job boards (Vrabotuvanje, Joberty, Kariera) and MASIT's ~160-member directory as the actor-map pointer. Two structural limits shape the cell: security-conscious integrators such as InterWorks refuse on principle to publish client references, and vendor leadership moves are effectively LinkedIn-only, with no MK-language appointment announcements found for the largest Skopje delivery centre. Highest-value next moves are wiring in the Central Registry of RSNM for registry-grade management and financial data, and systematising the 'bank PDF supplier fingerprint' technique across all MK banks.

North Macedonia has no domestic fintech conference of its own, so the A5 'speaker list as org chart' signal concentrates in four places: the AETM Regional eCommerce Conference in Skopje (nine editions, per-year speaker archives carrying exact title+employer for bank cards/payments managers), the National Bank's own conference and workshop pages (including at least one downloadable participant list for a payments and market-infrastructures conference), the Macedonian Banking Association's presidency and news pages (effectively a live roster of sitting bank CEOs), and regional forums such as the Balkan Payment Forum and the EACHA annual conference that KIBS hosted in Skopje. Training and academia add a second, slower layer: ABIT — the Macedonian banking academy, EBTN member and now an EFPA-linked regional certification hub — plus the UKIM Faculty of Economics Chair of Finance, UACS's finance and banking programmes and internship calls, and an ACAMS North Macedonia presence for the AML/compliance segment. Two assumptions in the cell brief did not survive: there is no individual 'banker of the year' award for MK (only institution-level The Banker / Global Finance awards, repeatedly to Komercijalna Banka) and no national CFA society, while the Seavus event series appears defunct — MASIT's Business ICT Forum and the Skopje AI Tech Summit are the live vendor-side substitutes. The main risks to this cell are archival (organisers delete prior-year speaker pages, which is exactly what title-drift analysis needs) and linguistic (Albanian-language events and faculties fall outside the run's mk/en scope).

Workforce-level organisation change in North Macedonian banking is documented across three separable layers: a labour-side layer centred on СФОМ (sfom.mk, the finance-sector union covering banks, insurers and the central bank — note the seed name 'Синдикат на работниците од финансиското работење/СФР' does not exist and must be corrected), with ССМ and the government social-partnership portal carrying the tripartite collective-agreement frame; a regulatory/statistical layer where NBRSM's quarterly banking-system reports and indicator tables carry employees, branches and ATMs per bank, backed by State Statistical Office NACE-K employment and wage series; and a corporate-disclosure layer of MSE issuer announcements, bank annual reports and — the highest-frequency and most under-used signal — public branch-locator pages whose month-over-month diffs expose network consolidation before anyone narrates it. The main access risk is nbrm.mk returning 403 to plain fetches (bot defence, not a dead resource), and the main evidence gap is that mk-language media searches surfaced no MK-specific bank layoff or branch-closure reporting at all, so this cell should be collected from registries, regulator tables and corporate pages rather than from news. Unresolved: whether СФОМ is affiliated to ССМ or UNI Europa Finance, whether the Labour Inspectorate publishes annual reports online, and an entirely missing Albanian-language and Central Registry pass.

For North Macedonia, the international-monitor layer splits cleanly in two: rating agencies and IFIs (Fitch, Moody's, IMF Article IV, World Bank/FSAP, EBRD, EC enlargement reports, EBA, BIS) give authoritative but slow, institution-level commentary on supervisory capacity, fit-and-proper regimes and board governance — they almost never name an individual MK bank executive — while the wire/trade layer (SeeNews above all, plus bne IntelliNews, S&P Global MI and The Banker) is where actual named appointments surface in English, usually paywalled but with free headline metadata and a usable Reuters/SEE News syndication mirror on TradingView. Local-language search proved essential and outperformed the English seeds: bankarstvo.mk, biznisvesti.mk and nezavisen.mk report supervisory-board session numbers, dates, and crucially predecessor-successor pairs that the English wires omit, and the Службен весник plus NBRNM's 'Одлука за издавање согласности' define the legal paper trail every appointment must leave. The archive leg of the cell is intact in principle but unproven in practice here: the Wayback CDX API was unreachable from this environment (a tooling restriction, not a dead source), so no archived 'Управен и Надзорен одбор' pages could be enumerated — this should be the first thing the collection pipeline retries. Two seed-named outlets, Balkan Insight and Emerging Europe, and Finextra's regional desk showed no MK banking-personnel coverage at all, and no international source covers rank-and-file hiring, leaving the 'jobs/hiring' topics of this run essentially uncovered by this cell.

North Macedonia's commercial banks are the primary publishers of their own leadership and organisational news, but the quality of that channel is sharply tiered. NLB Банка (year-indexed press archive back to 2016), Стопанска банка (a /novosti/ feed plus a fully named Management and Supervisory Board page) and Комерцијална банка (separate управен одбор, надзорен одбор, акционерско собрание and вработување pages) are strong, structured first-party sources; Шпаркасе and УНИБанка expose enumerable AGM-session archives that carry the statutory board-election decisions. At the smaller end — Централна кооперативна, Silk Road, ТТК and ProCredit (which lives at pcb.mk, not the assumed procreditbank.com.mk) — there is often no dated newsroom at all, and governance signals survive only as loose PDFs, so the Македонска берза issuer pages and the trade portal bankarstvo.mk are essential pointer/mirror layers. Practically all personnel and AGM content is Macedonian-only, with Cyrillic-encoded or query-string URLs and PDF-first delivery, so collection for this cell must be built Cyrillic-first with per-domain URL normalisation.

For MSE-listed banks, leadership and organisation changes surface through a genuinely machine-listable channel: the Macedonian Stock Exchange's SEI-NET stream, available as a whole-market RSS feed (mse.mk/mk/rss/seinet) and as per-issuer feeds, with items labelled by statutory disclosure category — 'Промени во органите на управување и раководење' being the exact key for this cell — and linking to the filed PDF on seinet.com.mk. Only seven banks carry that obligation (Комерцијална, НЛБ, Стопанска Скопје, АЛТА/Стопанска Битола, ТТК, УНИ, ЦКБ), so the feed is high-precision but covers barely half the sector. A material correction to the cell premise: prior approval of bank management- and supervisory-board members is granted by the National Bank under Законот за банките and the 'Одлука за издавање согласности', not by the Securities Commission, whose consent power runs to brokerage-house directors; КХВ also publishes no dated decisions register, only annual reports. Practical design: MSE RSS as the fast trigger, NBRM supervision and Central Registry тековна состојба as the confirmation and unlisted-bank layer, Macedonian business media as corroboration only.

North Macedonia's payments-ecosystem labour market has three distinct source layers. The authoritative layer is regulatory and registry-based — the NBRSM registers of e-money institutions, payment institutions and payment-system operators, plus the Central Registry (crm.com.mk) whose управител/board change filings are the only legal ground truth for leadership moves — but nbrm.mk is bot-defended (HTTP 403 on plain fetch) and CRM is partly fee-based, so both need engineered collectors rather than simple crawls. The company layer is thin but high-value: KIBS AD Skopje and КаСис/CaSys are the two infrastructure employers, joined since the 2022 Law on Payment Services by a young licensed PSP/EMI population (Paysera Македонија, Iute Pay, Флекс пеј) and foreign entrants such as Nayax. The media layer carries most of the early signal and is almost entirely Macedonian-language — bankarstvo.mk (which reprints payments job ads verbatim), faktor.mk (board/governance churn at KIBS), mkd.mk and pari.com.mk (licence grants), and ecommerce.mk interviews that reliably pin name-to-title for payments executives; there is no dedicated MK appointments column for non-bank payments, so leadership tracking must be reconstructed rather than subscribed to.

For North Macedonia, scheme-side coverage is highly asymmetric: Mastercard is well documented and directly collectable - it opened a Skopje representative office, runs MK inside a Bulgaria-anchored Bulgaria/North Macedonia/Albania/Kosovo country-manager cluster under a Southeast Europe general manager, and announces these moves through its own newsroom, the AmCham Bulgaria channel, SeeNews and MK-language outlets. Visa by contrast exposes no MK-specific country role at all, only CEMEA-level structure, and its name collides so badly with travel-visa content that any automated query needs forced disambiguation. Vendor-side, Printec is the clearest case with a named MK country lead and a dedicated MK entity page, ASEE (ex-ASSECO SEE) is trackable through MK registry aggregators and MK-language tech/banking press, while Diebold Nixdorf, NCR Atleos, Nexi, Worldline, BPC, OpenWay, Wise, Revolut and MoneyGram produced no MK-scoped personnel signal - a finding that itself reads as absence of a local team rather than a search failure. The practical spine of this cell is therefore four channels - corporate newsrooms, SeeNews, the Macedonian-language pair bankarstvo.mk and it.mk plus the vrabotuvanje.com.mk job board, and manually reviewed LinkedIn - with the MK Central Registry and Albanian-language media as the two most valuable additions not yet secured.

North Macedonia's vacancy landscape for banking and payments is dominated by three general boards — Vrabotuvanje.com.mk (by far the largest, with a usable 'Banking' position filter), Pazar3.mk and KARIERA.mk — flanked by smaller ones (Apliciraj.mk, Vraboti.se, ESI.mk) and by the statutory AVRSM register at e-rabota.av.gov.mk, which is authoritative but locked behind an ASP.NET postback UI. The standout discovery is bankarstvo.mk/category/kariera/, a sector trade site that republishes the vacancy announcements of essentially every MK bank plus processors like CaSys and fintechs like Iute, giving a ready-made, pre-filtered banking vacancy feed; fakulteti.mk, pari.com.mk and the UKIM economics and electrical-engineering faculty pages fill in the vendor- and graduate-recruitment side. Local-language querying was essential and productive for AML, payment-operations and card roles, but produced no hits at all on the highest-value programme keywords (instant payments, ISO 20022), indicating those ads live inside the boards' internal search rather than in web indexes. The two structural weaknesses to plan around are the absence of any historical ad archive — nothing is recoverable once an ad expires, so own-archiving must start on day one — and the fact that leadership moves are only visible on LinkedIn, which cannot be collected automatically.

North Macedonia's professional-network layer is real but largely un-crawlable: MK banks and CaSys maintain active LinkedIn company pages (NLB, TTK, UNIBanka, ProCredit, Capital, Post Bank, CaSys confirmed), yet member-level 'new role' posts — the cell's core hypothesis — never surfaced in open web search in either English or Macedonian, so this signal is reachable only from inside a logged-in LinkedIn session or a licensed LinkedIn data product. The recruiter sub-layer is smaller than the brief assumed: DEKRA Arbeit Macedonia, vrabotuvanje.com, headhunting.mk, ESC Macedonia and Motivi are the local actors (no Manpower MK entity was found), and none publish named placements — they yield open-mandate demand signals rather than confirmed appointments, with Stanton Chase Belgrade covering MK for regional C-suite searches. The practical compensating strength is that MK institutions themselves publish unusually precise formal records — Komercijalna banka's 'Оглас за избор на Директор' notices, Stopanska's and the Development Bank's governance pages — which, combined with bankarstvo.mk's appointment and vacancy feed, give a datable ground truth to validate anything LinkedIn suggests. Recommended posture: treat LinkedIn as a manual monthly human routine with quarterly headcount snapshots, anchor every claim on the institutional record, and get a legal position on person-level storage before scaling.

North Macedonia's business-media layer for banking personnel intelligence is small, concentrated, and unexpectedly well-instrumented at the top: Bankarstvo.mk is a genuine sector-specialist that publishes named board appointments, regulator consents and mandate expiries almost as a matter of record, with Kapital Media Group, Faktor.mk, Pari.com.mk and eMagazin.mk forming a solid second ring that adds interviews, executive profiles and 'why he left' context. Time.mk is the practical entry point for the whole ecosystem, clustering the same appointment across every outlet and doubling as an outlet-discovery mechanism, while Portalb.mk (plus Alsat, Telegrafi's MK vertical and Gazeta KOHA) covers the Albanian-language market that mk-only searching misses — though in this pass that Albanian layer proved far stronger on public-institution appointments than on commercial-bank ones. Two structural weaknesses dominate: no outlet runs a dedicated people-moves rubric, so collection must be keyword-driven over broad economy sections with a validated mk/sq/en alias table; and layoff/restructuring reporting could not be surfaced at all with the assumed vocabulary, leaving the organisation-change branch of A5 the least instrumented part of this cell. Every media appointment story observed ultimately derives from an NBRM consent decision or an MSE issuer filing, so this cell should be operated as the context and biography layer on top of those primary records rather than as the fact-of-record itself.

Leadership and reorganisation news for MK's systemic banks originates upstream at the parents: NLB (Ljubljana), Steiermärkische Sparkasse/Erste (Graz/Vienna), Türkiye Halk Bankası (Ankara, via KAP), ProCredit Holding (Frankfurt) and Central Cooperative Bank (Sofia), with Silk Road Capital (Zurich) behind Silk Road Bank; OTP has no MK presence. The strongest, openly crawlable feeds are NLB's press-release and investor-news sections, ProCredit Holding's ad-hoc news and annual/disclosure PDFs, and Steiermärkische's Graz press archive, while the Turkish and Bulgarian legs require direct polling of KAP and BSE-Sofia X3News rather than web search. The MK-side confirmation layer — nlb.mk's Соопштенија за јавност, NBRM согласност decisions, and SEINet issuer filings — is essential because an appointment only takes effect on NBRM approval, and MK-language trade media (bankarstvo.mk, kapital.mk, faktor.mk) reliably surface moves that parents do not headline. Main risks are JS-gated disclosure platforms (SEINet, KAP), unconfirmed news paths on the two subsidiary sites, and stale ownership metadata in commercial aggregators.
