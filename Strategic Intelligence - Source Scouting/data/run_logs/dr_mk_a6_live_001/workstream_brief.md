For North Macedonia, the legally mandated procurement record is concentrated in one system — the ЕСЈН at e-nabavki.gov.mk, operated by the Биро за јавни набавки under the Ministry of Finance — which publishes plans, notices, award decisions and concluded contracts for every contracting authority including НБРСМ. It is rich but hostile to collection: a plain fetch of the public-access page returned 403 (bot defence suspected, not dead), award content sits behind a JavaScript grid and is effectively invisible to web search, and there is no documented public API — only an undocumented dossier endpoint with Id/Level parameters and ASMX services, plus a reported machine-readable republication of the concluded-contracts module on data.gov.mk that still needs verifying. The highest-value surrounding layer is the corroboration ring: ДКЖЈН appeals decisions (which expose losing bidders and annulled awards), Државен завод за ревизија audit reports (which narrate IT and payment-system contracts in detail), contracting authorities' own tender-document mirrors (fuller technical specifications), and the Центар за граѓански комуникации / opendata.mk monitoring output for case studies. Next steps should be technical rather than exploratory: prove a headless read path into the portal, verify the open dataset's schema and freshness, and build the Macedonian banking/payments keyword and CPV filter set that this cell currently lacks.

Donor- and EU-financed procurement touching North Macedonian financial infrastructure is overwhelmingly English-first and splits across four layers: mandated gazettes (TED, which formally covers the Republic of North Macedonia, and the EU Funding & Tenders Portal for directly-managed IPA), IFI-specific portals (ECEPP — now the sole EBRD channel after migration off ebrd.com — plus World Bank STEP-derived open data and procurement-plan documents, WBIF for blended EIB/EBRD/KfW financing, and UN channels), the national mirror layer (CFCD at the Ministry of Finance for indirectly-managed IPA, and e-Nabavki which republishes World Bank/EBRD/EU-fund notices in Macedonian), and indirect reveal layers (EBRD/EIB project registers, commercial-bank IFI credit-line pages, Macedonian Stock Exchange announcements, and State Audit Office reports). The single richest machine-readable target is the World Bank Finances One contract-award dataset for North Macedonia, which carries supplier identity and value; the strongest confirmed on-topic instance found this run is the WBG/National Bank of North Macedonia RFx 0002007835 'Electronic Payments in North Macedonia' (April 2024). Macedonian-language searching produced no local-language donor-tender index — confirming the cell's English-first premise — but did surface the CFCD/IPA contracting authority and the reveal-indirectly layer that no English index carries. Priority next steps are exercising the TED and ECEPP native search facets, locating the National Bank's own procurement pages, and building a historical vendor league table from the World Bank and OpenTender bulk data.

North Macedonia's hiring-signal landscape is small, ephemeral and heavily intermediated: a handful of general boards (Vrabotuvanje.com.mk, Pazar3.mk, Vraboti.se, Kariera.mk) plus one sector-specific aggregator, Bankarstvo.mk, which republishes nearly every bank vacancy and is the highest-leverage single feed for this cell. Product-level disclosure splits by language — Macedonian ads describe duties generically ('Администратор на картичен систем', 'Дирекција за апликации'), while Latin-script product names (Oracle FLEXCUBE, MS SQL Server, ISO 8583) surface mainly in English LinkedIn/vendor ads and in individual CVs, of which the LinkedIn corpus is login-gated and ToS-restricted. The strongest direct sources are the self-published career pages of the banks (Stopanska, NLB, Komercijalna), the processor CaSys, and the vendors ASEE/Asseco SEE Skopje and Printec, complemented by JobMatch.mk's automated skill-frequency statistics and Joberty's employee-declared tech stacks. Because ads are deleted at deadline and no national vacancy archive exists, this cell only works as a continuous capture-at-publication monitor keyed to an employer watchlist built from the NBRSM bank list and the MASIT member directory — retrospective search will not reconstruct it.

North Macedonia has no standalone domestic banking/fintech conference brand; the vendor-courting signal is spread across four domestic platforms — the National Bank's Conference on Payments and Market Infrastructures (which publishes named participant lists, the richest MK artefact found), the Macedonian Stock Exchange annual conference, MASIT's ICT annual conference plus its specialised SEPA event, and the AETM regional e-commerce conference with its explicit sponsor tiers (Mastercard as general partner, Kasys and Payton gold, Komercijalna banka as sponsor). Regional exposure with MK relevance runs through INFINTECH (Montenegro), Money Motion (Zagreb, with NLB among 2026 backers) and the Balkan Payment Forum (Tirana), each of which maintains public speaker, agenda and sponsor-tier pages. Award signals are almost entirely international restatements — The Banker 'Bank of the Year', Global Finance and EMEA Finance for banks, with E-commerce Awards MK the only domestic programme touching payments. The main weaknesses are that sponsor rosters were mostly evidenced through mk trade press (bankarstvo.mk) rather than scraped primary pages, that no MK-affiliated speaker at a regional event was confirmed this run, and that nothing yet archives sponsor walls year over year.

Government digital-finance policy in North Macedonia is split across four authoritative publishers: the Ministry of Finance (fiscal strategy, treasury, procurement bureau), the Public Revenue Office (fiscalisation and the national e-Faktura mandate effective October 2026), the Ministry of Digital Transformation (e-services portal, digital ID, ICT/SMART-MK 2030 strategies), and the National Bank together with the bank-owned clearing house KIBS (MIPS ISO 20022 modernisation and the instant-payments platform). The highest-leverage collection targets are structural rather than editorial: ЕНЕР for draft laws and impact assessments before adoption, е-набавки for tender and awarded-contract records that name winning vendors, and the Official Gazette (best reached via institutional mirrors) for binding text. Two authoritative registers that would directly enumerate competitors — UJP's approved fiscal-device suppliers and any accredited e-invoicing provider list — were not surfaced by search and need a targeted crawl of ujp.gov.mk. Vendor explainer sites and MIA/Makfax reporting are useful early-warning proxies but must always be corroborated against UJP, ENER or NBRM before any claim is treated as fact.

North Macedonia's audit-and-enforcement trail is anchored by four institutional registers that all publish in Macedonian: the State Audit Office (dzr.mk) with final audit reports plus a forward-looking planned-audits list and a dedicated central-bank audit area; the Commission for Protection of Competition (kzk.gov.mk) with separate administrative, misdemeanour, court and merger-notification streams — the merger notifications alone already disclose bank ownership moves such as the 2026 AIK Grup / Podravska Banka filing; the State Commission for Public Procurement Appeals (dkzjn.gov.mk) where losing bidders name rival IT and payment vendors and disputed specifications; and the e-procurement portal (e-nabavki.gov.mk) that carries the underlying award and concluded-contract notices. Around these sit useful secondary and complementary sources: the anti-corruption commission (dksk.mk), which opens cases directly off audit findings and has a cooperation memorandum with the National Bank, the data-protection agency (azlp.mk), the FOI platform slobodenpristap.mk as a legal route to unpublished documents, the consumer organisation ОПМ for grievances against financial-service providers, and Macedonian media as a change-detector over registers that are poorly indexed. Two structural weaknesses stand out: the National Bank publishes supervision rules and licence/consent decisions but no visible per-institution sanctions register, and no MK consumer-complaint statistics by bank/PSP were found — both likely require the Official Gazette, court records or FOI to reconstruct. Practical access notes: dzr.mk returned 403 to an automated fetch (bot-defended, unverified rather than dead), the English-language KZK decisions path 404s while the Cyrillic path is live, and the appeals commission appears under three hosts whose canonical instance is unresolved.

North Macedonia's non-bank payments landscape is small and unusually legible: a single dominant domestic card processor (CaSys/cPay) sits behind a handful of acquiring banks, and the regulator (NBRNM) publishes registers for e-money institutions, payment institutions and fast-money-transfer providers that together define the challenger universe. The most partnership-revealing sources are not the banks but the intermediaries — CaSys, Monri/Payten, integrator plugin docs — plus the e-commerce association AETM, whose public e-merchant register enumerates merchants with live card acquiring. The clearest recent structural change is the 2025 arrival of the first non-bank EMI (Iute Pay), which makes the NBRNM e-money register a high-frequency watch item, while a second domestic wallet (MKPEJ) remains traceable only through press. Main gaps are company-registry resolution, per-operator remittance mapping, app-store telemetry and Albanian-language coverage.

For North Macedonia, the regional/international layer splits cleanly into three lanes: subscription newswires (SeeNews above all, with bne IntelliNews and The Paypers as supplements) that time-stamp bank deals, capital moves and scheme milestones in English; open IFI/EU institutional reporting (EC enlargement SWDs, IMF Article IV, World Bank policy notes and the joint NBRM electronic-payments-acceptance work, EBRD/EIB digitalisation lending, BIS-republished NBRM speeches) that gives citable, dated structure on the payments and regulatory environment; and the EPC SEPA participant register, which is the single hardest structured indicator of which MK banks have actually completed SEPA-readiness projects. MK-language material is almost absent from this layer - the aggregators write in English - the one exception being the Ministry of European Affairs' Macedonian translations of the EC report, which are best used as a glossary source. The archive lane (Wayback CDX over MK bank and vendor domains) remains the highest-value unexploited route for recovering deleted vendor reference lists and pre-merger bank pages, but it could not be executed here because this environment's fetch tool refuses web.archive.org; that is a local tool restriction, not evidence the archive is unavailable.

The National Bank of RNM (НБРСМ / NBRSM, nbrm.mk) is the single richest and most authoritative node for the North Macedonian payments landscape: under the PSD2/EMD2-transposing Закон за платежни услуги и платни системи (Off. Gazette 90/2022, in force 2023) it is legally obliged to maintain and publish three enumerating registers — payment institutions, e-money institutions, and payment-system operators — alongside bank/savings-house directories, payment statistics, the payments rulebook index, an annual payments-developments report series, and the 2023-2027 inter-regulator FinTech strategy. Content is published primarily in Macedonian with a predictable `-en.nspx` English mirror that NBRSM itself declares non-authoritative, so mk-first collection is mandatory. The critical operational constraint is that every nbrm.mk URL tested returned HTTP 403 to both WebFetch and curl-with-browser-UA, including static PDFs; the pages are current and indexed, so this is bot defence rather than link rot, and collection will require a real browser engine or the statutory FOI route. Priority next steps are a headless-browser pass over the four register/hub URLs, isolating the limited-network exemption register, and pinning the NBStat API for machine-readable payment statistics.

MK commercial banks form a two-layer source landscape: each bank's own Macedonian-language newsroom and annual-report section (NLB's year-filtered 2016-2026 press archive and Stopanska's /novosti feed are the richest; Komercijalna, Sparkasse, TTK and CKB expose reports reliably but news less so), sitting above SEINet/Macedonian Stock Exchange as a single regulated pointer into every listed bank's price-sensitive filings. Critically, no bank in this cell publishes a procurement or tender page — procurement is private, confirming the cell rationale — and the bank releases found here describe new capability without ever naming the supplier (the lone exception being KIBS in a Stopanska item). Vendor identity therefore has to be recovered indirectly: vendor-side collateral (Asseco SEE hosts a named Komercijalna banka case study and MK delivery news), mobile-app package fingerprints (NLB mKlik ships under an Asseco 'jimba' Android namespace), open-banking/API registries, and trade media bankarstvo.mk and it.mk which reprint releases the banks do not archive. Next-pass priorities are pinning the newsroom paths for Halkbank, ProCredit, UNIBanka and CKB, testing SEINet's extractability, and mining MK bank IT job ads for installed-platform names.

North Macedonia's payment-rail landscape is concentrated in three named operators: KIBS AD Skopje (retail interbank clearing, licensed instant-payments operator since Sept 2025, plus the OneID trust/eID business), the central bank's MIPS RTGS (live since 2001, ~20 participants, currently under an EU-supported replacement programme with a first test cycle in July 2026), and CaSys / Internacionalen Karticen Sistem AD — the country's only Visa/Mastercard-certified independent processor, also processing Diners Club, and itself a settlement system for domestic-brand cards. Operator websites are open and rich on service catalogues (switching, ACS/3-D Secure, personalisation, ATM/POS management) but publish no member or client lists, so participant and supplier mapping depends on the NBRNM cluster — which is the highest-value node in the cell yet returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching and must be collected via a browser session. The reliable secondary layer is MK-language trade press and associations (bankarstvo.mk, mba.mk e-bulletin, ecommerce.mk, ecommerce4all.mk) plus registry aggregators for ownership and financials; regional gateway/terminal vendors (Monri, Payten/Asseco SEE) are present but publish no MK-specific references. The largest open gaps are procurement channels around the MIPS modernisation and bank-level acquiring disclosure.

Vendor-published customer references naming North Macedonian banks are real but sparse and asymmetrically distributed: the richest disclosures come from regionally-embedded suppliers with Skopje operations (Printec, Nextsense, ASEE/Payten, CaSys, G+D Netcetera) rather than from global OEM newsrooms, which were silent on MK in this pass. The strongest confirmed pointers are Printec's MK country page (NCR SelfServ ATMs at HALKBANK AD Skopje), Nextsense's announcement of Komercijalna Banka's digital onboarding platform, and Entrust's Google Pay push-provisioning release for the same bank; core-banking attribution (Temenos T24, Oracle Mantas AML at Stopanska Banka) currently rests only on a third-party technographics aggregator and needs vendor- or bank-side corroboration. Macedonian-language 'case study' vocabulary is largely unproductive — local wins are published as news items, association coverage (ecommerce.mk) and LinkedIn posts — so collection should target 'соопштение/партнерство/во соработка со' plus bank brand names, and exploit high-signal indirect sources such as app-store package IDs and vendor partner directories. Priority next moves: a per-bank sweep across the eight uncovered MK banks, per-segment sweeps for AML/card-issuance/HSM vendors, and a public-procurement cross-check to harden vendor-bank pairings.

The MK local-integrator channel is documented far better in Macedonian trade media and business directories than on the integrators' own websites: zk.mk's 'Електронско банкарство' category and MASIT's member registry are the two best pointer sources, while bankarstvo.mk, it.mk and smartportal.mk regularly name the vendor behind a bank's launch (e.g. Komercijalna Banka's new e-banking portal attributed to Nextsense). Only three local actors publish genuine reference material of their own — Nextsense (news items naming Komercijalna Banka and ProCredit), Aspekt (a full customers page of core-banking and microfinance case studies) and Netcetera (a deep-linked Halkbank North Macedonia wallet reference) — whereas the named seed actors Semos, Duna and Edusoft expose no bank references at all and must be tracked indirectly. The highest-yield untapped techniques are vendor partner directories filtered to North Macedonia (Thales already lists Nextsense) and app-store publisher metadata, which attributes MK bank apps to their real supplier (NLB mKlik ships under an Asseco package namespace). The two serious holes are the ATM/POS hardware reseller-and-service channel, for which mk-language search returned nothing at all, and the unresolved corporate domain of ASEE North Macedonia, probably the largest banking-software supplier in the local channel.

North Macedonia's corporate-filing landscape is highly centralised: the Централен регистар на РСМ (crm.com.mk) operates the Trade Register, the Register of Other Legal Entities, the Register of Annual Accounts and the Register of Beneficial Owners in one institution, so essentially every payments/banking vendor with an MK presence — including branches (подружници) of foreign companies, which must file through a registration agent and publish annual accounts — is captured there. Basic entity data (ЕМБС, legal form, status, activity, address) is positioned as free and public under Open Government commitments, but the operationally valuable layers are gated: annual accounts and related-entity confirmations are fee-based, bulk extraction runs through the paid Дистрибутивен систем, and beneficial ownership is available only via formal fee-bearing procedure pending an OGP commitment to open it. The practical collection design is therefore a name/ЕМБС watchlist against CRM's free profile service, backed by a licensed aggregator (CompanyWall) for financial statements at scale, with the State Statistical Office's active-business-entity series by НКД as the population denominator and MSE issuer filings, МАСИТ's member directory and OpenCorporates as pointer layers that convert soft actor names into hard registry records. Main open risks: the exact free-search endpoint, the correct NKD codes for this sector, and unread licensing terms.

North Macedonia's association layer is small, concentrated and unusually collectable: MASIT (masit.org.mk) offers a paginated ICT member directory of ~80+ vendors with per-company profile pages, and the Macedonian Banking Association (mba.mk) covers the entire licensed banking demand side plus KIBS and the Credit Bureau, publishing committee rosters, quarterly e-bulletins and the SKIBOR reference-bank panel. The Economic Chamber of North Macedonia (mchamber.mk) adds cross-sector groupings — financial organisations, non-bank financial companies, insurance brokers — that widen the buyer map beyond banks, while ecommerce.mk supplies a payments-side merchant register and steady coverage of instant payments and SEPA accession. There is no standalone incorporated fintech association with a public member list; fintech representation is dispersed across MASIT, the chamber's non-bank group, the alternative-lenders body AFD, and the regional Balkan Fintech Association. Highest-yield future collection is event-driven — association forums, MoUs (e.g. MASIT–SETPE Digital Bridge 2026) and sponsor lists surface partnerships well before contracts appear; the main blind spots are Albanian-language chamber sources and historical roster depth.

North Macedonia's MK-language business press is a small but workable layer: a specialised vertical (Bankarstvo.mk) plus business portals (Kapital, Faktor, Ekonomija i biznis, Pari, Bloomberg Adria's Skopje hub) and an ICT outlet (IT.mk) that is typically first with fintech licences and app launches. TIME.mk is the key pointer asset — it aggregates essentially the whole MK media field and its outlet directory is the fastest route to portals not yet in the registry. Albanian-language coverage (Portalb, Zhurnal, Aktuale, Telegrafi's MK economy stream) is a genuine second channel, carrying public-payments and diaspora-banking items that MK-only monitoring misses. The critical limitation observed is that MK media report the bank and the customer-facing feature but almost never name the technology supplier, so this cell should be treated as a high-value timing and actor-mapping signal that must be joined to vendor PR, procurement and annual-report sources for vendor attribution.
