Národná banka Slovenska is unambiguously the legally mandated compiler of Slovak monetary, banking and payment statistics, but its public statistics estate is split across three distinct surfaces: the modern nbs.sk/statisticke-udaje portal (MFI balance sheets, time series, interest rates, macroeconomic database, OpenStats dashboards, SDDS Plus, methodology and code lists), the Payments section (SIPS and TARGET payment-system transaction statistics, plus explainer pages on cards), and a decaying legacy www.nbs.sk file store holding pre-redesign historical payment-system tables. Critically, no standalone NBS dataset for card counts, POS/ATM terminal counts or card transaction volumes was found on nbs.sk — those NBS-compiled series are disseminated in machine-readable form through the ECB Data Portal payments statistics datasets (PSD/PCP/PAY, reference area SK), which should therefore be treated as the primary retrieval channel for this cell's headline topics. No NBS SDMX or documented API was found, so collection from NBS itself will rely on scheduled file harvesting driven by the published data release calendar, with headless-browser or endpoint reverse-engineering for the interactive OpenStats and macroeconomic-database tools.

The NBS non-statistical layer is a single-publisher but multi-channel landscape, all reachable from one hub at nbs.sk/publikacie/: serial reports (výročná správa, správa o finančnej stabilite, supervision activity reports), a dense commentary tier (analytické komentáre, rýchle komentáre, makroprudenciálny komentár, čo hovoria dáta, blog), a methodological tier (odbor štatistiky metodika, metodické informácie a oznamy, supervisory metodické usmernenia), and a high-volume press stream (aktuality) that carries the operative payment-market interventions such as the contactless limit rise to EUR 50 and the October 2025 opening of SIPS/TARGET-SK to non-bank PSPs. Cash-usage narrative is not consolidated in any dedicated cash report — it is distributed across annual-report emission chapters, the bankovky a mince section, and staff blog pieces — and there is no NBS narrative series dedicated to ATM or POS acceptance infrastructure, which is the sharpest gap for this workstream. Two structural quirks matter for collection: a partial EN mirror at nbs.sk/en/publications/ that leaves some material SK-only, and a still-serving legacy tree (www.nbs.sk, /_img/documents/) that holds the pre-redesign historical PDFs and the discontinued Biatec journal. All listed URLs are candidate locations with stated confidence; none were verified for liveness in this pass.

Slovakia's procurement disclosure stack is unusually rich for this cell: ÚVO's Vestník verejného obstarávania (with an XML open-data feed and profile registry) covers notices, the Centrálny register zmlúv publishes full signed contracts of state bodies and municipalities under Act 211/2000, and the EKS electronic marketplace captures the high-frequency sub-threshold purchases where terminal, cash-register and cash-handling quantities are itemised; TED duplicates all above-threshold SK notices in structured, machine-readable form. The highest-value single actors are NBS (its own zmluvy/objednávky/faktúry disclosures on cash-cycle equipment), Slovenská pošta via its ERANET instance, and state-owned SZRB whose full contract set is readable in CRZ. Aggregators (UVOstat, TenderRadar, tender.sk, FinStat) accelerate supplier-concentration analysis but are variously token-gated or paywalled and should not be primary. The binding constraint is not access but filtering: web search does not index individual notices, so collection must be driven by a validated CPV whitelist plus Slovak keyword sets (platobný terminál, bankomat, akceptácia platobných kariet, spracovanie/prevoz hotovosti) applied inside each register's own interface or bulk feed.

For Slovakia, the supplier side splits cleanly into three tiers. Tier one is genuinely productive and SK-language: the acquiring JV Global Payments Slovensko (which publicly quantifies >37,000 terminals and >EUR 2bn annual volume), Printec Slovakia as the NCR/ATM channel (legally the former NCR Slovensko), Euronet as the independent ATM deployer, and SK integrators - Asseco Central Europe, Softec, Millennium, EEA - that publish named bank references covering core banking, card processing, branch systems and CRM. Tier two is the global OEMs (Diebold Nixdorf, NCR Atleos, OpenWay), whose case-study libraries are rich for CEE but yielded no Slovakia-named deployment in this pass. The binding constraint is documented rather than incidental: SK business press reports that banks white-label vendor work and sometimes bar suppliers from naming them, so the highest-value SK-specific quantification is likely to come not from vendor marketing but from official filings - PMU merger decisions, EC case M.10378 on the three largest banks' JV, the NBS entity register, and ORSR/FinStat vendor accounts - with public procurement (UVO/TED) the largest untouched surface.

Slovakia's analytical-intermediary layer for banking and payments splits cleanly by language and channel: English-language commercial research houses (GlobalData, Argus Advisory, Verisk Financial, Statista) sell country cards-and-payments sizing and forecasts with only abstracts free, while rating agencies (Moody's with free rating-action press releases; Fitch/S&P apparently gated) and the IMF FSAP supply the most rigorous free sector metrics. In Slovak, the productive channels are institutional rather than commercial — the IFP think tank at the Ministry of Finance via analyzy.gov.sk, the SBA's štatistiky a analýzy section with its Ipsos-run perception surveys, the tripartite sector council's SWOT analysis of banking and payment services, and NBS research papers on cash in circulation and digital-euro attitudes. A noisy but high-frequency stratum consists of scheme-commissioned surveys (Mastercard/Visa) distributed through SK press wires and finance portals (oPeniazoch, Finsider, FinReport); these carry usable cash-vs-card-vs-mobile splits but are promotional and must always be traced back to fieldwork details. The biggest remaining gaps are bank-owned economic research units and SK academic repositories, neither of which was covered in this pass.

Slovak payment/ATM statistics surface in trade media through three distinct layers: specialist finance/fintech portals (Finsider.sk, FinReport.sk, Banky.sk, oPeniazoch.sk) that publish recurring data-driven explainers and often obtain per-bank figures directly; news agencies (SITA's vofinanciach desk, TASR via Teraz.sk) that carry the earliest timestamp on bank- and ministry-supplied numbers before wide republication; and daily/business press (Index SME, Denník N, plus the not-yet-captured Trend, HN and Forbes SK) whose deepest reporting sits behind paywalls. The IT-trade angle is thin but real, with TOUCHIT.sk covering ATM modernisation, cardless/NFC withdrawal and scheme survey data via its brandit PR wire. Most media figures trace back to a small number of upstream releases — above all the SBA's 'Štatistika vydávania a prijímania platobných kariet' — so the analytical value of this cell lies precisely in isolating the bank-specific device and fee numbers that journalists obtain by asking banks directly and that never appear in those releases. Principal risks are paywall-restricted access to the highest-credibility outlets and the absence of any dedicated Slovak POS/acquiring trade title, which forces broad, low-yield monitoring.

Slovakia's weak-signal payments surfaces cluster around four families: job boards (Profesia.sk dominant, plus NBS careers and non-bank ATM deployer Euronet Services Slovakia) that expose system names and ATM/POS operating models; conferences without a dedicated payments congress, so figures surface inside New Retail Summit, ITAPA and Finweek agendas and their trade-press recaps; complaint and ADR bodies (NBS consumer protection, Inštitút ARS SBA as the ombudsman's successor, fininfo.sk, and the MH SR ADR register) that count disputes by product; and merchant-side lobbying via Zväz obchodu SR, SAMO and practitioner media such as Podnikajte.sk, which publish concrete card-acceptance cost figures around the mandatory-cashless/QR obligation. Zlatá minca provides an unusually clean annual census of SK card products via its nomination lists. Main weaknesses: the retail associations' primary domains remain unconfirmed, ARS SBA statistics may not be published at all, and conference programmes are ephemeral and need archival capture.

Slovakia's archival and machine-readable long tail for payments splits into three tiers. Tier one is supranational and genuinely API-first: the ECB Data Portal (SDMX 2.1, including the discontinued API-only PSS dataset that preserves pre-break payment/terminal history for SK) and the IMF FAS, plus the ŠÚ SR data.statistics.sk REST API for macro and household context — these are the only surfaces offering repeatable programmatic ingestion today. Tier two is national but file-based: NBS publishes time series, SIPS statistics since 2003, quarterly Štatistický bulletin PDFs since 2011 and Výročná správa PDFs from a predictable /_img/documents/ document store, with OpenStats as a dashboard layer and no evidenced public SDMX endpoint; the national catalogues data.gov.sk / data.slovensko.sk (DCAT-AP-SK, SPARQL) act as the discovery and continuity mirror. Tier three is recovery-only: Wayback captures of retired NBS and SBA pages, legacy www.nbs.sk paths, the NBS institutional archive (which holds records, not statistics), and CRZP theses that reproduce figures whose original sources are gone. Two live continuity risks dominate the cell — the ECB PSS/SDW retirement and the ongoing DATAcube-to-STATdata migration — and the Wayback Machine, the single most important surface here, was unreachable from this agent and must be exercised by the deterministic pass.

The ECB/Eurosystem layer is the strongest and most machine-readable source of Slovak payment, card and ATM/POS statistics: the ECB Data Portal carries harmonised SK rows for payment services, card payments and card-accepting devices under Regulation ECB/2020/59, all reachable through a free SDMX 2.1 REST API with bulk download, plus a discontinued annual dataset holding the pre-2022 back-history. On the demand side, the SPACE consumer payment-diary study (2020/2022/2024 waves, with statistical annexes) is the only harmonised evidence on Slovak cash usage and payment preferences, complemented upstream by ECB Occasional Paper 201 and interpretively by SUERF policy briefs from ECB authors. Fraud and infrastructure are covered by the joint EBA-ECB payment fraud reports (successor to the ECB card fraud series, with country tables) and by the TARGET Services Annual Report, which flags Slovakia as one of the three largest contributors to euro instant payments in TIPS. The main practical constraints are that this layer is English-only in substance (Slovak surfaces only via NBS relays, EUR-Lex SK texts and the localised banknote-circulation page), that the 2021/22 annual-to-semi-annual regime change creates a real series break, and that concrete SK SDMX series keys still need to be enumerated before collection.

For Slovakia, the EU/international aggregator layer outside the ECB splits cleanly into three usable channels: Eurostat's ICT-usage datasets (isoc_ec_ifi20, isoc_ci_ac_i, isoc_ec_ib20) for harmonised demand-side internet-banking and e-commerce series with NUTS2 regional cuts; the World Bank Global Findex plus IMF FAS (mirrored in World Bank Open Data and FRED) for account ownership, card use and ATM/POS access-point density; and the Commission's policy-indicator layer (Digital Decade/DESI, the DG GROW EU Payment Observatory, ECA SR 01/2025) for context and benchmarking. The key structural finding is that BIS CPMI Red Book carries no Slovakia rows — SK is not a CPMI jurisdiction and appears only inside the euro-area aggregate, so BIS is a comparator and a methodology standard here, not a SK data source. Local-language coverage in this cell is thin by nature: only data.europa.eu/sk and Eurostat's Slovak Statistics Explained pages offer genuine sk surfaces, while BIS and the World Bank publish none. Collection should be API-first (Eurostat REST, BIS SDMX, IMF SDMX, World Bank Indicators API) with annual refresh cycles, avoiding commercial re-publishers such as Trading Economics whose terms prohibit systematic collection.

Slovakia's non-central-bank fiscal and statistical bodies form an unusually rich, fully open source landscape for payments intelligence, anchored by two systems: the Financial Administration's ODFS open-data portal, which publishes ~10 recurring eKasa datasets (receipt counts weekly/monthly, turnover in EUR, and both split by self-governing region and SK NACE2 industry code, plus counts of registered online and virtual cash registers), and the Statistical Office's DATAcube database with a JSON-stat/CSV API at data.statistics.sk covering retail and internal-trade turnover, household surveys and ICT/e-commerce use. The critical limitation is that eKasa aggregates are not broken down by tender type, so cash-versus-card at the till must be inferred by using eKasa turnover as a denominator against card-acquiring volumes, or obtained through a freedom-of-information request — the data exists inside the system but is unpublished. A secondary analytical and oversight layer (IFP and ÚHP at MF SR on the VAT gap and tax-collection efficiency, the MF SR cash-payment-limit page, NKÚ's ongoing-audit register and budget opinions, and NR SR explanatory memoranda) supplies interpretation, policy direction and forward-looking impact estimates, with Act No. 384/2025 Z. z. on evidencia tržieb, effective 1 January 2026, as the live regulatory event to watch. Two structural risks for any pipeline: SLOVSTAT is retired and DATAcube is migrating to STATdata, and everything of substance is Slovak-language and distributed as XLSX or PDF rather than via a stable API.

Slovakia's association layer is unusually concentrated: Slovenská banková asociácia (SBA, sbaonline.sk) is effectively the only banking-sector body, covering ~24 members and near-100% of sector assets, and it publishes the monthly 'Štatistika vydávania a prijímania platobných kariet' as a downloadable Excel — card counts, transaction counts and volumes, and ATM/POS terminal transactions compiled by NBS from member data. That release plus the SBA members list (which also exposes infrastructure actors such as Regional Card Processing Centre, Nexi Group and CDCP) is the highest-value pair in this cell. There is no independent domestic clearing house or national card scheme: SIPS retail clearing was operated by NBS and interbank traffic moved to TARGET2/TIPS after euro adoption, so throughput data sits with the central bank and Eurosystem, while the instant-payment scheme layer lives in the jointly-authored Národný plán zavedenia okamžitých platieb and SBA's SEPA/API standards projects. Shared market infrastructure is rounded out by the bank-owned credit registers SBCB/SRBI and NBCB/NRKI (public structural pages only — register content is GDPR-restricted), with ALS SR and the two fintech associations covering the non-bank side.

Slovak commercial banks publish plenty of quantitative material, but almost none of it as structured data: for SLSP, VÚB, Tatra banka, ČSOB SK, 365.bank and Prima banka the recoverable card, ATM, POS, client and digital-transaction figures sit inside výročné správy (increasingly ESEF/iXBRL with 'neoficiálna' PDF twins), NBS Opatrenie 16/2014 Pillar 3 disclosures, sadzobníky/cenníky and dated press releases. Each bank keeps stable, crawlable document paths (vub.sk /document/documents/, tatrabanka.sk /files/archiv/, csob.sk /documents/11005/, 365.bank /media/, primabanka.sk /preview-file/), so a per-bank PDF-harvest plus text-extraction pipeline is the realistic collection method; tatrabanka.sk showed bot defence (403) on automated fetch and needs browser-like access. The two foreign-bank branches, mBank SK and UniCredit CZ&SK, publish no Slovakia-only operational report, leaving a structural gap that must be filled from press statements, branch filings and parent-group segment reporting (Erste for SLSP, RBI for Tatra banka, KBC for ČSOB, Intesa for VÚB), where Erste's Slovakia segment is the most usable quarterly cross-check. The biggest untapped opportunities are the banks' own ATM/branch locator JSON backends for live per-bank ATM fleets, and the acquiring/merchant-services side, which this pass only touched via VÚB's terminal product page.

Slovakia's non-bank payments layer is enumerable but not measurable: the NBS PSD2 register (with an apparent JSON endpoint), Obchodný register and Register účtovných závierok give a complete, authoritative actor list with statutory financials for every payment institution, EMI and acquirer branch, while FinStat makes that joinable at scale. Actual acquiring data, however, is published only as undated marketing claims by individual players — Global Payments (~37k terminals in SK), SKPAY (~28k POS), Comgate (21k merchants), GoPay (7k), Besteron (1.5k e-shops) — with no provider maintaining a recurring statistical release. The only regular acceptance-side series come from the industry bodies, SBA's card issuing-and-acceptance statistics and ZBK's commentary, amplified by SITA and FinReport, which is also where headline coverage metrics (about 16 terminals per 1,000 inhabitants against an EU average near 32; mobile payments about 22% of card transactions) originate. The practical collection design is therefore: register-driven actor enumeration on a monthly diff, annual text-mining of výročné správy, and periodic snapshotting of acquirer and gateway corporate pages, all normalised against NBS/ECB payment statistics that this pass did not yet cover.

For Slovakia, the card schemes are the most productive publishers in this cell: Visa runs a genuine SK-language press centre plus a Mynewsdesk newsroom, while Mastercard has no SK newsroom and reaches the market through Slovak PR-service and trade-media mirrors (TOUCHIT, Finsider, FinReport), which still carry the survey percentages. Wallet providers publish nothing at country level, so Apple/Google Pay penetration in SK must be reconstructed from issuer announcements, media-maintained support trackers, and scheme survey figures. The cash side is thin but concentrated: Loomis SK (ex-G4S) dominates CIT and cash processing and Euronet is the visible independent ATM deployer, with their filed financials acting as proxies for cash-cycle volume; no public NBS register of licensed money processors was found, which is the main structural gap. SBA's monthly card/ATM/POS statistics should serve as the benchmark series against which all scheme marketing claims in this cell are validated.

Slovakia's register layer is unusually well structured for pointer work: NBS runs the statutory PSD2 register with a daily-updated, digitally signed JSON endpoint at subjekty.nbs.sk, backed by a broader supervised-entity database and a register index that also covers creditors, financial agents, CERI disclosures and the new MiCA/CASP licensing stream. Above it, the EBA PSD2 register and ECB MFI/SSM lists give an EU-consistent, bulk-downloadable view including cross-border passporting into SK that the national list understates. Below it, the company layer — ORSR, the statistical RPO with SK NACE codes, RPVS for beneficial ownership, Obchodný vestník for dated entry/exit events, and above all the Register účtovných závierok open API — converts any register-derived entity list into revenue and asset time series, which is what turns this pointer cell into quantitative market-structure output. The main structural gap is that acquiring, ATM deployment and POS operation are not separately licensed and therefore appear in no register; those cells must be built by joining licence lists to NACE-coded entity sets and to NBS payment statistics, and entry/exit history must be manufactured by snapshotting from now on, since no register publishes its own historical states.
