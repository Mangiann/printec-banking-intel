In Slovakia the regulator-gatekeeper thesis holds legally but only partially in data: NBS must consent to bank and payment-institution board members and key function holders under §28 of the Banking Act, and it publishes the operative parts of those decisions in a dated, filterable register (výroky právoplatných rozhodnutí, plus a non-final and an archive tier and pre-2013 PDF compendia) — but as free text inside decisions, not as a person-level dataset, and no dedicated fit-and-proper register was found. The actor spine is strong and largely machine-readable: subjekty.nbs.sk / registre.nbs.sk and the PSD2 register enumerate every licensed bank, branch, payment and e-money institution and agent, mirrored and bulk-downloadable via the EBA credit-institutions and PSD2 central registers, while the ECB SSM entity list decides whether a given bank's appointment is decided in Frankfurt (no person-level publication) or in Bratislava. Because ECB fit-and-proper outcomes are not published by name, the operative confirmation layer for 'who holds which seat' is national: ORSR extracts for current state and Obchodný vestník for the dated change events. Highest-value next steps are confirming machine-readable endpoints on the NBS hosts, enumerating the decision-type facets, and materialising an IČO/ORSR-ID watchlist from the NBS and EBA registers.

Slovakia's statutory record of organisational change is unusually well plumbed but fragmented across four state systems: ORSR (orsr.sk, plus the newer sluzby.orsr.sk portal) holds the dated ground truth on štatutárny orgán, prokúra, dozorná rada, organizačné zložky and share capital; Obchodný vestník publishes projekty premien and general-meeting notices roughly a month ahead of the register entry, making it the earliest legal warning of a merger or restructuring; Register účtovných závierok supplies the annual reports with headcount and governance detail; and RPVS adds verified UBO/control chains for anyone contracting with the state. The critical structural finding is that ORSR itself cannot be searched by SK NACE — the population of SK banks, payment institutions and fintechs must be enumerated through the ŠÚ SR RPO register and its CC BY 4.0 REST API (cross-checked against NBS supervisory registers), then monitored entity-by-entity in ORSR. Because no official change feed exists, collection means diffing: either self-built polling of the state portals, or riding the Slovensko.Digital open-data hub (APIs plus weekly SQL dumps of RPO, Vestník and RÚZ) and, for latency, the paid FinStat monitoring feed, always tracing each event back to the register for its authoritative date. Everything of evidentiary value here is Slovak-language only; English exists solely as a BRIS/e-Justice gateway.

Slovakia's association layer is a strong POINTER substrate for A5: SBA (24 member banks, a named 7-person prezídium tied to CEO seats, and ~14 expert commissions incl. HR and payments) anchors banking, while AOCP, Slovenská FinTech asociácia, ALS, SLASPO and AFISP enumerate the capital-markets, fintech, leasing, insurance and intermediation actor populations. Cross-sector bodies (SAPIE ~170-200 members, ITAS 100+ ICT firms, AmCham 300+ members with a 17-person named board, Britcham, AHK, SOPK, RÚZ, AZZZ) extend the map to the technology vendors and shared-service employers that generate most banking-adjacent hiring signals. The main structural limitation is that governing bodies are published while working-group and committee rosters mostly are not — so the named-individual layer must be reconstructed from standards documents, election announcements and, above all, conference speaker lists, which remain the biggest un-scouted gap. Klub 500 is a dead end as a directory (membership deliberately unpublished) and no standalone Slovak association of payment institutions exists.

Slovakia's events channel for org-chart intelligence has one clear anchor and several reliable satellites: Finweek Bratislava (10th edition, 13-14 Oct 2026) publishes name + title + employer speaker cards that already include NBS section heads and Tatra banka staff, while ZBK (Zdruzenie pre bankove karty) runs the country's only dedicated cards/payments event calendar (five events Sep-Nov 2026 at Crowne Plaza Bratislava) and IBV NBS FinNext covers AML/compliance function holders twice a year. Awards form a parallel dated snapshot: Zlata minca (20 years, 210-member expert jury, 13 banking product categories) plus the Top Slovak Fintech Awards it co-runs with FINAS, and TREND TOP / The Banker 'Banka roka'. On the hiring side, Profesia Days Bratislava (22-23 Apr 2026) exhibitor lists and the AIESEC Narodne dni kariery / Kariera EXPO fairs show which employers are buying stands in SK. Two structural weaknesses: no public SBA banking congress could be found, and no CEE cash-cycle/ATM conference exists for 2026, so those functions stay invisible through events; also, listing pages rarely carry speaker names, so value depends on a second-level crawl of event detail pages and PDFs plus a Wayback backfill for year-over-year title diffs.

The Slovak public-sector route is unusually rich for A5: three statutory layers stack cleanly — the ÚVO Vestník (plus TED for above-threshold notices) reveals who buys payment services and names the responsible contact person; the Centrálny register zmlúv publishes the resulting contracts in full text, since a state contract only takes effect the day after publication (NBS being the sole exemption); and CISŠS carries civil-service vacancies 15 working days ahead of selection. On the leadership side, the state-linked financial actors publish their own governance directly — SZRB and Eximbanka board rosters on szrb.sk/eximbanka.sk, and, more valuably, Štátna pokladnica and Finančná správa publish dated organizačný poriadok PDFs whose revisions can be diffed for real organization changes. Aggregators (UVOstat with an API, verejnesutaze.sk, the national open-data catalogue) make bulk collection feasible without scraping the ÚVO front-end, which returned an error during this pass and should be treated as unverified rather than dead. The main structural weakness is that appointments to state-owned banks have no single register: they must be triangulated across vláda SR session records, MF SR press releases, the institutions' own pages and TASR/SITA wires.

Slovakia's systemic banks are private and publish nothing about procurement, so the supplier side is the only externally visible trace — and Slovak trade coverage confirms banks deliberately avoid naming IT suppliers, with several vendor references anonymised to 'a financial institution'. The richest sources are domestic vendors that do name projects (Softec's branch-system and digital-onboarding projects, Asseco CE's banking solution and reference pages plus its press-clipping archive, DATALAN and EEA reference lists), complemented by Big-4 SK newsrooms (KPMG, PwC, Deloitte leadership pages) whose partner appointments are themselves A5 events. Quantitative supplier-headcount tracking is best served by Profesia employer pages and FinStat company records for a fixed vendor watchlist, with Erste Digital's Bratislava/Kosice hubs the closest proxy for Slovenska sporitelna's programme staffing. Main gaps: Accenture/IBM/DXC SK have no located reference or newsroom source, Finastra shows no SK-named customer, and LinkedIn — where most FS-leadership posts appear — needs a compliant access route.

Slovakia's workforce/legal channel for banking organizational change rests on three statutory pillars: the ÚPSVaR collective-redundancy notification regime (§73 Zákonník práce) — legally mandatory but published only in aggregate, not per employer; the Obchodný vestník plus the new pre-insolvency/liquidation/insolvency register at justice.gov.sk, which carry liquidation, merger and bankruptcy notices for payment institutions in structured form; and Slov-Lex/NR SR, where bank-governance law (Act 483/2001, amended by 30/2026 Z. z. on key-function holders and internal control) reshapes leadership roles before any appointment is announced. The union side is unusually informative right now: OZBP has publicly filed a higher-level collective agreement proposal, been refused sector bargaining by the Slovak Banking Association, and asked MPSVR for a mediator — a live dispute publishing the version of bank restructuring that employers do not. The main structural gap is granularity: no named, case-level redundancy feed exists, so per-bank headcount and branch attrition must be triangulated from trade press (FinReport), Eurofound's ERM factsheets, and an infozákon request to Ústredie práce.

Slovakia's regional banking/payments employment signal does not live in financial press: it originates with SARIO and the Ministry of Economy (investment-aid approvals naming investor, city and committed job counts), is amplified by SITA and regional newsrooms such as Korzár, and is institutionalised through AmCham's Business Service Center Forum and the Košice IT Valley cluster. Košice is by far the best-documented node — Deutsche Telekom IT Solutions Slovakia is visibly building a Credit Risk and Fraud competence centre there, and the city itself publicises service-centre openings with headcount figures — while Žilina, Banská Bystrica, Nitra and Prešov produced no financial-sector-specific evidence in this pass. The highest-value quantitative instruments are employer- and city-scoped Profesia vacancy snapshots and ÚPSVaR district-level open data (including mandatory collective-redundancy reporting), which together allow announced headcount plans to be tested against realised hiring. Principal weaknesses are an incomplete per-employer/per-city actor map, unused company-registry data, and leadership-move tracking that has no legally collectable source given LinkedIn's terms.

The Slovak systemic banks are privately held and therefore self-publish their leadership and org-structure news first, in Slovak, through two distinct surfaces: a dated press feed (slsp.sk/sk/aktuality, vub.sk tlacove-centrum, csob.sk tlacove-spravy, tatrabanka.sk/sk/blog/tlacove-spravy, jtbanka.sk, 365.bank/pre-media, sk.media.mbank.pl) and a slow-moving management roster page ('vedenie banky' / 'manažment banky'), the latter being the only channel that reveals portfolio reshuffles never announced as a release. Coverage quality is highly uneven across actors: SLSP, VÚB, ČSOB and J&T maintain rich, crawlable Slovak feeds, whereas Prima banka publishes essentially nothing beyond its management page and annual reports, and the foreign-branch entities (UniCredit CZ&SK, mBank, Oberbank) push their announcements up to group hosts in Czech, Polish or German. Two cross-cutting layers make the cell robust: orsr.sk gives dated statutory confirmation of every predstavenstvo and dozorná rada change, and the verbatim wires tlacovespravy.sme.sk and omediach.com mirror releases from banks whose own sites resist automated fetching — Tatra banka's press index returned a 403 and should be treated as bot-defended rather than dead. The main outstanding work is the jobs/hiring dimension, where only the SLSP and VÚB careers hubs were pinned down and the underlying ATS vendors remain unidentified.

Slovak banking is owned almost entirely from abroad, and the English parent-group layer is a genuinely productive channel: Erste (Slovenska sporitelna), Intesa Sanpaolo (VUB), RBI (Tatra banka), KBC (CSOB and, since Jan 2026, 365.bank), UniCredit (a Prague-domiciled branch, not an SK entity) and J&T Finance Group all publish English press releases, ad-hoc disclosures and segment reporting that name Slovak leadership and organisational change. The strongest live sources are the Erste and KBC newsrooms plus Intesa's price-sensitive archive, backstopped by wire and exchange mirrors (EQS, Wiener Boerse, OeKB's OAM, GlobeNewswire) that preserve the legally mandated text with reliable timestamps. Two structural corrections matter for collector design: OTP has had no SK bank since 2020, and UniCredit SK is a foreign-bank branch whose disclosure lives at unicreditbank.cz, so keying on Slovak domains would miss it entirely. The dominant near-term event to instrument is the announced legal and operational merger of CSOB Slovakia and 365.bank, which is where headcount, branch and executive change will concentrate.

Slovakia's payments-employer intelligence is register-first rather than newsroom-first: the highest-value, freely collectable signals for country-manager and org changes sit in NBS's supervised-entity and PSD2 registers, ORSR statutory-officer extracts, Obchodný vestník transformation notices, and registeruz.sk headcount filings, with FinStat as a convenient aggregator. Hiring and hub-trajectory signals come from Profesia company pages, the state sluzbyzamestnanosti portal, and employer ATS pages (Euronet/Trakstar, Diebold Nixdorf SK openings, Mastercard Workday CZ&SK postings), while the statutory hromadné prepúšťanie notification to ÚPSVaR is the key layoff early-warning channel — though it may be FOI-gated rather than published. Local media coverage is thin and generalist (Finsider, TASR OTS, SITA); there is no dedicated SK payments trade outlet, so appointments surface first on LinkedIn, which is auth-walled and ToS-restricted and must be handled manually or via a licensed provider. Notably, Visa/Mastercard appear to run Slovakia inside a Prague/CEE cluster rather than through discrete SK country managers, so scheme monitoring should point at CZ/CEE channels, and no SK footprint was evidenced for NCR Atleos or Wirecard successors.

For Slovak fintech, neobank and crypto/payments scale-ups, hiring and leadership intelligence has no editorial home and must be assembled from three tiers. The register tier is unusually strong and cheap: NBS publishes the PSD2 TPP list as a daily-updated JSON API at subjekty.nbs.sk/api/psd2, runs a post-MiCA crypto supervision section (only six licensed SK CASPs remain), and the Obchodný register — surfaced with change alerting by FinStat — captures statutory-body changes that are frequently the sole public trace of a C-level hire at a seed-stage firm. The signal tier is job boards (Profesia dominates; a live example is an MiCA-regulated SK fintech advertising a lead compliance officer) plus company team-page diffs, backed by sk-language trade and startup media — FinReport, Forbes.sk, Startitup/Fontech — for funding rounds that precede the hiring wave. The main risks are that the richest channels (founder LinkedIn posts, FinStat alerts, Crunchbase/Tracxn) are all walled or paid, that seed actor names are already stale (TrustPay now trades as finby, with a reported Malta licence), and that key role titles circulate in English inside Slovak text, so keyword sets must be bilingual within a single query.

Slovak hiring signal for banking and payments is concentrated in a small, highly structured landscape: Profesia.sk is the dominant board and doubles as a pointer layer through stable per-employer C-ID pages, while nearly every major bank also runs its own Slovak-language ATS (topjobs.sk for Slovenská sporiteľňa and 365.bank, kariera.tatrabanka.sk for Tatra/Raiffeisen, vub.sk, csob.sk with LMC's csob.jobs.cz behind it, and kariera.nbs.sk for the regulator). Two state channels — ISTP.sk and the ministry's sluzbyzamestnanosti.gov.sk portal — carry statutorily reported vacancies and are the best low-risk route to branch-level roles that commercial boards under-represent. The main constraints are that no third-party machine-readable ATS endpoints (Workday/SmartRecruiters/SuccessFactors) were confirmed for Slovak bank entities, LinkedIn is auth-walled for the senior and leadership-move signal, and payments vendors have little or no Slovak-language recruiting presence. Because vacancy disappearance is itself the wind-down signal, longitudinal snapshotting must start now rather than being reconstructed later.

The professional-social layer for Slovak banking and payments is concentrated on sk.linkedin.com company pages, which are confirmed and canonical for the largest employers (Slovenská sporiteľňa, Tatra banka, ČSOB, 365.bank, NBS, the Slovak Banking Association) and for payments actors such as TrustPay and Besteron. The decisive constraint found in this run is structural rather than topical: search engines expose LinkedIn profiles and company pages but effectively never expose the individual announcement posts that carry the earliest leadership-move signal, so this channel cannot be worked search-only. The practical architecture is therefore a curated watchlist of company pages, executives and executive-search consultants (Pedersen & Partners, Amrop, KPMG, KiTalent), monitored manually or under licence, with Slovak-language media — especially the banky.sk top-management-changes column, plus Startitup and Forbes SK — as the corroborating and name-generating layer. Local-language querying was productive for the media and institution layer but noisy at the post layer, where 'platby' and 'nová výzva' collide with unrelated content; queries should anchor on institution names plus SK role nouns.

Slovakia's hiring intermediaries publish plenty about the market but almost nothing about individual placements: Amrop (ranked largest by Index/SME), Grafton/Gi Group, ManpowerGroup SK and Hays (via its Czech salary guide and its Profesia.sk employer page) are the productive direct sources, offering salary benchmarks, quarterly hiring-intent indices and candidate-behaviour commentary that flag which banking and IT functions are tight. Employer-side signals come mainly from Profesia's ecosystem — the Najzamestnávateľ award with its dedicated banking/insurance category, the Profesia Days exhibitor roster, and firma.profesia.sk job-portal analytics — supplemented by HR-industry venues (HRhub.sk, TREND HR manažment conferences) whose speaker lists name bank HR leaders and their change programmes. Pointer layers (Index SME rankings, greenpages.sk, personalneagentury.sk) are the fastest route to a complete actor map including SK-owned boutiques not covered here. The main structural gaps are LinkedIn (untouched, and where recruiters actually talk), the paywalled Index ranking, and the absence of any named-placement feed in Slovak, which forces leadership-move detection onto media and company press rooms instead.

Slovak domestic press coverage of banking personnel and restructuring splits into three tiers. The primary tier is the wires and the people-columns: SITA's financial desk (sita.sk/vofinanciach) with its 'personalne zmeny' tag archive, TASR via teraz.sk, and Pravda's dedicated ekonomika/ludia column — all open-web, dated and quotable, and the cheapest reliable feed for appointments. The analytical tier — TREND, HNonline, Index SME and Denník E/Denník N — carries the substantive reporting on layoffs, 'Fit to purpose'-style cost programmes and branch-network shrinkage, but is uniformly paywalled and, for TREND and HN, refused direct fetch in this pass (bot defence assumed, not death). Two non-obvious channels earn priority: the bank and insurance workers' union OZBP (ozbp.sk), which documents the sector collective-bargaining fight with the Slovak Banking Association and dismissals of union organisers that no bank announces, and the trade title FinReport.sk, whose keyword archives function as ready-made banking-people feeds. Below that sits a large low-credibility rewrite layer (Startitup, Topky, 24hod, Androidportal, Kryptomagazin) that is useful only for recall and salience, never for citation.
