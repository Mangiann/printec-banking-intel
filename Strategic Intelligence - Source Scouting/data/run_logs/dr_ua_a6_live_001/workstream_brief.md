Ukraine's regulated actor census is unusually well-defined but poorly machine-accessible. The authoritative spine is the NBU: the Реєстр платіжної інфраструктури (live since 01.08.2024 under the Law 'Про платіжні послуги'), which for the first time enumerates технологічні оператори платіжних послуг — the payment-technology vendor population — alongside the licensing register of the nine categories of payment service provider, the Довідник банків with its documented branch-level API, and the PROSTIR national scheme with its ~48 participants and published ATM/POS/kiosk estate. These are complemented by a genuine second census in the ФГВФО participant list, the non-bank Державний реєстр фінансових установ, and NBU open-data mirrors on data.gov.ua. The practical obstacle is access, not availability: every scripted fetch of bank.gov.ua and data.gov.ua returned 403 (presumed WAF, not dead — NBU PDFs under /admin_uploads/ and the CKAN API remain the viable routes), and the register content itself is Ukrainian-only, so English-only collection would systematically under-count non-bank and technological operators. The material blind spot is that unregulated technology suppliers — ATM, self-service and branch-automation vendors — appear in NO sector register at all, and must be enumerated instead by triangulating Prozorro award records for the state banks against ЄДР KVED filtering and the Дія.Сіті resident register.

Ukraine's public e-procurement layer is unusually rich for this cell: Prozorro (prozorro.gov.ua) plus its open OpenProcurement API publish, by legal mandate, the buyer, winning supplier, price, losing bidders and often the technical specification for every covered purchase by the state-owned banks and public bodies. Around that core sits a full supporting stack — the free BI Prozorro analytics module and DOZORRO for scoping and risk signals, authorised brokers (Zakupivli.Pro, e-tender, uub.com.ua) as redundant indexes and as the practical way to enumerate the many branch-level buyer entities, Clarity Project and Opendatabot to resolve a winning supplier into an ownership and contract-history profile, and AMCU complaint decisions plus State Audit Service monitoring conclusions as the highest-signal evidence of spec-locking to a particular hardware or software brand. Buyer-side pages (Oschadbank purchases, Ukrgasbank tender committee with its annual procurement plans, PrivatBank's own tender portals) add forward-looking plans and any procurement falling outside Prozorro. Two constraints shape collection: item-level discovery must run on CPV filters through the API rather than keyword web search, and the phrase 'POS-термінал' in Prozorro usually means a bank selling acquiring services rather than a vendor supplying terminals, so buyer/seller direction must be classified before any vendor map is drawn.

Ukraine's indirect vendor-stack signal is concentrated in four layers: mass job boards (work.ua, robota.ua) whose vacancy texts name core-banking and card platforms outright — АБС Б2 by CS Ltd, Is-Card, СЕП/ISO 20022 — plus ATM brands (NCR, Diebold, Wincor, Glory) in field-service roles; IT-community boards (djinni.co, jobs.dou.ua) covering the large in-house bank IT teams and the dominant third-party processor UPC; first-party career portals for banks that run their own ATS (PrivatBank, PUMB, NBU) and for small banks invisible elsewhere; and DOU editorial, which supplies the annual bank-IT-headcount ranking that prioritises every other source. First-party bank engineering blogs are effectively absent in this market — technical disclosure flows through DOU articles/podcasts and vacancy texts instead, so collection effort belongs there. The main uncovered layer is the PSP/acquirer side, and the main methodological weakness is that vacancy bodies index poorly in general search, so on-site keyword search per board plus snapshot archiving is required to turn these sources into a usable time series.

Ukraine has an unusually rich corporate- and contract-transparency layer: three commercial aggregators (Clarity Project, YouControl, VKURSI.PRO) plus Opendatabot resell searchable views over the state registry (ЄДР), the court-decisions register (ЄДРСР) and the full Prozorro procurement database, all of which are also reachable directly and free via prozorro.gov.ua's API, spending.gov.ua contract disclosures and data.gov.ua datasets. For state-owned banks this makes vendor-to-client mapping close to a solved problem — a supplier's entire public-sector portfolio, owners and litigation history can be traced from one contract. The hard boundary is private banks, whose supplier relationships appear in no register; there the only public traces are commercial tender marketplaces (SmartTender, E-Tender, PrivatBank's Zakupivli24), Prozorro.Sale liquidation lots, and court judgments naming contracts. Practical constraint: the highest-value aggregator views are subscription-gated with anti-scraping terms, so collection should be designed around licensed APIs (YouScore, Opendatabot, VKURSI) and the free Prozorro/spending/data.gov.ua primaries.

Ukraine's donor-financed banking and payments modernisation is documented through two parallel stacks that must both be tracked. The IFI stack is English-language and supply-side: EBRD's ECEPP portal plus TC/investment Project Summary Documents, the World Bank's projects and documents portals, the IFC Disclosure Portal (which uniquely reaches private banks such as UKRSIBBANK, Raiffeisen and OTP Ukraine via risk-sharing facilities with paired advisory components), and EIB TA calls that surface on TED. The Ukrainian stack is demand-side and local-language: the statutory МТД register of registered technical-assistance projects with attached procurement plans, the Prozorro donor-procurement module opened in 2023, and NBU/Ministry of Finance programme pages that name donors and workstreams. The weakest link is vendor attribution: IFI and USAID programme communications routinely describe scope but not the contractor, so award-level identity has to be reconstructed by joining these registers to USAspending, TED award notices and oversight reports; ECEPP's non-enumerable notice IDs are the main structural obstacle to systematic collection.

Ukraine's open-banking and state-rail landscape is unusually regulator-anchored: the NBU publishes the regime (Regulation on Open Banking, Board Resolution No. 80 of 25.07.2025, in force 01.08.2025 with an ASPSP compliance deadline of 01.01.2026), the Payment Infrastructure Register lists authorised TPPs and technology operators, and the SEP pages date the instant-payment rollout from SEP-4.1 (01.12.2024) — together giving a dated spine against which vendor activity can be timed. Vendor identity, however, is published almost entirely by suppliers rather than buyers: Open API Hub (EMA + GD Next) and UPC (with Tietoevry, Berlin Group NextGenPSD2) name their bank partners on their own sites, Salt Edge courts the market through co-hosted webinars, and every ASPSP's own Open API page discloses its standard and go-live. The state-digital leg is well documented and quantified through integration.diia.gov.ua and Mintsifra, which publish bank integration counts and, since 2025, a paid tariff for bank document-sharing. The main structural weaknesses are the absence of any public pilot-participant list (the authorisation register is the real artefact), no disclosed supplier for the NBU's own SEP core, and the fact that most Ukrainian banks are private and so leave no Prozorro trace — pushing vendor attribution toward conference recaps, job ads and vendor self-reporting.

English-language international coverage of Ukrainian banking and payments is real but shallow and vendor-driven: Finextra and The Paypers are the two reliable high-frequency nodes, and most of their Ukraine value sits in vendor-supplied press releases (UPC/Tietoevry open banking, Mastercard-NBU and Mastercard-Government MoUs, PSP entries and exits such as Paysera in and PayU out) rather than in staff reporting. The general wires do not cover banking-IT procurement in Ukraine at deal level — a targeted Reuters search for a bank technology-supplier contract returned only privatisation and governance material — so bne IntelliNews is the better wire-grade substitute, being syndicated and archived while Ukrainian originals are not. The single richest artefact in this cell is not journalism at all but the UAFIC Ukrainian Fintech Catalog's English editions, a survey-based actor map of 257 companies produced with the NBU, IFC/SECO and the Ministry of Digital Transformation, complemented by the Ukrainian-language 55-participant payment-infrastructure taxonomy from YouControl. No vendor-neutral Ukraine-specific vendor-share report was found, so competitive share will have to be reconstructed bottom-up from announcements, tenders and competitor sites such as the Kyiv cash-automation integrator I.B.S.

Ukraine's payments assurance layer runs on two parallel regimes: the international PCI stack (QSA/ASV/PIN/3DS audits, evidenced through the PCI SSC registries plus banks' and processors' own compliance pages) and a Ukraine-specific state regime built on NBU cyber-protection resolutions (including the banking cyber-protection Provision and Postanova 143 for non-bank institutions), Держспецзв'язку КСЗІ state expertise, and the attested independent information-security auditor register for critical infrastructure. The richest named-entity sources are regulator registers — the NBU register of technological payment service operators, the Держспецзв'язку auditor list, NAAU's accredited-laboratory register — while CERT-UA and CSIRT-NBU advisories provide the independent, non-marketing evidence path to deployed products. Local QSA-claiming firms (Compliance Control, Allcontrols, and others) form a small, enumerable competitive set, but their accreditation claims must be validated against the PCI SSC directory. No domestic EMVCo-accredited terminal lab was found, and no bank IT-audit report at supplier granularity is public, so Prozorro procurement records become the primary substitute for supplier-level evidence.

Ukraine's private and foreign-owned banks all run first-party press centres, and these are genuinely the primary public disclosure channel for technology projects — Raiffeisen (raiffeisen.ua/about/press), PUMB (about.pumb.ua/presscenter, with separate news / press-about-us / notification streams), OTP (parallel uk and en newsrooms), Credit Agricole (credit-agricole.ua/o-banke/pres-centr/novini) and ProCredit are all open, paginated HTML with no confirmed RSS. The weak points are the edges of the cell: Universal Bank (monobank's licence holder) is bot-defended (HTTP 403 on direct fetch), A-Bank has no first-party newsroom and is reachable mainly via minfin/finance.ua feeds, and Concord Bank was liquidated in May 2026 and is now an archival-only actor. Crucially, buyer-side posts usually announce the capability but not the supplier — only PUMB (MiddleWare/Corezoid digital core) and group-level OTP (Intellect Design Arena eMACH.ai core, Iliad Solutions t3) named vendors in this pass — so the bank feeds must be paired with vendor newsrooms, trade press (psm7.com, banker.ua, dev.ua), the Interfax-Ukraine telecom wire and, as the highest-value untapped channel, bank vacancy pages and annual reports.

Ukraine's association layer is unusually generous for vendor and competitor discovery: EMA (ema.com.ua) publishes an open, link-rich member grid spanning Visa/Mastercard, ~35 banks, PSPs and hardware suppliers including Printec, plus a partner page that maps the whole institutional network and conference pages that expose tiered vendor sponsors. UAFIC complements it on the fintech/startup side, and its annual Ukrainian Fintech Catalog (257 companies in the 2025 edition, NBU- and IFC-backed) is effectively a downloadable actor census with a 2023 edition available for churn comparison. The bank demand side is triangulable across three overlapping rosters — NABU (>95% of system assets), AUB (70+ members, with an English mirror) and UKBS (~92 institutions) — while IT Ukraine and APITU cover the integrator and IT-equipment channels. The clear structural hole is ATM/cash-handling: no Ukrainian sector association for that segment was found in Ukrainian-language search, so that vendor set must be reached via NBU cash regulation, EAST via EMA, and tender records instead. All directories are Ukrainian-language-only in practice; NABU rate-limited on first contact and fintechua.org is client-rendered, so both need a more patient collector rather than being written off.

For Ukraine, the global vendor-reference channel is unusually thin: neither the ATM OEMs (NCR Atleos, Diebold Nixdorf, Hyosung, GRG) nor the international core-banking vendors (Temenos, Finastra, Tietoevry) surface a Ukrainian named-customer case study, and Diebold Nixdorf's divestment of its Russia/Ukraine assets makes withdrawn references a live hypothesis best tested through the Wayback Machine. The disclosure that does exist comes from three other places: payments-platform vendors with genuine UA history (OpenWay/WAY4 with Oschadbank, BPC/SmartVista's Ukrainian office), channel partners whose stated territory includes Ukraine (Transaction Systems reselling Auriga WWS), and non-obvious suppliers such as AWS, which published a detailed PrivatBank case study. The Ukrainian-language layer is dominated by the domestic core-banking vendor CS Ltd, whose /cases and press-centre pages name client banks (Bank Lviv, Pivdennyi, BIS Bank) and are the richest single local-language source in this cell, complemented by banker.ua and ain.ua as press-release mirrors. Because the buyer/supplier gap stays open on hardware, Prozorro procurement data and vendor partner-locator snowballing should be treated as first-class substitutes for missing vendor case studies.

Ukraine's banking-equipment channel is a Ukrainian-language-only layer: English search returns global vendor noise and essentially zero local actors, while uk queries surface a dense population of integrators (Servus Systems Integration, SystemGroup, Incom, IT-Integrator, BMS), multi-vendor field-service firms (PUSK, Ban Com Service, UMICON), single-brand cash-equipment distributors (Cassida UA, Deep2000, UHL-Mash) and a security/CIT tier (Venbest, CIT Security, Comservice) alongside Oschadbank as the largest outsourced ATM-servicing provider. The richest signal sits in vendor-partner and client logo walls on these companies' own homepages — which are almost always unlabelled images without dedicated /clients pages, so collection must combine HTML snapshots with logo capture/OCR. Because partner claims are one-directional self-assertions and brand names collide across unrelated entities, EDRPOU-keyed entity resolution via Opendatabot/YouControl plus Prozorro award records should serve as the hard-evidence backbone, with the EMA association partner directory and distributor partner-locator pages as the main snowballing routes. Biggest open risks: post-2022 status of these firms is unchecked and regional field-service subcontractors remain unenumerated.

For Ukraine, the scheme layer splits cleanly: Visa and Mastercard maintain active Ukrainian-language press rooms (visa.com.ua newsroom, mastercard.com EEMEA uk-ua) that announce issuing/acquiring and co-brand partnerships in detail, while UnionPay has no UA-local property and must be tracked through its global media centre plus bank counterparts such as PrivatBank and Ukrgasbank. The national switch, PROSTIR, is unusually transparent because it is NBU-owned: prostir.gov.ua and the NBU's payment-systems and oversight pages carry participant lists, joiner news and volumes, and the statutory Payment Infrastructure Register at bank.gov.ua/ua/supervision/payment-services/reg-ps is the authoritative roster of registered payment systems, their participants and technological operators (processors). No scheme publishes a Ukraine-scoped certified-vendor or certified-processor list; the workable proxies are the Visa Global Registry of Service Providers PDFs (country-filterable), Mastercard's CSPCDP, the NBU technological-operator register, and processor sites themselves (UPC, UkrCard). Trade press psm7.com is the most efficient pointer feed back into all of these primaries.

Ukraine's payments-native competitive layer is publicly legible but unevenly documented: the major gateways (LiqPay, WayForPay, Fondy, Portmone, iPay, Platon, Tranzzo) plus newer ecosystem entrants (RozetkaPay, NovaPay, plata by mono, EasyPay) all maintain product, tariff and developer-documentation pages, and Fondy is the only one with an explicit tiered channel-partner program. Formal merchant case-study libraries are largely absent, so merchant-level evidence must be reconstructed indirectly from integrator and CRM/agency integration pages, iPaaS connector catalogues, and Ukrainian fintech trade press (psm7.com, fintechinsider.com.ua), which is also the fastest channel for acquiring-partnership announcements. The Concord cluster (Concord Fintech Solutions, MustPay, ProCard-style processing) is the least transparent part of the cell and needs entity-level disambiguation. The NBU payment infrastructure register and the September 2025 acquiring rule change should anchor the whole cell: the register enumerates legitimate PSPs and the rule change is already reshaping PSP-bank-merchant contractual structures.

Ukraine has a dense, mostly open Ukrainian-language trade-media layer for banking and payments: FinClub and PaySpace Magazine (psm7.com) are the two genuinely sector-specialist outlets, Ekonomichna Pravda, Mind.ua, Forbes Ukraine and AIN.ua supply deal, ownership and funding reporting, and Interfax-Ukraine provides the English wire that carries Ukrainian items into international coverage. The most efficient way to work this cell is a two-layer design: pointer sources (UAFIC's annual fintech catalogue, PaySpace Awards long-lists, UKR.NET per-outlet feeds) to build and maintain the actor roster, then keyword-filtered section crawls of the outlets themselves. Two structural caveats: sponsored/branded content (FinClub spetsproiekty, special.ain.ua, Interfax press-release channel) is abundant and must be tagged as vendor-authored rather than treated as independent reporting, and paywalls at Forbes, NV and the full Interfax wire will hide deal detail. The clearest weakness is that no Ukrainian outlet specialises in core banking, processing or ATM/self-service technology, so that vendor niche must be covered from regulator, procurement and vendor primary sources rather than from this cell.

Ukraine's banking/payments event and awards ecosystem is concentrated in a handful of durable organisers: the EMA association (Payments & Security conference series since 2001, Open Finance & Payments Summit, SOC DAY) and UAFIC/fintechua.org (Ukraine Fintech Week, Pay.Forum, Digital Lending, Ukrainian Fintech Awards), flanked by commercial organisers (BANKIR club, DIGITALFORUM.PRO, FinRetail) and the NBU's own regulator-hosted forums. The awards layer is media-run and stable — FinAwards (Minfin + Finance.ua, 40+ nominations), PSM Awards (PaySpace Magazine, since 2016) and Finclub's «Банки року» — with winner pages durable but nominee longlists ephemeral, so capture must be timed to the Jan-March voting window. Retail-channel exposure for POS, self-checkout and kiosk vendors runs through RAU Expo and TradeMaster rather than any UA banking-hardware show, with EuroShop serving as the offshore directory where UA manufacturers such as Modern Expo declare their product lines. Practically all partner/sponsor tier data sits inside individual event pages rather than indexes, so collection should be per-event fetch plus annual diffing, with acronym disambiguation (НАБУ, ЄМА, АУБ) built into query expansion.
