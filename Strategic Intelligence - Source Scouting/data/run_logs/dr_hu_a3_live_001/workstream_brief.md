MNB is the primary emission point for Hungarian banking and payments supervisory rules, but it does not publish them from one place: binding-by-supervision soft law (ajánlás, vezetői körlevél, módszertani kézikönyv) sits under mnb.hu/felugyelet/szabalyozas/felugyeleti-szabalyozo-eszkozok, legally binding reporting decrees and their drafts sit on the separate aszp.mnb.hu portal, payments-authority inspection criteria sit under mnb.hu/penzforgalom, and green instruments sit on zoldpenzugyek.mnb.hu, with a legacy /archivum CMS holding PSZÁF-era history. The highest-value low-cost ingestion channels are MNB's own RSS feeds (felügyeleti hírek /rss/11, piacfelügyelet /rss/23, kiadványok /rss/7), which reliably fire on 'Új MNB ajánlás megjelenése' events; the highest-value forward signals are aszp.mnb.hu/rendelettervezetek and the annual ellenőrzési terv. Two structural weaknesses define this cell: there is no public consultation register for draft recommendations (drafts circulate to industry bodies behind password access), and effective dates and reporting deadlines are buried in PDF bodies rather than listing metadata, so a Hungarian-language extraction ruleset for 'hatályba lép' / 'legkésőbb … munkanapig' is a prerequisite for any deadline calendar.

Hungary's binding-text layer for banking and payments is unusually clean: everything becomes law only on promulgation in Magyar Közlöny (magyarkozlony.hu, authentic signed PDFs), and the state-run Nemzeti Jogszabálytár (njt.jog.gov.hu) provides free point-in-time consolidated texts with explicit in-force/repealed/not-yet-in-force status — together these two cover essentially all hard dates the cell needs, anchored on Hpt. (2013. évi CCXXXVII. tv.), Pft. (2009. évi LXXXV. tv.), Mnbtv., and the MNB payment decrees led by 35/2017. (XII. 14.) MNB rendelet. Early warning comes from three upstream channels — the Government's semi-annual törvényalkotási program, parliamentary irományok on parlament.hu, and ministry draft decrees in társadalmi egyeztetés on kormany.hu with stated comment deadlines — typically weeks to months ahead of gazette publication, with EUR-Lex NIM supplying the EU-directive-to-Hungarian-act join. The main constraints are engineering and legal rather than availability: no official API or bulk export exists on either primary source, so the pipeline rests on URL-pattern scraping plus PDF extraction, and automated collection should stay on the state-run NJT rather than the commercial net.jogtar.hu and Arcanum consolidations. Everything binding in this cell is Hungarian-only in practice; no authoritative English consolidation of the financial statutes was found, and no Hungarian PSD3 transposition instrument or national deadline has yet surfaced.

Hungary's systemic banks are private and publish nothing about procurement, so the usable public trail runs entirely through the supply side: global vendor newsrooms (Tietoevry on OTP card acquiring, Thought Machine on Magyar Bankholding/MBH core banking), hyperscaler customer-reference pages (Google Cloud's OTP story, which names MNB requirements outright), and Hungarian integrators and consultancies (Dorsum, MINDSPIRE, Integra, 4iG) whose Hungarian-language blogs and reference pages name banks, platforms and compliance constraints. The richest pointer layer is domestic: Portfolio's Financial IT / Banking Technology conference sponsor and speaker pages effectively enumerate the whole bank-IT supplier market, with ITBUSINESS and FinTechZone providing Hungarian-language project news that ties spend to specific rules (DORA, PSD2/PSD3, AFR/qvik). These indirect traces are only interpretable against the regulatory driver set — MNB's IT, outsourcing and cloud recommendations, MNB enforcement press releases, and GIRO's instant-payment specifications — so the two layers should be collected in tandem. Main confirmed blind spots: no supplier-side visibility at all on AML/KYC remediation despite active MNB fines, and no coverage yet of ATM/self-service suppliers or formal partner directories.

Hungary's hiring-signal landscape for regulatory weak signals is concentrated and tractable: profession.hu dominates the general board market (~90% share) with CVOnline.hu and Jobline.hu behind it, while every major institution also runs its own career portal — OTP, MBH, Erste, K&H, Raiffeisen, CIB, UniCredit — several on the shared karrierportal.hu ATS, which makes one parser reusable across tenants. The highest-signal direct sources are the two MNB-linked ones: the MNB's own Karrier/Álláslehetőségek page, where supervisory capacity build-out (DORA/ICT-risk, payments oversight, adatszolgáltatás) is visible, and GIRO Zrt.'s portal, since GIRO operates the BKR clearing and instant-payment infrastructure. Consultancy staffing (Deloitte Risk & Regulatory FSI, KPMG, EY, PwC) and specialist agencies (Hays, Randstad) provide the earliest programme-capacity signal, though attribution to a named bank stays inferential. The main constraints are that no HU-specific compliance-jobs vertical exists (all signal is keyword-extracted), LinkedIn is login-walled, and instant-payments/AFR hiring is invisible to search engines and must be pulled directly from board and employer-portal search forms.

The Hungarian banking/payments event landscape splits into three tiers: (1) the regulator itself — MNB runs supervisory 'szakmai konzultációk', payments consultations, financial-stability presentation archives and thematic green-finance events across four separate sub-sites with no unified calendar; (2) commercial conference organisers — Portfolio.hu is dominant (Financial IT, Future of Finance), alongside payments-specific shows (Digital Payment, PayTechShow/TechShow X, Financial Fraud Summit) whose programmes name MNB directorate staff as speakers and preview AFR/ISO 20022, PSD3/PSR, DORA and MNB's AI expectations; and (3) the training layer — Nemzetközi Bankárképző Központ, Budapest Institute of Banking, IIR Hungary and Big-4 webinars, whose course catalogues cite specific MNB decrees and recommendations and therefore act as a demand-side index of which obligations banks are actually staffing for. Agendas and speaker lists are reliably public while the content itself is paywalled or registration-gated, so the practical collection target is agenda + speaker + affiliation metadata plus opportunistically published slide PDFs. Hungarian-language monitoring is mandatory: English mirrors exist only for Portfolio and aggregators, and the training catalogues that carry the sharpest deadline signals are Hungarian-only.

Hungarian coverage of banking supervision and payments regulation is concentrated in a small, stable set of Hungarian-language outlets: Portfolio.hu is the clear leader for MNB fines and payment-law breaches, with Vilaggazdasag, hvg.hu and Napi.hu as mainstream cross-checks and Magyar Nemzet supplying the government/MNB-leadership framing. A distinct tier of small fintech trade blogs (FinTechZone, Fintech.hu, FintechRadar, Privatbankar's fintech tag) carries the most operationally useful signal for this workstream: instant-payment (AFR) rulebook releases, feature postponements and concrete effective dates that rarely get standalone official press releases. English-language coverage of Hungarian financial regulation is thin in local press and is better served by vendor and legal channels — Vixio for payments enforcement, Lexology and Baker McKenzie's jurisdiction guide for draft rules and deadlines. Practical caveat: generic keyword search surfaces mnb.hu and net.jogtar.hu ahead of journalism, so this cell should be collected via per-outlet tag pages and RSS rather than search, with paywalls limiting free access to headline and date.

Hungary's enforcement and dispute landscape for banking/payments is unusually centralised: the MNB is simultaneously prudential supervisor, financial consumer-protection authority, market-supervision body and host of the Pénzügyi Békéltető Testület, so hatarozatkereso.mnb.hu, the MNB press room, the piacfelügyelet/figyelmeztetés registers and the PBT ajánlások/kötelezések pages together carry the bulk of the primary record. Around that core sit three adjacent enforcers with their own public decision databases — GVH (Versenytanács decisions plus a live 2026 investigation into bank card terminal operators), NAIH (GDPR decisions against financial controllers), and the courts via BHGY and the Kúria's jogegységi határozatok, which determine whether supervisory positions and consumer-contract practices actually hold. All primary material is Hungarian-only with no discovered API or RSS on the most important register, so collection is scraping-plus-translation work; English exists only as MNB summaries, occasional institution-published decision translations, and compliance trade media. The main residual risks are recall (no sector filters in NAIH/BHGY, keyword mining required) and two unscouted areas — payment-fraud/APP reimbursement disputes and the scheme/infrastructure layer — both flagged as gaps.

Hungary's regulatory pointer/archive layer is unusually well-provisioned at the official end and thin at the aggregator end. MNB anchors it with a confirmed 11-channel RSS index (mnb.hu/rss, with /rss/11 Felügyeleti hírek and /rss/5 Hírek as the regulatory trunk lines), a layered soft-law archive reaching back through the PSZÁF era, and — most valuable for the deadline topic — a genuine supervisory reporting calendar on the Adatszolgáltatói Portál; njt.hu and magyarkozlony.hu supply the authoritative statute and gazette corpora with time-state and signed-PDF evidence, while EUR-Lex's Hungarian interface plus its saved-search RSS is the only clean machine-readable route into EU-law-in-Hungarian. Pre-decisional coverage is the weak flank: consultation intelligence rests on kormany.hu's társadalmi egyeztetés portal (with famously short comment windows) and the independent torvenyfigyelo.hu aggregator, since no MNB consultation register surfaced. No Hungary-specific commercial horizon-scanning product was found — the global RegTech vendors (Vixio, FinregE) are paywalled and unproven on HU, so a HU deadline tracker must be assembled in-house from gazette hatálybalépés dates, MNB's calendar, and EUR-Lex application dates.

Hungary's executive-branch pre-legislative channel is highly centralised: essentially every ministry draft bill and its comment deadline passes through kormany.hu — the egyeztetes.kormany.hu consultation portal for open items, the Dokumentumtár 'jogszabálytervezetek' shelf (filterable to Nemzetgazdasági Minisztérium, the owner of financial-sector and payments law) for the full packages, and cdn.kormany.hu for the actual draft texts, indokolás and impact-assessment attachments. The process is statutorily framed by the 2010 Jet. law, decree 301/2010 and government decision 1352/2022, which together mean comment windows are very short — five working days internally, roughly eight days publicly — so this cell requires continuous polling rather than periodic sampling, and no RSS or API was confirmed on the current site. Coverage is only workable in Hungarian; English-language routes are effectively limited to EU TRIS notifications of Hungarian draft technical rules. Redundancy against kormany.hu's JavaScript-rendered filters should come from the independent aggregator torvenyfigyelo.hu, the parliamentary iromány register and the Kormány's half-yearly legislative programme (the earliest forward signal), with Magyar Bankszövetség reporting as an indirect confirmer of what the sector was asked to comment on.

Az EU-eredetű magyar banki/pénzforgalmi kötelezettségek forrástája kétrétegű: az upstream réteg (EUR-Lex HU nyelvi változat, EBA/ESMA compliance table-ök és single rulebook, Bizottság Have Your Say) adja a normát és az EU-határidőt, a downstream magyar réteg (MNB felügyeleti hírek, ajánlások, vezetői körlevelek, ERA DORA-felületek, njt.hu, Magyar Közlöny, kormányzati társadalmi egyeztetés) adja a ténylegesen kötő hazai dátumot és formátumot. A kritikus felismerés, hogy nincs egyetlen hivatalos EU→HU státusz- és határidőtábla: az MNB magyarul, hírfolyamban kommunikálja az ESA-fejleményeket, a formális megfelelési státusz viszont csak az EU-oldali compliance table-ök Hungary-soraiban látszik, így saját crosswalk-nyilvántartás építése elkerülhetetlen. Magyarország több dossziéban az EU-átlagnál szigorúbban vagy korábban lép (MiCA 2025.07.01. hazai megfelelési határidő, KVR visszaélésszűrés 2025-től), ezért az EU-határidő önmagában félrevezető. Az iparági és tanácsadói réteg (Bankszövetség, FinTechZone, Big-4 HU, ITBusiness) másodlagos, de a megfelelési rés és a késlekedő részletszabályok jelzésére a leggyorsabb csatorna.

Hungary's payments infrastructure obligation stream is concentrated in three operator families: GIRO Zrt. (BKR batch clearing plus GIROInstant/AFR, with GIROLock PKI and GIROHáló network as mandatory access services), the MNB acting as de facto qvik scheme owner and approver of GIRO's business rules, and the KELER / KELER KSZF pair on the securities settlement and CCP side. All three publish binding documents with explicit Hungarian-language effective dates ('hatálybalépés'), but the signalling mechanics differ sharply: KELER runs an explicit Hírcenter announcement feed naming each leirat and its effective date, while GIRO signals changes mainly through newly dated PDF filenames in its public document repository with no change feed, and MNB mirrors approved BKR annexes under mnb.hu/letoltes. The weakest cell coverage is the card scheme layer — Visa and Mastercard rulebooks and mandate bulletins are member-confidential, so open-source tracking must rely on proxies such as Hungarian acquirer merchant acceptance manuals, PSP release notes and trade outlets like FinTechZone, which in practice report Hungarian rulebook changes faster and more concretely than the operators themselves. Upstream, EPC rulebook versions and the EU Instant Payments Regulation / Verification of Payee milestones set the calendar that Hungarian scheme changes are increasingly pegged to.

Hungary's regulated-actor population is unusually well enumerable: the MNB runs a single master register portal (Intézménykereső) plus a documented hub of intermediary, special and cross-border databases, backed by a searchable supervisory-decisions database and domestic warning lists that explain register movements. For payments specifically, the MNB national register is mirrored daily into the EBA PSD2 central register, which is the only confirmed bulk-downloadable route to the HU payment-institution and e-money-institution universe; the ECB MFI list plays the same role for credit institutions. Legal-entity resolution and financials come from the statutory cégjegyzék (e-cegjegyzek.hu, partly fee-based via the Céginformációs Szolgálat) and the free e-beszámoló filings portal, while GIRO's klíringtag list and the Bankszövetség membership give the operational, infrastructure-level view. Main open risks are the absence of a confirmed machine-readable export from the MNB portal itself, an unclear free/paid boundary on the company register, and no located official list of newly licensed MiCA crypto-asset service providers.

Hungarian regulated-entity own disclosures are dense and highly structured, but almost entirely as HTML notice boards plus versioned PDFs — no feeds. Every one of the seven target banks runs a 'hirdetmény / kondíciós lista / ÁSZF' board, and several (MBH, legacy MKB) organise it as an MNB-mandated 'felügyeleti dosszié'; K&H, UniCredit and Raiffeisen additionally publish archives with explicit announcement-vs-effective date pairs, which is the highest-value signal in this cell for measuring statutory notice periods and regulatory lead times. Issuer-level regulated filings are best caught centrally at the MNB's OAM (kozzetetelek.mnb.hu) and BÉT's KIB store rather than bank-by-bank. The weakest spot is Pillar 3: only CIB and legacy MKB disclosure reports surfaced, so the capital/supervision leg of this cell needs a direct on-site crawl rather than search-engine discovery.

Hungary's association layer for banking and payments is concentrated: Magyar Bankszövetség is the dominant node, publishing news, press releases, quarterly reports and — critically — a maintained ~40-entity member directory plus a working-group map that reveals which regulatory files the sector staffs. The fintech-side counterweight is the Magyar Fintech Szövetség (MAFISZ), which runs a TPP/open-banking working group and a regulatory white-paper track, with adjacent bodies (Lízingszövetség, Faktoring Szövetség, PVOE, MABISZ, MKIK, HVCA) covering leasing, receivables, non-bank lending, insurance, chamber and investor interests. The main structural weakness is that no association publishes a clean per-consultation response archive — positions are embedded in press releases and PDF reports, and some fintech-side material is member-gated — so extraction will be text-mining over prose plus trade-press tag feeds (fintechzone.hu, fintech.hu, portfolio.hu/cimke/*) as corroboration. Searches found no dedicated Hungarian payments/e-money, crypto, or debt-collection association, and no live standalone savings-cooperative federation; those interests appear to route through the Banking and Fintech associations instead.

Hungary's professional-interpreter layer for banking and payments regulation splits cleanly into three tiers: Big Four regulatory desks (KPMG Hungary's Financial Risk & Regulation monthly digest is the standout, with a live Hungarian-language blog behind it; Deloitte Legal and PwC/Réti Várszegi follow, and EY is a confirmed blank), international law firms publishing dated English alerts on Hungarian instruments (CMS, Wolf Theiss, Schoenherr, Kinstellar, DLA Piper — Schoenherr and CMS notably covering MNB soft law and DORA scoping before official English texts), and Hungarian-first boutiques of which Gárdos Mosonyi Tomori is the deepest on pénzforgalom and MNB decrees. The fastest route to the whole universe is the pointer layer: fetching Mondaq's Hungary finance-and-banking hub directly enumerated active Hungarian contributors, and Legal 500/Chambers rankings give the full ranked firm list for a per-firm site sweep. Collection should key every interpreter alert to the primary instrument on mnb.hu or Net Jogtár, since the interpreters' value is the date-stamping and translation, not the underlying obligation.

Hungary's public-sector procurement route for banking and payments intelligence rests on three mandatory, mutually reinforcing publication layers: the national Közbeszerzési Értesítő (daily signed PDF issues) with its searchable Közbeszerzési Adatbázis, the EKR e-procurement platform that additionally exposes the technical documentation behind each call, and TED for above-threshold notices in machine-readable eForms. Around this core sit the key buyers and gatekeepers — Magyar Államkincstár (whose decentralised municipal account-keeping change from 1 October 2025 should trigger a wave of számlavezetés tenders), the Digitális Kormányzati Ügynökség as the compulsory channel for state IT buys, plus MNB, NISZ, NAV and Magyar Posta, each with its own Üvegzseb/közadat disclosure page carrying forward-looking annual procurement plans. Secondary layers add interpretive value: the Közbeszerzési Döntőbizottság decision database exposes contested technical requirements in credit-institution IT tenders, while Opentender and investigative outlets such as Átlátszó help with vendor-concentration and pricing analysis. The main constraints are the absence of an official national API (PDF and HTML extraction will be required), Hungarian-only content below the EU thresholds, and unconfirmed publication routes for state-adjacent payment operators such as GIRO Zrt.
