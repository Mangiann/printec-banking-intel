Prozorro is unambiguously the master index for Ukrainian public banking and payments demand: the legal system of record publishes every above-threshold notice, bid and award, and exposes it three ways — the prozorro.gov.ua search UI, the OpenProcurement 2.5 REST change-feed at public-api.prozorro.gov.ua (the only route that scales), and the daily-refreshed BI Prozorro Qlik modules built by Transparency International Ukraine. Around that core sits a well-developed derivative ecosystem — the ДАСУ audit/monitoring API for contested procurements, the Prozorro Market e-catalogue for below-threshold buys, data.gov.ua as the statutory open-data mirror, spending.gov.ua for realised treasury payments, and third-party layers (Clarity Project, DOZORRO, Zakupivli.Pro, SmartTender, dzo.com.ua) that join tenders to company registry data. The main practical risks are not access but recall and scope: relevant purchases hide under generic CPV codes so a keyword layer in Ukrainian is mandatory alongside the ДК 021:2015 allow-list, no bulk archive export was found so backfill means a long paginated crawl, and non-state commercial banks procure entirely outside the system. Real observed codes to anchor the filter set are 66110000-4, 66170000-2, 32440000-9 and 72260000-5; the seed's 30123400 did not appear in any live Ukrainian notice.

Ukraine's tender landscape is hub-and-spoke: prozorro.gov.ua is the public record, but bids are filed through ~14 currently accredited private marketplaces listed on prozorro.gov.ua/for-places, each of which re-publishes, re-classifies and alerts on the same central data — and, critically, several (Zakupki.Prom.ua, SmartTender, PlayTender, Tender-online) also host COMMERCIAL and below-threshold tenders that never reach the central portal, which is where private banking and payments buying is visible. A parallel accredited network exists on the sale side (prozorro.sale brokers, cross-listed by the State Property Fund), relevant for liquidated-bank asset disposals. Above the marketplaces sits a paid monitoring/analytics tier — Clarity Project, YouControl, the Prozorro paid analytics module and law-firm monitoring services — pointed to by DOZORRO's tools directory, Prozorro's own Infobox and a Ministry of Economy source inventory. The main constraints found are that notice-level content is not search-indexed (per-platform search or the OpenData API is required), that PrivatBank's Zakupivli24 appears to have wound down its tender hosting in 2023, and that no marketplace-level public API has yet been identified.

Ukraine's procurement-watchdog layer is unusually mature and tightly interlinked: Transparency International Ukraine sits at the centre, operating DOZORRO (portal, community of monitoring NGOs, blog) and having built the BI Prozorro public and professional analytics modules, with Infobox Prozorro supplying the methodology explanations and Clarity Project/YouControl providing the register cross-joins that identify who actually wins. A second ring — StateWatch (co-author of new Prozorro contract-amendment risk indicators), KSE's Centre of Excellence in Procurement, AntAC/ACREC, and investigative outlets Наші гроші and Bihus.Info — supplies the narrative and named-actor commentary, while RISE Ukraine/DREAM and Open Contracting Partnership set the transparency norms for reconstruction-linked contracting and the Government Transparency Institute supplies the single-bidding red-flag methodology. Two practical constraints dominate: the two most central domains (dozorro.org, ti-ukraine.org) returned 403 to plain HTTP fetches and will need headless-browser collection, and nothing in this cell is scoped to banking or payments — sector signal must be produced by applying a Ukrainian-language keyword/CPV filter to these generic watchdog datasets and feeds rather than by reading curated sector pages.

Payments-ecosystem demand outside Ukrainian banks splits cleanly into a highly documented public half and a nearly undocumented private half. The public half — municipal e-ticketing (Kyiv's KP GIOTs/Kyivpastrans, Lviv's LeoCard, Kharkiv's KharkivPass), parking (KP Kyivtransparkservice) and utility kiosks — is fully visible through Prozorro's open API, BI Prozorro analytics and DoZorro's investigative layer, but only if queries are driven by ДК021/CPV transport and IT codes rather than banking keywords; the CPV list is the single highest-value missing artefact. The private half — PSPs, acquirers, fuel and retail chains — publishes almost nothing tender-shaped, so it must be tracked through the NBU Payment Infrastructure Register as the authoritative actor list, EMA and UAFIC membership rosters, and trade press (psm7.com) partner announcements. Two structural warnings: some city e-ticket operator selections were awarded via local-newspaper notices entirely outside Prozorro, and the iBox terminal network appears to have collapsed with its bank's licence revocation, so historical kiosk data is stale.

Ukraine's supply-side disclosure landscape for banking/payments technology is dominated by a small, stable set of domestic vendors and integrators — CS Ltd (АБС Б2), UNITY-BARS, ProFIX, Servus Systems Integration, IT-Integrator, SI BIS, the BMS group — most of which publish product, client and news pages in Ukrainian that name bank customers directly, including private-bank deals that never appear in ProZorro. The single richest pointer is the annual «Мережі та Бізнес» SI Ukraine integrator ranking, which enumerates the bidder universe and can be snowballed into a vendor press-room crawler; trade outlets psm7.com and finclub.net act as a catch-net that dates announcements. International OEMs are the weak link: neither the ATM majors nor the POS terminal makers publish Ukraine-specific deal news or an authoritative local partner list, so hardware attribution has to be reconstructed indirectly from acquirer help-centre pages that list exact terminal models and from bank self-service fleet pages. Highest-value next steps are enumerating vendor social channels (where wins are announced first), the ATM servicing/cash-management outsourcing tier, and per-vendor newsroom sweeps of the international core and card platforms.

Ukraine's banking and payments weak-signal layer is unusually well organised around four associations — EMA (payment-system members), NABU and AUB (banks), UCBS (financial-credit institutions) and UAFIC (fintech) — whose member and partner directories together enumerate almost the entire buyer and supplier universe and therefore serve as the master POINTER set for all other A2 cells. Event infrastructure is concentrated in UAFIC-run properties (UAFIN.TECH, Pay.Forum, Ukraine Fintech Week, Ukrainian Banking Summit) plus trade-media summits (Power Payment Summit via PSM7) and the IT Ukraine Association calendar; their agendas and sponsor tiers read as a forward schedule of the modernisation themes banks intend to fund — SEPA readiness, instant payments/A2A, open banking, payment security. The hiring channel is served by work.ua, robota.ua, DOU and aggregators, where 'фахівець з тендерів' and 'менеджер з закупівель' postings at banks (a UKRSIBBANK/BNP Paribas procurement role was already visible) expose internal buying structures months before publication, though the richest senior-role signal sits on LinkedIn behind a login wall and ToS restriction. Nothing here was fetched or liveness-checked: association member-list page structures, banks' own career pages and processor/PROSTIR participant lists remain the priority follow-ups.

Ukraine's special and restricted procurement regimes are legally centralised in CMU Resolution 1178 and its frequent amendments, so the primary sources are the act texts (zakon.rada.gov.ua, kmu.gov.ua, me.gov.ua) plus Prozorro's own guidance on how exempt and defence purchases are reported back into the system as 'звіт про договір' records. Because a material share of wartime spend never appears as a competitive tender, the highest-value observation points are the Prozorro API's reporting-procedure records, treasury payments on Spending.gov.ua/Є-Data, and post-hoc audit output from Держаудитслужба and Рахункова палата. Reconstruction-programme buyers — Ukrposhta, Ukrzaliznytsia, Ukrenergo and the oblast military administrations — publish on their own portals and buy payment-acceptance, ticketing and cash-desk equipment under transport, postal and IT classifications, so CPV-based rather than keyword-based querying is essential, complemented by the DREAM ecosystem for forward pipeline. Defence Procurement Agency (ДОТ) purchases are largely closed by law and remain a structural blind spot addressable only through audit reports and deferred disclosure.

Procurement by Ukraine's state-owned banks is structurally bifurcated: the legally mandated layer sits on Prozorro (addressable per buyer via ?buyer=<EDRPOU> or, properly, the OpenProcurement API), while each bank also runs its own channel — PrivatBank's tender.privatbank.ua, Ukreximbank's /business/zakupivli/ plus competitive selections buried in its newsroom, Ukrgasbank's tender-committee page with downloadable annual plans and draft contracts, and Ukrposhta's unusually rich hub carrying technical-specification justifications. Oschadbank and Sense Bank are the weak points: neither exposed a buyer-side procurement page in this pass, and Oschadbank additionally fragments its buying across ~20 regional branch EDRPOUs that any head-office-only filter would silently miss. Ukrposhta is the standout demand signal in the cell — a ~17,000-unit bank terminal tender tied to the licensing of Укрпошта Банк — and Ukrainian-language querying is non-negotiable, since equivalent English searches returned nothing usable.

For this cell the source landscape is unusually good: Ukraine's Prozorro system, its OpenProcurement API and the BI Prozorro analytics module give machine-readable, legally open coverage of the NBU (EDRPOU 00032106) and the Banknote Printing and Minting Works (EDRPOU 21575489) as buyers, with annual procurement plans providing the earliest signal and Spending/Є-data closing the loop on actual payments. Around that core sit strong secondary layers — DOZORRO and TI-Ukraine for contested or exempted purchases, Clarity/YouControl/Opendatabot for entity resolution and supplier snowballing, and authorised marketplaces as redundancy mirrors. The main structural blind spots are the private processing operators (notably PrJSC Ukrainian Processing Centre), which fall outside the Public Procurement Law entirely, the PROSTIR scheme, which has no separate buyer identity and must be tracked under the NBU itself, and any central-bank purchases made under statutory or wartime exemptions. bank.gov.ua — the most authoritative source here — returned 403 to a plain fetch and should be treated as bot-defended-but-live, requiring a browser-grade collection path rather than being marked dead.

Private and foreign-owned banks in Ukraine sit entirely outside public procurement law, and the cell splits cleanly into three tiers of visibility. A minority publish genuinely usable direct sources: OTP Bank is the outlier with both a full published tender rulebook and a live, dated tender board that sometimes names winners, while Credit Agricole and Raiffeisen publish detailed onboarding routes (questionnaire, vendor mailbox, dated standard terms) without publishing the tenders themselves. A second tier — UkrSibbank, ProCredit, PUMB — offers only a supplier expression-of-interest form or nothing at all, with UkrSibbank's opacity confirmed as deliberate BNP group policy against open bidding; for these, the three commercial marketplaces (Zakupivli.Pro, E-Tender, SmartTender.biz), queryable by buyer EDRPOU, are the only demand-side window and should be treated as the backbone of collection rather than a supplement. A third tier — monobank/Universal, A-Bank, Pivdenny — yielded no procurement channel at all in Ukrainian-language search, leaving parent-group supplier codes (RBI, BNP, OTP Group) and regulatory forced-disclosure such as audit-firm selection competitions as the residual signal.

Donor-financed procurement touching Ukraine's financial infrastructure is published across three distinct layers: lender-owned notice boards (EBRD's ECEPP, World Bank projects/documents including STEP-derived Procurement Plans, UNDP's notice portal, UNGM/UNOPS eSourcing), EU instruments (TED, the EU Funding & Tenders Portal, EU4Business and the EU Delegation), and the Ukrainian national layer where, under the 2023 CEB/EBRD/EIB/World Bank harmonisation memorandum, sub-threshold IFI packages now run inside Prozorro under a purpose-built donor procedure. The richest forward-looking artefacts are World Bank Procurement Plans and EBRD consultancy/TC assignment notices, with the DREAM ecosystem positioned as the earliest public view of the reconstruction pipeline under the Open Contracting Data Standard. Two structural cautions: the highest-value portals (DREAM, World Bank opportunities) are JavaScript-rendered and returned only loading placeholders on plain fetch, and the US channel has moved from USAID to the State Department, so SAM.gov rather than USAID pages is the correct target. Primary notices in this cell are effectively English-language; Ukrainian-language monitoring belongs on the rules, government-announcement and Prozorro layers rather than the lender portals.

The EU-level notice landscape for Ukraine-linked banking and payments procurement rests on three primary pillars: TED (the legally mandated, English-language, API-accessible archive of above-threshold contract and award notices), the EU Funding & Tenders Portal together with DG NEAR's grants-and-tenders route for Commission-contracted technical assistance, and the EEAS/EUAM in-country channels that publish below-threshold delegation and mission procurement never seen in TED. A second, cross-border pillar sits alongside them: IFI portals — above all EBRD's ECEPP, plus EIB operations under the Ukraine Investment Framework — which carry financial-sector consultancy and bank-facing contracts under their own publication rules. The decisive structural boundary is that Ukraine Facility money is procured mostly under Ukrainian national law through Prozorro, so TED holds only the EU-institution slice and the two systems must be tracked jointly to avoid both gaps and double-counting. Practical collection must be API-first and entity-name-driven: general web search does not index notice-level TED pages, Ukrainian-language search reaches only the explanatory and bridging layer, and reliable recall of Ukrainian buyers or awardees will depend on a pinned CPV set plus an alias table of Ukrainian bank and PSP name transliterations.

Ukraine's contract-execution layer is unusually rich and rests on two state pillars: the Ministry of Finance E-data ecosystem (spending.gov.ua treasury transactions plus its contracts API, openbudget.gov.ua, and bulk mirrors on data.gov.ua) and the Prozorro contracting API with its free BI Prozorro analytics module, which together link an award to a signed contract, its amendments, and the actual treasury payments to a named payee. A secondary ring of aggregators (Clarity Project, Opendatabot) and oversight bodies (Рахункова палата, ДАСУ, DOZORRO) makes supplier-level lookups fast and explains anomalies that raw payment data cannot. The critical caveat for banking and payments is scope: state-owned banks such as Oschadbank and PrivatBank procure ATMs and POS terminals through Prozorro but pay outside the State Treasury, so their execution is visible in the contract register rather than in transaction feeds. The main work before collection is entity resolution — a vendor EDRPOU dictionary and a ДК 021:2015 code set — plus confirming the exact E-data API paths and any martial-law disclosure restrictions.

For Ukraine, tender history is effectively a solved data-join problem: Prozorro carries the supplier's EDRPOU under the 'UA-EDR' identifier scheme, and a layer of commercial aggregators — Clarity Project, YouControl, VKURSI.PRO (Tender BI) and Opendatabot — joins that key to the state company register to produce vendor pages listing every tender a firm bid on, its buyers, competitors and owners. That makes supplier-first enumeration of the UA banking/payments technology market feasible: pull the supplier population from the free official BI Prozorro 'Постачальники' sheet or the OpenProcurement API by CPV, then hydrate each EDRPOU through the aggregators, cross-check payment reality on Є-Data/spending.gov.ua and screen against the AMCU bid-rigging lists and blacklist. Two structural caveats bound the cell: none of the platforms offers a ready-made banking/payments vertical, so the CPV/KVED taxonomy is ours to define and verify; and EDR open data is only partly restored under martial law, with defence and strategic entities withheld, so beneficial-ownership coverage has declared blind spots. Free official sources (BI Prozorro, Prozorro API, data.gov.ua EDR, AMCU, Є-Data) should carry the automated backbone, with the commercial aggregators used for affiliation mapping and targeted lookups under their own licence terms.

Ukraine's procurement oversight layer is unusually well documented but structurally split: the AMCU appeals board (орган оскарження) issues reasoned complaint decisions, the State Audit Service (Держаудитслужба) publishes per-tender monitoring conclusions (висновки) that by law must appear in the electronic system within three working days, the Accounting Chamber issues long-form audit reports as PDFs, and all of these end up litigated in administrative and commercial courts whose full texts sit in the unified court register (ЄДРСР). The single most collectable channel is the Prozorro central API, where complaints and monitoring artefacts attach directly to the tender record and can be filtered by CPV/keyword for ATM, POS, card and processing procurements; the AMCU site itself is bot-defended (403 on a plain fetch) and exposes no bulk decision export I could confirm. Court material is best taken from the State Judicial Administration's daily-refreshed annual open-data dumps, with Opendatabot, Clarity Project and ZakonOnline as commercial shortcuts for entity-linked lookups. All primary oversight material is Ukrainian-only, so sector filtering must happen inside the corpora rather than through web search — targeted uk queries pairing oversight bodies with banking terms returned only generic process material.
