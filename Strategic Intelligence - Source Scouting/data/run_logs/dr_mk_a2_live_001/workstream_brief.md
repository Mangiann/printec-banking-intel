North Macedonia's public procurement intelligence is unusually centralised: the Electronic System for Public Procurement (ЕСЈН, at e-nabavki.gov.mk), operated by the Bureau for Public Procurement under the Ministry of Finance, is the legally mandated publication channel for notices, award notices, cancellations, concluded and realized contracts — so essentially every state-sector POS, card, cash-handling, payment-software and core-banking lot must surface there. The practical problem is access, not existence: the portal root returned HTTP 403 to a plain fetcher (bot-defence hypothesis, not a dead site), individual notices are not search-engine indexed, and the advertised machine-readable 'Отворени податоци' export could not be pinned to a concrete URL in this pass. A resilient register should therefore combine a browser-driven ESJN collector with three complementary layers: institutional mirror pages and annual procurement plans (earliest forward signal, notably НБРСМ's own јавни набавки page), oversight bodies that expose contested detail (Државна комисија за жалби по јавни набавки, Државен завод за ревизија, the CCC watchdog), and TED/EU Publications Office for the IPA- and EBRD-funded financial-sector programmes that never enter the domestic system. Commercial aggregators exist but are paywalled and ToS-constrained — corroboration only, never the system of record.

For North Macedonia's central-state buyers, procurement intelligence is dual-published: every contracting authority must post plans, notices and awards to the central ESJN portal (e-nabavki.gov.mk, run by the Bureau for Public Procurement inside the Ministry of Finance), while several also maintain their own richer sections — Customs and the Ministry of Finance in particular expose full tender dossiers and annual plans as PDFs on their own domains, and MoF additionally runs CFCD for all EU/IPA tenders. The Health Insurance Fund and the Pension Fund are the heaviest banking/payment counterparties but publish less on-site: FZO behind a WAF, PIOM with no dedicated procurement page, so ESJN plus FOI via ASPI is the practical route for them. The State Audit Office is the strongest indirect source, naming actual suppliers, contract values and procurement irregularities for exactly these institutions. Main frictions are technical rather than legal: everything is public information, but finance.gov.mk and fzo.org.mk refused plain HTTP fetches and ESJN is a stateful ASP.NET app, so collection needs MK-locale headless browsing.

North Macedonia's pointer/registry layer for banking-and-payments procurement is compact and mostly state-run: ESJN (e-nabavki.gov.mk) is the centre of gravity, carrying the economic-operator registration, the concluded-contracts register per contracting authority, and the negative-references record that functions as the country's only public exclusion list; the Public Procurement Bureau layers annual statistics and 'red flag' risk reports on top. Actor enumeration on the demand side comes from the Macedonian Banking Association members page (banks, savings houses, plus KIBS and the Macedonian Credit Bureau) reconciled against NBRSM's licensee lists, while the supply side is best enumerated from the MASIT ICT chamber member directory and, secondarily, the national chamber's finance association. Identity resolution runs through the Central Registry (crm.com.mk), which is authoritative but legally restricted to non-commercial use — bulk work must go through its paid distribution system rather than scraping. Main open risks: nbrm.mk returns 403 to plain fetches (bot defence, presumed live, unverified), the appeals commission appears under three competing domains, and non-bank payment/e-money institutions are absent from every association list found.

North Macedonia's oversight-and-complaint trail for procurement is concentrated in four state bodies — ДКЖЈН (appeals), ДЗР (state audit), ДКСК (anti-corruption) and КЗК (competition) — with a judicial review layer above ДКЖЈН at the Управен суд and a criminal layer via Јавно обвинителство. All of them publish, but almost exclusively in Macedonian and mostly as unstructured PDFs on Drupal/WordPress file trees rather than as queryable registers; the two highest-value sources (dkzjn.gov.mk, dzr.mk) both resisted a plain fetch (empty JS-rendered body and HTTP 403 respectively) and must be treated as bot-defended-but-live, not dead. The single most valuable structural asset found is ДЗР's taxonomy-term indexing, which maps each audited institution to its report set at a stable URL, plus ЦГК's independent monitoring reports which already analyse ДКЖЈН appeal proceedings and can act as a targeting layer into the primary decisions. Critically, no bank- or payments-specific appeal decision was actually located through public search indexing, so the cell's core premise remains a well-founded hypothesis until the ДКЖЈН case register is opened with a headless browser and queried by procurement subject.

North Macedonia's procurement transparency layer has one clear centre of gravity: the Center for Civil Communications (ccc.org.mk), which is simultaneously the country's main monitoring NGO, the operator of the opendata.mk family of databases (Rationality Index, municipal procurement, million-euro and direct contracts), the national member of Balkan Tender Watch, and the publisher of the Active Transparency Index. Around it sit a machine-readable layer (Opentender MK via the Government Transparency Institute, registered in the OCP Data Registry; data.gov.mk; the Ministry of Finance's Otvoreni Finansii transaction portal), a policy layer that documents what data is promised and what is actually delivered (OGP commitments MK0073/MK0144/MK0146/MK0185, the national ovp.gov.mk portal, an OSF Western Balkans policy brief), and an investigative layer (IRL, Prizma/BIRN Macedonia) whose stories surface contract and ownership detail that notices never carry. The decisive limitation for this run's sector focus is that nothing in this layer is organised by industry: no banking- or payments-specific procurement monitor exists, so financial-sector contracts must be filtered out of horizontal datasets by buyer, CPV code and supplier name. Generic Macedonian procurement vocabulary is also noisy — searches for 'јавни набавки' are swamped by mandatory ministry pages — so discovery here works best anchored on actor names rather than topic terms.

North Macedonia has a small but genuinely differentiated Macedonian-language business-media layer: one banking vertical (bankarstvo.mk), three or four economy portals (faktor.mk, pari.com.mk, ekonomijaibiznis.mk, biznisvesti.mk, denar.mk), mainstream desks that carry central-bank payment-infrastructure news (mkd.mk, makfax.com.mk, novamakedonija.com.mk), and a strong investigative tier for procurement (Prizma/BIRN, Telma's КОД, plus the CCC watchdog's monitoring reports). Time.mk is the highest-leverage entry point because it aggregates essentially the whole outlet set into one economy stream and continually reveals new domains. Crucially, this press almost never reports contract signatures as such — it reports launches and implementations ('лансира', 'имплементација', 'во соработка со'), so vendor identity must be inferred from product coverage, which makes it a strong indirect signal channel but a weak direct one. Main structural gaps are the untouched Albanian-language media, the state agency MIA and broadcast desks, and the absence of tested archive/search endpoints for retrospective work.

For North Macedonia's weak-signal layer, the landscape splits into three usable strands. First, hiring: bankarstvo.mk functions as a de-facto sector job-ad aggregator for essentially all MK banks and is the single highest-value entry point, backed by the commercial boards (vrabotuvanje.com.mk, kariera.mk, pazar3.mk, vraboti.se), the state e-Работа register, and individual bank career pages - with LinkedIn the richest but login-walled and non-scrapable channel. Second, events and sponsorship: KIBS (host of EACHA 2026 in Skopje), the МБА, МАСИТ's annual conference, the e-commerce association's regional conference, MicroBalkans 2026 and the Macedonian Stock Exchange conference collectively constitute the MK banking-and-payments event calendar, though sponsor and exhibitor rosters exist only as prose in news coverage rather than structured pages, so capture must be calendar-driven and manual. Third, vendor-side disclosure: international press wires and MK business media (faktor.mk) carry implementation announcements naming the local implementation partner, which is the actor set that bids on subsequent work. The critical structural fact for this cell is that MK commercial banks are outside the public-procurement regime and publish no RFI channel, so these indirect signals are frequently the only public trace an upcoming procurement leaves - and because job ads and event microsites expire quickly, archiving at capture time is not optional.

For this cell the usable intelligence sits almost entirely outside North Macedonia: MK subsidiary bank websites (nlb.mk, halkbank.mk, sparkasse.mk) publish no procurement notices at all, so the visible layer is parent-side (NLB d.d. javni razpisi in Slovenian; Erste Group Procurement in Vienna, which publishes supplier rules but no tenders; Halkbank T.A.Ş. Turkish announcements and potentially EKAP) plus IFI channels, above all EBRD's ECEPP, where MK-relevant contracts bypass the national portal entirely. The strongest archive answers are structural rather than ad hoc: Opentender.eu/DIGIWHIST holds a bulk OCDS dataset for North Macedonia (CC BY-NC-SA, non-commercial), TED's dataset explicitly extends to MK, the official arhiva.finance.gov.mk preserves delisted notices, and a Wayback CDX query on e-nabavki.gov.mk returns captures back to 2007. Commercial Balkan aggregators (nabavke.com, ekapija-tenderi.com) advertise Macedonian coverage and would give cross-border correlation of the same vendor appearing in several NLB or Sparkasse markets, but both are JS-rendered subscription products whose MK depth and retention are unverified. Two caveats should carry forward: the brief's Şekerbank–TTK ownership premise found no supporting evidence, and 'оглас' is badly polluted by job adverts, so MK query fleets must always pair it with 'набавка' or 'тендер'.

For NBRNM as a procuring entity, the authoritative chain is: the bank's own Јавни набавки page -> the national ESJN platform at e-nabavki.gov.mk where it publishes as a договорен орган -> TED/EU Publications Office for above-threshold notices (a 2024 coins supply notice, CPV 18512100, confirms this path) -> DKZJN appeal decisions and State Audit Office regularity audits for what the notices themselves do not say. For NBRNM as payments regulator, the payment-systems subtree of nbrm.mk (MIPS, the KIBS oversight page, the regulation repository, payment statistics and the statutory Register of Payment Institutions) plus the Official Gazette carry licensing decisions and technical standards, with KIBS's September 2025 instant-payments operator licence as a recent worked example. The dominant practical risk in this cell is access, not availability: every fetch of nbrm.mk returned 403, so the single most important publisher is presumed live but unverified and needs a browser-based collector. Secondary interpretive layers (Center for Civil Communications monitoring, open-data mirrors, Macedonian financial press) are useful for outlier detection and timeliness but must never displace ESJN and the Official Gazette as primaries.

For North Macedonia's payment-rail and state-IT cell the source landscape splits cleanly in two. The public half is well served: ЕСЈН (e-nabavki.gov.mk) is the mandatory central e-procurement system, flanked by the Public Procurement Bureau, the appeals commission ДКЖЈН (which publishes complaints and decisions and exposes contested IT tenders), and the State Audit Office, whose dedicated IT audits are the richest open description of the state's information systems; the Ministry of Digital Transformation — successor to МИОА since 2024 — publishes its own procurement plans and runs uslugi.gov.mk, TrustEID and the interoperability agenda. The private half is opaque: neither КИБС (clearing house, also the country's first certification authority) nor ЦДХВ (the CSD) shows any tender channel, so their modernisation demand must be inferred from company news, KIBSTrust policy documents, NBRM oversight material and — for CDHV, a listed issuer — its mandatory disclosures on the Macedonian Stock Exchange. Donor channels (EBRD ECEPP, World Bank procurement plans, EU/IPA publications) are the earliest-warning layer, often naming packages years before national tendering. Main open risks: NBRM's own procurement page is unlocated, the Official Gazette and any open-data export of ЕСЈН are unchecked, and all URLs here await the deterministic liveness pass.

For state-owned enterprises and municipalities as buyers of collection, POS and cash services in North Macedonia, the landscape is strongly centralised: ЕСЈН (e-nabavki.gov.mk) is the statutory choke point through which essentially every notice and award from Град Скопје, the municipalities, ЈП Водовод и канализација, ЈСП, АД ЕСМ and АД МЕПСО must pass, with its open-data layer as the structured companion (currently 403-ing to plain fetches). Buyer-side sites (ЕСМ, МЕПСО, Град Скопје, ЈП ВиК) add annual procurement plans and supplier-revealing e-payment front ends that act as leading indicators, while donor-financed work for the same buyers bypasses ЕСЈН entirely and surfaces on EBRD's ECEPP and WBIF in English. Independent layers — Државен завод за ревизија, ДКЖЈН appeals, Центар за граѓански комуникации monitoring, and Prizma/BIRN document mirrors — supply the context, contested-tender detail and historical depth that raw notices lack. The main open risks are terminology calibration for payments-specific notices inside ЕСЈН, complete absence of Albanian-language coverage, and no confirmed machine-readable access path.

Supranational and donor-funded procurement touching MK banking and payments runs through three distinct, non-overlapping layers: EU channels (TED, the EU Funding & Tenders Portal, EEAS Delegation Skopje, and nationally the CFCD in the Ministry of Finance, which is the sole IPA contracting authority under indirect management), IFI channels (EBRD's ECEPP, World Bank procurement plans plus the Socrata contract-awards dataset and WB corporate RFXs such as the explicitly payments-focused 'Electronic Payments in North Macedonia' assignment with the National Bank), and UN/bilateral channels (UNDP notices, UNGM, KfW-financed fund management units). Award-level vendor names are most reliably obtained from the World Bank contract-awards dataset, EBRD contract-award notices, and CFCD's contracted-projects listing, while procurement plans and donor policy briefs give six-to-eighteen months of forward warning before any notice appears. Macedonian-language search is productive only on the national IPA side (ЦФСД, СЕП, МЕП, Министерство за финансии) — every IFI publishes in English only, and donor-funded lots following the national market approach are diverted to e-nabavki.gov.mk, so the cell cannot be collected completely without a hand-off to the national-portal cell. Main residual risks are the retirement of UN Development Business as a GPN venue, registration/JS gating on ECEPP, UNGM and e-nabavki, and the absence of a fixed CPV code set to replace keyword search.

North Macedonia's commercial banks are privately owned and therefore sit entirely outside the Law on Public Procurement, and this pass confirms empirically what that implies: neither Komercijalna (kb.mk) nor Stopanska (stb.com.mk) — nor, on search evidence, NLB MK, Halkbank, Sparkasse, ProCredit, UNIBank or TTK — operates any tender, RFP or supplier-registration page, and the local terms оглас / јавен повик / повик за понуди are used by these banks almost exclusively for recruitment and for selling foreclosed assets, which makes naive keyword collection nearly all noise. The only genuinely direct procurement disclosure in the cell comes from the state-owned Development Bank (mbdp.com.mk Јавни набавки and Јавни огласи), with the National Bank's Јавни набавки page and e-nabavki.gov.mk serving as adjacent benchmarks for banking-grade specifications, price levels and incumbent vendors. For the private banks the practical spine is indirect and mandatory-disclosure-driven: SEI-NET and the MSE issuer pages for price-sensitive filings and audited statements, and the banks' own annual reports, where mid-tier banks such as UNIBank do narrate concrete capex items. Group-level channels (NLB Group's javni razpisi, ProCredit's sister-bank tender pages) plus MK trade press are the remaining routes, and the biggest open gaps are parent-group supplier portals and a vendor-side/job-ad channel to substitute for the award notices that will never be published.

Vendor-side evidence for the North Macedonian banking/payments stack is real but thin and concentrated in a small integrator layer: Printec (ATM/self-service, unattended POS), ASEE/Asseco SEE and its Payten payments brand (banking software, POS, acquiring), CaSys International (domestic card processor for roughly eleven banks) and Nextsense/KIBS (eID and digital onboarding). These vendor sites and newsrooms function as a de-facto contract register, since MK banks rarely publish awards themselves. ATM/POS hardware OEMs (Diebold Nixdorf, NCR Atleos) and core-banking vendors publish nothing MK-specific that surfaced here, so core-platform identity remains the largest open gap. Macedonian-language vendor press is scarce — the local-language signal lives in trade press such as bankarstvo.mk and in vendor social feeds, the latter usable only under manual, non-scraped review.

The MK local supplier landscape for banking/payments is small and highly concentrated: CaSys (the only domestic card processor and certified personalisation bureau) and bank-owned KIBS (clearing, digital trust) are the two structural nodes, surrounded by a handful of integrators — Nextsense, Semos, Duna, Netcetera, plus regional players Printec and Asseco SEE/Payten with local operations. Award-level detail is rarely published by banks, so it surfaces instead in supplier news pages, vendor-supplied PR on faktor.mk / bankarstvo.mk / ecommerce.mk, e-Nabavki contract PDFs that name the same suppliers on public-sector deals, and LinkedIn posts. Pointer sources — MASIT's member directory, the IT.mk IT Index, zk.mk and CompanyWall — are the practical route to enumerating the long tail of smaller integrators and resolving Cyrillic/Latin name variants. The clearest blind spots are cash-handling/CIT and security-printing suppliers, for which no named MK company was found in Macedonian-language search, and systematic LinkedIn monitoring, which needs an access-compliant method rather than search-engine sampling.

North Macedonia's non-bank payments layer is young and regulator-shaped: the 2022 Law on Payment Services and Payment Systems (in force 2023, PSD2/EMD2-aligned) created licensed payment institutions and e-money institutions whose authoritative roll is the NBRSM public register set — the single most valuable source in this cell, and currently bot-defended (403 on plain fetch, presumed live). Alongside them sit two structural infrastructure buyers, CaSys International (Visa/Mastercard-certified card processor and personalisation bureau, now also Diners Club processor) and the KIBS clearing house, plus PSPs and wallets such as Monri, Paysera Macedonia, Flex Pay, IutePay and the telco-bank MobiPay axis. The decisive finding for workstream A2 is that none of these private actors publish tenders: the national portal e-nabavki.gov.mk only captures them when they win public-sector payment-acceptance contracts, so procurement must be inferred from register diffs, licensing events, scheme mandates and trade press (bankarstvo.mk, pari.com.mk, ecommerce.mk, SeeNews). Local-language coverage was strong on regulator and media terminology, but no Macedonian term was found for a standalone bill-pay/kiosk network, suggesting that sub-segment may not exist locally rather than merely being undiscovered.
