The CZ regulator layer is unusually well instrumented at the ENTITY level and largely silent at the PERSON level. ČNB concentrates everything in JERRS, exposed three ways — an interactive register, seven open CSV datasets under nařízení vlády 425/2016 Sb., and documented WS JERRS XML web services — making new entrants, licence scope and licence withdrawal cheaply diffable, and this is reinforced by the pravomocná rozhodnutí archive, the annual Zpráva o výkonu dohledu, and ČNB's own press releases and Bank Board page for regulator-side leadership and reorganisation. What was NOT found is any public ČNB list of individuals approved under the souhlas s výkonem funkce / vhodnost a způsobilost regime, nor any disclosure of the 30-day advance notifications of statutory-body changes; the practical A5 route is therefore an IČO-keyed join from ČNB registers to obchodní rejstřík / ARES statutory-body diffs, with EBA's PSD2 central register as an independent cross-check on the payments population. Priority build order: WS JERRS and ČNB open data, then the commercial-register officer diff, then press releases, enforcement decisions and the ČNB vacancy board as corroborating and leading indicators.

Czech bank newsrooms are a strong, primary-source-rich surface for A5: the big four (KB, Česká spořitelna, ČSOB, MONETA) plus Raiffeisenbank all run structured, year- or date-partitioned Czech press archives that name incoming and outgoing board members, their portfolios and exact effective dates, and the listed issuers (KB, MONETA) duplicate the same events in mandatory investor disclosures that are cleaner still. Smaller and newer players (Trinity, Partners Banka, Creditas, Air Bank, Fio) publish real leadership news but on thinner, less regular surfaces — often a single slug page rather than an indexed archive — so their index paths need nav resolution before crawling. Czech payment institutions are the weak flank: Global Payments Česko and Worldline CZ have localised sites but no observable Czech appointment-release stream, pushing that signal to the ČTK Protext wire and to registry sources outside this cell. Recommended posture: weekly crawls of the five large newsrooms plus investor-disclosure pages as the backbone, monthly crawls of the small banks, and Protext/Peníze.cz as pointer feeds that flag when a primary release exists.

The Czech public-sector route to banking/payments org-change intelligence rests on four statutory registers — NEN (nen.nipez.cz), the Věstník veřejných zakázek (now vvz.nipez.cz), Registr smluv (smlouvy.gov.cz, administered by DIA since April 2023) and TED — which together name both the state project owners and the winning supplier ecosystem. Hiring and leadership signals come from a parallel statutory feed, ISoSS (portal.isoss.cz) for civil-service posts, complemented by the career pages of the state-enterprise layer: SPCSS, ČNB, DIA, ČSSZ and Česká pošta. Hlídač státu is the most efficient pointer/aggregator for cross-linking contracts, tenders and subjects via a documented API, with portal.gov.cz and portal-vz.cz as the authoritative map of which register currently lives where. The main weaknesses of this cell are the missing municipal tier, the absent audit layer (NKÚ, ÚOHS), and the fact that no URL here has yet been fetched — every endpoint is search-derived and awaits the deterministic liveness pass.

CZ systemic banks are private, so the supplier layer is the primary observable: Temenos (Komerční banka core migration, with Syncordis and LTIMindtree as named partners) and Diebold Nixdorf (ČSOB case study, KB as DN Series pilot with its COO quoted) are the highest-value vendor-published traces, and domestic integrators — Trask, Aricoma/Cleverlance, Asseco CE, Monet+, ArnaThora — carry the rest. Leadership-move signal in this cell is concentrated in Big Four Czech newsrooms (KPMG's annual partner-promotion release, EY's cs_cz newsroom, Deloitte's leadership roster) with epravo.cz, cfotrends.cz and ceskenoviny.cz acting as fetch-friendly mirrors; Accenture and PwC lack an equivalent CZ-language channel. A notable back-door found this run is smlouvy.gov.cz, where private banks' own cash-handling standard contracts (e.g. KB's universal cash-in-packaging agreement) become public whenever the counterparty is a public body, complementing the Loomis and G4S CZ service pages on the cash-handling side. The main unclosed gap is vendor-side job postings — the most direct A5 hiring signal here — which need per-vendor careers-page crawling plus ARES/Sbírka listin statutory-body tracking rather than search-engine discovery.

The CZ events-and-awards layer is unusually rich for role-census purposes: Zlatá koruna's Finanční akademie publishes a named ~400-person roster with employers every year under a stable /soutez/{YEAR} archive going back to roughly 2009, and Mastercard Banka roku publishes a bio'd expert jury plus a CEO-level conference panel each autumn — together these give a free, self-updating, deeply archived time series of who holds which seat. The conference layer splits cleanly by role class: ČBA's Payments Summit and CGI's Trendy v platebním styku expose the payments-operations and transaction-banking layer, IT ve finančnictví the CIO/CISO layer, FinTech Roadmap the founder/innovation layer, VŠE's Rozvoj a inovace finančních produktů and ČNB's speech archive the board and regulator layer, and Blue Events' Retail Summit the merchant/acceptance side. The main structural weaknesses are archive depth outside Zlatá koruna and ČNB (Payments Summit showed no visible past-edition archive on the page fetched, so self-snapshotting plus a Wayback backfill is required to get a two-edition baseline), a strong Prague bias, and no dedicated CZ acquiring/POS-vendor event. Note that two names in the cell brief — 'Banking Summit' and 'Bankovní inovace' — did not resolve to real standalone CZ event brands and are logged as negative findings rather than invented sources; note also that this cell yields incumbency and title-drift signal but essentially no vacancy or hiring-volume signal, which must come from other cells.

Workforce-level org change in Czech banking is legible through three distinct layers. First, the union layer is unusually concentrated: a single sector union, OSPPP (member of ČMKOS), covers ~150 basic organisations across 27+ banks and insurers, publishes a higher-level collective agreement, and is the actor that reliably makes downsizing visible — its firm-level ZO entities are individually registered in the public registry, giving a clean actor map. Second, the statutory layer (Labour Code mass-redundancy notification to Úřad práce ČR, plus mandatory consultation with the union or works council) generates a paper trail, but ÚP publishes only aggregates and sector groupings, so firm-level detection depends on unions, regional Deník reporting, or a 106/1999 Sb. information request. Third, the statistical layer — ČSÚ NACE-K employment and wages, ISPV occupational earnings, and ČNB branch/business-place counts, with Peníze.cz maintaining a usable per-bank branch-count series and Eurofound ERM logging ≥100-job Czech events — lets contraction be measured even when nobody announces it.

The non-Prague layer for CZ banking jobs/organisation signals rests on three legs: the Deník regional network (one edition per kraj, of which Moravskoslezský, Brněnský and Hradecký are mapped here) plus independent regional outlets such as Magazín PATRIOT and POLAR in Moravia-Silesia; municipal and investment-agency publishers (Ostrava city press releases, Invest in Ostrava's centre directory, ABSL's annual sector report) which quantify shared-service-centre footprints in Brno, Ostrava and Hradec Králové before employers announce anything; and the employee-voice layer, where Atmoskop.cz is clearly the highest-value asset — stable per-employer numeric IDs, review paging and a statistics trend page, with reviews explicitly discussing reorganisation, turnover and layoff fear. Job boards (Jobs.cz sector+kraj filters, cz.indeed.com, Jooble, Práce.cz) are best treated as a counting instrument: regional vacancy volumes per bank are the cleanest early proxy for back-office build-out or contraction, and Eurofound's restructuring database provides a structured cross-check. Main weaknesses this run: no standalone Czech forum was found, Glassdoor is assumed login-walled and scraping-prohibited (unverified), Brno Daily and Ostravan.cz could not be confirmed, and the union / Úřad práce mass-layoff channels — likely the earliest hard signals — remain entirely unmapped.

For CZ banking/payments people intelligence, the reliable English-language layer is not the trade press but the foreign parents themselves: Erste Group, KBC, Société Générale and RBI announce Czech CEO, management-board and country-head changes in English regulated releases, mirrored on GlobeNewswire, Euronext and EQS — these are free, timestamped, and the earliest record. International aggregators (Finextra, The Paypers, FinTech Futures, IBSi, XPRIMM) do cover appointments but showed no systematic Czech coverage in this pass, while bne IntelliNews is the strongest genuine CEE desk and is paywalled; SeeNews and Emerging Europe did not substantiate as CZ people sources. The archive dimension is the cell's distinctive contribution: CZ bank management pages such as rb.cz's Bank Management are stable diff targets, and a Wayback CDX watchlist over 'vedení / představenstvo' pages would catch the org changes companies never announce. Local-language search was run and produced two useful Czech-side anchors (Kurzy.cz corporate wire and oPojištění.cz's dedicated 'Jmenování a odchody' rubric) for cross-dating what the English trackers aggregate.

The CZ payments-employer source landscape splits into three usable layers: company-owned channels (Global Payments CZ, Worldline en-cz, Nexi Central Europe, GoPay's dedicated jobs subdomain, Twisto's 'pro média'), Czech-language trade and business media that actually run appointment columns (fintag.cz 'Personálie', ProfitOnline 'Lidé a společnost', Fintree, FinTech Cowboys, CzechCrunch, Lupa, E15, plus ČTK's Protext wire as the PR backbone), and structural registries (ČNB JERRS/open data for the actor universe, obchodní rejstřík statutory-body changes as the hardest dateable proxy for country-manager churn). LinkedIn is the richest single newsroom for GoPay and Comgate but is login-walled and ToS-restricted, so it must stay a manual-review source. The weakest coverage is the terminal/ATM estate — Euronet, Diebold Nixdorf and NCR/Atos country teams publish essentially nothing in Czech — and card-scheme CZ offices, both of which should be picked up via register monitoring and a dedicated scheme-focused pass. Local-language searching was decisive: the highest-value finds (Protext, fintag Personálie, Twisto and Skip Pay appointment PR) surfaced only on cs queries, not en ones.

Czech people-moves intelligence for banking splits into two layers. The prestige business press — Hospodářské noviny, Týdeník Ekonom, Deník N, and to a lesser degree E15, Euro and Seznam Zprávy — is where unannounced exits, forced departures and restructuring numbers actually break (HN on the ČNB exodus around the Michl governorship; Seznam Zprávy quantifying Komerční banka's ~1,840-post reduction), but three of those are hard-paywalled and none maintains a standing personalia rubric. The de-facto ledgers are elsewhere and mostly open: Bankovnictví online's 'Personálie' column is the sector-precise one, with Newstream's /stitek/personalie and /stitek/kariera tags, Měšec.cz /n/personalie, Peníze.cz's personální-změny topic page, Management News and MediaGuru's annual marketing/PR personnel roll-ups covering the rest. Practical design: use the open ledgers plus Patria's hot-news archive for high-recall event detection, and reserve paid or licensed press-archive access for the paywalled dailies where the reasons behind a departure live. Czech-language collection is non-negotiable here — English-language coverage of these moves is close to nonexistent.

The CZ HR/recruitment intelligence layer for banking splits into three tiers: (1) recurring quantitative instruments — ManpowerGroup's quarterly Index trhu práce with an explicit finance/insurance sector index, plus the annual Hays Mzdový průzkum, Grafton salary survey/Mozaika pracovního trhu, Randstad's salary-benefits and employer-brand studies, and Reed's CZ guide; (2) retained executive search boutiques with Prague offices (Amrop, Boyden, Stanton Chase, Hays Executive) whose practice pages and placement news carry leadership-move signals; and (3) a dense Czech-language pointer layer — hrnews.cz, hrforum.cz (Asociace personalistů/People Management Forum), personalista.com, plus PR wires kurzy.cz, e15.cz and penize.cz — that republishes the gated recruiter reports in open form. No recruiter publishes a banking-only CZ report: sector signal must be reconstructed from sector chapters and index values. Two non-obvious high-value finds sit outside the classic recruiter set: finak.cz's monthly tally of financial-adviser moves between distribution networks, and the sector trade union OSPPP, which surfaces headcount, branch-closure and collective-bargaining pressure at named banks.

CZ vacancy intelligence is unusually concentrated: LMC's Jobs.cz ecosystem is both the dominant board and the white-label host for most large banks' own career sites (kb., csob., rb., moneta., unicreditbank.jobs.cz), so one enumeration pattern yields near-complete official coverage of the incumbents. Around it sit StartupJobs.cz and ATS-hosted fintech boards (Twisto on Recruitee) whose ad text discloses tech stack and compliance posture in detail, Prace.cz for branch-network staffing, and the Úřad práce statutory vacancy register as the only officially-mandated feed. The richest leadership signal — LinkedIn Jobs and Glassdoor reviews — is auth-walled and ToS-restricted, so that half of the cell must be budgeted as manual analyst review rather than automated collection. The core methodological point is that no single ad is evidence: value comes from repeated diffing of listing indices over time, which means snapshotting must begin now to establish a baseline.

The self-reported professional social signal for CZ banking and payments is real and rich but structurally hard to collect: LinkedIn is where Czech bankers announce moves first, in both Czech ('nastupuji do', 'jsem hrdý', 'jmenování ředitelem') and English ('excited to join'), yet only profile pages — not post permalinks — are reachable from a logged-out client, so the announcement text itself resists automated capture. X retains official bank accounts (Air Bank, Raiffeisenbank ČR confirmed) but is login-walled and low-signal for personnel news, and Bluesky shows no meaningful Czech banking community at all. The practical architecture is therefore two-layer: a curated manual watchlist of named executive and specialist LinkedIn profiles for early sight, backed by an automated crawl of Czech personnel-change rubrics — bankovnictvionline.cz/personalie above all, plus MediaGuru, e15, Peníze.cz, ManagementNews and NejBusiness — which mirror the same self-reports within days and are openly accessible. The largest remaining gaps are payments-sector actors (acquirers, PSPs, processors) and the mid-level lateral tier that the cell exists to catch.

The CZ statutory-filing layer is unusually strong for A5: every change of statutory body, prokura, branch or registered office is legally mandated, dated twice (den vzniku funkce vs. zapsáno dne), and available both through the official UI (or.justice.cz, migrating to verejnerejstriky.msp.gov.cz) and as bulk open data (dataor.justice.cz) plus a documented ARES REST API — so a diff-based org-change monitor can be built without scraping. Two structural changes reshape the landscape: the přeměny amendment moved merger projects and creditor warnings out of Obchodní věstník into the Sbírka listin (making the document collection, not the gazette, the M&A early-warning channel), and the Ministry of Justice closed the public Evidence skutečných majitelů and its open data on 17 December 2025, removing free beneficial-ownership tracing. Commercial aggregators (Kurzy.cz relationship graphs, Peníze.cz, Merk, sledovani-insolvence.cz) and the NGO platform Hlídač státu add value chiefly as pointer and alerting layers — person-to-entity pivots that reveal unknown subsidiaries and SPVs — but every lead they generate must be confirmed against the official register. The cell cannot serve 'jobs'/'hiring' at all: CZ registers record only registrable legal facts, not staff-level recruitment.

The CZ regulated-market disclosure channel for banking A5 signal has a clear two-tier shape: ČNB's Centrální úložiště regulovaných informací (CUri/OAM) is the statutory archive of every MAR inside-information item, AGM convocation and annual report for Czech-listed issuers, while the Prague Stock Exchange functions mainly as a timing tripwire that points onward to issuer IR sites. Only MONETA Money Bank and Komerční banka give genuinely Czech, calendar-driven leadership disclosure — their 'povinně uveřejňované informace' and 'valná hromada' pages carry AGM agendas naming supervisory-board candidates roughly a month before appointment, the cell's rare forward-looking signal. For Česká spořitelna, ČSOB and Raiffeisenbank the CZ entity is unlisted, so coverage depends on the foreign parents' ad-hoc feeds (Erste, KBC, RBI, Société Générale, VIG) plus their EQS/Euronext mirrors, which are English-first and reliably dated. Czech-side collection will be scraping-and-PDF work with no RSS or API found, and the biggest structural gap is that payment institutions and unlisted bank subsidiaries fall entirely outside this cell and must be covered by the commercial register and regulator-registry cells.

The Czech association layer is unusually rich as a people-and-pointer source: ČBA (banks, >99% of the sector), ČLFA (leasing/consumer credit), AKAT (capital markets, 100+ members), Česká fintech asociace/ČEFTAS (50-80+ digital-finance firms), SBK (cards/payments) and ČKMA (crypto) all publish member directories and elected governing bodies staffed by named serving executives, and SP ČR's sectoral-association registry lets you enumerate these bodies exhaustively rather than guessing. Bilateral chambers add an English-language layer — AmCham CZ exposes a Finance Committee, a board of country CEOs and a corporate directory — though ČNOPK and the British Chamber could not be confirmed to publish finance-committee rosters in this pass. The highest-value analytical move is diffing these rosters over time and anchoring every person-level fact in the spolkový rejstřík, which timestamps statutory-body changes independently of what the associations choose to publish. Main friction is technical rather than legal: content is public and collectible, but ČBA's committee pages did not yield content to a static fetch and several sites need headless rendering or on-site link discovery.
