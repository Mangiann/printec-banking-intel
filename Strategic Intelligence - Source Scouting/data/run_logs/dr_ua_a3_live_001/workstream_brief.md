The National Bank of Ukraine is a single-origin publisher: every binding prudential requirement for banks and, since the 'split', for payment and non-bank financial service providers, appears first as a numbered постанова Правління on bank.gov.ua, whose website placement is itself a statutory form of official publication alongside Офіційний вісник України. The practical landscape is therefore one authoritative but bot-defended primary site (a plain fetch of the legislation section returned 403 — treat as WAF-blocked, not dead), one fully fetchable state mirror at zakon.rada.gov.ua carrying roughly 4,176 NBU resolutions from 1991 to 2026 with consolidated texts and status flags, and a thin layer of consultation, association and media sources that surface deadlines earlier or explain them. The critical structural weakness for the A3 deadline workstream is that no machine-readable register of effective dates or transition periods exists: dates live inside the operative clause of each act, so extraction must be text-based and cross-validated between the NBU site, the Rada mirror and the NBU news announcement of the same resolution. Ukrainian is the only authoritative language here; English material is derivative and should never be used to fix a date.

The NBU runs public consultations without a dedicated consultations page: every громадське обговорення is published as a news item carrying the tag 'drafts_discussion' (shown as 'обговорення'), whose body states the comment deadline in Ukrainian prose and whose attachments follow a strict, date-stamped pattern at /admin_uploads/article/ — proekt_YYYY-MM-DD (draft postanova), analiz_YYYY-MM-DD (аналіз регуляторного впливу), tabl_proekt_YYYY-MM-DD (порівняльна таблиця), plus a fixed shablon_propozyciy.xlsx comment template. Upstream of that, /ua/regulatory-activity/planning publishes notices that work on a draft act has started, been suspended or postponed, and the legislation search exposes typed document classes 11 (plan of draft regulatory acts), 13 (regulatory activity) and 21 (effectiveness-tracking schedule), giving a two-tier early-warning structure ahead of the consultation itself; white papers and консультаційні документи sit even earlier. The main operational risks are technical rather than informational: bank.gov.ua and the adjacent government hosts (drs.gov.ua, mof.gov.ua, kmu.gov.ua) are bot-defended and return 403 to naive fetchers while serving normally to browser-like clients, no RSS exists, the tag query parameter is not reliably applied server-side, and no closed-consultation archive or published feedback statement was found. Industry reaction — the dimension the regulator never publishes — has to be picked up separately from НАБУ, ЕМА, the Ukrainian financial and legal press, and law-firm digests such as PwC Ukraine and CMS.

Ukraine's banking/payments adviser layer is a genuinely strong bilingual bridge for workstream A3: domestic majors (Asters, Sayenko Kharenko, Avellum, Arzinger, INTEGRITES) publish dated client alerts that name the NBU resolution number and its effective date, and Asters, Sayenko Kharenko, PwC and KPMG maintain parallel uk/en trees, while Kinstellar's paired UKR/ENG payment-services PDFs are an unusually clean terminology bridge. The standout periodic source is PwC Ukraine's recurring 'Key NBU Changes in <month>' digest, which segments changes by regulated population (banks, financial companies, payment institutions, insurers, collectors) — no adviser in this cell publishes an actual structured deadline tracker, so dates must be parsed out of prose. Lexology and Mondaq are the most efficient discovery pointers into firms that never surface in general web search, but both are wall-protected and should be used for metadata only, with substantive collection done at the originating firm. The main weakness is lifecycle: advisers are excellent post-adoption interpreters and only occasionally flag drafts, so this cell should be paired with regulator-side consultation monitoring rather than treated as the early-warning channel.

Ukraine's forward-dated financial-services obligations are set in two EU-side stacks and mirrored in two Ukrainian ones: on the EU side, the Commission enlargement machinery (Ukraine country page, annual SWD country report, Ukraine Facility conditionality) and the payments/supervisory bodies (EPC geographic-scope criteria and versioned SEPA country list, EBA equivalence work, ECB/SSM cooperation pages); on the Ukrainian side, the Cabinet's 'Пульс Угоди' task-level monitor plus the annual AA implementation reports, and NBU as coordinator of negotiating Chapter 9 'Фінансові послуги'. The highest-value, lowest-noise triggers are version diffs of the EPC List of SEPA Scheme Countries and the Ukraine Plan indicator tables, because both convert political intent into datable, checkable events. Key negatives: no per-chapter Ukraine screening report was found in public form, Ukraine is not yet a SEPA member, and no EBA–NBU MoU or Ukraine-specific ECB Opinion surfaced. Main gaps to close next are NSSMC (capital-markets acquis), EU-side consultation feeds, and the Council document register.

Ukraine's external-assessor surface for financial-sector regulation splits cleanly into three publisher families: the IMF (Ukraine country page plus mof.gov.ua's Ukrainian mirror of the Memorandum of Economic and Financial Policies, where the dated 'структурні маяки' live), the AML standard-setters (MONEYVAL's Ukraine jurisdiction page on coe.int with PDFs on rm.coe.int, mirrored and localised by Держфінмоніторинг), and the conditionality lenders (World Bank DPO prior actions, EBRD technical-cooperation projects funding the NBU's reform PMO). A fourth, increasingly dominant stream is the EU's Ukraine Facility Plan, whose 173 quarter-dated indicators now rival IMF benchmarks as the binding reform calendar. Local-language coverage is essential but asymmetric: the primary documents are English, while the Ukrainian layer (MoF translations, FIU, finclub.net, АУБ) supplies terminology, faster signalling and the domestic implementation view. Basel/BIS is the weak leg — no Ukraine RCAP and no Ukraine-specific FSAP was found, so Basel implementation must be tracked indirectly through NBU acts and IMF reporting.

Ukraine's banking/payments trade-press layer is dense and unusually explicit about dates: FinClub, Дебет-Кредит (news.dtkt.ua), PaySpace Magazine and Banker.ua routinely name NBU consultation cut-offs, transition periods ('90 days') and effective dates, often while a measure is still a draft (проєкт Положення) under громадське обговорення. A second tier — Mind.ua, Ekonomichna Pravda, NV Business, RBC-Ukraine, sud.ua — supplies banker reaction and lay-language 'what changes from <date>' explainers useful for detecting lobbying pressure and slippage, while FINBALANCE uniquely covers NBU's own supervisory reorganisation. English coverage is thinner and concentrated in Interfax-Ukraine (partly paid), Kyiv Post syndication, The Paypers and Ukrainian law-firm legal alerts, which are the most precise English source for resolution numbers and in-force dates. The main structural weaknesses are distribution (much of this ecosystem's fastest signal moves through Telegram channels, only one of which was captured) and the absence of association media (EMA, NABU) whose consultation submissions are the earliest indicator that a deadline is contested.

For Ukraine A3 weak signals, the landscape is anchored on the NBU's own news feed, which carries speeches, interviews and press-briefing texts under formulaic Ukrainian title prefixes rather than in a dedicated speeches section, and is bot-defended (403 on direct fetch) though clearly live. Around it sits a dense, well-defined conference circuit run by a handful of repeat organisers — the banking associations NABU (nabu.ua, not the anti-corruption bureau) and AUB, the fintech association UAFIC with UAFIN.TECH and Ukraine Fintech Week, the Financial Club round tables, the BANKIR club forums, and the European Business Association's committee meetings with NBU deputy governors — whose agendas and speaker lists name both the rule topics under negotiation and the officials who own them. Trade press (PaySpace Magazine, Banker.ua, Finclub) and Telegram supply the fastest and often the only record of what was actually said on stage. The binding constraint is modality, not availability: the highest-value content is spoken and untranscribed, so ASR over NBU briefing video plus a synthetic forward event calendar would raise this cell's yield more than adding further sites.

For Ukraine, consumer-protection supervision signal is concentrated in two structurally separate publishers: the NBU (a conduct-supervision rulebook on zakon.rada, a quarterly complaint-statistics PDF series, and a monthly enforcement-measures news release) and the judiciary (the free full-text ЄДРСР register, Supreme Court legal positions and cassation practice digests), with Opendatabot as the practical API layer over the latter. There is no functioning financial ombudsman publishing dispute outcomes — the bill has been pending since 2018 — so complaint and litigation data carry the entire early-warning load, and the АМКУ misleading-advertising decisions form a second, commonly overlooked enforcement channel against banks. The main collection obstacles are bot protection on bank.gov.ua (403 on plain fetch, treated as live-but-defended) and the absence of any structured, machine-readable enforcement or complaint dataset, which forces PDF/table and news-text extraction. English-language discovery was effectively empty for this cell; all yield came from Ukrainian terms such as 'заходи впливу', 'звернення споживачів', 'несанкціоноване списання' and 'обмежені платіжні послуги'.

Ukraine's payments rulebook is unusually concentrated: the NBU is both legislator-in-practice and supervisor, and nearly every binding artefact in this cell — the Law on Payment Services (No. 1591-IX), Resolution No. 80 (open banking, 25.07.2025), Resolution No. 81 (non-financial PSP authorisation), the SEP-4.1 ISO 20022 rulebooks, and the payment infrastructure registers — originates on bank.gov.ua. That concentration is also the main operational risk: bank.gov.ua returned 403 to every plain fetch in this run, so collection needs a browser-based fetcher, with zakon.rada.gov.ua and docs.dtkt.ua as authoritative-enough mirrors. The genuinely non-NBU intelligence sits one layer out: the EMA Association's Open API Group actually drafts the Berlin Group-based API specification, UAFIC and the banking associations signal industry readiness and pushback, and the Verkhovna Rada bill tracker gives lead time before secondary acts appear. Highest-value watch items for the next cycle are the NBU consultations page (comment deadlines), the still-unpublished open banking technical API specification, and register diffs showing which PSPs and technological operators have completed re-licensing.

Ukraine's primary-legislation channel for banking and payments is unusually well instrumented and almost entirely Ukrainian-language: the Verkhovna Rada bill register ('Законотворчість', itd.rada.gov.ua/billinfo) carries a per-bill card with initiators, lead committee, attached explanatory notes and comparative tables, reading dates and vote events, while zakon.rada.gov.ua holds the resulting acts as dated consolidated redactions whose 'Прикінцеві та перехідні положення' set the transition deadlines the NBU subsequently implements. Critically, the same material is republished as bulk open data (data.rada.gov.ua datasets zpr, zak, zal, meetings, explicitly free to reuse commercially), so the pipeline can be tracked machine-readably rather than by scraping an ASP.NET search form. Around this spine sit the upstream feeder (Cabinet draft laws at kmu.gov.ua/bills), the substantive shaping layer (the Finance, Tax and Customs Policy committee at komfinbank.rada.gov.ua, with meeting protocols, stenograms and висновки), and the closing gates (presidential signature at president.gov.ua and official publication in «Голос України»/«Офіційний вісник України», which fixes the date entry-into-force clauses count from). English coverage is a thin news layer only, and commercial systems such as LIGA:ZAKON are useful cross-checks but paywalled and not collectable.

Ukraine's publication-of-record layer is split across three official vehicles — «Офіційний вісник України» (Minjust-founded, published by ДП «Українська правова інформація», commercially distributed and with no free official full-text site), «Урядовий кур'єр» (executive branch daily, freely readable online), and «Голос України» (parliament) — with the Ministry of Justice's Єдиний державний реєстр НПА and the Rada's ІПС «Законодавство України» publication indices acting as the free, machine-reachable surrogates for the authoritative publication timestamp that A3 deadlines run from. On the act-origination side, the Cabinet's npas repository, the Ministry of Finance's per-year наказ indices and its 'банки державного сектору' section, and the State Treasury's накази together carry the binding banking/payments acts — cashless-settlement obligations for merchants, state bank governance, treasury payment procedures — that never appear on NBU channels. The pre-adoption stage is decentralised: there is no single national consultation portal, so draft regulatory acts must be picked up from each ministry's 'повідомлення про оприлюднення' feed with Державна регуляторна служба acting as the oversight and digest layer. Principal risks for collection are bot defences on zakon.rada.gov.ua (one fetch returned a socket hang up — hypothesis, not a verdict), the paywalled gazette itself, and the legacy Lotus Domino interface of the Minjust register, for which the Data.gov.ua open-data export is the preferred workaround.

Ukraine's non-NBU supervisory burden on banks and PSPs is split across three unrelated domains with three different vocabularies: NSSMC (nssmc.gov.ua) for capital-markets rules, AMCU (amcu.gov.ua) for merger clearance and payments-market competition cases, and the SFMS/FIU (fiu.gov.ua, with normative acts issued as Ministry of Finance orders) for AML/CFT reporting mechanics. NSSMC is the most tractable — bilingual, with a predictable weekly 'ключові рішення' bulletin and an explicit regulatory-activity/consultation section — while AMCU publishes only unstructured dated news bulletins with no queryable decisions register, and the SFMS portal refused automated fetching (HTTP 403, treated as bot-defended rather than dead). The reliable spine for binding text and effective dates is zakon.rada.gov.ua, backed by drs.gov.ua for cross-authority draft acts, data.gov.ua for SFMS open data, and the court register plus SRO associations (ПАРД, УАІБ) as indirect corroboration. One cell assumption did not hold: consumer-protection supervision of banks sits with the NBU, not with Держпродспоживслужба or the AMCU, so that stream should be tracked in the NBU cell instead.

Ukraine's cyber/infosec rulebook for banks and PSPs is written by two disjoint authorities that must be tracked in parallel: the NBU (Regulations No.95/2017 and No.178/2022, updated by No.69/2025 on financial-sector critical infrastructure and No.143/2025 for non-bank financial service providers, with a reported end-2026 compliance horizon and a DORA-alignment framing), and SSSCIP/Держспецзв'язку together with CERT-UA, whose authority now flows from Law No.4336-IX of March 2025 and CMU Resolution No.712/2025 on security profiles and security authorisation. Operationally, CSIRT-NBU (cyber.bank.gov.ua) is the highest-value single source because it carries both the 72-hour incident-reporting duty and sector guidance, while bank.gov.ua's legislation and news pages give the earliest sight of drafts and consultation windows. The data-protection strand is separate again and currently supervised by the Verkhovna Rada Ombudsman, with GDPR-style reform pending as bill 8153 — track the Rada bill card for stage changes rather than commentary. The main blind spots are the non-public register of designated critical infrastructure objects and the not-yet-published financial-sector sectoral security profile, both of which would sharpen who is in scope and by when.

Ukraine's register landscape for banking and payments is centralised and unusually well-structured: the NBU owns the two decisive surfaces — the Реєстр платіжної інфраструктури (payment systems, participants, technological operators, commercial agents, and by extension open-banking TPPs) and the КІС НБУ online registers holding the Державний реєстр фінансових установ — while a bank-and-branch directory API and the NBUStatService REST OpenData service provide machine-readable identifier and statistics planes. The critical operational finding is access, not existence: bank.gov.ua returned HTTP 403 to every automated fetch attempted here, and the data.gov.ua HTML pages did too, so this cell's collection must run through browser-emulating fetchers plus the data.gov.ua CKAN API (package_show/package_search), which mirrors the DRFU and, since 19 January 2026, the restored EDR company register. The register picture is completed from the negative side by the Deposit Guarantee Fund's lists of insolvent and liquidated banks and by the NSDC State Register of Sanctions (with OpenSanctions as a normalised mirror), and expanded indirectly by commercial aggregators (YouControl, Opendatabot, Clarity Project) that resell the same state registers with change-tracking. Local-language search was clearly decisive — the actual data surfaces surfaced only under Ukrainian terminology, while English queries returned law-firm commentary.

Ukraine's banking and payments associations form a dense, mostly Ukrainian-language, and unusually document-rich consultation layer: NABU and AUB on the banking side, EMA on payments, UAFIC on fintech and VAFK on non-bank financial companies, with UKBS as the one named actor we could not reach. Three artefact types matter most and all were located: formal submissions to regulators (NABU's «Вихідні листи»), member-facing plain-language deadline digests (NABU's weekly «Моніторинг змін у фінсекторі», EMA's «Новини платіжного ринку», VAFK's rule explainers), and genuine self-regulation — EMA's Open API Group, which sets Ukraine's open-banking specification jointly with the NBU, and the association-coordinated Memorandum on payment-market transparency that imposes de-facto compliance deadlines outside formal regulation. Access is easy on ema.com.ua, aub.org.ua (which uniquely maintains a full English mirror), fintechua.org and vafk.com.ua, while nabu.ua is rate-limited (HTTP 429, bot-defended rather than dead) and ukbs.org.ua failed DNS resolution and must be re-tested before being written off. The main traps are naming: НАБУ collides with the National Anti-Corruption Bureau, the association renders its own name as both «Незалежна» and «Національна», and EMA appears as ЄМА/ЕМА/EMA — all of which must be encoded into query sets and entity resolution.

For Ukrainian systemic and private banks, the observable regulatory-change channel is not a compliance page but the public-offer amendment machinery: dated 'Повідомлення про внесення змін до Публічної пропозиції / Умов та Правил' notices plus versioned terms PDFs. The richest structured streams are PUMB's numbered notifications feed, OTP's 'Повідомлення про зміни' news items, UKRSIBBANK's order-numbered legal_documents PDFs, monobank's date-stamped umovy PDFs and Raiffeisen's 'оголошення' PDFs; PrivatBank and Oschadbank publish equivalent material through their news archives and conditions portals. Effective dates are almost always stated, but the driving NBU act is rarely cited, so bank-side timing will need to be joined against the regulator's own timeline (bank.gov.ua) and interpreted with help from NABU committee coverage and specialist media (minfin, finclub). Principal risks are overwritten terms PDFs destroying history, aggregator noise around state-bank tariff stories, and an in-app notification channel that is invisible to web collection.
