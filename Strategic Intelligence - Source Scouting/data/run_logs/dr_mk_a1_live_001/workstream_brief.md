NBRSM (Народна банка на Република Северна Македонија, nbrm.mk) is a dense, single-domain source landscape that behaves as both the compulsory publisher and the master pointer for MK banking and payments: it hosts licensed-institution registers (banks, savings houses, payment institutions, e-money institutions, payment system operators, exchange offices), the quarterly banking-system indicator sets with a published release calendar, the flagship Annual and Financial Stability reports, payment statistics with card/POS/ATM series back to 2005, and the oversight documentation for MIPS (its own RTGS) and KIBS (the privately operated retail clearing system). Content is bilingual via a consistent '-en.nspx' mirror convention, but Macedonian is the primary and likely leading edition, so MK-language collection is mandatory. The decisive operational constraint is access, not availability: every nbrm.mk URL tested returned HTTP 403 to non-browser clients and portal.nbrm.gov.mk did not respond at all, so this cell requires a real browser engine and a document-level crawl of the /content/ PDF root, and every URL here should be treated as high-confidence but unverified until that pass runs.

North Macedonia's listed-bank disclosure landscape is unusually well-centralised: essentially every price-sensitive filing from Komercijalna, NLB, Stopanska, Halkbank, Sparkasse, UNI Banka, TTK and peers flows through SEI-Net (seinet.com.mk), the Macedonian Stock Exchange's statutory electronic disclosure system, which the exchange re-broadcasts as a dated, issuer-tagged RSS feed at mse.mk/mk/rss/seinet — a near-ideal ingestion spine confirmed live in this pass with a bank filing dated 21.8.2026. The MSE layers per-issuer profile pages (/mk/issuer/{slug}, with audited/unaudited statements, quarterly income statements and prospectus PDFs in a predictable /Repository/ object store) plus listing rules and corporate-governance compliance reports on top of that stream. The regulator KHV (sec.gov.mk) is the complementary, structurally separate node: it approves prospectuses and share/bond emissions, keeps participant registers including authorised and depository banks, and records its actual decisions in narrative Месечни извештаи rather than a filterable bank register — so capital-raise detection is a parsing job, not a feed subscription. Main technical caveat: SEI-Net document pages appear client-side rendered and yielded empty bodies to plain fetch (a rendering limitation, not evidence the source is dead), so production collection should assume headless rendering or a discovered JSON endpoint.

North Macedonia's association layer is small, concentrated and unusually pointer-rich: the Macedonian Banking Association (mba.mk, bilingual mk/en) is the authoritative direct source for the deposit-taking sector, exposing in one place the full member roster (13-14 banks, 2 savings houses, KIBS and the Credit Bureau), a commission structure that includes payment systems and information security, the SKIBOR benchmark portal with its reference-bank panel, and a rolling announcements/events stream. Around it sit the chamber system — Стопанска комора на Северна Македонија with its Здружение на финансиски организации (broader than MBA: insurers, leasing, non-bank financial companies), MASIT as the ICT chamber whose paginated member directory is the densest available map of the bank-technology supplier layer, and the foreign-investor bodies (FIC with its annual White Book, AmCham, and the German DMWV whose member directory is published as a parsable PDF). Two segment-specific bodies were found beyond the obvious ones: AFD for non-bank/fintech providers and ACI Macedonia for treasury and financial-markets practitioners. The principal collection caveats are technical rather than legal: mchamber.mk and fic.mk are legacy ASP.NET sites with session-id and query-string URLs requiring normalisation, MBA publishes mostly as HTML rather than as a PDF report series, afd.mk failed DNS on a single attempt and must be retried rather than written off, and no Albanian-language or payment-institution-specific association search has yet been run.

North Macedonia's state financial-policy layer is unusually well exposed online and splits cleanly into four functions: rule-making (Ministry of Finance, plus ENER for drafts, Sobranie for passage and Службен весник for enactment), tax/fiscal operations (UJP, which owns both cash-register fiscalisation and the incoming mandatory e-Faktura regime), aggregate numbers (State Statistical Office via stat.gov.mk and the MakStat PX-Web database, complemented by data.gov.mk), and assurance/oversight (State Audit Office, the Deposit Insurance Fund, and the Financial Intelligence Office inside the MoF). For a payments-and-banking watcher the highest-value targets are ENER — where a bill is visible with a 30-day comment window long before the gazette — and the UJP e-Faktura document stream, since it sets the technical standard that MK POS and ERP vendors must implement. Nearly all of it is public, free and PDF-heavy in Macedonian with partial English mirrors; the practical obstacles are access flakiness (finance.gov.mk refused our connection once, most likely bot/geo filtering rather than being down), opaque item ids on ENER, and possible subscription gating on the Official Gazette's full-text and consolidated-text services.

Macedonian-language business media is a dense and genuinely productive layer for banking and payments intelligence: a specialist portal (Bankarstvo.mk) plus several business desks (еМагазин, Faktor, Пари, Економија и бизнис, Makfax) routinely convert quarterly filings into per-bank profit, deposit and fee-income stories, often within days of the disclosure. The most distinctive value is in signals that have no official counterpart — new tariff books and commission increases (Република, Expres), card/POS/ATM outages and customer disruption (СДК, Bankarstvo.mk, GRID), e-money licensing and wallet launches (МКД.МК, Пари), and management changes (Bloomberg Adria MK, еМагазин). Aggregators (TIME.mk, Daily.mk, GRID.mk, Skrol.mk) are the efficient discovery layer and should be used to dedupe and to snowball into outlets not yet mapped, while Provizii.mk provides the structured fee data underlying the fee journalism. Main open gaps: the business weekly Kapital's current web home was not established, broadcast economy coverage and Albanian-language outlets are unmapped, and no URL in this slice has been liveness- or paywall-tested.

For North Macedonia, the English-language external layer is unusually strong and highly structured: IFIs and EU bodies (IMF Article IV/PLL, EC enlargement reports, World Bank WBRER, EBRD transition assessments, EIB CESEE Bank Lending Survey, EBA equivalence annexes) supply dated, comparable, freely reusable assessments of the banking system and of payments integration, while BIS archives English translations of NBRNM governor speeches. The commercial layer splits into open-ish regional wires (SeeNews is the workhorse for MK bank earnings, rate decisions and M&A; bne IntelliNews adds analysis; BIRN adds investigations) and gated rating-agency output (Fitch BB+, S&P BB-/B), where the Ministry of Finance's own mirrors are the practical free substitute. A distinct and underexploited channel is foreign parent-group investor relations — NLB Group and ProCredit Holding publish audited English segment data on their MK banks that is more granular and timelier than most local disclosure. The main weaknesses are payments-specific international coverage, parent-group channels beyond NLB/ProCredit, and back-file access to paywalled wires; this cell contributes essentially no Macedonian-language coverage, which is confirmed as cell 13's job.

For North Macedonia the indirect-signal layer is unusually concentrated: bankarstvo.mk republishes nearly every bank job ad, award and sponsorship item, and vrabotuvanje.com.mk carries the department-level detail (e.g. 'Сектор за картични производи и директно банкарство'), making these two the backbone of a hiring-signal pipeline alongside individual bank careers pages such as stb.com.mk. Vendor and infrastructure signal runs through CaSys (independent card processor) and KIBS (clearing house, host of the EACHA annual conference in Skopje), with MASIT's member directory serving as the ready-made vendor actor map; there is no standalone MK payments conference, so sponsor-list intelligence must be harvested from MASIT forums, the Macedonian Stock Exchange conference, Macedonia2025 and e-commerce events. Awards are entirely foreign-issued (The Banker, Global Finance, EMEA Finance) and their MK citations double as informal digital-investment disclosures. The weakest link is complaints: OPM and the state consumer portal offer intake and guidance but no public itemised docket, and NBRSM publishes protection material rather than complaint statistics, so complaint intelligence will be qualitative until a register is found.

For North Macedonia the archival layer rests on three pillars: the Internet Archive (the only general web archive covering .mk bank and regulator sites, since no national web-harvest programme surfaced), statutory repositories that outlive corporate websites (SEI-Net for listed-issuer disclosures, the Central Registry for annual accounts of unlisted banks and payment firms, and Службен весник for licences and regulatory acts), and durable PDF stores at NBRM (including the orphaned /WBStorage/Files/ legacy path) and at individual banks, whose deep file paths persist long after the pages linking to them are rebuilt. Continuous time series come from NBRM payment statistics and the State Statistical Office MAKStat database rather than from the open-data portal, which exists but shows no evidence of financial-sector datasets. The academic and think-tank layer is real but fragmented: RePEc indexes NBRM and Finance Think output with stable handles, while UKIM's Economic Institute journal and faculty thesis PDFs are open but only reachable by site crawling, with no OAI-PMH repository found. Priority next moves are per-bank Wayback CDX enumeration across the full licensed-bank list, coverage of the SEC and Deposit Insurance Fund, and the payments-infrastructure operators (KIBS, CaSys) which this pass did not reach.

North Macedonia's commercial banks publish their primary disclosure almost entirely in Macedonian on their own sites, and the shape of that publication varies sharply by bank: Стопанска банка and Комерцијална банка run true IR hubs (share data, assembly minutes, dividend tables, a named IR officer at Стопанска), НЛБ splits shareholder material between a modern /za-bankata/za-akcionerite tree and a legacy Cyrillic-path ASPX archive, while Халкбанк, ТТК, УНИБанка, ЦКБ, Шпаркасе, ПроКредит and Капитал банка instead expose report folders or numbered shareholder-assembly document packs with highly predictable, enumerable URL patterns. Three second-layer channels — the Macedonian Stock Exchange issuer pages, SEINET and the SEC's SRIS repository — mirror the same bank-authored PDFs and should be used as a completeness and timestamping check, since some banks (notably Халкбанк, on the free market) route dividend and financial-data notices there rather than through an IR page of their own. The main operational risks for this cell are technical rather than legal: percent-encoded Cyrillic paths that must not be normalised, a kb.com.mk-to-kb.mk redirect, and at least one report listing (Халкбанк) that does not render in plain HTML extraction. The universe is not yet complete — the NBRSM per-bank registry should be scraped to seed every licensed institution, with Силк Роуд Банка the known open item.

North Macedonia's banking sector is overwhelmingly foreign-owned, so the richest English-language disclosure about MK banks sits abroad: NLB Group (Ljubljana) publishes an explicit North Macedonia market segment, ProCredit Holding (Frankfurt) folds MK into a South-Eastern Europe segment plus CRR country-by-country tables, Türkiye Halk Bankası fully consolidates Halk Banka AD Skopje, Steiermärkische Sparkasse (Graz) owns the merged Sparkasse+Ohridska entity, CCB Sofia/Chimimport owns CCB Skopje, and NBG remains the parent of Stopanska Banka. The best-verified channels this run are the NLB, ProCredit and Halkbank IR sites; the weakest are Steiermärkische (no annual-report PDF located) and NBG (IR path inferred only), and Silk Road Bank's Swiss private owner has no IR channel at all. Exchange disclosure platforms upstream of these parents — LJSE SEOnet, KAP, the Bulgarian exchange — plus MSE issuer pages and the NBRM bank registry are the highest-value pointer layer for catching ownership and capital events before they reach IR pages.

North Macedonia's statutory-filing layer is centralised in one institution: Централен регистар на РСМ (ЦРРСМ), which runs the Trade Register, the Register of Annual Accounts, the Register of Beneficial Owners, the Register of Pledges and the Register of Disqualified Persons, and publishes a daily statutory feed of registration changes plus a statistical bulletin. Because filing is a legal obligation independent of listing, this cell is the only reliable route to financials, ownership and governance changes for MK savings houses, payment institutions, e-money issuers and payments/IT vendors that publish nothing voluntarily. The practical constraint is access, not existence: free interfaces are per-entity and legally limited to personal, non-commercial use, there is no open bulk endpoint or activity-code browse, and systematic collection realistically runs through the contracted Дистрибутивен систем or through BIFIDEX, the registrar-owned Western Balkans portal that re-publishes MK statutory and financial data with regional comparability. The highest-value next step is to convert the supervisor's licensed-entity lists into an ЕМБС-keyed watchlist, after which the publications feed, the annual accounts and the UBO register become targeted, high-signal channels rather than an unnavigable haystack.

North Macedonia's payments infrastructure layer is small, concentrated and almost entirely non-listed: the settlement layer is NBRM's MIPS (RTGS, live since 2001), the retail clearing layer is Клириншка куќа КИБС АД, and the card switching/processing layer is Интернационален Картичен Систем АД (CaSys), with Diners Club MAK as a minor non-scheme issuer and NBRM as the designator and overseer of all three. The richest recurring disclosures are KIBS' own statistics page and NBRM's payment statistics and oversight pages; operator press releases (kibs.mk news, casys.com.mk MEDIA) carry launch-level detail but are JS-rendered and need a headless fetcher, and nbrm.mk answers plain fetches with 403, so bot-tolerant collection is a prerequisite rather than an optimisation. Because none of these operators publish investor-relations material, financial depth for the cell must come from Central Registry filings surfaced via aggregators, supplemented by trade press (bankarstvo.mk, ecommerce.mk, faktor.mk) that republishes processor announcements and reports ownership deals. The dominant forward story to monitor is the TIPS-clone instant-payment initiative connecting MK with Albania and Kosovo, which currently has no consumer brand and must be tracked on programme terminology ('иницијатива за инстант плаќања').

North Macedonia's non-bank payments actors publish almost nothing in IR form, so the usable source landscape for this cell is: (1) a small set of company-owned channels — CaSys, Halkbank/HalkPay, Nayax and, uniquely, the bond-listed Iute Group whose IutePay Skopje EMI licence generates wire-grade releases; (2) mk-language trade and business media (bankarstvo.mk, pari.com.mk, faktor.mk, mkd.mk, it.mk) which in practice reproduce company PR that has no other home; and (3) pointer registries — the NBRM PI/EMI registers, ecommerce.mk and AFD member directories, and Startup Macedonia — which are the only reliable way to enumerate new actors such as Flex Pay, MKPEJ/mkPay and MINT Fintech Group. App-store release notes are a strong under-used substitute for press releases among MK wallet issuers. Main weaknesses are an unresolved NBRM register deep link, missing mk vocabulary for SoftPOS/BNPL, and the total absence of remittance-operator PR in mk.

For North Macedonia, the schemes and vendors are a genuinely productive indirect route: Mastercard's Europe newsroom carries MK country announcements (the 2021 national digital ID launch names NLB Banka Skopje and operator Evrotrust), while Entrust, Netcetera and Nextsense each publish named MK bank deployments - Komercijalna Banka card issuance and online banking, Halkbank's tokenised wallet - that the banks themselves never announced. Visa, by contrast, has no MK-facing channel; its MK footprint is only visible through issuer product pages in Macedonian and through MK business media, which is also where Mastercard's local partnership announcements with Stopanska Banka and Komercijalna Banka actually appear. The domestic layer - CaSys as the sole certified card processor and KIBS as clearing house and CA - is the highest-value indirect source set, since almost every card and payment change in the market passes through them. The principal hole is core banking: no MK bank was found named in any Temenos, Oracle FLEXCUBE, Diebold Nixdorf, NCR, Euronet, BPC or OpenWay material, so platform ownership will have to be reached through procurement records or per-bank sweeps rather than vendor marketing.

Public procurement in North Macedonia is unusually well centralised for intelligence purposes: the ЕСЈН portal (e-nabavki.gov.mk) is the legally mandated single publication point for notices, awards and executed contracts across every contracting authority, backed by the Biro za javni nabavki as regulator, the Државна комисија за жалби as a dispute-disclosure layer, and the Државен завод за ревизија as a post-hoc audit layer that names banking and payment arrangements in text. The practical obstacle is retrieval, not availability — the portal is an ASP.NET application that general web search barely indexes, so collection should run through CPV-coded portal queries or the derived OCDS/Opentender datasets rather than keyword search. A second, entirely English-language stratum sits above the national portal: World Bank procurement plans (notably PFM project P176366 under the Ministry of Finance), EBRD/ECEPP, EIB, WBIF and TED carry donor-funded financial-infrastructure work with forward-looking package lists that appear months before any national advertisement. Highest-value unexplored ground is the roster of state-owned buyers (ФЗОМ, UJP, ЕСМ, Македонска пошта, municipalities) and Albanian-language notices, neither of which this pass covered.

North Macedonia's private banks publish essentially nothing about their own procurement: mk-language searches against Комерцијална, Стопанска, НЛБ, Халкбанк and Шпаркасе surfaced employment ads and product notices, but no 'набавки/тендери' board and no supplier-registration page. The traceable substitutes are therefore third-party: the EBRD Client eProcurement Portal (ECEPP), where private MK banks financed by EBRD must publish notices and awards, is the strongest single route; the Службен весник ТЕНДЕРИ section is the legally-published domestic fallback. Domestic aggregators (tenderi.mk, javnipovici.mk, mk.openprocurements.com, servisea.mk) and foreign resellers (macedoniatenders.com, tendersinfo, j360) mostly re-list ESJN data and their private-sector coverage claims need a paid-coverage test before being trusted. In the absence of tenders, vendor relationships become visible indirectly — through bankarstvo.mk and it.mk trade coverage, through Развојна банка's and the central bank's published procurement as category benchmarks, and through capex/supplier notes in bank filings on SEI-Net.
