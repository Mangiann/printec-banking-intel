Banka Slovenije is a well-organised, fully open first-party publisher: the /sl/pravni-okvir database (~1,208 filterable entries spanning national acts, EU regulations, ECB acts and EBA guidelines) plus topic hubs for banking supervision, payments oversight, financial stability and resolution give near-complete coverage of what is in force, while the legally binding text with its entry-into-force and 'rok za uskladitev' clauses lands in Uradni list RS and, consolidated, in PISRS — with an unverified but important signal that gazette publication migrated toward PISRS after 28 February 2026. Deadline flow is generated by predictable annual machinery (SREP/SEP, stress tests, the governor's letters to banks, macroprudential instrument settings, and versioned reporting XML schemas with 'velja od' dates) rather than by ad-hoc announcements, which makes calendar-driven collection viable. The notable structural gap is consultations: no standing public-consultation register was found on bsi.si, so consultation windows for the banking/payments framework surface instead on the state e-demokracija portal under Ministrstvo za finance, in Nacionalni svet za plačila minutes for payments matters, and via Združenje bank Slovenije as an indirect industry signal. Slug structure on bsi.si is unpredictable and English paths are not translations of Slovenian ones, so collection must be seeded from /sl/kazalo-vsebine and treat Slovenian as the master language.

Slovenia's non-NCB supervisory layer for banks and PSPs is concentrated in four bodies with markedly different publishing hygiene: UPPD (AML/CFT, on gov.si, clean and current — Aug 2026 ZPPDFT amendment and MONEYVAL 6th-round report both visible), IP-RS (a large, searchable opinions corpus with a dense bank/payments cluster, split across GDPR/ZVOP-1/ZVOP-2 vintages that must be validity-tagged), AZN (well-structured, with an explicit 'Predpisi v pripravi' consultation subsection and AML enforcement disclosures relevant to bancassurance), and ATVP (materially important for MiFID-scope bank activity but bot-defended — a plain fetch of the domain returned 403, so all ATVP entries are unverified hypotheses, not dead links). Binding force and hard deadlines converge on two cross-cutting publication points that should anchor the pipeline: Uradni list RS, where 'sklepi o uporabi smernic' acquire legal effect, and the eUprava/E-demokracija predlogi-predpisov portal, which carries dated 30–60 day consultation windows on draft acts. The main structural weakness is that ATVP and UPPD announce consultations as ordinary news items rather than in a structured register, so deadline tracking for those two will require keyword monitoring of news feeds plus PDF mirroring; enforcement-publication paths per supervisor remain the largest unmapped document type.

Slovenia's legal and advisory commentary layer for banking and payments is real but structurally thin and mostly unbundled: the top domestic firms (Jadek & Pensa, ODI, Rojs Peljhan, Šelih, Senica/Andersen) publish general mixed-topic article feeds where banking items appear intermittently, rather than dedicated financial-regulation newsletters, and the international offices in Ljubljana (CMS, Schoenherr, Wolf Theiss) supply the most deadline-explicit content — largely in English. The densest SI-specific index of practitioner commentary is Tax-Fin-Lex (partly paywalled), complemented by Bančni vestnik, where advisers and bank staff publish dated analyses of regulatory change such as ICT-risk rules ahead of DORA. Local-language coverage is genuinely available for DORA and domestic statutes (ZBan-3, ZPlaSSIED, ZPotK-2), but PSD3/PSR commentary in Slovenian was not found at all, so sl-language querying should be anchored on domestic statute acronyms rather than EU regulation names. Two of the highest-value channels (PwC SI Pravne novice, CEE Legal Matters) returned 403 to plain fetches and are treated as bot-defended-but-live, not dead.

Slovenia's sl-language business/financial media layer is narrow and heavily gated: Finance.si (the sole business daily) and STA (the agency wire that most other outlets republish) are the two indispensable nodes, and both are paywalled or licence-bound, while Delo/Svet kapitala, Dnevnik and Večer add policy and political framing behind partial paywalls. Free-access coverage of regulation, supervision and payment-rule deadlines is realistically obtained from RTV Slovenija, Forbes Slovenija, Bloomberg Adria and regional portals that republish STA, with Necenzurirano.si as the outlet most likely to surface supervisory failures and de-risking stories that official channels omit. The trade-press core is Bančni vestnik (ZBS, since 1951, EconLit-indexed) plus the ZBS newsroom, which together reveal which draft rules the industry is contesting; banks' own 'aktualne objave' notices proved the most concrete open source for compliance dates. All four direct media fetches attempted here failed (tool refusal, HTTP 403, and a redirect to delo.si's error page) — these are bot-defence hypotheses, not dead sources, and require re-verification with a browser-like agent.

Slovenia has no single banking ombudsman: the consumer-protection and dispute surface is split between Banka Slovenije (complaint and breach-reporting intake, consumer-credit and payment-conduct supervision, retail fee-comparison publications), the industry-hosted ZBS IRPS mediator that is the recognised out-of-court body for banking disputes and publishes anonymised opinions plus annual case counts, and the parallel insurance/investment schemes (SZZ varuh dobrih poslovnih običajev and mediacijski center, ZDU-GIZ arbitration, AZN and ATVP consumer pages). Zveza potrošnikov Slovenije is the loudest civil-society actor, analysing ADR success rates against banks and intervening in consultations, which run through the e-demokracija portal with dated comment deadlines and are echoed by CNVOS and OZS. The definitive actor list is the ministry-maintained IRPS register, best consumed via the OPSI open-data dataset and cross-checked against the EU FIN-NET Slovenia page. Weak spots: complaint statistics exist only as PDF narrative rather than structured per-institution data, the Tržni inšpektorat and the mandatory IRPS/consumer-protection annual reports are not yet pinned, and all URLs here are search-derived and still require deterministic liveness verification.

Slovenia's weak-signal layer for banking/payments regulation is real but fragmented across four independent supervisory sanction registers (Banka Slovenije, ATVP, AZN, the AML Office), a small set of employer career pages, and a handful of commercial event organisers — there is no aggregated national view of any of them. The highest-value, lowest-competition signals are the ZBS Training Centre catalogue (a new course dates an implementation window), Bankart's vacancy page (payment-infrastructure regulatory workload), and Banka Slovenije's own career page (supervisory programmes being staffed up). Slovenian job postings mix native 'skladnost poslovanja' with untranslated English 'compliance officer', 'AML' and 'DORA', so any keyword monitoring must run bilingual sets over the same corpus. Everything in this cell is ephemeral — postings close, agendas are overwritten, statutory sanction disclosures are time-limited — so snapshot-at-discovery is a precondition for building any usable time series.

Slovenia's fintech/e-money/crypto regulatory-interface landscape has a clear two-regulator spine: Banka Slovenije publishes statutory registers and supervisory policy for payment institutions and e-money institutions, while ATVP owns MiCA CASP authorisation, forms, technical guidance and registers, with ESMA and EBA registers providing an EU-level cross-check. The consultation and deadline layer is concentrated in the e-demokracija predlogi-predpisov portal and Ministry of Finance drafts (crypto disposal tax, ZPlaSSIED amendments, the July 2026 payment-means accessibility bill), supplemented by BS announcements and specialist legal media such as Tax-Fin-Lex. On the open-banking side, Bankart's PSD2 hub is the single most valuable technical source because it fronts most SI banks with Berlin-Group Swagger specifications, with NLB's own developer portal and Intesa's group portal as bank-level primaries; Flik scheme documentation adds instant-payment integration rules. The main weaknesses are the absence of an SI fintech trade association, no openly published dated API changelogs (change history appears gated behind developer registration), and an SI CASP population that press reports describe as heavily thinned post-transition but which must be enumerated from the ATVP register rather than from media counts.

Slovenia's archive/aggregator layer is unusually well structured: PISRS's Register pravnih aktov EU is a genuine first-party transposition table (CELEX, responsible ministry, transposition deadline, implementing Slovenian regulation) with a documented API, and it is mirrored EU-side by the EUR-Lex national-transposition collection and N-Lex's translation gateway. Uradni list RS anchors dates of record, while e-demokracija and the Državni zbor bill tracker carry the pre-adoption consultation and deadline trail — the volatile layer that most needs proactive Wayback/CDX snapshotting, since withdrawn drafts and closed consultations vanish from the live portals. The reporting-taxonomy strand runs EBA (framework 4.2 / DPM 2.0, xBRL-CSV switch) down into Banka Slovenije's poročanje pages, which restate the applicable implementing regulation, forms, frequencies and submission deadlines nationally. The main constraints are a JavaScript-rendered PISRS requiring API or headless collection, a paywalled commercial tracker layer (Tax-Fin-Lex/IUS-INFO), and uncovered Ministry of Finance and payments-statistics reporting corners.

Slovenia's primary-legislation channel for banking, payments and AML is unusually well instrumented but is mid-migration: uradni-list.si carries an explicit notice that official publication moved to PISRS on 1 March 2026 under Art. 33 ZoUL, so PISRS - and its documented developer API - is now the authoritative anchor while the old gazette remains the archive of record back to the 1990s. Upstream of publication, the pipeline is fully observable in Slovene: e-Demokracija exposes ministry drafts with hard comment deadlines (the Ministry of Finance ran the ZDUPS payment-means and central-credit-register consultations there), vladna gradiva carry the explanatory memoranda and consultation reports, and the National Assembly publishes every bill in procedure as a CC BY 4.0 XML dump (PZ.XML) that is the cleanest machine-readable deadline feed in the cell, with the Državni svet's suspensive veto as the main source of late schedule slippage. Everything authoritative is Slovene-only - English searching surfaced no official channel - so local-language collection is mandatory, with commercial aggregators (Tax-Fin-Lex, FinD-INFO) and free mirrors (racunovodstvo.net, zakonodaja.com) useful only as amendment-lineage pointers. The principal open risks are that PISRS renders client-side and its API contract was not directly read, and that the Constitutional Court and EU transposition-infringement channels remain unscouted.

For Slovenia, the binding regulatory calendar is written almost entirely at EU level and only annotated nationally: EUR-Lex's Slovenian-language Official Journal fixes the legally authentic dates, while EBA consultation papers, ECB/SSM consultations and supervisory priorities, EPC scheme rulebooks and ESRB national-policy tables supply 6-24 months of advance warning before anything appears in Slovenian practice. The Slovenia filter is not a country feed but a derived join: EBA per-NCA guideline compliance tables and the ECB list of supervised entities (NLB Group, Gorenjska Banka via AikGroup, OTP's SI operations) define who is bound, and Banka Slovenije's regulativni okvir and nadzorniška razkritja pages confirm how. Slovenian-language material exists downstream (EUR-Lex SL, EBA 'Smernice' translations, Bančni vestnik) but never at consultation stage, so English-language monitoring of EU bodies is structurally mandatory and SL is a terminology and confirmation layer. Highest-value targets are the EBA consultation register, EUR-Lex OJ L (SL daily view), the ECB IPR hub and the EPC rulebook library; the largest unscouted EU-origin deadline stream is the AML package under AMLA, plus SRB resolution policy.

Slovenia's payments market infrastructure is concentrated: Bankart d.o.o. operates the BIPS, BIPS IP (domestic SCT Inst), BIPS T (cross-border instant via TIPS) and SIMP-PS systems used by effectively all SI banks, with the Flik national instant-payment scheme layered on top, while Nexi Slovenija, Dinit and the Activa card-clearing system form a secondary operator tier. The binding scheme rulebooks are participant-only, so public visibility comes from three substitutable layers: Bankart's dated 'Opis plačilnih sistemov za procesiranje SEPA transakcij' PDF series, the ZBS standard-setting layer (UPN QR technical standard, upn-qr.si, SEPA implementation manuals) and the Banka Slovenije Nacionalni svet za plačila, whose minutes and 2024-2028 payment market strategy are the richest forward-deadline source. Practical deadline detection is best achieved indirectly, via vendor release notes (Halcom Hal E-Bank / MultiPay, which named 9 October 2025 as a hard cut-off for IPR and Verification of Payee) and participant banks' 'splošni pogoji'. All meaningful content is Slovenian-language; English versions exist only for the Bankart system description and parts of the ZBS site, and several bsi.si deep paths returned 404 during scouting, indicating a site URL migration rather than dead pages.

Slovenia's regulated-actor population is unusually easy to enumerate because Banka Slovenije consolidates almost everything into a single Register subjektov nadzora covering banks and savings banks, EEA passporting credit institutions, payment institutions, e-money institutions, AISPs, payment system operators, money changers, credit intermediaries and NPL servicers, with a companion 'Izdana dovoljenja' page that converts the static roster into a dated licensing-event stream. ATVP supplies the second national register hub (investment firms, MiCA/CASP pointers), while the EBA PSD2 and credit-institution registers plus the ECB SSM supervised-entity list provide the cross-border and passporting view that the national register alone cannot give. AJPES/PRS — with its twice-monthly open XML extract on OPSI — is the identity backbone that lets all of these lists be deduplicated to a single canonical entity per firm. The main practical risk is technical rather than editorial: the BS register appears to be an HTML-only filterable listing with no confirmed export, so collection should be designed around scraping with diffing and a browser-level inspection to find any underlying JSON endpoint.

Slovenia's private-bank disclosure landscape is concentrated and post-consolidation: after the 2024 merger of Nova KBM and SKB into OTP banka d.d., the demand-side view of regulation rests on roughly seven institutions — NLB (the only listed issuer, and therefore the only one with a low-latency statutory channel via SEOnet), OTP banka, Banka Intesa Sanpaolo, Addiko, Gorenjska banka, Delavska hranilnica, plus UniCredit and Sparkasse. Every one of them publishes CRR Part Eight 'razkritja' and annual reports in Slovenian, but almost none offer a feed: monitoring means scheduled HTML diffing of 'obvestila za stranke' and 'letna poročila in razkritja' index pages plus PDF harvesting, complicated by OTP's opaque numeric download IDs, NLB's cross-host redirect to nlbgroup.com, and Delavska hranilnica's two parallel domains. The richest early-warning signal is not the Pillar 3 PDF but the two-months-ahead customer notice of changed splošni pogoji and ceniki, which is where deadlines such as the 9 Oct 2025 Instant Payments Regulation become concretely visible; DORA, by contrast, has no dedicated bank-level channel and must be mined from annual-report risk sections. The Banka Slovenije register subjektov nadzora should anchor the crawl list so entity mergers and licence changes do not silently break coverage.

Slovenia's association layer for banking and payments is highly concentrated: Združenje bank Slovenije (ZBS-GIZ) is effectively the single self-regulatory and advocacy channel for banks, combining coordinated positions on draft legislation (e.g. the 2025/26 cash-accessibility and free basic payment account bill), a rich committee structure covering payments, cards, compliance, AML and information security, a self-regulatory code and guidelines, an ADR scheme, the Bančni vestnik journal, and a training calendar that functions as a leading indicator of regulatory deadlines. ZBS does not publish a structured archive of its consultation responses, so its positions must be reconstructed from its news feed, annual reports, EBA-hosted comment letters, and parliamentary consultation dossiers. Beyond ZBS, coverage is fragmented: GZS has no fintech or payments section (only ICT, cybersecurity and accounting-services bodies), no national fintech trade body appears to exist, and the self-organised digital/crypto voice sits with Blockchain Alliance Europe, Bitcoin društvo Slovenije and the Digitalna koalicija working groups. Counterweight and adjacent bodies worth tracking are Trgovinska zbornica Slovenije (merchant/acceptance side), Slovensko zavarovalno združenje (insurance, bancassurance overlap), AmCham Slovenia's Finance Committee, and advocacy groups such as VZMD that surface in parliamentary records.

Slovenia's public-buyer route is unusually clean: a single statutory notice portal (enarocanje.si, run by Uradni list RS) carries every contract notice, the state e-procurement system e-JN (ejn.gov.si, MJU) carries the documentation and bidding, and above-threshold procedures duplicate to TED where a free, unauthenticated Search API makes systematic monitoring practical. The high-value signal for banking and payments is the pairing of these tender feeds with Banka Slovenije's reporting/AML pages — regulatory reporting deadlines are what generate the compliance-driven IT procurements, and the notice usually lands months ahead of any market announcement. Below-EU-threshold work (municipal banking services, small compliance tools) never reaches TED, so enarocanje.si must be primary and KPK's ERAR used as the post-award net for what was never tendered publicly; DKOM decisions add depth and reveal award delays. Main open work is mechanical rather than exploratory: resolve buyer identifiers for the central bank, FIU, ATVP and state-owned financial entities, pin a validated CPV filter set from historical SI notices, and extend collection upstream to prior information notices and buyers' annual procurement plans.

Slovenia's supplier-side trace is real but thin and highly concentrated: because the systemic banks are private, almost all public evidence of regulatory-driven IT projects flows through Bankart, the sector's shared card/payment processor, and through NLB. The richest confirmed sources are foreign vendor newsrooms announcing Bankart and NLB wins (Diebold Nixdorf/Vynamic replacing BASE24 in 2024, Nets instant payments in 2019, Iliad payment testing in 2025, Temenos core at NLB) plus a domestic layer of Slovenian-language suppliers - Halcom, SRC, SETCCE, Mikrografija, Payten/ASEE, Comtrade - whose compliance marketing dates obligations precisely. A second, underused channel is advisory and event material in Slovenian (PwC Slovenija DORA pages, Akademija Finance's ZInfV-1/DORA events, ZBS's Bančna konferenca, INFOSEK), where speaker and sponsor lists snowball the vendor map faster than press releases do. The main hole is AML/KYC and regulatory reporting: no public vendor-bank pairing surfaced in either language, so that sub-cell needs job ads, annual-report outsourcing disclosures and conference agendas rather than vendor newsrooms.
