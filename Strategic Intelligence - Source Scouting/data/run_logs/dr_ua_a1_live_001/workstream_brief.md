The National Bank of Ukraine is a single, unusually dense A1 node: it operates the State Register of Banks directory, the supervisory-statistics section, a documented OpenData REST API (NBUStatService, plus NBU_BankInfo for the bank directory), the semi-annual Financial Stability Report, the quarterly Banking Sector Review with a fully predictable PDF filename pattern, and an active press service that publishes sector profit aggregates ahead of the media cycle. Because the NBU is also Ukraine's payments regulator, the same domain carries the payment-infrastructure register, licensing of payment service providers and technological operators, and FMI oversight categorisations — covering both halves of the sector scope. The practical constraint is access, not availability: every plain fetch of bank.gov.ua returned HTTP 403 in this run, so collection should use browser-like headers or a headless browser and lean on the data.gov.ua CKAN mirrors of NBU datasets, which are unobstructed. The main substantive gap is per-institution statutory financial statements, which appear to be migrating into Ukraine's unified Financial Reporting System (Єдина СФЗ) taxonomy rather than living as a browsable NBU index.

Ukraine's four state-owned banks publish IR-grade material, but through statutory disclosure pages rather than any true investor-relations function: PrivatBank via its financial-statements page plus a genuinely IR-quality English Integrated Report series, Oschadbank via 'звітність емітента цінних паперів' and an English news feed, and Ukrgasbank via the most complete and best-structured IFRS tree of the four (annual audited, interim, and regular issuer information). The Ministry of Finance acts as a shareholder-side aggregator hosting copies of all four banks' reports plus monthly performance decks, and SMIDA is the machine-readable statutory backstop keyed by EDRPOU. Weak points are Sense Bank, whose disclosure tree could not be mapped from search alone, and eurobond/LPN-specific investor documentation, which does not appear to live on issuer sites. Access is complicated by bot defences (mof.gov.ua 403, oschadbank.ua redirect loop) and by heavy English-language name collision with unrelated 'Privatbank' entities in Luxembourg, Latvia and Austria.

Ukraine has no compulsory SRO layer in banking or payments, so the actor population must be assembled by unioning several voluntary association rosters: three banking associations (НАБУ, АУБ, УКБС), the payments association ЄМА, the fintech association УАФІК, and non-bank bodies ВАФК/ВАКС/АФІ. The single richest page found is ЄМА's member directory, which enumerates card schemes, ~40 banks, PSPs, marketplaces and a credit bureau as linked entries and snowballs straight into corporate press/IR pages; АУБ adds a members list plus its own banking-statistics and Power BI analytics layer, and УАФІК's annual 'Каталог фінтех-компаній України' doubles as both a company roster and market-sizing study. Access is the main constraint rather than availability: nabu.ua rate-limited (429), fintechua.org is client-side rendered, and ukbs.org.ua failed DNS once — all treated as unverified, not dead, and all needing a rate-limited headless-browser pass. Registry cards (EDRPOU captured for all seven associations) and the NBU payment-systems participant register give an independent cross-check on any roster that turns out to be gated or stale.

Ukrainian business and financial media form a dense, fast, and largely open aggregation layer over bank disclosure: Minfin, Forbes Ukraine and Mind.ua each run methodology-backed recurring bank league tables, while Ekonomichna Pravda, Interfax-Ukraine, RBC-Ukraine, NV Business and FinClub push quarterly profit rankings within days of NBU data release. Interfax-Ukraine is the standout dual-purpose source — an English-language earnings wire plus a press-release channel that banks and PSPs use as a de-facto investor-relations distribution point. The main structural weakness is the payments side: no Ukrainian outlet appears to run a recurring PSP or acquiring league table, leaving psm7.com (PaySpace Magazine) as the only dedicated trade title and pushing payments financials back onto regulator and company sources. Almost everything here is indirect and traces to the same upstream NBU reporting, so the cell is best used for materiality signal, entity discovery and translation rather than as data of record; Opendatabot is the most useful pointer for jumping from a media table back to structured underlying figures.

Ukraine's register-and-aggregator layer is unusually rich: a statutory core (ЄДР via usr.minjust.gov.ua free search plus a bulk open dataset on data.gov.ua whose access was restricted in 2022 and reported restored in January 2026) sits under four mature commercial aggregators — YouControl, Opendatabot, Clarity Project and Vkursi — each of which normalises registration data, ownership and financial-statement indicators keyed on EDRPOU and each of which can enumerate every bank and payment company by KVED 64.x/66.x. For actual filings rather than indicators, SMIDA/stockmarket.gov.ua is the statutory disclosure archive holding issuer annual reports (banks file on a bank-specific form, deadline 30 April), and GLEIF's LEI Search plus Golden Copy/Level 2 files provide the international identity and group-ownership backbone for UA financial entities. The binding constraints are commercial: financial statements and APIs on all four aggregators are paywalled, so the wartime archive value of this cell depends on licence terms, not on technical access. Highest-leverage next step is joining the ЄДР bulk dataset with NBU registers and GLEIF UA records into one canonical EDRPOU+LEI entity table, then testing which aggregators retain original documents rather than only derived fields.

Ukraine has no national web archive, so archival continuity for banking and payments disclosure rests on foreign infrastructure — principally the Internet Archive (whose CDX API is the workhorse for recovering pre-2022 IR pages and PDF annual reports by domain), with SUCHO, Archive-It and archive.today as partial supplements of uneven coverage and accessibility. The strongest Ukrainian-side continuity assets are not archives at all but statutory repositories that outlive the institution: SMIDA/NSSMC holds each bank's full issuer-disclosure history independently of its website, the Deposit Guarantee Fund custodies the record of liquidated banks and publishes explicitly dated archives of the member-bank population, and the NBU's since-2014 reorganisation/liquidation register plus the Ministry of Finance's state-bank reporting provide the actor map and a government-hosted mirror. Ukrainian-language search was productive for this register layer and near-barren for archiving concepts, where English terminology carries the recall. The main unmet risks are the non-bank payment sector, which has no statutory repository and can only be preserved by proactive capture before deletion, and the occupied-territory successor institutions, which no source in this slice reaches.

Ukraine's vendor-side disclosure landscape splits cleanly into three tiers. First, domestic banking-software and processing vendors publish the richest indirect intelligence: CS Ltd (CBS B2, self-described #1 core banking system in Ukraine, naming OTP Bank, Bank Lviv and BISbank), UNITY-BARS (ABS BARS, COPR2/Corplight), UPC and UKRCARD on the processing side, and ProFIX on payments connectivity — their press centres and case-study pages are effectively the public register of which private bank runs what. Second, international vendors disclose Ukraine only sporadically but with high reliability when they do: Tietoevry's regulated releases document the UPC open-banking partnership and the earlier Card Suite cross-border acquiring launch, while no Ukrainian engagement could be found for the ATM OEMs, BPC/OpenWay/Temenos, or the card-personalisation majors — an unresolved gap rather than a confirmed absence. Third, because PrivatBank, Oschadbank and Ukrgasbank are state-owned, Prozorro carries genuine hardware procurement records (ATM and ATM-component lots, mobile POS/acquiring service contracts) that name suppliers and prices, giving a hard-evidence baseline against which private-bank capex can be inferred; local trade press (PaySpace Magazine, FinClub, AIN.UA) and the state company register via YouControl complete the actor map. Priority next steps are the Prozorro API by buyer EDRPOU and CPV code, and a Ukrainian-language glossary pass over the ATM, cards and cash-handling segments, which remain English-only and therefore under-covered.

Ukraine's public-procurement channel is unusually rich for banking and payments intelligence: ProZorro (prozorro.gov.ua plus the OpenProcurement API and BI Prozorro) publishes tender notices and award records under DK 021:2015 66110000-4, and confirmed live examples cover internet acquiring, settlement-cash servicing, payroll banking and QR/mobile payment acceptance — each naming the incumbent bank, the contract value and the expiry date. Spending.gov.ua (E-Data) adds the ex-post layer of actual treasury payments and signed contracts, while Prozorro.Sale and the DGF cabinet cover the distressed-bank asset side. Around the core sit pointers and control layers worth tracking: Clarity Project and DoZorro for entity resolution and snowballing, AMCU appeal decisions and State Audit Service monitoring for contested awards, and TED, the EU Funding & Tenders Portal, EBRD ECEPP and World Bank STEP plans for EU/IFI-funded work that never enters ProZorro. The main structural risks are the martial-law direct-contracting regime, which may move most banking awards into report-of-contract objects rather than open tenders, and the apparent absence of a standalone tender for payment acceptance inside the Diia ecosystem.

Ukraine-specific bank earnings in this cell flow through two structurally different channels that must both be collected. The group channel — RBI (Vienna), OTP (Budapest) and ProCredit Holding (Frankfurt) — carries Ukraine as a named country segment or subsidiary chapter inside English-language annual/quarterly reports, results files and earnings calls; OTP's quarterly 'Summary of results' PDF series is the single most granular and most automatable artefact found. The local channel — Raiffeisen Bank Ukraine, OTP Bank Ukraine, Credit Agricole Bank Ukraine and KredoBank — publishes Ukrainian-language IFRS statements, management reports and (for Crédit Agricole) a disciplined quarterly bilingual results press release, with SMIDA and the NBU registers acting as pointers/actor maps. Crédit Agricole is the exception to the cell's premise: Paris does not break Ukraine out as a segment, so its Ukraine data is subsidiary-published. Minority-foreign-stake banks such as Bank Pivdenny showed no foreign-parent IR channel at all and must be covered via local statutory disclosure.

Ukraine's domestic private banks have no shared investor-relations infrastructure: each publishes statutory material on its own site under «розкриття інформації», «фінансова звітність» or «інформація для акціонерів», with a different CMS and file-path scheme per bank, so collection must be bespoke per actor. The two highest-value targets are PUMB/FUIB (the most mature disclosure estate, with a full English mirror and a separate regulated-issuer section) and Universal Bank, the licence holder behind monobank, whose site is bot-defended (403 on plain fetch) but demonstrably live and must be collected via a rendering fetcher. SMIDA and stockmarket.gov.ua (NSSMC) are the only uniform fallbacks, carrying issuer-format annual reports and audit information for every bank that is a securities issuer, while the NBU ownership-structure register keeps the private-bank actor list honest. Concord has no successor — its licence was revoked in 2023 and liquidation completed in 2026 — so that part of the cell should be re-scoped to Deposit Guarantee Fund records and web archives; the Tascombank/izibank cluster remains the main unpinned actor.

Ukraine's securities-regulator disclosure layer is a two-site system: NSSMC (nssmc.gov.ua) sets the rules and publishes decisions, while the statutory content lives in the public database stockmarket.gov.ua and its operating agency SMIDA (smida.gov.ua), which runs the issuer index (~20,000 issuers since 2003), the live regulated-information feed, ESCRIN quarterly/special disclosures, the State Register of Securities Issues, the shareholder-stake database and the violations register. For any bank constituted as a joint-stock company this is a legally mandated, high-credibility filing venue independent of the NBU, and the issuer index doubles as an actor-enumeration pointer — though it has no sector filter, so the bank population must be resolved by EDRPOU joins against NBU registers. The most important structural caveat is temporal: the open-data v1 API and the stockmarket.gov.ua publication archive are documented as covering only through 30 June 2021, after which regulated information flows through SMIDA's feed and authorised publishers, and no successor API has been confirmed. Coverage is strong for banks and bond issuers and weak for non-bank payments firms, which are typically LLCs outside the issuer disclosure perimeter.

Ukraine's non-bank payment layer is regulator-documented but company-silent: the NBU's Register of Payment Infrastructure, its annual payment-systems oversight report and its enforcement newsroom are the reliable spine, while almost none of the named PSPs (Portmone, Fondy, EasyPay, City24, IBOX, LeoGaming) publish an annual report or run an investor-relations page. NovaPay is the standout exception — audited consolidated group statements posted on its own documents page plus a real bond-investor surface driven by fourteen issues and a uaAA rating — and Portmone is reachable indirectly through its owner Kaspi.kz. For everyone else, financials must be reconstructed from statutory filings via the data.gov.ua annual financial-statement datasets and the YouControl/Opendatabot registry aggregators, keyed on EDRPOU codes derived from the NBU register. Trade press (PaySpace Magazine, Finclub) and investigative outlets (Forbes.ua, Mind.ua, Ekonomichna Pravda) fill the remaining gap, being the only public places where volumes, ownership webs and sanctions on these firms surface.

For Ukraine's card-scheme and switching layer, the disclosure centre of gravity is the National Bank of Ukraine: it is simultaneously the regulator, the oversight body publishing card/POS/acquiring statistics, and the payment organisation behind the domestic PROSTIR scheme — so the NBU oversight report, quarterly digests, payments-market presentations and the payment-systems register carry essentially all the quantitative series, while prostir.gov.ua supplies participant-level detail and points onward to member banks. Visa and Mastercard publish no Ukraine-level results; their UA market-share figures circulating in the press are NBU-derived, making scheme IR a low-yield, context-only source. Non-bank processors and ATM network operators (UPC, UkrCard, TAS Link, and the EasyPay processing entity — the group the NBU designates as important payment infrastructure) disclose only marketing-grade volume claims, with audited numbers reachable only indirectly via SMIDA issuer filings and, for UPC, Raiffeisen Bank International group reporting. Main collection risk is technical rather than legal: bank.gov.ua returned 403 to a plain fetch and will need a browser-grade client, and several corporate URLs here remain unverified pending the liveness pass.

The Deposit Guarantee Fund (Фонд гарантування вкладів фізичних осіб, ФГВФО) runs an unusually rich and well-structured disclosure estate on fg.gov.ua, anchored by a statutory annual activity report with audited financial statements, a live statistics dashboard, four entity registers separating banks in liquidation, liquidated, sold to investors and sold to the state, and — most valuable — a per-bank portal template replicated across every failed institution (both as path-based pages and as dedicated *.fg.gov.ua subdomains) carrying financial information, Fund decisions, creditor-claim registers, payout notices and asset listings that have no equivalent for healthy banks. Around this core sit three complementary layers: the upstream NBU insolvency and licence-revocation decisions that trigger every DGF process, the Prozorro.Sale ЦБД1 auction system that holds lot-level pricing for insolvent-bank assets, and independent scrutiny from the Accounting Chamber (Рахункова палата), whose audits quantify recovery shortfalls the Fund does not self-report. The main collection risks are structural rather than availability-based: no machine-readable open-data feed was confirmed, so the pipeline will depend on HTML and PDF parsing; the per-bank entity list is paginated behind a load-more control and must be enumerated deliberately; and Prozorro.Sale refused plain fetches with HTTP 403, leaving its API surface unverified and presumed bot-defended rather than dead. Local-language coverage is strong — the operative vocabulary is process-based (виведення з ринку, ліквідаційна маса, надходження, задоволення вимог кредиторів, відкриті конкурси з врегулювання) rather than dataset-named, so query design must follow the process terms, and the English mirror should be treated as a secondary cross-check only.

Ukraine's bank-audit pointer layer is centralised on one regulator and decentralised everywhere else: ОСНАД/APOB (apob.org.ua plus register.apob.org.ua and a data.gov.ua mirror) is the sole authority on which audit entities may sign public-interest-entity — hence bank — statements, and it additionally publishes quality-control inspection outcomes and disciplinary decisions that qualify each auditor's reliability. Auditor transparency reports (звіт про прозорість, mandated by Art.37 of Law 2258-VIII) do exist annually for PwC, KPMG, EY and Grant Thornton Ukraine and for smaller local firms, but there is no central index — each firm self-hosts its PDF, so collection must run outward from the APOB Section IV firm list. The auditor→bank link can be triangulated from three independent public streams: bank-hosted IFRS opinions, SMIDA/NSSMC issuer audit-information forms, and ProZorro award records for state-owned banks, with bank tender notices ('конкурс з відбору суб'єктів аудиторської діяльності') giving forward visibility on rotation. NBU Resolution No. 90 supplies the regulatory calendar and content rules for bank audit reports; the NBU itself no longer maintains a bank-auditor register.

IFI and donor disclosure on Ukrainian banks is a dense, high-credibility and largely English-language layer built on four mandatory registers — IFC's disclosure portal, EBRD's Project Summary Documents, World Bank Projects & Documents, and MIGA's guarantee briefs — supplemented by EIB/EIF and US DFC press disclosure and by IMF EFF review staff reports, which carry the only systematic public banking-sector soundness indicators for Ukraine. In practice these sources reliably yield counterparty identity, instrument type, size, donor first-loss structure and system-level prudential conditionality, but not bank-level financial covenants, which remain in unpublished loan agreements. Ukrainian-language access to this material runs through intermediaries rather than the IFIs themselves: the Ministry of Finance IFI pages, NBU-published MEFP translations, EU4Business-EBRD programme sites and partner banks' own МФО pages. The main collection risks are the EBRD portal's client-side filtering (no stable deep-links), unconfirmed API availability across most institutions, and a genuine thinness on payments as distinct from bank lending.
