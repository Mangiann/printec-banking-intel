The Czech public-procurement notice layer for banking and payments is unusually well-structured and legally anchored: zák. 134/2016 Sb. forces contract and award notices into the Věstník veřejných zakázek, while NEN (nen.nipez.cz) carries the full procedure — tender documentation, Q&A, awards — for state and many municipal buyers, and confirmed cases such as card-acceptance-and-terminal-rental tenders show the cell produces real signal. Around that core sit a machine-readable layer (ISVZ OpenData, the NIPEZ Registr veřejných zakázek, TED for above-threshold notices, Registr smluv for the signed contract text) and an aggregator layer (Hlídač státu's API, ÚOHS review decisions) that together make systematic tracking feasible without an official API. The two structural weaknesses are fragmentation and invisibility: each authority picks its own certified e-tool (E-ZAK, Tender Arena, Gemin, vhodne-uverejneni, e-zakazky), so full documentation coverage needs a maintained ICO→profile-host registry, and both small-scale contracts and DNS mini-tenders bypass the headline notice streams entirely. Note also a hard scope boundary: private commercial banks are not contracting authorities, so this cell covers only public and state-financial demand (ČNB, NRB, MF, municipalities) — private-bank vendor selection must be sourced elsewhere.

For Czech banking and payments, the EU-level layer is highly concentrated: TED is both the legally mandatory venue for every above-threshold CZ notice and, since Feb 2024, a fully structured eForms archive with a documented Search API, an Open Data Service and a CSV subset — so this cell's value depends almost entirely on getting one well-built country+CPV query into production rather than on finding many sources. The national eSender (Věstník veřejných zakázek / NIPEZ) and MMR's portal-vz.cz form the mandatory Czech-language counterpart: they hold the original cs wording and explain the outages and format migrations that will otherwise look like data gaps. The EU-funds and recovery-plan strand (dotaceEU.cz seznam operací, planobnovy.gov.cz, MMR NPO rules, data.gov.cz) is an early-warning and enrichment layer rather than a notice source, revealing funded buyers and budgets before or alongside publication. Around this sit derivative aggregators — opentender.eu is the credible non-commercial one for supplier profiling, while commercial TED mirrors should be used only to locate a notice ID and never cited as the record.

The Czech tender-adjacent regulatory layer is unusually well documented and almost entirely open: ÚOHS publishes a full-text collection of procurement decisions from 1999 (~16k+), with curated sector digests, press releases flagging significant cases, and the reviewing court judgments republished alongside; NKÚ publishes unappealable audit conclusions plus a forward-looking annual audit plan in its Věstník, repeatedly covering payment-agenda and treasury IT projects. ČNB's JERRS register — with WS JERRS web services since July 2019 — is the authoritative feed for new, amended and withdrawn payment-institution and e-money licences, the best early indicator of who will bid for or buy into public banking contracts. ÚOOÚ is a weaker but non-trivial signal (bank inspections revealing systems and compliance failures), and MF's CHJ methodology plus the Státní pokladna/CSÚIS feed define how bids are evaluated and where treasury-side payment procurement is heading. Main technical risks are the absence of an official ÚOHS API (forcing reliance on scraping or the Hlídač státu mirror), a legacy session-based JERRS UI, and three concurrent .cz → .gov.cz domain migrations that will rot stored URLs.

CZ e-tendering is a concentrated commercial-platform market layered on top of the state NEN/VVZ backbone: QCM (Portál pro vhodné uveřejnění, E-ZAK, Gemin marketplace) is the largest host of buyer profiles, followed by Tendersystems' Tender arena, profilzadavatele.cz, OTIDEA's zadavatel.cz/e-zakazky, and the cross-border JOSEPHINE/PROEBIZ stack, with a long tail of ~15 further certified tools enumerated in MMR's official register at portal-vz.cz. Because publication duties push every public and sectoral buyer onto one of these tools, enumerating the platforms and then resolving buyer profiles on them indexes thousands of buyers at once — the Czech National Bank, for example, publishes via ezak.cnb.cz with parallel profiles on Gemin and PVU. Aggregated mirrors and alert layers (Datlab/TenderMan paid, Hlídač státu free with API, Opentender.eu open bulk data, plus subscription watchers TenderMonitor and dZakazky) collapse this fragmentation into queryable CPV-filterable feeds and are the most efficient collection entry points. The main blind spot is the private sector: no CZ commercial bank publicly names an e-sourcing platform or publishes RFPs, so bank-side demand signal must be sourced elsewhere; 'Bidmall' and 'Tender market' could not be confirmed as CZ platforms and are recorded as negative findings rather than invented.

For Czech weak signals, the usable landscape splits three ways: hiring, events, and announcements. Hiring is best served by first-party bank career portals (kariera.kb.cz, kariera.csas.cz, csob.jobs.cz, cnb.cz) plus the statutory MPSV/Úřad práce vacancy register, because the highest-value aggregators (LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor) are login-walled or ToS-restricted and realistically need analyst time or licensed access rather than crawling. Events are unusually concentrated and high-yield: cardforum (run by Sdružení pro bankovní karty), Trendy v platebním styku, the ČBA event calendar, Fintech Roadmap and Retail Summit together name almost everyone who owns a payments or card-platform budget in the country, and their agendas are public. Partnership/pilot and award signals come from bank press rooms, the MPO 'Česko platí kartou' programme, SystemOnline's banking-IT case studies and Zlatá koruna. The main structural gap is role-change monitoring, for which no clean automatable Czech feed was found in this pass.

Czech-language trade and business media form a usable but uneven A2 layer: procurement-specialist outlets (Ekonomický deník, ITBiz, Lupa.cz) and the NGO data platform Hlídač státu carry named suppliers, contract values and ÚOHS outcomes, while HN, E15, Týdeník Euro and the investigative desks (Seznam Zprávy, Deník N, iROZHLAS) supply the contested-tender and pricing-criticism angle that registers never show. Coverage skews heavily to public-sector IT tenders; commercial-bank supplier selections are essentially unreported because private banks are not contracting authorities, so vendor-deal intelligence must come from trade monthlies (Bankovnictví, Computerworld/ICT revue) and PR channels instead. Payments-specific journalism has no dedicated Czech title — terminal and acquiring signals are dispersed across E15's banking desk, Retail News (merchant side) and Zdopravy.cz (transit ticketing/EMV). Main operational constraints are paywalls (Deník N, HN, Euro, Bankovnictví) and ČTK wire licensing; everything else in this cell is openly crawlable.

The Czech open-data layer for procurement is unusually rich and almost entirely machine-readable: data.gov.cz (NKOD) indexes everything, VVZ/NIPEZ plus MMR's ISVZ OpenData carry the statutory notice and award record in XML/CSV, NEN exposes per-authority profile XML exports, and TED mirrors all above-threshold CZ notices through an unauthenticated Search API. The decisive asset for the A2 workstream is the Registr smluv (ISRS) bulk XML dump, which uniquely carries signed-contract metadata — counterparty, value and effective date — and is therefore the only realistic route to incumbent identity and contract-expiry timing, at the cost of an explicit GDPR-controller obligation the publisher places on every recipient. Supplier verification is well served by ARES (open REST API plus bulk dumps) joined to or.justice.cz / dataor.justice.cz for filed annual reports and ownership, with Hlídač státu, Opentender/Datlab and zIndex acting as normalised or methodological overlays rather than systems of record. Two structural weaknesses remain: no CZ dataset is scoped to banking or payments, so sector framing must be built from CPV codes and a curated buyer list; and expired or pre-migration notices are recoverable only through Wayback CDX prefix queries, with the Czech national Webarchiv still unassessed.

For Czech banking and payments, the group/parent layer is a policy-and-press landscape, not a tender landscape: Erste Group Procurement in Vienna (with a stated Czech subsidiary), KBC's Belgian procurement department, Société Générale's login-gated Sourcing Hub, UniCredit's shared-services procurement function and RBI's group category organisation all govern vendor selection reaching Czech subsidiaries, yet none of them publishes browsable tender or award notices. What they do publish — supplier codes of conduct, audit questionnaires, scorecards, trading conditions and group company lists — defines the qualification bar and the contracting entity, and the Czech-language surface is essentially limited to kb.cz/cs/dodavatele, confirming that a CZ-only search would miss this layer entirely. Actual award evidence must therefore be reconstructed indirectly: group and counterparty press releases (the Global Payments/CaixaBank/Erste CEE acquiring JV headquartered in Prague, the RBI/AKCENTA split between RBI AG and Raiffeisenbank a.s.), vendor case studies, procurement trade press, and the Czech contract register, whose filings leak group-authored procurement clauses into the open. The largest untested and probably highest-value route for this cell is the regulatory-obligation layer — DORA ICT third-party registers and CSRD value-chain disclosure — together with German, Dutch and Italian searching of the three HQ decision centres.

Czech central-state demand for banking and payment services is documented across a fragmented but almost entirely open stack: statutory buyer profiles (E-ZAK for MF, ČNB, GFŘ and SPCSS; TENDER ARENA for Česká pošta; NEN for the rest), the VVZ gazette and TED for notices, and registr smluv plus ISVZ open data for the machine-readable backbone. The single highest-value discovery for this cell is that public-administration payment gateways are procured centrally through a dynamic purchasing system on NEN, documented at platebnibrany.gov.cz with a technical specification on archi.gov.cz — but administered by the Ministry of the Interior rather than the Ministry of Finance, which is an actor gap in the current cell definition. The main structural blind spot is deliberate: below-threshold and sector small-scope purchases at ČNB and Česká pošta are awarded by internal RFQ and surface only as signed contracts, so contract-register monitoring is mandatory rather than supplementary. Czech-language querying was decisive throughout; English-only searching returned nothing usable beyond TED's structured fields.

Czech regional and municipal procurement of payments-related services is genuinely decentralised: the statutory profil zadavatele obligation is satisfied on at least five distinct hosting families (NEN/zakazky.gov.cz, dozens of self-hosted E-ZAK instances, Tender arena, e-zakazky.cz, and vhodne-uverejneni.cz/profilzadavatele.cz), so there is no single searchable municipal corpus. The practical architecture is: use the VVZ profile registry to enumerate buyer-to-platform mappings, run one parser per platform family, and cross-check with three high-value structured channels — Hlídač státu's procurement API, the statutory Registr smluv open-data dumps (which reveal awarded supplier and price even for small-scale purchases), and Prague's own open-data mirrors of the hl. m. Praha, DPP and THMP profiles. The strongest single buyers for payments signal are Prague and DPP, Brno and DPMB, and Ostrava/DPO, whose EMV open-loop ticketing rollouts are well documented in operator communications that can be used as pivots into the underlying contracts. A material share of relevant activity sits outside tenders altogether — Prague's parking payment market is now opened through a published third-party app onboarding scheme at a 4% fee rather than a competitive procurement — so operator and city announcement pages must be tracked alongside the buyer profiles.

The Czech contract register (ISRS, smlouvy.gov.cz, operated by DIA since April 2023 under Act 340/2015 Sb.) is the anchor source for this cell: publication above CZK 50,000 excl. VAT within 30 days is a condition of a contract taking effect, which makes it unusually complete and gives us the signed, priced counterpart to any tender notice — including the actual PDFs of municipal bank account and banking-services agreements. The practical architecture is bulk-first: monthly XML dumps at data.smlouvy.gov.cz (index.xml, XSD-validated, hashed) for the metadata backbone, attachment PDFs pulled and OCR'd for pricing detail, with Hlidac statu providing the full-text/OCR search, per-ICO contract books and risk analytics that the official portal lacks, and ARES supplying ICO resolution for a banking/payments supplier watchlist. Three constraints shape any collector built on this: the banking-secrecy and commercial-sensitivity exemptions redact part of exactly the content we want, the dumps are rewritten retroactively so historical series can change or lose records, and the data is explicitly GDPR personal data with a downstream deletion obligation. No ready-made banking/payments slice of the register exists in either Czech or English, so sector classification and amendment-to-parent linkage are ours to build and validate.

For Czech state-owned and sectoral (utility) contracting entities, there is no dedicated sectoral tender channel: since Act 134/2016 Sb. the sectoral character attaches to the individual contract, so everything flows through the national gazette (vvz.nipez.cz, formerly vestnikverejnychzakazek.cz), NEN, TED for above-threshold notices, and each entity's own buyer profile — which in this cell is fragmented across Tender Arena (České dráhy Z0003016, Letiště Praha Z0005204, VZP), E-ZAK instances (Správa železnic, regional hospitals), and E-ZAKAZKY.CZ (health insurers ZP MV ČR, OZP). The single richest source is Registr smluv (smlouvy.gov.cz), where the actual 'smlouva o akceptaci platebních karet' texts and their pricing annexes are published, with Hlídač státu as the best per-IČO aggregation layer and ÚOHS decisions as the best qualitative view of contested payment/ticketing procurements. Payment-acceptance deals with these buyers are frequently revenue-share and below threshold (the DPP contactless-terminal contract with Bankovní informační technologie/ČSOB and Mikroelektronika is the benchmark case), so tender-only monitoring will systematically under-count and must be paired with contract-register sweeps. Main remaining gaps are actor-level: regional dopravní podniky and IDS organisers, ministry-managed hospitals, the smaller health insurers and Česká pošta are not yet mapped to profiles.

Czech systemic banks publish supplier POLICY, never supplier DEMAND: across all eight targets I found codes of conduct, general purchase terms and responsible-purchasing charters, but not a single RFP, tender notice or award notice on any bank channel, and no target bank appears as a buyer on TENDER ARENA, proebiz or JOSEPHINE. The decisive structural finding is that sourcing is largely not local — Česká spořitelna's external spend runs through Erste Group Procurement (which has its own Czech subsidiary, hence no supplier page on csas.cz at all), ČSOB is bound by the KBC group supplier policy, KB by Société Générale's charter and online-only sourcing process, and Raiffeisenbank by the RBI group code; only KB and rb.cz maintain a genuine Czech-language supplier-facing page, while Moneta files its supplier ethics code among ordinary downloads and Air Bank, Partners, Fio and UniCredit CZ publish nothing at all. Practical consequences for collection: csob.cz returned HTTP 500 on every attempt and needs a headless-browser fetch path (unverified, not dead), Erste's Czech supplier documents sit behind a JS country drop-down, and no bank's actual e-sourcing platform could be named or entry-resolved — the single highest-leverage gap. Until that platform layer is cracked, the usable signal in this cell comes from policy-document diffs, group-level ESG/CSRD supply-chain disclosure, procurement-tech vendor case studies, and Czech responsible-business interviews such as Byznys pro společnost's ČSOB piece, which remains the richest open description of how a Czech bank actually tenders.

For private-sector CZ banking and payments awards, the vendor side is usually the only public trace, and this pass confirms the pattern is real and productive: Diebold Nixdorf publishes a fully named ČSOB case study (whole ATM estate, ~200 DN Series recyclers, end-to-end outsourcing) that no register would ever carry, Asseco CE maintains a Czech-language reference list of CZ banks and building societies, and KB SmartPay documents the Komerční banka–Worldline acquiring alliance. The landscape splits into four usable layers: OEM/global-vendor newsrooms and case-study indexes (Diebold, NCR Atleos, Auriga, Thales, Worldline), Czech integrator news and 'reference/případové studie' pages (Cleverlance, Enehano, Printec, Monet+), Czech-language aggregation and republication feeds that surface those claims faster than the vendors' own sites (bankovnictvionline.cz, penize.cz/tiskove-zpravy, kurzy.cz, systemonline.cz vendor-bylined articles), and an entity/corroboration layer (bank press rooms, IČO register mirrors, LEI) for confirming and dating a claim. The main weaknesses are that global platform vendors (Temenos, Backbase, Fiserv, Thales) name no Czech customers publicly, several key CZ vendors including Monet+ and Printec CZ have no discoverable local reference page, and the retail half of this cell — self-checkout and POS wins at Czech chains — remains entirely uncovered and should be the next pass.

The CZ payments-ecosystem cell splits cleanly into two source regimes. The public regime — ČNB's E-ZAK profile, VVZ/NEN, registr smluv, and the Hlídač státu aggregator over them — is well-instrumented and covers only the central bank and state bodies buying payment acceptance; CERTIS and instant-payment rules themselves are published authoritatively by ČNB but the supplier chain behind them was not reachable by search alone. The private regime — Bank iD, SBK, ČBA/COBS, Global Payments, Worldline, Comgate, GoPay, Twisto and the card schemes — never issues public notices, so vendor selection has to be reconstructed indirectly from corporate press rooms, partner directories, Sbírka listin filings, GitHub standard contributions and CZ trade press (Lupa, Měšec, Bankovnipoplatky). Mastercard maintains a Czech-language newsroom; no Visa CZ equivalent was found, and Besteron plus the scheme certification layer remain entirely uncovered.

Czech banking/payments associations are dense, well-organised and almost all publish open member directories in Czech, making this cell the cheapest available map of both the buyer side (ČBA ~34 banks, ČLFA ~35 leasing/consumer-finance firms, SBK card issuers/acquirers, AKAT, ČAP) and the supplier side (ICT UNIE ~60 ICT integrators, Czech Fintech Association 80+ fintech/payments vendors, APEK ~580 e-commerce and software firms). The single most on-topic body for workstream A2 is Asociace pro veřejné zakázky, which explicitly segments its members into zadavatelé, dodavatelé, administrátoři and law firms — a ready-made procurement role map — with SP ČR and Hospodářská komora acting as association-of-associations hubs for further snowballing. Critically, none of these associations publishes tender notices itself: their value is entity resolution and long-tail actor discovery feeding the portal-based tender cells, not primary notice collection. Main risks are URL instability (ČBA member-page paths 404 when guessed; AVZ uses legacy Joomla query-string routes), Czech-only rosters for most of the highest-value lists, and fragmentation of chamber and municipal membership across dozens of regional sites.
