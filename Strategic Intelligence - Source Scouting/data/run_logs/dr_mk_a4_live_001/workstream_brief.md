NBRSM is unambiguously the single highest-density primary source for North Macedonia's payment, card, ATM/POS and currency-in-circulation statistics, publishing in parallel Macedonian and English trees on www.nbrm.mk plus a modern PxWeb portal at nbstat.nbrm.mk. The landscape splits cleanly in two: NBStat is machine-readable, API-bearing and fetches cleanly, but in this pass covered only external statistics, financial accounts and monetary/interest-rate statistics (which is where currency in circulation lives) — payment, card and terminal statistics remain page-attached XLSX/PDF files on the main site. Every www.nbrm.mk page attempted returned HTTP 403 while remaining fully indexed and cross-linked, so the whole main-site set should be treated as live-but-bot-defended and collected with a browser-grade fetcher, or via the observably predictable static paths /content/statistika/Soopstenija/ and /WBStorage/Files/Platni sistemi_*. Highest-priority targets are the payment statistics pages (MK and EN, which must be diffed for granularity), the register of payment institutions and payment system operators, the MIPS and KIBS pages, the banking-system indicator tables, and the annual and financial stability reports.

In North Macedonia the non-central-bank statistical backbone splits cleanly: the State Statistical Office (ДЗС) supplies the demand-side and denominator data — monthly retail/wholesale turnover, the Eurostat-aligned ICT household and enterprise surveys covering online purchasing and e-commerce — all of it free and, crucially, machine-readable through the MAKStat PxWeb database and its API. The Ministry of Finance adds fiscal and treasury series plus the Open Finance transaction portal, which uniquely exposes public-sector spend on banking and payments vendors. The Public Revenue Office (УЈП) is the authority on fiscalisation and cash-payment registration and is the only public source touching the fiscal-device/cash-register installed base, but it publishes this narratively in annual-report PDFs rather than as data. The Central Registry (ЦРРСМ) is the decisive pointer for actor identity — trade register, beneficial owners, annual accounts — but is largely fee-based and licence-constrained, so budget or targeted per-entity lookups, not bulk scraping, should be planned. No card, ATM or POS transaction statistics live in this cell.

North Macedonia's association layer for payments is anchored by three well-published bodies: the Macedonian Banking Association (mba.mk), whose member list resolves all banks and savings houses plus KIBS and the Credit Bureau and whose соопштенија carry dated sector positions such as the March 2026 strong-customer-authentication start; the Economic Chamber of North Macedonia (mchamber.mk), whose здруженија/групации taxonomy is the master pointer into trade, ICT, financial-organisation and non-bank-fintech member populations; and the ICT chamber MASIT (masit.org.mk), which resolves the POS/ATM and card-processing vendor set. The richest genuinely quantitative association source is the Macedonian e-Commerce Association (ecommerce.mk), which publishes payment-method-preference and online-fraud content that official statistics do not carry, complemented by AmCham position papers and the Foreign Investors Council White Book on the advocacy side and by ОПМ for consumer grievance about bank, ATM and card fees. The main weaknesses in this cell are structural rather than technical: merchant-side sector bodies for petrol retail and hospitality were not resolved to their own publication streams, no association position paper specifically on card acceptance fees or cash limits was retrievable (that argument currently lives only in press quotes), and Albanian-language chamber content — notably OEMVP in the north-west — is entirely unexplored under the run's mk/en language scope.

North Macedonia's payments-relevant media landscape has one genuine specialist — bankarstvo.mk — which alone publishes branch-closure notices, card/POS/ATM outage reports and fee-tariff changes at operational granularity, making it the single highest-value target in this cell. Around it sits a tier of business/economy portals (Kapital, Faktor, Pari.com.mk, Denar, Ekonomski.mk) that re-report NBRM payment statistics but add quantified cash-vs-card framing and executive quotes not present in the official releases, plus general dailies (Sloboden Pečat, Nezavisen, Nova Makedonija, Večer) and wires (MIA, Makfax) that mainly establish diffusion timing. Albanian-language coverage is thin but non-redundant: Portalb.mk and Alsat surfaced merchant-acceptance reluctance and fintech/e-money licensing stories absent from MK-language results, though Albanian queries must be MK-constrained to avoid importing Republic-of-Albania data. TIME.mk and GRID.mk should be used as pointer layers rather than content sources, and the notable structural gap is that no MK outlet tracks the ATM/branch estate longitudinally — media can only supply event detection, not the series itself.

North Macedonia's weak-signal layer is real but Macedonian-only: English queries for cards/acquiring/ISO8583 vacancies return literally nothing, while the same search in Macedonian ('картично работење', 'платен промет', 'банкомати') surfaces named card-business roles at UNIBanka, TTK, NLB, Sparkasse and Komercijalna within one page of results. The practical backbone is a three-part watchlist: aggregators that republish bank vacancy text in full (bankarstvo.mk, vrabotuvanje.com.mk, apliciraj.mk, the state e-Rabota register), institution-level rosters and agendas (the MBA's Commission for Payment Cards and Digital Banking, which names the cards owner at each major bank; MASIT's specialised SEPA events; the annual Regional E-commerce Conference in Skopje), and the research/award layer (NBRSM working papers, UKIM theses that often reprint primary card and ATM series, and Global Finance / The Banker / EMEA Finance citations that date each bank's digital initiatives). Two structural constraints shape any collection design: LinkedIn holds the densest signal — KIBS, CaSys, Komercijalna, Nextsense all post there — but is auth-walled and ToS-restricted, so it needs analyst time rather than a scraper; and vacancy text is perishable, so ads must be archived at discovery or the historical hiring series is lost. Notable absences worth recording as findings in their own right: no standalone national payments conference exists, MK ads never use protocol jargon like ISO8583, and the Albanian-language layer was out of run scope and is entirely uncovered.

For North Macedonia, the cash-usage source landscape has three clear pillars: the National Bank (currency in circulation inside the monthly monetary-statistics releases, plus payment statistics covering cards, ATMs and POS, and an annual payments-developments report), the Public Revenue Office (fiscalisation rules, enforcement statistics, and the MojDDV receipt-scanning cashback scheme that functions as the de facto de-cashing incentive), and the Ministry of Finance (the 2023-2027 Strategy for Formalizing the Informal Economy with its action plan and the EU-facing Economic Reform Programme). Cash-payment limits sit in the AML law and step down over time (EUR 3,000 to 2,000 in 2024 to 1,000 in 2025, with lower ceilings for legal entities and virtual-asset exchange), but every figure found came from practitioner and media renderings rather than a primary Official Gazette text, which is the biggest citation gap. Shadow-economy magnitude is contested across roughly 21 to 40 percent, with CEA, Finance Think and the IMF as the three benchmark estimators; ATM density is cleanly available from the IMF Financial Access Survey and its World Bank/FRED mirrors. The main operational caveat is that nbrm.mk returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching in this run, so the central-bank URLs are bot-defence-suspected rather than dead and need a browser-grade collector.

For North Macedonia payments and card statistics, the aggregator layer splits cleanly into a free, API-addressable public tier (IMF Financial Access Survey and its FRED/DBnomics mirrors for ATM and POS counts, World Bank Open Data and Global Findex 2025 for density and demand-side cash/card behaviour, and the ECB Data Portal PAY dataset purely as an EU comparator since MK is not an ECB reporter) and a bot-defended commercial tier (CEIC, Trading Economics, Statista, TheBanks.eu, syndicated e-commerce vendors) whose MK content is largely modelled or re-derived and whose value is as a market-belief benchmark and pointer rather than as evidence. The archive layer is the strongest and most distinctive asset of this cell: a Wayback CDX query against nbrm.mk returned a rich inventory of superseded 'Годишна информација за движењата во плаќањата' annual payment reports (2017-2020) and Excel workbooks whose live URLs now redirect, 403 or 404 — making the Internet Archive the only practical route to MK historical payment series and withdrawn tariff books. Regional benchmarking is anchored by the World Bank / RCC / CEFTA Western Balkans Payments Modernization Project under the Common Regional Market agenda, with NBRSM's own Macedonian-language press confirming MK participation in the ECB and Banca d'Italia-supported regional interlinking work. Notably, no domestic Macedonian-language aggregator or archive service exists, no free aggregator was found carrying an MK POS-terminal series, and no primary rating-agency document on MK banks was publicly reachable — so the archive sweep plus NBRSM's PxWeb database should carry this cell's collection design.

MK acceptance-side intelligence is fragmented across merchant payment pages, municipal biller portals and supplier marketing, with no aggregated public dataset — but two structural levers make it tractable: nearly every MK webshop names its acquirer/processor on a 'Начини на плаќање' page and redirects to an identifiable gateway host (overwhelmingly CaSys c-Pay, with Monri entering as a challenger), and MK banks publish interest-free instalment merchant rosters that function as de-facto acceptance lists. Public-sector acceptance is concentrated in a handful of high-signal schemes: Skopje transit (Skopska card/app, validators), municipal parking (Parking.MK, POC app, four payment channels), motorway tolling (M-TAG device and M-Card contactless, distributed via Makedonski Telekom), and the two dominant billers EVN and Vodovod Skopje. Fuel forecourts run large closed-loop programmes (Makpetrol Vera, LUKOIL Club) that materially divert volume from open-loop cards, and foreign unattended-payment entrants such as Nayax are visible only through regional business press and vendor case studies. Priority next steps are a scripted gateway-fingerprint crawl of MK merchant checkouts and coverage of the untouched marketplace, delivery, airline and Albanian-language layers.

North Macedonia's payments regulatory layer is centralised almost entirely in the National Bank (НБРСМ), which under the PSD2-transposing Law on Payment Services and Payment Systems (in force 1 January 2023) both writes the secondary legislation and maintains the three statutory public registers — payment institutions (with simplified-access PIs and agents), e-money institutions, and payment system operators — making nbrm.mk the single highest-value pointer in this cell. The practical obstacle is technical rather than legal: every nbrm.mk fetch returned HTTP 403, so the registers are live but bot-defended and require a headless-browser collector; the Ministry of Finance ENER consultation portal, the Official Gazette, and the Ministry's legal compendium serve as earlier-warning and non-blocked mirrors respectively. Externally, the EPC SEPA participant register and the European Commission's annual enlargement report give dated, independently verifiable milestones (SEPA geographical scope from 6 March 2025, operational readiness 5 October 2025) that cross-check domestic self-reporting, while the FIU's AML law supplies the cash-usage lever via the EUR 3,000 cash-payment ceiling. Two substantive holes remain: no domestic interchange-fee cap instrument could be located despite the law defining 'заменски надоместок', and instant-payments evidence is still media-level rather than primary scheme documentation.

North Macedonia's public-procurement disclosure for payment and cash infrastructure funnels through one mandatory hub — ЕСЈН at e-nabavki.gov.mk, which carries notices, tender dossiers, awards and realized contracts for every public contract above MKD 500,000 — ringed by three official layers that add what the hub omits: the Bureau of Public Procurement (БЈН) for methodology and annual volume statistics, the State Audit Office (ДЗР) for operational detail on cash handling and bank fees inside audited entities, and the Appeals Commission (ДКЖЈН) for the losing-bidder and pricing detail that award notices suppress. The highest-value buyers in this cell are the state-owned post (АД Пошта на Северна Македонија), the National Bank as a buyer of cash-processing equipment, the tax authority УЈП (which both procures and licenses fiscal devices), and the utility/transport public enterprises that collect the bulk of the country's retail cash. The principal practical obstacle is technical rather than legal: ЕСЈН is a hash-routed single-page app that returned HTTP 403 to a plain fetch, so collection must run through headless browsing or its underlying JSON endpoints, with Opentender.eu and TED serving as normalised but licence-constrained and partially-covering fallbacks. Everything relevant is in Macedonian; CPV-code filtering rather than keyword search is the reliable way to enumerate ATM, POS, card-personalisation and cash-handling procurement, and that code mapping is the main outstanding piece of work.

The supranational and donor layer is the strongest English-language backbone for North Macedonia payments intelligence: IMF FAS supplies the supply-side ATM (and possibly POS) density series, World Bank Findex 2025 supplies the demand-side account/card/digital-payment behaviour, and IMF Article IV plus the EC Enlargement Package supply annual independent narrative on the banking and payment-system reform trajectory. The donor money layer - EU IPA and the Reform and Growth Facility, EBRD Go Digital credit lines to Sparkasse and NLB Banka Skopje, World Bank digital-economy and financial-sector operations - is documented through TED procurement notices, EU Funding & Tenders prospects and IFI project registers, all of which are machine-collectable and reveal vendors, budgets and system scope. Almost nothing in this layer is published in Macedonian; local-language monitoring must instead target the government mirrors (mep.gov.mk, finance.gov.mk, mdt.gov.mk) that republish EC reports and Reform Agenda milestones such as SEPA accession. The main open risks are whether MK actually reports POS terminals to FAS and whether Findex MK microdata is openly downloadable - both should be resolved before collectors are built.

North Macedonia's commercial banks disclose through three overlapping layers: their own websites (annual reports, AGM document packs, ATM locators and card/tariff pages), the exchange-and-regulator filing stack (Македонска берза issuer pages and the Announcement166 repository, SEI-Net, and the Securities Commission's SRIS portal), and foreign parent groups (NLB Group and ProCredit Holding confirmed; Halkbank TR, Steiermärkische and the CCB/OTP lineages still unexplored). For A4 purposes the layer that matters most is the annual 'Годишен извештај за работењето', which is where card-transaction growth, POS/e-commerce growth, merchant-point counts and digital-banking user growth actually appear — but almost always as year-on-year percentages inside Cyrillic PDFs, not as absolute fleet counts. Комерцијална банка is the only bank found publishing dated investor presentations intra-year; everyone else is annual-cadence, so intra-year monitoring must lean on KB, NLB Group quarterlies and bank ATM locator diffs. The practical build is therefore: MSE/SEI-Net as the harvest spine, bank sites for narrative and network pages, parent groups for standardised comparables, and a separate regulator/scheme source for absolute levels.

North Macedonian bank tariff books are an unusually rich and legally mandated public corpus: every licensed bank publishes a dated 'тарифа/тарифник на надоместоци' for physical and legal persons, plus 'општи услови' and framework agreements, and the National Bank both prescribes a standardised fee vocabulary ('најрепрезентативни услуги', 'информативен документ за надоместоците') and runs a fee-comparison entry point. This pass located tariff sources for the large majority of MK banks (Komercijalna, Stopanska Skopje and Bitola, NLB, Halkbank, Sparkasse, ProCredit, UNIBanka, TTK, CCB, Capital) together with two aggregators (provizii.mk, bankometar.mk) and a trade-press change-detection feed. The hard boundary is merchant acquiring: no bank publishes numeric MSC, POS rental or e-commerce gateway pricing, so acquiring economics must be triangulated from Stopanska's public card-acceptance general terms, the interchange definitions in the Law on Payment Services and Payment Systems, processor CaSys, and media/academic sources. Practical collection risk is technical rather than legal — nbrm.mk and provizii.mk bot-block plain fetchers, and tariff PDFs sit behind long URL-encoded Cyrillic filenames that change every revision, so link re-discovery plus immediate forward snapshotting is required.

North Macedonia's payment infrastructure layer is a duopoly of intermediaries — KIBS AD Skopje (bank-owned retail clearing house, domestic instant-payments operator, digital-trust provider, and MSE-listed issuer) and CaSys / Интернационален картичен систем АД Скопје (the country's only Visa- and Mastercard-certified independent card processor, running issuing, acquiring, ATM and POS network management and e-commerce) — settling into the NBRM-operated MIPS RTGS, which has been dual-currency MKD/EUR since 2017 and is mid-migration to ISO 20022. The decisive finding for collection design is that neither operator publishes statistics: quantitative truth about cards, ATMs, POS terminals and transaction mix comes almost entirely from the National Bank's payment-statistics pages and its annual 'Годишна информација за плаќањата' PDF series, with operator economics reachable only indirectly via the Central Registry, MSE disclosures and executive interviews in trade and association media (mba.mk, bankarstvo.mk, ecommerce.mk). Global schemes are a partnership- and product-signal source for MK rather than a data source — no Visa or Mastercard country-level MK statistics were found — while Diners Club Macedonia (in market since 1996) is the one confirmed local three-party-scheme licensee; no domestic card scheme exists, and no local American Express licensee could be identified. The main access risk is technical, not legal: nbrm.mk returns 403 to plain fetchers and kibs.com.mk fails TLS hostname validation against a kibs.mk certificate, so both must be treated as bot-defended-but-live and collected with a headless browser and a canonicalised host.

North Macedonia's non-bank payments landscape became legible only recently: the PSD2/EMD2-transposing Law on Payment Services and Payment Systems (in force 2023) created payment-institution and e-money-institution licences, and the first grants landed in 2025 (Paysera Македонија as the first domestic EMI, Iute Pay shortly after). The authoritative sources are NBRSM's public registers, card/ATM/POS statistics and crypto guidance — all on a bot-defended host that returned 403 to automated fetching and will need browser-grade collection. Around the regulator sit three high-value non-official layers: infrastructure operators (CaSys as the sole domestic processor with the cPay gateway; Monri/Payten regionally), the remittance distribution layer (UFS with a reported 250+ non-bank Western Union agencies, plus bank agent pages for WU/MoneyGram/Ria that disclose payout points and tariffs), and a strong intermediary-analytics layer (AETM/ecommerce.mk quarterly online-transaction values, IT.mk and Bankarstvo.mk for entrant news). The main blind spots are the un-enumerated PI register, the absence of any crypto-service-provider register, and no public split of non-bank versus bank transaction volumes.

North Macedonia's ATM/POS technology supply is a short, concentrated chain: global OEMs (NCR Atleos, Diebold Nixdorf, Ingenico, Verifone, PAX) do not appear directly in the market and instead reach banks through regional channel players - Printec (present since 2006, ATM supply and maintenance, Komercijalna Banka named as a reference) and the Asseco SEE / Payten group in Skopje (core banking, cards, ATM/POS services and outsourcing) - while CaSys AD Skopje, the domestic certified card processor, actually runs the ATM and POS networks and card personalisation for the banks and is therefore the single richest vendor-side source. Because MK commercial banks are private and tender nothing publicly, the usable intelligence sits in vendor country pages and case studies, Asseco group segment reporting, listed-bank investor presentations, and the small MK trade/business press (it.mk, ecommerce.mk, faktor.mk) that covers payment launches and payments-industry ownership. A parallel cash-side cluster - Nikob's regional cash-processing centre, Perpetum Mobile's daily-takings transfer service, and NLB's own commercial cash centre - is the best indirect read on cash intensity and on banks outsourcing cash handling. The main unresolved holes are the POS hardware import/distributor tier, the absence of a published UJP register of approved fiscal-device suppliers, and no company-registry or Albanian-language coverage yet.
