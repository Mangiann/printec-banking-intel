The ČNB core cell is unusually well-consolidated: a single hub (/cs/dohled-financni-trh/legislativni-zakladna/) fans out into roughly 22 sectoral rule bases covering banks, credit unions, payment and e-money institutions, AML, DORA and crypto-assets, and it is flanked by a small number of very high-value specialist registers — the consultation and draft-vyhláška list for forward look, the dohledové benchmarky for stated supervisory expectations, the ESA comply-or-explain notices for the EU-to-CZ transmission belt, and the pravomocná rozhodnutí register for what ČNB actually enforces. Legal authenticity, however, sits in the Věstník ČNB and the Sbírka zákonů rather than in the browsable web pages, so the Věstník's per-year částka PDFs must anchor any authoritative collection, and the hub URL published for it currently 404s and needs re-resolution. The corpus is Czech-first by design: the /en/ mirror reproduces the structure but not the substance, with benchmarks and Věstník issues Czech-only, so English-only collection would miss both the binding texts and the supervisory-expectation layer. No open-data API or bulk endpoint surfaced in this pass, and ČNB's RSS explicitly excludes three application-driven registers, so the cell should be planned as scheduled HTML plus PDF scraping with a handful of bespoke collectors rather than as API ingestion.

For CZ consultations and deadlines, the landscape is bifurcated: ČNB owns supervisory/reporting timetables (Konzultační materiály a návrhy, Sdělení ČNB on EBA guidelines with their application dates, the informační povinnosti / výkaznictví tree, and the SDAT collection pages that carry actual due dates), while the Ministry of Finance owns genuine public consultations on payments and financial-market legislation, with ODok/VeKLEP exposing every draft in its inter-ministry comment window and its deadline. There is no single machine-readable comment-window or obligor-deadline register in Czechia: deadlines live as free text inside decrees, SDAT collection descriptions and ODok entries, so the calendar must be reconstructed rather than ingested. Czech-language monitoring is non-negotiable — English queries surfaced EBA/ESMA rather than ČNB, and no EN mirror of the consultation index was found. Upstream EBA ITS consultations (e.g. the supervisory-reporting simplification package running into mid-2026) are the best 12-24 month leading indicator of what will land as Czech reporting obligations.

For CZ, regulated entities themselves are a genuinely productive bottom-up channel: nearly every licensed bank runs a TPP/developer portal (21 mapped via a community index, headline nodes being Erste/ČS, ČSOB, KB, Raiffeisenbank, Air Bank, MONETA, UniCredit, mBank, Fio), and the best of these — Air Bank's 'Aktuálně k API' — publishes dated version deadlines that name the regulatory driver, while ČSOB's announced 2 Jan 2026 legacy-endpoint shutdown shows the same pattern at scale. The second, parallel channel is contractual: banks and payment institutions publish 'Změny ve smluvních podmínkách a Sazebníku', VOP versions and 'Oznámení o změně VOP' with a statutory two-month pre-notice, giving a hard practical effective date for legal change; KB, ČS, Fio and GoPay all emit these as dated, filename-datable PDFs or slugs. The ČNB's own index of banks' 'povinně uveřejňované informace' pages is the authoritative seed for enumerating this publisher population. The main constraints are structural rather than legal: no standardised changelog feeds, meaningful registration gating on some developer portals, in-place replacement of documents with no publisher-side archive, and no canonical Czech vocabulary for deprecation notices — so collection needs per-institution HTML/PDF diffing, day-one snapshotting, and a disjunctive cs+en keyword pattern set.

Czech-language media coverage of financial regulation splits into three usable layers: mass/business desks (HN, E15, Seznam Zprávy, iROZHLAS) that reveal political conflict, delays and enforcement pressure; specialist consumer/SME portals (Měšec.cz, Peníze.cz, Podnikatel.cz) that translate rules into effective dates and concrete obligations; and practitioner channels (Bankovnictví's Regulace rubric, epravo.cz, FINREG PARTNERS, FinTech Cowboys) that carry the compliance detail and the industry's criticism of drafts. The most valuable layer is also the most gated — HN and the Bankovnictví print edition are subscription-only — so a Czech media-monitoring licence is the main unlock for this cell. ČNB's own newsroom and enforcement register should be tracked alongside as the terse official baseline, since the analytical value here is precisely the delta between what the regulator published and what journalists reported. All URLs in this slice come from observed search results or inferred site conventions and are explicitly unverified for liveness.

The Czech machine-readable legal corpus now has a clear authoritative centre: e-Sbírka (e-sbirka.gov.cz), operated by the Ministry of Interior under Act 222/2016 Sb., which publishes consolidated and time-versioned texts with dated URL paths and, since January 2024, open data plus a public API whose documentation is reportedly incomplete. The forward-looking pipeline is visible through ODok/VeKLEP (drafts, RIA, mezirezortní připomínkové řízení) and the Chamber of Deputies' UNL bulk dumps, while the supervisory layer that never reaches the Sbírka — opatření and úřední sdělení ČNB — lives in Věstník ČNB (full text online from 2001) and in ČNB's explicit archives of repealed notices. The main structural risk is archival: CZ regulator domains are migrating to .gov.cz, Webarchiv NK ČR serves most of its holdings only on National Library terminals, so the Internet Archive CDX API is the only automatable fallback for rotted regulator URLs and should be wired in from day one. Secondary corpora (Zákony pro lidi, Esipa) are worth keeping as low-rate reconciliation checks given reported completeness doubts about e-Sbírka at launch.

For CZ supervisory outputs beyond rules, the landscape is overwhelmingly single-actor: the Czech National Bank publishes essentially every artefact in this cell across four parallel series — the annual Zpráva o výkonu dohledu nad finančním trhem, the semi-annual Zpráva o finanční stabilitě with its stress tests, the quarterly Rozhodnutí bankovní rady v oblasti finanční stability, and the legally binding opatření obecné povahy that set the proticyklická kapitálová rezerva and úvěrové ukazatele with explicit 'platnost od' dates. Supervisory intent between rule changes surfaces in a separate, numbered Dohledová úřední sdělení a benchmarky series plus the Strategie dohledu priorities page, which together substitute for a dedicated thematic-review feed that does not appear to exist as its own publication line. Most content is dual-published cs/en on cnb.cz with stable, enumerable URL patterns and open access, making structured quarterly and semi-annual crawling straightforward; the Ministry of Finance's Zpráva o vývoji finančního trhu adds the legislative-pipeline dimension ČNB does not cover. The main open risks are the missing EU-layer registers (ESRB/EBA), thin payments-sector coverage relative to banking, and no confirmed machine-readable feed, so collection must poll HTML listings with change detection.

The Czech weak-signal layer for A3 is unusually well developed and is anchored on four repeat events — ČBA EducaPlus seminars, Trendy v platebním styku (CGI), CARDFORUM (SBK) and FinTech Roadmap (Czech Fintech/Wealthtech associations, hosted in the ČNB congress centre) — whose titles and abstracts name obligations (DORA, PSD3/PSR/FIDA, eIDAS 2.0, SEPA Instant, IReF, ESG risk) and their expected timing before CZ-specific guidance appears. A second tier of commercial trainers (VOX, Verlag Dashöfer/kursy.cz) and Big-4/law-firm readiness marketing (Deloitte akce, PwC ČR, KPMG/EY ČR, epravo.cz monthly regulatory column) makes deadline claims explicit because they are selling preparation, and two aggregators (konferenceseminare.cz, EduCity) serve as the best pointer surfaces for discovering new organisers. The hiring channel is real but structurally harder: individual ads on jobs.cz, JenPráce, Práce za rohem and bank career pages do name DORA/MiCA/AML duties, yet they are query-driven and largely invisible to general search, so they need direct parameterised polling rather than search-engine discovery. Main residual gaps are LinkedIn event/post monitoring, a per-institution career-page registry, and Slovak-language coverage of the shared ČR & SR events.

The CZ register landscape for banking and payments is centred almost entirely on the Czech National Bank, which is statutorily obliged to publish who is licensed and who has been sanctioned. The authoritative layer is JERRS (Seznamy regulovaných a registrovaných subjektů), reachable three ways with very different access profiles: a public web application, seven free open-data CSV/JSON datasets that notably exclude payment and e-money institutions, and the certificate-gated WS JERRS SOAP services that are the only sanctioned bulk machine interface. The enforcement face sits separately under Pravomocná rozhodnutí and the ABOK decision database, fragmented across at least eleven sectoral publication duties (payments under §236 z. 370/2017, AML under §53 z. 253/2008, plus new MiCA/DORA sanction duties), complemented by the Upozornění warning list of unlicensed actors. Highest-value next steps are applying for WS JERRS access, mapping the JERRS query grammar and the vznik/zánik delta feed, and closing the passporting-notification gap via the EBA and ESMA registers that ČNB itself links to.

Statute-level banking and payments change in the Czech Republic moves along a fully public, well-instrumented executive-to-legislature chain: MF ČR announces the draft and its consultation on its finanční trh sections, the full text plus důvodová zpráva and RIA appear in VeKLEP/eKLEP with a hard comment deadline, the government approves it at a dated jednání vlády, it becomes a numbered sněmovní tisk on PSP.cz (with a stage-by-stage historie page), passes the Senát, and is promulgated in e-Sbírka with an explicit účinnost date. Every stage is free, open and in Czech only — English-language coverage exists solely as private law-firm commentary, so Czech-language keyword lists are mandatory rather than optional. The highest-leverage monitoring points are the ODok comment-procedure novelties feed and the government's Plán legislativních prací (6-24 months of forward visibility), with Hospodářská komora's připomínkované materiály as an easier-to-parse mirror carrying explicit deadlines. Main engineering caveats: parallel legacy/new domains (mfcr.cz vs mf.gov.cz, apps.odok.cz vs odok.gov.cz), no confirmed API for eKLEP (unlike e-Sbírka, which has one), and no ready crosswalk between eKLEP PID, sněmovní tisk number and final Sbírka number.

For EU-origin rules binding Czechia, the source landscape splits cleanly into three layers: EUR-Lex (binding Czech-language text and the date-of-application article), the EU rule-makers (Commission/DG FISMA, EBA, ECB) who own the level-2/level-3 detail and the consultation windows, and the Czech adoption layer (ČNB legislativní základna and its per-guideline 'Sdělení ČNB', ISAP transposition tracking, MF ČR as transposition gestor, FAÚ for the AML package, NÚKIB for the DORA/NIS2 boundary). The highest-value and most distinctly Czech artefacts are the ČNB compliance statements on ESA guidelines and the ISAP gestor/transposition records — neither has an EU-level equivalent, and both convert a soft EU text into a dated national supervisory expectation. Upstream signal is English-only in practice: EBA consultation papers, compliance tables and trilogue material have no Czech version, so a cs-only watch would systematically miss deadlines while they are still forming. Two dates dominate the current cycle and should anchor collection: the non-euro-area instant-payments timetable for CZ (reported July 2027 for euro instant payments, June 2028 for euro payments from koruna accounts) and AMLR application on 10 July 2027, with PSD3/PSR still pre-publication and therefore currently sourceable only from secondary commentary.

The Czech AML/CFT and sanctions supervision landscape for banks and payment providers is anchored on a single dominant primary source, fau.gov.cz, which publishes the numbered metodické pokyny (notably č. 3 skutečný majitel, č. 4 OPO, č. 5 omezující opatření, č. 7 PEP, č. 9 kontrola klienta, č. 11 hodnocení rizik a systém vnitřních zásad), topic-sorted stanoviska, the sanctions hub with its oznamovací-povinnost pages, the national risk assessments and the annual enforcement statistics. Two structural facts shape collection: AML/CFT supervision of financial-sector obliged entities is shared between FAÚ and ČNB under § 35 AMLZ, so the ČNB AML/CFT supervisory strategy, dohledové benchmarky and RS-numbered opinions must be tracked alongside FAÚ; and the Czech national sanctions list under Act 1/2023 Sb. is owned by MZV (with a CSV distribution via data.gov.cz), not FAÚ, even though FAÚ inspects screening against it. The forward-looking obligation pipeline runs through MF ČR drafting of AMLZ novely, MONEYVAL enhanced follow-up pressure (targeted financial sanctions and virtual assets still only partially compliant), and increasingly through AMLA's EU-level RTS and consultations. Main weaknesses in this pass: no confirmed machine-readable feed on the FAÚ side, no entity-level enforcement register, and the official pre-legislative consultation venues (e-Sbírka, VeKLEP/eKLEP, psp.cz) remain to be pinned with concrete URLs.

Compliance pressure on Czech banks and PSPs from non-financial regulators concentrates in four legally-mandated publishers: ÚOOÚ (GDPR inspections, protocols and annual enforcement statistics), NÚKIB (the NIS2 transposition act 264/2025 Sb. effective 1 Nov 2025, the regulated-services decree 408/2025 Sb., and the Portál NÚKIB notification machinery with its 60-day/30-day/one-year deadline chain), ÚOHS (sole national supervisor of Regulation (EU) 2015/751 on interchange fees since 13 Jan 2017, via its Platební styk department), and ČOI plus the Kancelář finančního arbitra on the consumer-conduct side. Almost all authoritative content is Czech-only, largely HTML plus PDF with no confirmed feeds or APIs, so collection needs cs-language crawling and PDF extraction rather than syndication. The strongest upstream early-warning pointer is ODok/VeKLEP, where these authorities' draft acts and decrees appear with comment settlements before promulgation. Main open gaps are the absence of a located ÚOHS competition/payments decision register, unpinned ÚOOÚ and NÚKIB official-notice-board URLs, and unscouted adjacent actors (ČTÚ, MPO, e-Sbírka).

The Czech association layer for banking and payments is real, active and well-populated, but its regulatory output is published very unevenly: ČLFA and AKAT maintain explicit, dated positions/documents libraries (clfa.cz/pro-odbornou-verejnost/stanoviska-clfa, akatcr.cz/Dokumenty/Dokumenty-AKAT), whereas ČBA — by far the most important actor, with 34 members and over 99% of the sector — publishes no discrete consultation-response index and instead disperses its regulatory reading across news, press releases, its commissions structure and its self-regulatory codes-and-standards library (open banking, client mobility, banking identity), which are themselves quasi-regulatory instruments with their own effective dates. SBK covers the card perimeter with public statistics, security rules and a legislation/regulation remit but keeps member material behind a login, and the Czech FinTech Association advocates informally rather than through published papers. The decisive structural finding is that the association's actual, itemised, dated objections to Czech drafts live not on association sites but in the government's ODOK/VeKLEP inter-ministerial comment procedure and, at EU level, in EBA/EFAMA consultation archives — so this cell should be collected as association-site monitoring plus a process-side pipeline over ODOK, using cs terms 'připomínky'/'vypořádání připomínek' rather than the noisy 'stanovisko'.

The Czech adviser layer for banking/payments regulation has two genuinely periodic, deadline-bearing series: Schönherr's monthly 'Novinky z české a evropské regulace finančních institucí' published through epravo.cz, and EY CZ's quarterly 'Výběr regulatorních novinek' — these should anchor the cell. Deloitte CZ runs a comparable 'Regulatorní novinky' digest but its URL is mid-migration from www2.deloitte.com to deloitte.com and needs canonical-path resolution; KPMG and PwC CZ offer topic hubs rather than a dated series. Below the Big Four, the highest-signal domestic channels are the regulatory boutique FINREG PARTNERS, HAVEL & PARTNERS' newsletter category, CMS Prague's jurisdiction-tagged legal updates, and DORA-readiness artefacts from Glatzová and Pierstone; the international Prague offices (A&O Shearman, Clifford Chance, White & Case) publish at EU/group level and yielded no CZ-tagged alert stream. Practically, deadlines live inside Czech prose alerts, PDFs and seminar agendas rather than in any structured tracker, and epravo.cz plus Právní prostor (cs) and Lexology/Mondaq (en) are the pointer layers that make new adviser actors discoverable.

The binding 'payments regulation with a deadline' for Czech actors sits in four distinct rule stacks, each with its own publisher and cadence: ČNB's CERTIS Rules plus the CZK instant-payments scheme (Czech-language, versioned PDFs whose filenames encode effective dates); the EPC's SEPA rulebook family (SCT, SCT Inst, VoP) with formal consultation windows, adherence registers and hard dates such as 15 Nov 2026 for structured addresses and 9 July 2027 for non-euro-area VoP/instant obligations; the card schemes' consolidated public rulebooks (Visa Core Rules, Mastercard Rules), which are English-only and reach CZ actors indirectly through acquirer merchant manuals; and the domestic ČBA COBS open-banking standard plus Bank iD's dated contractual annexes. Coverage is materially asymmetric by language: CERTIS, COBS and Bank iD are Czech-first and well documented, while nothing at the card-scheme layer is published in Czech, so a CZ-only monitoring strategy would silently miss the card mandate calendar. The highest-leverage automated collection targets are the Czech-BA/COBS GitHub repository, the ČNB CERTIS download gallery, the EPC registers of participants filtered to CZ, and periodic hash-diffing of the two scheme rulebook PDFs.
