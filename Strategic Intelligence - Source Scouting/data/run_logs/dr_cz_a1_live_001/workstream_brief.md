The four systemic Czech banks do not form the symmetric IR estate the cell brief assumed. The Czech statutory quarterly disclosure set is one of the best-organised source cells in the region because the regulator itself maintains the index: ČNB publishes six 'Přehled odkazů na webové stránky…' pages mapping every bank, third-country branch, credit union, financial holding, investment company, pension company and securities dealer to the URL of its own disclosure corner — 24 banks and 6 credit unions were enumerated with deep links from this slice alone, covering unlisted institutions (Fio, Air Bank, CREDITAS, Trinity, PPF banka, Partners Banka, Artesa, NEY, Citfin) whose quarterly XLSX files are their only structured public financial reporting, including ownership, organisational chart, branch counts and FTE headcount. For Czech banking and payments, the statutory disclosure floor is the Ministry of Justice public register and its Sbírka listin: every Czech-incorporated bank, payment institution, processor, ATM operator and IT supplier must lodge účetní závěrka, výroční zpráva, zpráva auditora and (in groups) zpráva o vztazích there, free to read, which makes it the only channel where a silent private payments company still reveals revenue, auditor and intra-group relations — confirmed live on a bank with an unbroken 2018-2024 series. The practical architecture is two-layer: ARES (open REST API, IČO/NACE/legal form) and the ISVR bulk open data build and refresh the actor universe, then per-entity or.justice.cz URLs (rejstrik-$firma?ico= -> vypis-sl-firma?subjektId= -> vypis-sl-detail?dokument=) fetch the actual filings, with úplný výpis supplying name changes, shareholders and branches that no company website lists. The binding constraints are legal and mechanical rather than editorial: the register web UI is capped at 3000 requests/day and 50/minute with random CAPTCHA, the ISVR open-data licence states non-commercial use and GDPR controller status, there is no bulk feed of the deed PDFs (which are Czech-only and often scanned), and the beneficial-ownership register ESM was closed to the public on 17 December 2025 — so ownership depth now needs a lawful access route or a commercial provider (CRIBIS, Dun & Bradstreet CZ, Merk). Priorities: enumerate the actor list from ČNB's licensed-entity register plus NACE sweeps in ARES, settle the ISVR licence question, and build the OCR/extraction stage before scaling collection.

The Czech debt-investor disclosure channel has an unusually clean spine: ČNB publishes the closed list of six banks licensed to run a covered block (UniCredit Bank CZ&SK, Česká spořitelna, Hypoteční banka, MONETA Money Bank, Raiffeisenbank and Komerční banka) with LEI/IČO and the ISINs entitled to the 'European Covered Bond (Premium)' label, and runs two Oracle-based registers — approved prospectuses and final terms/emisní dodatky — that together date-stamp essentially every bank bond programme and tranche in the country, plus an OAM store that captures regulated filings from debt-only issuers invisible to equity IR. The rich data, though, lives on the issuer side and is uneven: Komerční banka and MONETA publish exemplary debt pages (quarterly Covered Bond Investor Reports, machine-readable HTT workbooks, cover-pool overviews, full AT1/T2/SNP prospectus stacks), Česká spořitelna began HTT reporting in 2026 on a CZK 308.5bn cover pool, UniCredit CZ&SK runs the largest labelled XS franchise behind a 403-guarded site, while Raiffeisenbank and Hypoteční banka hold licensed cover pools with no cover-pool disclosure surface located at all. Terminology is the main recall risk — three coexisting Czech renderings (hypoteční zástavní listy, kryté dluhopisy/krytý blok, evropský krytý dluhopis) and no native term for senior non-preferred — and four issuer hosts (UniCredit, ČSOB, ČSOB Hypoteční banka, CREDITAS) returned 403/500 to plain fetches while their PDFs remain reachable, so a headless-browser tier is a prerequisite rather than an optimisation. Pan-European scaffolding (Covered Bond Label HTT database, ECBC Fact Book, free NORD/LB Floor Research) fills the interpretive layer and is the fastest way to detect a new Czech filing.

The Czech non-bank licensed segment is unusually well mapped by its own regulator: ČNB maintains five parallel 'přehled odkazů' index pages that deep-link each licensed entity to its own statutory disclosure page, and between them they define this cell's entire universe — 5 building societies (ČSOB Stavební spořitelna, Modrá pyramida, MONETA, Raiffeisen, Buřinka), 6 credit unions, 9 pension companies, 8 investment companies and 35 investment firms — with JERRS and the Sbírka listin as authoritative backstops. Disclosure quality is highly uneven: Raiffeisen and MONETA publish bilingual annual reports on disciplined schedules and Artesa publishes parseable quarterly XLSX, while the smaller credit unions and several bank-group managers require commercial-register retrieval, and English-language coverage is close to absent outside those two building societies. The cell's distinctive payoff is already visible — Modrá pyramida's ~190 branches converting to KB advisory points with ~500 advisors absorbed and servicing migrating to KB+, and Patria Finance describing its own trading-platform development — none of which is broken out in the parent groups' consolidated reports. The main risks to collection are stale regulator links after rebrands (cmss.cz), TLS/host mismatches on entities mid-integration (modrapyramida.cz), and archive loss during the several site migrations now under way.

The Czech public-sector financial landscape in this cell is unusually well documented but almost entirely PDF-based and Czech-first: NRB, ČEB, EGAP, GSFT, the CNB, the Financial Arbiter and MF all maintain deep, stable annual-report archives (EGAP 2003-2025, GSFT 2005-2025 on predictable paths, ČEB 2012-2025, NRB 2002-2025, CNB with a genuine English mirror), and MF adds a near-monthly state-ownership register that makes the ownership chain over EGAP and the ČEB/NRB group traceable in near-real time. Because these bodies publish on a public-accountability rather than investor basis, three routes exist that commercial-bank cells do not have: statutory reporting to the Chamber of Deputies (EGAP and CNB submissions, findable via fulltext.psp.cz), independent NKÚ audit conclusions that have historically contradicted the institutions' own accounts, and full procurement exposure through E-ZAK/NEN/TenderArena plus the Registr smluv, which names IT and services suppliers and contract values outright. The main risks to a collection plan are structural rather than technical: ČEB is being absorbed into NRB under the 2024 National Development Bank Act, so reporting perimeters and time series will break; the same statutory duty appears under two near-identical Czech slugs (povinně uveřejňované vs. zveřejňované), and MF content straddles mfcr.cz and mf.gov.cz. No first-party API exists for any core entity, so machine-readable collection must lean on Hlídač státu, MONITOR státní pokladna and the Registr smluv, with EGAP's tokenised download links re-scraped rather than cached.
