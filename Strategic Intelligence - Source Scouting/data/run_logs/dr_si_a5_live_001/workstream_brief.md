Slovenia's supervisory layer splits cleanly into three publication regimes for leadership moves. Banka Slovenije is the strongest: its 'Izdana dovoljenja' table publishes dated, name-level authorisations for bank and savings-bank management and supervisory board members under ZBan-3, alongside qualified-holdings and services authorisations, and it maintains statutory registers for payment and e-money institutions. ATVP and AZN are legally the fit-and-proper authorities for investment firms, fund managers and insurers, but only their application forms, guidance and firm-level registers were confirmed public — person-level decisions were not, so those sides likely need AJPES ePRS and issuer disclosures as fallbacks. As employers, all four bodies plus the Ministry of Finance and UJP are well covered: AZN and ATVP post vacancies on their own sites (AZN confirmed live with three dated Aug-2026 posts), Banka Slovenije uses its kadrovski-razpisi application and the legacy apl.bsi.si endpoint, and the central gov.si vacancy register carries ministry, UJP and even agency-director competitions, while the very top appointments (governor, Council members) run through the President's nomination, the National Assembly vote and a Sklep o imenovanju in Uradni list.

Slovenian bank corporate channels are a rich, mostly open, Slovenian-first source layer for leadership and org-change intelligence: NLB and SID banka run dated, well-structured investor/news feeds, Delavska hranilnica maintains an explicit 'Obvestila' announcements index, and Gorenjska banka, Addiko SI, Intesa Sanpaolo SI and Hranilnica LON each publish appointment notices on their own sites. Two structural anchors sit above the individual banks: SEOnet (statutory regulated-information repository) for listed issuers, and the Bank of Slovenia register of supervised entities as the canonical actor map — with SDH acting as an upstream announcer of supervisory-board nominations at state-owned banks. Consolidation has reshaped the cell: Nova KBM and SKB are now a single OTP banka entity and Sberbank's successor was absorbed into NLB, so legacy newsroom assumptions must be revalidated. The main uncovered ground is the hiring side (no career/vacancy portals enumerated yet), the smallest savings banks with no press function, and the statutory registers that would give legally-dated confirmation of each appointment.

Slovenia's banking-supply side is almost entirely private and small, so hiring and leadership signal is fragmented across four layers: vendor careers pages that genuinely publish (Bankart, Halcom, Kontron SI, Endava Ljubljana), national job aggregators and the statutory ESS/PDM-1 vacancy channel that catch the vendors who do not, the AJPES business register which is the only near-authoritative record of country-manager and representative changes at private d.o.o. suppliers, and local IT trade press (Racunalniske novice, Finance.si Top IKT) for appointment announcements. The global ATM OEMs (NCR Atleos, Diebold Nixdorf) show no Slovenia-specific leadership publication at all, so for them LinkedIn plus registry-level entity monitoring is the only realistic route — and both LinkedIn and Finance.si are access-restricted, which is the single largest structural constraint on this cell. Slovenian-language querying was essential and productive: 'zaposlitev', 'prosta delovna mesta', 'zastopnik/novi direktor', 'bancne resitve' and 'karticno poslovanje' surfaced entities and pages that English queries never returned. The main uncovered actor group is cash-handling/cash-cycle suppliers, and no URL here has been liveness-verified.

Slovenian banking and payments leadership news in the foreign-parent chain splits into three distinct publication patterns rather than one. Intesa Sanpaolo and OTP genuinely publish first at HQ - the CEO appointment at Intesa Sanpaolo Bank Slovenia was announced from Milan and the merged OTP banka leadership was announced by the Budapest chairman in Maribor - which validates the cell's premise; but Steiermaerkische/Erste inverts it, with Banka Sparkasse d.d. announcing its own board changes in Slovenian only, and Nexi, VIG and the leasing arms publish essentially nothing Slovenia-scoped at all. The reliable spine is therefore a three-layer stack: group press rooms and Addiko's regulated IR disclosures for the announcement, Banka Slovenije's supervised-entity register plus SEOnet for the authoritative and time-stamped record, and annual-report governance chapters plus company-registry data as the backfill that catches everything the PR layer misses. Highest-value next moves are a Hungarian/Italian HQ query track and a primary AJPES registry integration to cover the non-bank subsidiaries that have no press room whatsoever.

Slovenia's statutory-disclosure record for banking and payments staffing rests on three pillars: SEOnet at the Ljubljana Stock Exchange for real-time regulated announcements (effectively NLB plus bond issuers), AJPES JOLP and ePRS as the universal filed-report and representative register covering every non-listed bank, savings bank and payments company, and the banks' own investor pages carrying annual reports plus Pillar 3 'razkritja po 3. stebru' with CRR remuneration and identified-staff tables. Banka Slovenije's Register subjektov nadzora defines the entity universe and should drive the collection target list; SDH's capital-investment report adds the state-appointment trail for NLB and SID banka. Coverage is strongest for NLB, OTP, UniCredit, Intesa Sanpaolo, SID and Gorenjska banka and thinnest for the small hranilnice and the non-bank payment institutions, which must be reached through AJPES rather than their own sites. Everything of substance is Slovenian-language and mostly PDF, so parsing must be SL-native and the AJPES ~20-entity daily view cap has to be designed around.

Slovenia's banking/payments weak-signal event layer is small, stable and highly scrapeable: ZBS anchors the sector with the Dan slovenskih bančnikov / Bančna konferenca plus a public terminski plan, while DSI (informatics, with per-year archived speaker pages), INFOSEK (security), Večer's Finančna konferenca and the regional Money Motion in Zagreb supply the recurring speaker rosters where bank titles are stated explicitly. Professional-chapter bodies (ISACA Slovenija, IIBA Slovenia) and business-community programmes (AmCham) add a mid-senior layer of audit, BA and digital roles. The strongest and most quantified pipeline signal is not the conferences but the university channel: NLB's kadrovske-štipendije programme and the faculty-hosted scholarship calls at EF, FE, FRI and FMF (including SID Banka) name sponsoring bank, target study programme and intake window months before any vacancy appears. Main structural risks are that most conference sites overwrite last year's roster (so archiving must be built in from day one), that programmes are often PDF-only, and that no Slovenian individual banker-of-the-year award or national fintech association exists to provide a ready-made person directory.

Slovenia's workforce-side banking intelligence rests on a narrow but high-quality set of actors: Sindikat bančništva Slovenije (representative since 1993, negotiator of the Kolektivna pogodba dejavnosti bančništva) plus the ZSSS-affiliated SFOS, with company-level house unions such as Sindikat NLB acting as separate and sometimes opposing signatories — the 2026 tarifna priloga was signed by Sindikat NLB but rejected by SBS over inflation indexation. The paradox of the cell is that these unions publish almost nothing themselves: SBS has no news feed or RSS, and house unions have no websites, so their positions become observable only through Delo, Večer, Megafon, RTV SLO and 24ur's 'zapiranje poslovalnic' tag, while the authoritative texts land in Uradni list, PISRS and the MDDSZ collective-agreement register (available as an OPSI open dataset). Quantitatively the cell is anchored by SURS SiStat and ZRSZ 'Trg dela v številkah', both only at SKD sector K granularity and both broken by the SKD 2008 to SKD 2025 changeover; the strongest leading indicator — the employer's mandatory 30-day collective-dismissal notification to ZRSZ — exists but is not published, making Eurofound's ERM Slovenia database and the ZBS branch-hours directory the best practical early-warning proxies. Local-language searching was decisive: identical English queries returned only 2020-era restructuring material, while Slovenian queries surfaced current bargaining and the OTP/Nova KBM/SKB integration staffing story.

Slovenian banking and payments people-moves reach English readers through a narrow, well-defined relay: SeeNews and STA English do the bulk of the translating, The Slovenia Times and bne IntelliNews add narrative context, and parent-group newsrooms (Intesa Sanpaolo, OTP Group, NLB's int-en investor stream) are frequently the true upstream that the aggregators re-report. The payments trade press this cell hypothesised - Finextra, The Paypers, FinTech Futures, IBS Intelligence - proved low-yield for Slovenia specifically; the only genuine dedicated regional people-moves column found is CEE Legal Matters' 'On The Move', which skews legal and compliance. Access, not existence, is the binding constraint: SeeNews, Central Banking, Bloomberg and the full STA wire are paywalled, while FinTech Futures (403) and Total Slovenia News (525) resisted a plain fetch and remain unverified rather than dead. Slovenian-language searching confirmed the relay is one-directional - sl-language results resolve to domestic media, so this cell should monitor the English re-publication layer and leave local-language origination to the local-media cell.

Slovenian bank hiring is published through two clearly separated tiers. Foreign-owned groups route vacancies to group ATS platforms — NLB to Cornerstone OnDemand (nlb.csod.com), Intesa Sanpaolo Bank Slovenija to a SAP SuccessFactors portal with a dedicated Slovenia category, Banka Sparkasse to the Erste Group careers portal, and OTP banka (now absorbing Nova KBM and SKB) to FledgeHR — while domestically owned banks and savings banks (Gorenjska banka, Delavska hranilnica, Dezelna banka Slovenije, Hranilnica LON, Primorska hranilnica Vipava) publish plain dated CMS lists with email-only application, or delegate entirely to the MojeDelo portal as BKS Bank does; Workday, SmartRecruiters and Talentlyft were not observed at any Slovenian bank in this pass. The single highest-value source for this cell's payments/cards/ATM focus is not a bank at all but Bankart d.o.o., the shared processing centre most Slovenian banks outsource card, POS and ATM processing to, alongside Banka Slovenije's separate vacancy application (apl.bsi.si, currently empty) for supervisory capacity. Concrete weak signals were already visible during scouting — Delavska hranilnica hiring into a 'sektor placilnih sistemov' and a data-governance role, Gorenjska banka hiring an RDARR validation specialist and a Windows systems engineer, Intesa Sanpaolo hiring a Digital Channels Specialist for Koper/Ljubljana — confirming the cell's premise. Main residual risks are two unreadable feeds (OTP/FledgeHR redirect loop, UniCredit's JS-rendered portal), the absence of any historical series, and an incomplete actor list that should be closed against the Bank of Slovenia register of authorised credit institutions.

Slovenia's payments-first employer landscape has a clear three-tier source structure: a regulator layer (Banka Slovenije registers of plačilne institucije and družbe za izdajo elektronskega denarja) that authoritatively enumerates who exists, a statutory company layer (AJPES ePRS and eObjave) that records leadership and organizational changes before any press coverage, and a thin first-party careers layer concentrated in the few large processors — Bankart (national processor, BIPS/Flik), Halcom (payments software, split across Halcom d.d. and Halcom plačila d.o.o.), Nexi/Mercury (Ljubljana and Koper) and Bitstamp. Below that tier, smaller PSPs and EMIs such as Paywiser, mBills and Eligma/GoCrypto largely lack persistent careers pages, so their hiring is only visible through Slovenian aggregators (Optius, BetterCareer, ZRSZ) and university notice boards, which double as an early-warning channel for graduate-pipeline build-up. The binding constraint is that the two richest leadership-move surfaces, LinkedIn and Glassdoor, are auth-walled and forbid automated collection — so A5 must lean on AJPES court-register publications for verified appointments and reserve LinkedIn for manual analyst review.

Slovenia's register landscape for leadership and organisation change is unusually strong and highly centralised: AJPES holds the population and representation layer (ePRS, the court register and its eObjave publication stream, plus JOLP annual reports with headcount), while Banka Slovenije's consolidated Register subjektov nadzora enumerates roughly 372 supervised entities across banks, payment institutions, e-money issuers and payment system operators, with ATVP covering the investment-services side including a person-level tied-agent register. The EBA EUCLID CIR and PIR registers mirror the SI population in a standardised, country-filterable form and additionally expose agents and passporting entities, making them the best identifier backbone for joining register data to job and news signals. The binding constraints are historical depth and access regime rather than availability: registers show current state, the eObjave feed that carries dated appointment decisions appears to have a limited retention window, personal-data publication was narrowed by the ZSReg-E amendment, and JOLP is explicitly capped at 20 entity lookups per user per day. Two structural gaps warrant immediate follow-up - no located public feed of Banka Slovenije fit-and-proper approvals for bank management board members, and no verified commercial aggregator offering historical representative-change series.

Slovenian banking and payments hiring is visible mainly through sector CATEGORIES on a handful of general national boards rather than any sector-specialist portal, which does not appear to exist for this market. The practical backbone is Optius.com (confirmed bančništvo-finance category with ~39 ads and named bank employers such as OTP banka, Addiko Bank and Triglav, plus a clean filter taxonomy and footer RSS) and Zaposlitev.info (banking category dominated by NLB, with per-category RSS and email alerts - the cheapest machine-readable feed found); MojeDelo.com is almost certainly the largest source but could not be fetched in this pass and remains structurally undocumented. For Slovenian banking and payments, the professional-network and talent-intelligence layer splits cleanly into three tiers: gated but highest-signal social channels (LinkedIn company pages for NLB Group, Banka Slovenije, Intesa Sanpaolo SI, Bankart and Halcom; Glassdoor employer profiles), openly crawlable Slovenian job boards (Optius with a dedicated Bančništvo/Finance category, MojeDelo, Careerjet.si) plus first-party careers pages such as Bankart's, and an executive-search tier of Ljubljana-based firms (Competo/Glasford, Amrop Adria, TRESCON/CFR, Boyden Slovenia, Kadring) alongside international networks (Pedersen & Partners, Stanton Chase Belgrade). Slovenian personnel and org-change reporting on banks is spread across general business desks rather than concentrated in named appointments columns: the highest-value breakers are Finance (paywalled and bot-defended in this run), the STA wire (subscription), and Delo's Gospodarstvo/Kariera, while Siol's 'Posel danes', Forbes Slovenija on the N1 platform, N1 Slovenija and RTV SLO provide the strongest free, open-access streams with stable URL patterns and confirmed bank-CEO coverage. Main weaknesses of this cell are paid-archive access to the four leading dailies and near-absent coverage of non-bank payments actors.

Slovenia's association layer for banking and payments is unusually concentrated: Združenje bank Slovenije (ZBS, a GIZ) is effectively the only sector chamber, and its About/Organi page publishes a dated 13-member supervisory board in which each seat is held by a named executive of a member bank — making it a compact, high-credibility index of senior banking leadership, with its Members page serving as the authoritative employer population for all downstream SI collection. Around it sit general and bilateral chambers (GZS and its ZIT branch, AmCham Slovenia, AHK Slovenija, OZS) and profession-level bodies (Združenje nadzornikov Slovenije, Združenje Manager, ZPFS, ACI Slovenia, SZZ, ZDU-GIZ), all of which publish dated board rosters and election press releases in Slovenian, typically mirrored on the O-STA wire and in Delo/RTV SLO/Siol/N1. The notable negative is that no dedicated Slovenian fintech association exists and GZS has no financial-services section — fintech leadership signal must be reconstructed from Blockchain Alliance Europe, the Slovenian Digital Coalition's blockchain working group and GZS ZIT. Associations as employers are weakly served: none publishes a careers feed, so secretariat hiring must be inferred from EU Transparency Register FTE declarations, AJPES/Bizi register profiles and (manually only) LinkedIn.

Slovenia's public-sector route to hiring and organisation intelligence in banking/payments rests on two statutory pillars: procurement publication under ZJN-3 (Portal javnih naročil / enarocanje.si, the e-JN system, and TED for above-threshold contracts, all mirrored as machine-readable OPSI datasets) and civil-service vacancy publication under ZJU (javni natečaj notices on UJP, FURS and Ministrstvo za finance pages, aggregated on GOV.SI and duplicated to the ESS employment service). A third, distinctively useful layer is oversight and money-trail infrastructure — DKOM review decisions expose the staffing and technical criteria buried in contested tenders, ERAR exposes actual payments from state financial bodies to named payments/IT vendors, and Računsko sodišče audits describe organisational structure directly; SDH's javni razpisi and annual portfolio reports are the earliest formal signal of leadership change at state-held banks and insurers. The main structural risk for this cell is the reported March 2026 move of gazette announcements off Uradni list RS onto proposers' own sites, which fragments what used to be a single crawlable venue and breaks time-series continuity. Collection should be portal-native (CPV and buyer filters, TED API, OPSI bulk data) rather than web-search-driven, since general search engines index the notice bodies poorly and produce false negatives.
