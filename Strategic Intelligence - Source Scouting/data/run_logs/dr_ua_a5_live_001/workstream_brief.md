For Ukraine, the authoritative leadership-move layer sits in three official surfaces: the NBU's bank-managers registry and press releases (approvals of board members, supervisory boards and fit-and-proper hearings), the NSSMC's stockmarket.gov.ua disclosure database where joint-stock banks must file 'особлива інформація' on any change in officials, and the Ministry of Finance / Cabinet track that appoints state-bank supervisory boards ahead of NBU approval. The main operational obstacle is that bank.gov.ua is bot-defended (a 403 was observed on a plain fetch), so collection should run through a browser-grade client with data.gov.ua and the roundtable.bank.gov.ua mirror as fallbacks. Payments-side leadership is the weakest link: the NBU licenses payment providers and assesses their managers' business reputation, but no person-level register of PSP key-function holders was found, so that layer must be reconstructed from the NBU entity registers joined to EDR director-change monitoring (Opendatabot/YouControl). Specialist media — FinClub, Minfin, Interfax-Ukraine, LB.ua — provide fast, named, dated interpretation and should be used for event triangulation rather than as primary record.

Leadership change at Ukraine's state banks is unusually well documented because three separate bodies have a publication duty over the same event: the Ministry of Finance and the Cabinet publish the competitive-selection announcement and the appointment act, the NBU publishes the fit-and-proper approval that makes it effective, and each bank files an 'особлива інформація' officer-change disclosure with the NSSMC. A robust tracker for this cell should treat MoF/Cabinet as the earliest datable signal, NBU as the confirmation gate, and the NSSMC issuer-disclosure XML as the structured system of record, with the banks' own press centres supplying names, biographies and the management-board layer that the state feeds omit. Media aggregators (minfin.com.ua, Ukrinform, Forbes.ua, Interfax, RBC, kurs.com.ua) are best used as tripwires and as the only practical coverage of contested or scandal-driven exits, notably at Sense Bank, whose DGF/MoF governance route lacks the public vacancy-notice channel the other four have. The main structural weakness is that no consolidated roster of current board members exists, so composition must be reconstructed by joining these feeds, and annual/corporate-governance reports plus executive-search firms remain unscouted.

Ukraine country-leadership signals for foreign parents, DFIs and card schemes split cleanly into three layers: the foreign principal's own English-language newsroom (Mastercard EEMEA, Visa CEMEA, RBI, EBRD, IFC, World Bank), the Ukrainian subsidiary or representative office's local disclosure (visa.com.ua in Ukrainian, raiffeisen.ua and otpbank.com.ua board and org-structure pages), and the regulatory/registry confirmation layer (NBU's bank-managers register and NBU approval news, plus EDR-derived officer records at YouControl/Opendatabot). Visa is the notable exception that does publish Ukraine appointments in Ukrainian; Mastercard, EBRD and IFC do not, confirming that an English-language collector is mandatory for this cell. The weakest actors are SWIFT and Western Union, which expose no public Ukraine leadership channel at all and must be tracked indirectly via careers portals and partner announcements. Ukrainian trade press (Forbes.ua, Minfin, FinClub, NV, Interfax-Ukraine) is fast and reliable as a confirmation and context layer but conflates appointment generations in aggregated form, so every record should be anchored to a dated primary artefact.

The Ukrainian public-sector route is unusually rich for this cell: Prozorro is a legally mandated, fully indexed, OCDS-API-accessible record covering the NBU, all state-owned banks, the Treasury and the Diia ecosystem, and individual tender pages carry named responsible units and contact officials — organisational intelligence that precedes any press announcement. Around it sits a mature secondary layer (DOZORRO, BI Prozorro, Opendatabot, spending.gov.ua, State Audit Service monitoring) that joins procurement activity to registry changes and to actual payment execution. For leadership moves specifically, the Cabinet of Ministers, Ministry of Finance and the Rada finance committee are statutory publishers of supervisory-board competitive-selection notices, with MoF also tendering the executive-search firms themselves. The main residual gaps are operational rather than discovery-related: an EDRPOU buyer watchlist and a validated CPV taxonomy must be built, and non-Prozorro SOE 'commercial proposal' channels plus bilateral donor portals remain unmapped.

For Ukraine, the private-bank indirect route runs mainly through domestic suppliers rather than the global names in the cell brief: CS Ltd (АБС Б2, iFOBS), UNITY-BARS (ABS BARS) and ProFIX on the core/back-office side, and UPC, ProCard, TAS-Link and UkrCard on the card/processing side, all of which publish client-naming project news in Ukrainian. Searches produced no Ukraine-specific customer story for Temenos, Oracle FLEXCUBE or OpenWay, and the only BPC/Ukraine trace found is an older SmartVista go-live at Credit Dnepr Bank republished by trade press — so the multinational branch of this cell is thin and should be monitored opportunistically rather than as a core feed. The strongest recurring signals are vendor press centres, Ukrainian fintech trade media (PSM7, Fintech Insider UA, Banker.ua) that publish actor-mapping market overviews, and hiring instruments (jobs.dou.ua company pages, SoftServe's country-filtered vacancy feed, IT Ukraine Association headcount rankings) that quantify delivery-centre expansion. NBU newsroom items — operator designations, open banking from 1 Aug 2025, ISO 20022 — provide the timing skeleton that explains when vendor-side activity should spike.

For Ukraine, the professional/social weak-signal layer is real but unevenly distributed: LinkedIn carries individual self-announcements (mostly in English, behind an auth wall and ToS limits), while Telegram and Facebook carry the community reaction and the fastest institutional chatter in Ukrainian. The highest-value, lowest-friction entry points are the publicly readable Telegram web views (@nbu_ua, @uafic), the industry-association pages (EMA/ЄМА, НАБУ, АУБ) whose event and committee posts name current payments and retail leads, and FinClub's feed, which functions as the de-facto register of bank management-board appointments and dismissals. X/Twitter produced no Ukrainian banking-personnel signal in any query and should be deprioritised; anonymous insider channels such as Bankova Mail lead the news but must be treated strictly as hypotheses. The binding constraints are access (LinkedIn login/ToS, closed Facebook groups) and verification: this cell needs a named-person watchlist and a mandatory promotion rule against NBU/registry sources before its output can be trusted.

Ukraine's banking/payments event and award landscape is small, concentrated in Kyiv, and organised around four hubs that between them produce nearly all the dated title data in this cell: the ЄМА association (26 editions of Payments & Security, with a full conference archive), the UAFIC fintech association (Pay.Forum, UAFIN.TECH, Digital Lending, Ukraine Fintech Week), the banking association NABU (Fintech & Payments Forum, corporate-directors forum, training centre), and the media-run award franchises — FinAwards (Мінфін/Finance.ua), whose jury explicitly includes the board chairs of the 17 largest banks, and the competing «Банк(и) року» rankings from Фінансовий клуб and МФК «БАНКИРЪ». Forbes Ukraine's Forbes Banker and the Kyiv International Economic Forum add a cross-sector layer, and KIEF is the only source found with a clean multi-year per-edition speaker directory URL pattern; cfo.ua's cumulative «Всі спікери» page is the cheapest single bulk extraction target. Executive education (KSE, kmbs) is a real but slower actor layer — kmbs's Master in Banking and Finance is the only banking-specific programme identified, while KSE offers general executive programmes only. The main risks are structural rather than access-related: almost no roster page was fetched and parsed in this pass, prior editions are routinely overwritten, and the acronyms EMA and НАБУ each collide with a much larger unrelated organisation, so collection must pin to domains, probe one page per domain to fix a parse recipe, and archive every snapshot for year-over-year diffing.

Workforce intelligence on Ukrainian financial services rests on a narrow but solid official base — Держстат for KVED-K employment and wages, NBU for branch-network counts, system headcount and every senior-appointment approval under Art.42, and the State Employment Service for monthly vacancy/unemployment trends. Around it sits a commercial and media layer: job boards (Work.ua, robota.ua) as near-real-time hiring proxies, EBA and KSE Institute for employer-side surveys and human-capital research, recruiters (Brain Source, ANCOR — the latter RU-linked and needing screening) selling comp benchmarks, and Forbes Ukraine / FinClub / FINBALANCE / NV as the practical event feed for leadership moves, branch closures and mobilisation-deferment rules. The distinctly Ukrainian drivers — mobilisation, staff deferment (бронювання) and relocation — are well regulated and widely narrated but almost entirely unquantified in public sources, and no maintained per-bank headcount series exists. Collection should therefore be Ukrainian-language-first, anchored on the two state datasets, with the paid layer (EBA survey, Opendatabot API, FinClub) treated as a deliberate licensing decision rather than a scraping target.

Ukraine's private and foreign-owned banks are well covered by their own first-party newsrooms, but on inconsistent URL conventions and with no RSS: OTP Bank is the strongest (explicit top-management appointment releases, a ~283-page dated archive, and a dated organisational-structure PDF), followed by PUMB, Raiffeisen, Credit Agricole, ProCredit and Universal Bank/monobank; A-Bank and Sense Bank currently lack a confirmed first-party channel. Because these banks announce arrivals far more readily than departures, the first-party layer must be paired with three corroboration layers: board-roster and org-chart page diffs, the NBU bank-managers register plus ЄДР registry change events, and the specialist aggregator finclub.net, whose per-banker dossiers and board-change feed are the best entity-resolution backbone available for this cell. Hiring signal is strong and cheap — Raiffeisen exposes a clean Workable ATS feed, PUMB runs career.pumb.ua, and Work.ua/Robota.ua/DOU cover the rest. The main risks are alias fragmentation (Райффайзен vs Райфайзен, the 2024 'Аваль' drop, А-Банк/Абанк/àбанк, Альфа-Банк→Sense Bank) and the state-sector noise that swamps generic Ukrainian appointment terminology, both of which require bank-name-anchored or domain-scoped querying.

Ukraine's payment-institution segment has a strong regulatory spine but weak public people-reporting: the NBU payment-infrastructure register and NBU newsroom (including professional-suitability rulings on heads of payment service providers) are authoritative and should anchor the actor map, while executive-hire news is scattered across psm7.com, Finclub, Liga Finance, Interfax and Ekonomichna Pravda with no sector-specific appointments desk. Because mid-size PSPs, terminal networks and money-transfer operators (Portmone, EasyPay/FC Kontraktovyi Dim, City24/Swift Guarantee, LeoGaming) almost never announce management changes, the highest-yield layer for this cell is registry change-monitoring on ЄДР director/signatory records via YouControl or Opendatabot, keyed on EDRPOU codes drawn from the NBU register. Hiring intensity is best proxied by DOU and Work.ua employer pages plus first-party careers pages (NovaPay, Fondy), and industry bodies (EMA, UAFIC) plus conference agendas (AmCham Power Payment Summit) provide the actor and executive-title dictionaries. The main open gaps are LinkedIn/professional networks, full entity-level resolution of the licensed register, and court/enforcement registers as leading indicators of forced management change.

For Ukrainian neobanks and card-issuing challengers, people-level intelligence is bifurcated: hard confirmation lives in state and regulator data (ЄДР via Opendatabot/YouControl, NBU approval of bank managers), while the early weak signal lives on founder-owned social channels and employer hiring channels — above all Oleh Horokhovskyi's Telegram 'ОГо!' and the official 'Робота в monobank' channel, supplemented by mono's careers page and DOU/robota.ua/work.ua listings whose role titles expose new verticals. Trade media (PaySpace Magazine, Фінансовий клуб, Fintech Insider) and the УАФІК association member list/event speaker rosters serve as pointer layers that keep the actor map current and surface appointments at smaller challengers. Two structural cautions: sportbank appears defunct since April 2024, and izibank has no brand-level governance channel — it must be tracked through Tascombank. The largest remaining gaps are LinkedIn (unscouted) and the personal channels of founders/product leads other than Horokhovskyi.

Ukraine's vacancy landscape for banking/payments splits into four usable layers: two dominant general boards (work.ua, robota.ua) that carry the bulk of retail-banking and managerial postings with stable, keyword-parameterisable search URLs; two tech-talent platforms (Djinni, DOU.ua) that expose per-employer vacancy pages for banks and fintechs (mono/Fintech Band, PrivatBank, OTP Bank UA) and are the sharpest gauge of payments-platform and digital investment; aggregators (Jooble UA, TRUD.COM) plus curated niches (Happy Monday, banki.ua) that extend recall into small towns and long-tail employers; and a licence-clean state layer — the Єдиний портал вакансій and the State Employment Service's form 3-ПН datasets on data.gov.ua — which is the only bulk source free of ToS risk and the right anchor for regional analysis. The highest-value first-party sources are the NBU's own recruiting page (its payment-market supervision hires telegraph regulatory direction) and individual bank/PSP career pages, which must be assembled into an employer registry seeded from the NBU licence lists. Two structural constraints shape the cell: LinkedIn — the natural home of senior banking roles and the only real source for leadership moves — is login-walled and ToS-prohibited, and no published banking-sector vacancy time series exists, so the leading-indicator value only accrues from the moment snapshotting begins.

Ukrainian business and general media reliably record banking leadership changes, but almost never in a dedicated appointments rubric — a direct check of finclub.net returned its full section list with no personnel rubric, and no 'призначення' tag page could be confirmed for Forbes Ukraine, NV Business or Ekonomichna Pravda. The practical structure is verb-driven keyword filtering (очолив, призначив, звільнився, припинив повноваження, відсторонив) over per-company tag archives, with finclub.net, Interfax-Ukraine's economic wire, LIGA.net's banking vertical and Ekonomichna Pravda as the four highest-yield feeds and Ukrinform's /tag-bank archive as the best backfill surface. Media clearly deliver the negative half of the signal that banks suppress — Cabinet terminations of supervisory-board chairs, NBU fitness-and-propriety proceedings, and internal suspensions of directors below board level — and ZN.ua in particular reports these before they are formalized. The main weaknesses are a heavy skew toward state banks and one scandal cluster, thin coverage of payments/fintech personnel outside PaySpace Magazine, and no confirmed RSS/API anywhere in the cell.

Ukraine's banking/payments personnel signal lives almost entirely in Ukrainian-language specialist trade press, with FinClub (finclub.net) the single highest-value node — it is the only cell source with an advertised RSS feed and it routinely names individual board chairs, deputies and even NBU department directors. Around it sits a second tier of strong but structurally different sources: Minfin and Mind.ua for volume and entity-keyed streams, Banker.ua and PaySpace Magazine (psm7.com) for banking- and payments-specific beats respectively, Censor.net's 'Наглядова рада' tag and NV Business for state-bank and regulator staffing, and Forbes.ua for the best structured people data (CEO rankings, full bank landscape, functional-role appointments such as HRD/CTO). Industry associations — ЄМА for payments and UAFIC for fintech — are the most promising pointer layer, since their conference speaker rosters expose exactly the sub-board roles the press ignores. The two real weaknesses of this cell are that payments-side personnel coverage is much thinner than banking-side, and that trade-press announcements are not legally confirmed appointments, so every record should be reconciled against NBU decisions before being treated as fact.

For Ukraine, the statutory-register layer is unusually strong for leadership-move intelligence: a director change is legally constituted by its entry in the ЄДР, so the Ministry of Justice register (free search, plus a full bulk open-data dump on data.gov.ua administered by NAIS) is the earliest and most authoritative signal, ahead of any press release. Above it sits a mandatory disclosure stream — «особлива інформація» on «зміна складу посадових осіб» filed to SMIDA/stockmarket.gov.ua — which adds named, dated, narrative detail for banks that are securities issuers, while the NBU State Register of Banks and the Реєстр платіжної інфраструктури define the entity universe that these diffs must be filtered against. The practical bottleneck is not availability but operationalisation: turning a register snapshot into a change event is a commercial product (Opendatabot, YouControl, Clarity Project, VKURSI), and both bank.gov.ua and opendatabot.ua returned 403 to plain fetchers, so collection must assume browser-grade access or licensed APIs. Registers cover the legal signatory only, so this cell should be read as the confirmation-and-timing backbone for A5 rather than a source of hiring or staffing signal.

Ukraine's banking and payments industry-body layer is small, well-defined and unusually rich for A5: three banking associations (NABU/НАБУ — renamed from Independent to National Association of Banks; AUB with ~70 members and a 13-seat Council; the older, possibly dormant UKBS), one payments association (EMA/ЄМА, whose 24 Oct 2025 General Assembly seated a board of Mastercard, Visa and the top Ukrainian banks, and whose verified 70+ member directory is the best actor map for the payments side), one fintech association (UAFIC), plus adjacent bodies for financial companies, credit unions and compliance and the foreign chambers (AmCham, EBA) whose committee co-chairs are serving bank executives. Governance pages and General Assembly / committee-election press releases are the highest-precision feed — they name a person, a title and an employer bank in one dated record — while member directories double as the census of actors worth scouting elsewhere. The main operational risks are technical rather than access-related: nabu.ua rate-limits automated fetches (429), fintechua.org renders client-side, aub.org.ua serves duplicate URL forms with one path resolving to a 2004 archive article, and the acronym НАБУ collides with the National Anti-Corruption Bureau, so any keyword-driven feed must be entity-disambiguated. This cell should be scoped to leadership and organisation change; it yields essentially no job-vacancy data.
