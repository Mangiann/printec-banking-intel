Slovenia's supervisory disclosure layer is concentrated in three publishers: Banka Slovenije (banks, payment institutions, e-money institutions, payment-system oversight and payment statistics), ATVP (issuers, investment firms, prospectuses, and the OAM regulated-information repository), and AZN (insurers, pension companies, and the distribution register). Banka Slovenije is by far the richest and most collectable: an open, well-structured site with a filterable announcements hub, a publications catalogue with an advance release calendar, statutory supervisory disclosures, a named-entity enforcement list, and registers that together form the canonical actor map for both banking and payments; its English mirror covers publications and statistics but not everything. ATVP holds the highest-value annual-report and issuer-disclosure material (OAM, ESEF filings, enforcement measures) but its domain returned 403 to a plain fetch, so it should be treated as bot-defended-but-live and approached with a headless browser rather than written off. Slovenian-language terminology is mandatory throughout - nadzorniška razkritja, izrečeni ukrepi, register subjektov nadzora, nadzorovani subjekti, letno poročilo - since ATVP and AZN publish essentially only in Slovenian; the main remaining gaps are the EU-level layer (ECB/SSM, EBA, ESMA registers filtered to SI), the AML office and Ministry of Finance, and any structured/SDMX data endpoint behind the payment statistics.

Slovenia's disclosure infrastructure is unusually centralised: AJPES is the single statutory sink for essentially all corporate annual reports (JOLP), the business and court registers, and official corporate/insolvency announcements (eObjave), while LJSE's SEOnet is the mandated venue for listed-issuer regulated information, backed by the INFO HRAMBA storage mechanism and mirrored on the regulator side by ATVP's registers and the referenced oam.si. For A1 this means two primary ingestion points (AJPES for the unlisted majority, SEOnet for NLB and the handful of listed financial issuers), plus three pointer registers — ePRS/OPSI for SKD-coded entity discovery and Banka Slovenije's register subjektov nadzora for the licensed bank/payment-institution universe. The main constraints are commercial and technical rather than legal: free JOLP access is rate-limited and five years deep, scalable structured financials sit behind the paid FI-PO/ponovna-uporaba tariff, and no public API or RSS was found for SEOnet, so disclosure ingestion will likely need a maintained HTML crawler.

The English-language layer over Slovenian banking and payments is thin, commercial and concentrated: STA's English service is the upstream translator, and SeeNews plus bne IntelliNews are the two regional wires that actually carry Slovenian bank results, M&A and ratings for an investor audience — all three are subscription-gated, with free restatement available mainly via The Slovenia Times and Total Slovenia News. Genuine English primary material does exist and should be preferred: NLB's int-en investor-relations and press-release streams, its LSE/RNS filings mirrored free on Investegate, the LJSE SEOnet English interface, and Banka Slovenije's fully translated Financial Stability Reports and payments pages. For payments specifically the EU trade layer (Finextra, The Paypers, FinTech Futures, European Payments Council) carries Bankart/Flik infrastructure news that domestic general press covers only in Slovenian. The decisive constraint found in this pass is that SEOnet states only the Slovene text has legal value and NLB's Slovenian investor-news pages have no guaranteed English twin, so the English layer must be treated as a discovery and cross-check channel over a Slovenian-primary collection design, never as a substitute for it.

Third-party assessment of Slovenian banks splits cleanly into three tiers. Tier one is EU supervisory and resolution data — EBA transparency exercise and the new EDAP portal, ECB Supervisory Banking Statistics (Slovenia is an explicit SI country dimension, repeatedly the EU's highest net interest margin), SRB MREL outputs and IMF Article IV — all open, machine-readable and the backbone of any Slovenia bank benchmark. Tier two is the rating agencies (Moody's, S&P/BICRA, Fitch, plus Scope and DBRS on the sovereign): rich and forward-looking but largely paywalled, so in practice they are collected through issuer IR mirrors (NLB Group ratings page, OTP banka ratings page) and Slovenian/regional media (RTV SLO's 'bonitetna ocena' archive, SeeNews). Tier three is the audit and consultancy layer: no Slovenia-specific Big Four banking study appears to exist, so Slovenia is best tracked as a country line in Deloitte's CEE Banking M&A Study and Raiffeisen Research's CEE Banking Sector Report, while the audit angle is best worked through the regulator ANR's public registers and oversight decisions plus Big Four transparency reports. Priority follow-ups are the auditor-to-bank mapping and ICT/operational-risk supervisory outputs, neither of which this pass covered.

Slovenia's hiring-signal landscape for banking and payments rests on three layers: a small set of general SI job portals (MojeDelo, Optius, Zaposlitev.net) plus the state ZRSZ/ESS vacancy register, which together give near-complete but shallow coverage; employer-owned careers pages (NLB, OTP, Intesa Sanpaolo, Deželna banka) and — most valuable — the shared infrastructure and vendor players Bankart and Halcom, whose ads name the exact payment platforms and programmes being stood up sector-wide; and LinkedIn, which carries the richest stack and seniority detail but is login- and ToS-restricted, so it needs manual or licensed access rather than scraping. Optius is the best structured entry point because it exposes stable per-employer URLs for most Slovenian banks, and BetterCareer.si adds IT-specific depth. The dominant operational risk is ephemerality: ads are deleted on expiry and no public archive of past Slovenian bank vacancies was found, so all value depends on capturing and timestamping ad text at first sighting. Language-wise, SL framing words (prosta delovna mesta, zaposlitev, kariera, plačilni promet) must be combined with English technical tokens, which SI bank IT ads use verbatim.

Slovenia's banking-events landscape is unusually centralised: Združenje bank Slovenije (ZBS) is both the convener (spring bančna konferenca, the annual Dan slovenskih bančnikov, and a dozen recurring posveti including card business, payment services and IT) and the publisher of record via Bančni vestnik, its news page and its YouTube recordings. Outside ZBS, the durable venues are the ICT conference Dnevi slovenske informatike with its published zbornik, the Ljubljana Stock Exchange/Finance 'Finančna konferenca', Delo's payments-flavoured Mobilne tehnologije conference, and the regional CEE stage Money Motion in Zagreb where Slovenian fintech and bank executives regularly speak. Awards function as self-declared disclosure: WEBSI digital-project shortlists, Moje finance 'naj/najugodnejša banka' rankings, and Global Finance / Euromoney country winners, all of which banks amplify on their own /nagrade pages. The main structural weakness is that slide decks are essentially never published — agendas, speaker lists, video recordings, proceedings and post-event press coverage must carry the intelligence load, and because conference microsites are overwritten annually, a Wayback-backed snapshot discipline is required from the outset.

The Slovenian acceptance side is unusually well documented at source: large merchants run their own press rooms and, in several cases, their own payment instruments — Petrol's Petrol Pay Loyalty Visa card, Mercator's Pika card and Moj M / M Pay wallet, Lidl's in-app payment — while SPAR and Big Bang publicly dated the arrival of Flik instant payments at POS and online respectively. Transport and municipal operators (LPP/Urbana, Slovenske železnice, IJPP, EasyPark and municipal news feeds) form a second, highly reliable layer that flags the shift from closed-loop cards toward open-loop and app-based acceptance. Listed names are anchored by SEOnet regulated disclosures, while the large non-listed grocers (Spar, Hofer, Lidl, Tuš) are reachable only through AJPES filings plus national-media quotes, which is where the coverage is thinnest. The main blind spots are utility and telco billers beyond Telekom, tolling, hospitality, and above all the acquirer/PSP relationships behind these merchants, which surface only in merchant terms documents and public procurement notices.

Slovenia's consumer-complaint and dispute signal for banking/payments is split across four layers: a supervisory complaint channel at Banka Slovenije, an industry-run out-of-court ADR scheme operated by Združenje bank Slovenije (opinions non-binding on banks), the ordinary courts via the sodnapraksa.si case-law database, and the consumer association ZPS. There is no statutory banking ombudsman and no central complaints register, so institution-level failure rates must be reconstructed from ZBS and Banka Slovenije annual reports plus court rulings. The sharpest operational signal comes from SI-CERT/Varni na internetu and Policija advisories on bank-impersonation phishing (which name banks and tie fraud waves to events such as the NKBM/SKB–OTP merger) and from per-bank 'obvestila za stranke' outage notices, which media coverage shows understate migration failures such as the BKS platform move. Data-protection enforcement is the weakest link: IP-RS publishes bank-related opinions and an OPSI open dataset, but inspection decisions are not routinely published and will need FOI requests or third-party aggregators. All of this is Slovene-language only, so local-language crawling is mandatory.

NLB Group runs the only full dual-listed IR operation in Slovenian banking, and its disclosure landscape is unusually well-structured: a single IR hub at nlbgroup.com mirrored in Slovenian (/si-sl/vlagatelji), feeding a document library that spans 2013-2026 with annual reports, quarterly interim reports, results presentations, Pillar 3 disclosures, factsheets, Excel key-financials and XBRL under predictable /content/dam/... paths. Around that core sit investor news, a financial calendar, webcast events, an Investor Day microsite, agency rating reports, an analyst coverage/consensus layer, and a live Addiko takeover disclosure page - together the richest source of digital/IT investment and SEE-expansion commentary available for any Slovenian bank. Two authoritative venues sit outside the issuer's own site and remain only partially resolved: SEOnet at the Ljubljana Stock Exchange (statutory Slovenian regulated-information storage, ASP.NET and form-driven) and the London RNS stream for the BNY Mellon GDR line (bot-defended, best reached today via the Investegate mirror). The main collection frictions are the absence of any RSS feed, audio-only earnings Q&A, and legacy pre-2023 Slovenian PDFs still living under nlb.si rather than nlbgroup.com.

Slovenia's unlisted and foreign-owned banks have no investor-relations layer to speak of: their disclosure is a Slovenian-language annual report PDF, a CRR 'razkritja' document that is sometimes standalone (Intesa Sanpaolo, UniCredit, Addiko, Sparkasse, Gorenjska) and sometimes folded into the annual report, and a press room that carries everything else. Cadence is therefore annual plus episodic, with two exceptions worth exploiting: UniCredit Banka Slovenija publishes quarterly razkritja, and Addiko's Vienna-listed parent reports Slovenia as a country segment every quarter. Cross-entity comparability and small-subsidiary discovery come from three pointer layers rather than the banks themselves — AJPES JOLP for statutory filings, SEOnet for the debt-listed (DBS bonds), and the Bank of Slovenia supervised-entity register, which is also what reveals structural change such as SKB's deletion on 22.8.2024 after merging into OTP. The main structural risk to coverage is treating the merged or renamed as merely silent, and the main open gap is the parent-group side: OTP Nyrt., Intesa/PBZ, UniCredit S.p.A., Erste/Steiermärkische and AIK were not yet swept.

Slovenia's payments infrastructure layer is unusually concentrated and unusually quiet: Bankart d.o.o., owned by the banks, runs card issuing/acquiring processing plus the three designated payment systems (BIPS/BIPS-IP, SIMP-PS, card settlement) and operates the domestic Flik instant-payment scheme, while Nexi Slovenija d.o.o. (formerly Nets CEE / Mercury Processing / Intesa Sanpaolo Card) is the second processing pole. The highest-value disclosure is not press releases — Bankart publishes only about one or two a year — but its statutory annual reports and its mandatory payment-system public disclosure documents, neither of which is linked from site navigation and both of which therefore need PDF-path and AJPES-based discovery. The regulatory layer (Banka Slovenije oversight pages, the annual payment and settlement systems report, payment statistics reporting, and the National Payments Council e-newsletter) plus ZBS gives reliable, high-credibility coverage in Slovenian, and the EPC SCT Inst register plus ECB TIPS material covers scheme reachability from the European side. Pan-European acquirer coverage is real but low-recall: Worldline mentions Slovenia inside multi-country release bodies rather than in titles, and no Slovenian-language newsroom was found for Nexi Slovenija, Mastercard SI or Visa SI, so full-text country filtering of group newsrooms is required.

Slovenia's non-bank payments and crypto cell discloses through three distinct layers rather than through investor relations: statutory registers (Banka Slovenije's supervised-entity and EMI registers plus EEA passporting lists; ATVP for MiCA/CASP, cross-checkable against the ESMA and EBA EU registers), statutory financial filings at AJPES JOLP (which is the only systematic financial disclosure for these mostly private d.o.o. companies), and a thin but distinctive announcement layer of company blogs and local trade media. The crypto cluster is unusually prominent for a market this size — Bitstamp (now Robinhood-owned, ATVP-supervised), Eligma/GoCrypto's merchant-acceptance network anchored on BTC City, and NiceHash — and its signals arrive through corporate blogs, partner-merchant press rooms and Slovenian outlets (Bloomberg Adria, Delo, Siol, Računalniške novice) rather than filings. Unlicensed wallet and BNPL players such as Leanpay surface mainly through the startup.si ecosystem directory, industry associations (Blockchain Alliance Europe, SBCA) and interview-driven business media, so pointer sources carry disproportionate weight in this cell. Main structural weaknesses are the absence of a structured Slovenian funding-round dataset, the lack of a national CASP list independent of ESMA, and registration/paywall friction on the highest-value financial sources.

For Slovenian banking-technology vendors the public record is almost entirely vendor-authored: with systemic banks private and outside procurement law, the only disclosures of who supplies what come from vendors' own reference pages, case studies and press releases. The single richest find is HRC d.o.o. (Hibis core banking), whose 'O nas' page names eleven Slovenian financial institutions and whose 'Aktualno' section documents core-system migrations at Deželna banka Slovenije, OTP banka and Nova KBM — exactly the procurement-invisible events this cell exists to capture; Halcom (Hal E-Bank, e-signature), Mikrografija (mDocs/mSign at Delavska hranilnica), Comtrade SI, Bankart (bank-owned card processor) and Payten/Asseco SEE plus Euronet on the ATM/POS side complete the core actor map. Because most of these vendors are private and publish little themselves, three indirect channels carry disproportionate weight: the Računalniške novice press-release wire (often the only archived copy of a vendor's bank announcement), Monitor.si trade journalism, and financial/registry aggregators (Bizi, CompanyWall, AJPES) where revenue jumps proxy for large project wins; bank app-store publisher fields are a cheap, reliable vendor fingerprint. Local-language search proved mandatory and decisive — the identical English query returned only global SEO listicles, while the Slovenian one surfaced HRC and a named bank migration on the first page.

Slovenia's public-procurement route to banking and payments intelligence is unusually well-structured: a statutory national notice portal (enarocanje.si, run by Uradni list), the transactional e-JN system with an OPSI open-data mirror of live tenders, and EU-level duplication on TED for above-threshold notices — together naming buyers, winning vendors, values and dates. Two transparency layers make it exceptional: UJP JN Plačila, where since 2023 every contracting authority must report the payments actually executed under procurement contracts, and KPK's Erar, which exposes transaction-level public-sector spend down to individual suppliers (with a documented data-quality caveat). Depth beyond the notices comes from DKOM review decisions, which expose technical specifications and competing bidders, and from Court of Audit reports on payment and tax IT systems; the state-owned side is covered by SID banka's annual reports and press releases and by SDH's reports to the National Assembly on state capital investments, which also bridge into privately-held banks. The main open risks are access mechanics — no confirmed API for enarocanje.si and an unverified public export for JN Plačila — and the absence of a validated CPV taxonomy, without which sector filtering will be unreliable.

Slovenia's association layer is unusually concentrated: Združenje bank Slovenije (ZBS, zbs-giz.si) is simultaneously the member directory for banks, savings banks and leasing firms, the sector-statistics aggregator, the inter-bank standards body, the press-release channel and the publisher of Bančni vestnik — the country's flagship banking journal since 1952. That makes ZBS the single highest-value node in this cell, with the important caveat that Bančni vestnik full texts are login-gated and the members page renders as logos rather than linked, machine-readable names. Around ZBS sit GZS and its ICT branch association ZIT for the vendor/technology side, TZS for the merchant/acceptance side (largely member-gated), and adjacent-sector associations (Slovensko zavarovalno združenje, ZDU-GIZ) that round out the financial-actor map. Notably absent is any dedicated Slovenian fintech or payment-institution association — that role is filled only partially by blockchain/DLT bodies (Blockchain Alliance Europe, SBCA) — so PSP and processor populations must be reconstructed from the supervisory register plus GZS/ZIT membership rather than from a single association directory.

Slovenia's business-press landscape for banking is small, concentrated and heavily gated: Časnik Finance (finance.si, Bonnier-owned, online-only since 2024) is the single highest-yield outlet, with Delo/Gospodarstvo and its Svet kapitala vertical, Dnevnik's business desk and Večer's Moj uspeh forming the second tier, and the state-founded STA wire sitting upstream of nearly all of them. Two industry-adjacent channels materially raise the value of the cell: Bančni vestnik (ZBS's journal since 1952, written by serving bank executives) for stated strategy, and Monitor.si for the back-office IT and payments detail that consumer business press omits. The dominant constraint is access rather than availability — Finance, STA and Bančni vestnik full text all require subscription or login, so a headline-trigger plus licensed-read workflow is more realistic than open crawling, with Delo's STA tag page and Računovodja.com's STA republication as partial free surfaces. The main uncovered ground is investigative long-form (Necenzurirano, Pod črto), regional press, and a retrospective archive route; note also that English-language querying cross-contaminates Slovenia with Slovakia, so Slovene tokens and .si anchoring are mandatory.
