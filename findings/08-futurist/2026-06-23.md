## 3–5yr projections

### ATM fleet & cash-recycling / deposit automation

**Cash-recycling & deposit-automation upgrade cycle on a flat ATM base — the recycler share of the installed fleet keeps rising even as total ATM numbers stay flat-to-down** — Footprint-wide (CEE/SEE) · _growth_ · High confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

Total CEE ATM installed base flat (≈232k in 2023 to ≈232k by 2028, CAGR -0.1%), but the recycler/deposit-capable SHARE of that base rises materially — recyclers are the fastest-growing ATM segment globally (low-double-digit CAGR). Continued upgrade demand into 2029-2031.

*Why:* The structural signal from both RBR volumes is consistent: the device count plateaus while functionality deepens, and deposit automation/recycling is where the spend goes. The mature Western-Europe market (recyclers already ~64% of deposit ATMs) is the template CEE/SEE is tracking with a lag, so the recycler upgrade cycle has a long runway across the footprint into 2031. For Printec this is the single most durable ATM-hardware opportunity: positioning NCR Atleos recycling/intelligent-deposit ATMs plus Glory and Sesami cash-recyclers and Teller Cash Recyclers (the Bank of Cyprus Glory Vertera 6G deal saving ~20 FTEs is the proof point), wrapped with x-core true-self-service software, APTRA/OptiCash cash optimisation, and managed cash-forecasting services to monetise the whole upgrade rather than just the box.

Drivers:
- Shipments are increasingly replacement-led (84% to 91% of shipments by 2028) toward fewer, more functional, deposit-capable machines, not net fleet expansion (RBR CEE p.48-49)
- Recycling/deposit automation is the central CEE investment priority — CIT-cost and queue savings drive profitability (RBR CEE p.29 commentary)
- Western-Europe leading indicator: cash-recycling share of deposit ATMs already 62%->64% and rising even as the WE fleet shrinks ~1.7-1.8%/yr (RBR WE p.15, 23-24)
- External 2025 evidence: recyclers cut cash-handling costs ~30% and CIT spend ~75%, and banks now treat recycling as a must-have rather than optional

**Divergent ATM-base trajectories — IAD-led / financial-inclusion growth markets vs cash-displacement decline markets shape where new and replacement units land** — Growth pole: Serbia, Czechia, Romania, Hungary, Slovakia + 'Others' (Slovenia, Bosnia, N. Macedonia, Albania); Decline pole: Bulgaria, Croatia, Ukraine · _mixed_ · Medium confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

Split market persists to 2028 and beyond: secondary/tertiary 'Others' is the growth engine (33,720->40,820, CAGR +3.9%); Serbia +1.2% and Czechia +1.1% lead the primary growers; Bulgaria declines fastest among primaries (-1.7%, 4,800->4,400) with Croatia -1.1% and Ukraine -1.0% (front-loaded, war-conditional).

*Why:* There is no single footprint direction — Printec must read each market separately. Growth markets (Serbia, the 'Others' tier of Slovenia/Bosnia/N. Macedonia/Albania, plus Czechia/Romania/Hungary/Slovakia) still absorb net-new and replacement deposit-capable units, often via IADs; decline markets (Bulgaria, Croatia) shift the opportunity from unit sales toward rationalisation, recycling retrofit and managed-services consolidation. The implication for Printec is a two-track go-to-market: lead with new/recycling ATM supply and IAD partnerships in the growth tier, and lead with fleet-optimisation, recycling upgrades and managed services in the declining tier — while keeping Ukraine planning scenario-based given its conditionality. Note Greece, Austria, Cyprus, Montenegro and Kosovo are outside the CEE volume, so calls there rest on the Western-Europe template and structural reasoning, not this report.

Drivers:
- IAD expansion is the main growth lever in the growth markets — tourism, under-banked/branch-poor areas, retailer partnerships (RBR CEE p.46); WE confirms IADs rising 16%->18%+ via bank-to-IAD estate transfers (RBR WE p.1, 5)
- Financial inclusion + low ATM density power the 'Others' growth engine (RBR CEE p.45-46)
- Decline driven by cash displacement (mobile/QR/cards), fleet rationalisation and higher surcharges, sharpest in Bulgaria (RBR CEE p.45-46)
- Ukraine trajectory is the weakest part of the forecast — front-loaded war damage then stabilisation, explicitly conditional (RBR CEE p.45-46)

**ATM pooling / ATM-as-a-shared-utility and bank-to-IAD estate transfers migrating from Western Europe toward CEE/SEE — reshaping who owns and operates the fleet** — Footprint-wide, earliest in higher-cost / faster-declining markets (e.g. Croatia, Bulgaria, Slovenia) · _growth_ · Low confidence · 4-6 years (2028-2031+); early signals to 2028

Directional, not yet a CEE figure: pooling utility models and bank-to-IAD transfers spread from their Western-European base into CEE/SEE in the latter half of the horizon. Today these are concentrated in NL (Geldmaat ~3,800), Belgium (Batopin, completing end-2025), Luxembourg, France and Turkey (TAM) — not yet live in the Printec footprint.

*Why:* Pooling and bank-to-IAD transfer are the structural mechanisms that follow once an ATM market matures into multi-year decline, and the Western-Europe report flags them explicitly as something to watch spreading toward CEE/SEE. The footprint is earlier on this curve, so this is a Low-confidence, later-horizon call — but it is strategically large because it changes the customer from many banks to a few shared operators. For Printec the implication is to get ahead of it as a neutral multivendor integrator: position ATM-as-a-Service / managed pooling operations, multivendor telemetry and monitoring (any vendor/type), APTRA/OptiCash cash optimisation and the 500+ engineers across 100+ service points as the operator-of-the-shared-fleet capability — turning a threat to box sales into a recurring managed-services and pooling-operations revenue stream. This should be treated as a structural opportunity to prepare for, and flagged for human/commercial validation given its conditionality.

Drivers:
- Pooling is a recurring structural lever in mature markets: Batopin (BE), Geldmaat (NL, pushed 96% of Dutch ATMs off-site), TAM (Turkey) (RBR WE p.2, 12, 25)
- IADs already taking over bank estates — banks transferring fleets to non-banks, IAD share 16%->18%+ (RBR WE p.1, 5)
- Rising ATM operating costs + branch closures make the shared-utility economics compelling once decline bites (RBR WE p.1, 23-25)
- Regulated cash-access backstops (UK LINK) emerge as the policy companion to estate consolidation (RBR WE p.23); external 2025 evidence shows pooling actively spreading across Western Europe but not yet documented in CEE

### Cash-to-digital shift & branch transformation

**Persistent ATM-estate rationalisation: footprint converges on the Western-European 'flat-to-shrinking fleet, growing function' template** — Footprint-wide · _decline_ · High confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

CEE installed base broadly flat-to-slightly-down through 2028 (CAGR ~-0.1%), then bending toward the mature Western-European trajectory of ~-1.5% to -2.0%/yr device shrinkage in the back half of the decade. Cash-withdrawal VOLUME continues to fall (~-2%/yr) while VALUE per withdrawal keeps rising.

*Why:* The RBR data shows CEE is not collapsing but rationalising: the device estate stays roughly flat near-term then bends toward the Western-European ~2%/yr shrinkage as cashless adoption and branch closures compound, while each remaining machine handles fewer but larger withdrawals. The mandatory instant-payments rails layered on top from late 2025 only sharpen the volume decline. For Printec this is a managed-decline market, not a growth-units market: the durable revenue is in helping banks run a smaller, smarter fleet rather than sell more boxes. That pulls demand toward NCR APTRA/OptiCash cash-forecasting, multivendor ATM monitoring and telemetry, and managed services on the installed base — recurring revenue lines that grow even as terminal counts flatten.

Drivers:
- CEE installed base essentially flat 2023-2028 at CAGR -0.1% (236,507 down to 231,895), with shipments increasingly replacement-led: replacements rise from 84% to 91% of shipments, so growth is fleet renewal not net expansion (RBR CEE p.45, p.48-49)
- Cash-withdrawal volume falls in 8 of 12 primary markets (fastest Poland -4.4%, Czechia -2.6%), with regional volume CAGR ~-1.8% to <4.0bn by 2028; value rises +2.7% CAGR as people withdraw larger sums less often (RBR CEE p.50, p.52)
- Western Europe is the leading indicator: base peaked in 2018 and has contracted every year since, falling to ~316,126 by 2028 at -1.7%/-1.8% CAGR, paced by cashless shift, branch closures and rising operating costs (RBR WE p.1, p.23-24)
- EU Instant Payments Regulation made receiving instant euro transfers mandatory from 09/10/2025 (with Verification of Payee), structurally accelerating account-to-account displacement of cash across the eurozone-bound footprint

**Replacement-led shipments concentrate on cash-recycling and deposit automation as banks defend branch economics with self-service migration** — Footprint-wide · _growth_ · Medium confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

Recycler/deposit-capable share of the installed base rises steadily even as total ATM numbers stay flat; the recycling frontier in CEE follows Western Europe where ~64% of automated-note-deposit ATMs already recycle. Directionally a rising majority of new shipments are deposit-capable by the early 2030s.

*Why:* Because almost all shipment spend through 2028 is replacement rather than expansion, the competitive battle is over what banks replace WITH — and the RBR commentary plus the Kazakhstan and Western-Europe signals all point to deposit-capable, multifunction recyclers that cut cash-in-transit costs and let banks thin teller headcount. This is the sweet spot of Printec's stack: NCR cash-recycling/intelligent-deposit ATMs, Glory and Sesami teller cash recyclers, Consillion non-ATM deposit devices, and the x-core 'true self-service' software that unifies withdrawal/deposit/bill-payment on one machine. The opportunity is to position Printec on the renewal RFPs as the recycling-and-assisted-service integrator, capturing the FTE-savings narrative that the Bank of Cyprus case already proves.

Drivers:
- Shipments rebound to >113,000 units 2023-2028 (~19,000/yr, +8.3% CAGR) but with replacements climbing to 91% of the total — renewal is the spend, and recycling/deposit automation is the central investment priority for CIT-cost savings and profitability (RBR CEE p.48-49, recycling outlook)
- Kazakhstan anchor case: growth is explicitly tied to deposit-capable deployment (Kaspi Bank deploys only deposit-capable ATMs), a directional signal for where renewal spend goes (RBR CEE p.46-47)
- Western-Europe benchmark: cash-recycling keeps rising as a share of deposit ATMs (62% to 64%), valued for cost/queue savings, even as the total fleet shrinks (RBR WE p.15)
- Branch transformation: banks migrating teller transactions to assisted/self-service to defend branch economics — Printec's named references (Raiffeisen RS x-core branch self-service; Bank of Cyprus Glory Teller Cash Recyclers saving ~20 FTEs) (Printec presentation slides 105-107)

**Off-site / IAD / pooling utility model migrates from Western Europe into CEE/SEE as banks shed branch-attached ATM estates** — Czechia | Hungary | Slovakia | Austria · _growth_ · Medium confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

IAD and off-site share of the estate rises across the footprint's growth markets; the shared-utility/pooling model (Geldmaat/Batopin-style) begins appearing in 1-2 CEE markets within the horizon. Off-site share trends toward the Western-European ~40%+ level.

*Why:* The mature-market playbook is unambiguous: as branches close, banks hand their ATM estates to independent deployers or pool them into a shared utility, and the surviving fleet shifts off-site to retail/transit/tourist locations. RBR shows CEE's growth markets are already IAD-led, and Austria proves the off-site catch-up dynamic inside the footprint, so the pooling model is the most likely next import within 3-5 years. Printec wins either way it lands: as a vendor-agnostic operator it can supply and run off-site/IAD fleets (NCR off-site ATMs, telemetry/monitoring, cash forecasting, field maintenance via 500+ engineers across 100+ service points) and is well placed to bid for the managed-service contract behind any pooling JV — the device may move off bank premises, but the service-and-monitoring annuity moves to whoever runs the utility.

Drivers:
- The six CEE markets forecast to GROW their base to 2028 (incl. Czechia, Hungary, Slovakia) do so on IAD expansion offsetting cash decline — Hungary is the highest IAD-growth market; secondary/tertiary 'Others' grow fastest at +3.9% CAGR on low density and financial inclusion (RBR CEE p.45-46)
- Western-Europe structural mechanisms: IADs rose 16% to 18% of the base (partly via banks transferring estates to non-banks), off-site share rose 38% to 40%, and pooling JVs (Netherlands Geldmaat pushed 96% of ATMs off-site; Belgium Batopin; Turkey TAM) are the recurring cost-optimisation lever (RBR WE p.1, p.5, p.12, p.25)
- Austria is the contrarian footprint case: the only core Western-European market forecast to grow to 2028 (+92 terminals), and all growth is IAD-led off-site catch-up with banks doing small-scale rationalisation (RBR WE p.24-25, p.58)
- Branch closures strand on-site ATMs, the explicit Western-Europe decline driver now arriving in CEE via mobile-app migration of overdrafts/credit applications (RBR WE p.23-25)

**Split footprint: a cash-resilient SEE tail (tourism + cash culture) sustains ATM demand while euro-adopters accelerate digital migration** — Serbia | Bulgaria | Croatia | Romania · _mixed_ · Medium confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

Serbia and the 'Others' tail (Slovenia, Bosnia, North Macedonia, Albania) hold up or grow ATM demand (Serbia +1.2% CAGR, Others +3.9% CAGR to 2028); Bulgaria and Croatia decline faster as euro adoption and mobile payments accelerate rationalisation (Bulgaria -1.7%, the fastest primary fall). Expect a one-off cash-handling spike around Bulgaria's 01/01/2026 euro changeover, then resumed decline.

*Why:* The footprint is bifurcating rather than moving as a bloc: a cash-resilient, under-penetrated tail (Serbia, Western Balkans, Slovenia) still rewards ATM availability and financial-inclusion deployment, while euro-track markets (Bulgaria already in, Croatia in, the eurozone-bound rest) compress their fleets faster as instant payments and mobile banking bite. This argues for a two-speed go-to-market: in the resilient tail, Printec should push net-new deposit-capable self-service and IAD/off-site deployment to capture inclusion-led demand; in the euro-track markets it should lead with rationalisation tooling — cash forecasting/optimisation (NCR OptiCash/APTRA), multivendor monitoring, assisted-service branch conversion (x-core, Bank Kiosk, QMS), and the recurring managed-services annuity that survives the unit decline. The Bulgaria euro changeover is a near-term cash-logistics opportunity (smart safes, recyclers, CIT optimisation) before the structural decline resumes.

Drivers:
- Serbia is the fastest-growing primary CEE market (3,150 to 3,350, +1.2% CAGR) on robust cash usage, rising cardholding and expansion projects; cash-withdrawal volume still rising ~+1% (RBR CEE p.45-46, p.50)
- 'Others' (incl. Slovenia, Bosnia, North Macedonia, Albania) are the regional growth engine at +3.9% CAGR (33,720 to 40,820) on low ATM density, rising banked population and financial inclusion (RBR CEE p.45-46)

### Payments modernization — instant payments, SoftPOS/acquiring, ISO 20022, digital euro

**Instant payments mandate (EU Instant Payments Regulation) driving switching, Verification-of-Payee and real-time fraud-monitoring upgrades across all euro and EU/EEA payment service providers** — Footprint-wide (sharpest in euro-adopters: Greece, Cyprus, Slovakia, Slovenia, Croatia, Austria, now Bulgaria) · _strong growth_ · High confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

Directional: a multi-year, regulation-forced wave of switching, payee-verification and real-time transaction-monitoring/fraud projects. Non-euro EU members (Romania, Hungary, Czechia, Bulgaria's surrounding peers) face their own staged deadlines into 2027, so the project pipeline runs through the whole horizon rather than ending at one cut-off.

*Why:* Instant payments shift from optional to mandated across the footprint over 2025-2027, and the regulation explicitly couples real-time settlement with free Verification of Payee — which forces banks to upgrade switching, payment-hub and especially real-time fraud-monitoring stacks rather than batch systems. The pull is reinforced structurally by the cash-displacement trend RBR documents as the primary force shrinking the ATM estate. For Printec this is the strongest near-term payments pull: switching/payment-facilitator capability (Worldline, FIS), real-time transaction analytics and fraud (INETCO Insight, FICO), and AML/screening (IMTF Siron) all map directly onto IPR/VoP compliance work that banks cannot defer.

Drivers:
- EU Instant Payments Regulation: euro-area PSPs already had to offer instant-receive and free Verification of Payee from 09/10/2025; non-euro PSPs follow on a staged basis through 09/07/2027 — a hard, regulatory (non-discretionary) demand driver.
- Instant rails plus mandatory payee-name/IBAN matching require real-time, always-on fraud and transaction monitoring — exactly the INETCO Insight / FICO / IMTF Siron capability set Printec resells.
- Underlying structural pull is cash displacement by digital/mobile payments, the dominant ATM-decline driver RBR cites for CEE (RBR CEE p.46) and the mature-market template in Western Europe (RBR WE p.1, 23-25) — instant account-to-account rails are part of what is replacing cash.
- Bulgaria's euro entry on 01/01/2026 pulls it into the euro-area instant-payments and VoP perimeter immediately, compressing its modernization timeline.

Confidence is Medium because SoftPOS adoption pace and acquirer consolidation vary sharply by market.

Drivers:

**ISO 20022 migration and payment-hub modernization — switching, reconciliation and HSM demand as legacy message formats are retired** — Footprint-wide (sharpest where banks run older switches and are entering euro/instant perimeters: Bulgaria, Romania, Serbia, Greece) · _growth_ · Medium confidence · 3-5 years (2027-2031)

Directional: a steady, infrastructure-led upgrade cycle. ISO 20022 is now the lingua franca of instant rails, cross-border (Swift), and SEPA — migration is effectively mandatory, so the question is timing per bank, not whether. Expect sustained switching, data-reconciliation and HSM refresh work through the horizon, weighted to laggard banks.

*Why:* ISO 20022 is the quiet, mandatory backbone of payments modernization: it does not generate headlines but it forces switch and payment-hub renewal, richer data handling, and more cryptographic processing across every modernizing bank. The structural parallel to RBR's replacement-led ATM shipment trend is that payment infrastructure spend in 2027-2031 is overwhelmingly renewal of existing estate, not net-new — which favours integrators with switching, reconciliation and crypto depth. For Printec the implication is a durable, less-cyclical pull on payment-switching (Worldline/FIS), data integrity & reconciliation (FIS), and crucially payment HSMs (Thales payShield) plus PCI-PIN key-injection services, all of which scale with instant-payments and ISO 20022 volume. Flag: HSM/key-injection and switching work is payment-security-critical and must be treated as requiring human review and testing, not deployed as production-ready off the shelf.

Drivers:
- ISO 20022 is the message standard underpinning SEPA instant and cross-border modernization; banks must migrate switches and payment hubs, and richer structured data flows raise demand for reconciliation/data-integrity tooling (Printec resells FIS reconciliation).
- Every new instant/real-time and card-present flow expands cryptographic operations — PIN, key management, tokenization — driving payment-HSM capacity (Thales payShield 10K) and secure key-injection / Payments Room (PCI PIN) services.
- Euro adoption (Bulgaria 01/01/2026) and instant-payments onboarding force exactly the switching + crypto + reconciliation stack refresh at the country level.
- Replacement-led, modernization-not-expansion is the consistent RBR pattern: CEE shipments are 91% replacement by 2028 (RBR CEE p.48-49), echoing that the spend is renewal of installed infrastructure rather than greenfield growth — the same logic applies to payment back-ends.
