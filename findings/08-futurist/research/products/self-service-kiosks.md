# 3-5yr demand outlook: self-service kiosks / assisted-service / bank kiosks

## Direction & summary (2027-2031)

The arc is clearly **upward and structural**, not cyclical. Across consulting, regulator, multilateral and vendor sources the same picture recurs: banks are shrinking branch real estate and headcount while pushing the bulk of routine transactions onto self-service and *assisted* self-service hardware. The fastest-growing slice is the **assisted/video-teller (ITM/VTM)** category — machines that fold an ATM, deposit automation and a remote human teller into one box — which is where the "bank kiosk" demand is migrating. Independent market trackers converge on a **mid-to-high-teens CAGR for the bank-kiosk / self-service-kiosk category through 2030-2033**, with the video/interactive-teller sub-segment growing fastest (high-teens to ~20%+ CAGR).

The structural pull is three-fold and well evidenced: (1) **branch networks keep contracting** (euro-area bank offices fell ~43%, from ~186k in 2008 to ~106k in 2023), forcing banks to maintain presence and cash access through machines rather than staffed counters; (2) **cash is declining but not disappearing** (ECB SPACE: cash fell to 52% of POS transactions by volume in 2024, from 59% in 2022, yet 62% of consumers still want cash as an option), which keeps cash-handling self-service (recyclers, deposit-capable kiosks) relevant even as teller cash desks close; and (3) banks explicitly want to **migrate 80-90%+ of simple transactions to assisted/self-service formats** and redeploy staff to advisory work.

**Confidence: medium-high on direction, medium on magnitude.** Direction is corroborated by primary regulators (ECB) and top consulting (McKinsey). The dollar-size CAGRs come from commercial market-research firms (Mordor, Astute Analytica, Grand View, Global Growth Insights) whose absolute base figures *diverge materially* (bank-kiosk 2025 base ranges from ~USD 1.3B to ~USD 21B depending on segment definition) — so treat the **growth rates and direction as the robust signal, and the absolute market sizes as indicative only.** Vendor sources (NCR Atleos, Diebold Nixdorf) confirm the product roadmap (ITM + video + on-demand assistance) but are interested parties.

## Key drivers

- **Branch network contraction is forcing a machine-led presence model.** Euro-area bank offices fell from ~186,000 (2008) to ~106,000 (2023), i.e. from 58 to 30 offices per 100,000 inhabitants; bank staff fell ~21% over the same window — banks need self-service/assisted hardware to retain coverage as counters close. [ECB Data Portal — changing landscape of bank offices](https://data.ecb.europa.eu/blog/blog-posts/changing-landscape-bank-offices-euro-area)
- **Cash is declining but remains structurally important**, sustaining demand for cash-capable kiosks/recyclers rather than killing it: cash fell to 52% of euro-area POS transactions by number in 2024 (from 59% in 2022) and 39% by value, yet 62% of consumers still consider cash an important option. [ECB SPACE 2024 press release](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ecb.pr241219~172b929461.en.html); [ECB SPACE 2024 report (PDF)](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024~19d46f0f17.en.pdf)
- **Banks intend to migrate the vast majority of simple transactions off the teller counter.** McKinsey: the goal is to migrate "more than 90 percent of simple customer activities to assisted or self-service formats"; smart/digital branch layouts flip floor space from 70% teller / 30% self-service to the reverse, yielding 60-70% improvement in branch effectiveness, with average branch FTEs shrinking from six to four by 2030. [McKinsey — Reimagining the bank branch for the digital era](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/reimagining-the-bank-branch-for-the-digital-era)
- **The vendor roadmap is converging on assisted self-service (ITM + remote/video teller + on-demand help).** Diebold Nixdorf states its branch-automation portfolio can migrate "up to 80% of traditional teller transactions to ATMs" via core integration; NCR Atleos positions the 80-series as a single device combining ATM, deposit automation and interactive teller, with centralized/remote tellers serving multiple branches to extend hours without added staff. (Interested-party vendor claims.) [Diebold Nixdorf branch automation (StockTitan)](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/DBD/diebold-nixdorf-unveils-new-branch-automation-solutions-yfgkl4jy054a.html); [NCR Atleos — what is branch transformation](https://www.ncratleos.com/insights/what-is-branch-transformation); [NCR Atleos — transforming the self-service channel](https://www.ncratleos.com/insights/transforming-self-service-channel)
- **Video/interactive-teller is the fastest-growing sub-segment**, reflecting demand for hybrid (machine + human) service models: Mordor flags virtual/video-teller machines at ~19.5% CAGR (2026-2031) vs ~15.5% for the overall bank-kiosk market. [Mordor Intelligence — Bank Kiosk Market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/bank-kiosk-market)
- **Digital financial inclusion is widening the addressable base in emerging/CEE-type markets** — the assisted-kiosk "branch in a box" is the low-cost way to serve newly-banked and rural customers. World Bank Findex 2025: global account ownership rose to 79% of adults (from 74% in 2021), with low-income economies +11pp and lower-middle-income +8pp between 2021 and 2024. [World Bank — Global Findex 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex)
- **Onboarding/biometrics are being folded into the kiosk**, expanding the use case from cash to account opening, instant card issuance and identity verification — pulling demand toward higher-value, software-rich kiosks. [NCR Atleos — what is branch transformation](https://www.ncratleos.com/insights/what-is-branch-transformation)

## Printec implication

The demand vector points squarely at **Printec's assisted-self-service / bank-kiosk and cash-recycling lines, increasingly bundled with video/remote-teller software and onboarding (KYC/biometrics) — all wrapped in managed services.** Concretely:

- The single highest-growth opportunity is the **ITM / video-teller "branch in a box"** — a device that lets a shrinking branch network (CEE/SEE is mid-cycle on closures vs Western Europe, so the runway is longer) keep cash access and human-assisted service without staffed counters. This aligns directly with Printec's NCR Atleos partnership (NCR Atleos 80-series ITM line) and pulls demand from plain ATMs toward **deposit-capable, recycling, interactive-teller machines**.
- Because cash is declining-but-persistent (ECB), the **cash-recycling kiosk** stays central in Printec's footprint — banks consolidate cash handling into fewer, smarter machines rather than eliminating it. This favours Printec's recycling/self-service hardware plus the cash-management managed service around it.
- The kiosk is becoming a **software + onboarding platform** (account opening, instant card issuance, biometric ID). Printec's onboarding, security and compliance capabilities are a natural attach to the hardware, raising deal value and stickiness.
- Delivery model: banks want this **as a managed service** (Diebold/NCR both push ATM/branch-as-a-service). Printec's managed-services arm should lead with outcome-based ITM/kiosk fleet management, not box sales.

Net: this unit's demand is **growing and up-stack** — fewer plain cash-dispense ATMs, more multi-function assisted kiosks with video and onboarding, sold as managed services across the 17-country footprint.
