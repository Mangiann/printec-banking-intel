# Western Europe 3-5yr (leading indicator for CEE/SEE): ATM decline, pooling, recycling, IADs

## Direction & summary (2027-2031)

Western Europe is the **mature-market template that Printec's CEE/SEE footprint will follow with a lag of several years.** The arc through 2027-2031 is a **managed contraction of the total ATM estate combined with a structural re-shaping of who owns and operates it.** Four forces run together:

1. **Total bank-owned ATM counts keep falling — slowly, not collapsing.** Datos Insights/RBR group Western Europe among the regions where the installed base "will continue falling," contributing to a ~4% global decline by the end of the decade; the regional driver is the banking sector removing terminals as branches close and cash-at-POS shrinks. The UK is the clearest signal: free-to-use ATMs fell from 52,040 (2018) to 33,710 (end-2025), a ~35% drop in seven years; Germany dropped from ~59,000 (2018) to ~49,750 (2024). Pace through 2031: single-digit annual declines continuing, steepest in Nordic/UK "vanguard" markets, slower in cash-intensive Germany/Italy.

2. **Pooling / shared "ATM utility" companies are becoming the dominant operating model.** Where cash falls below ~40% of POS volume, banks de-brand and consolidate fleets into a single shared operator: Geldmaat (Netherlands, 3 banks), Batopin (Belgium, 4 banks), Bankomat (Sweden, 5 banks), Automatia (Finland), and partial utilities in Denmark/Norway. This **shrinks unit counts further but concentrates the surviving fleet into fewer, higher-spec, more-utilised machines run on an as-a-service basis** — and these utilities are the buyers of next-gen hardware and managed services.

3. **Within the shrinking estate, recycling / intelligent-deposit is the technology floor and growth engine.** Globally, for the first time >50% of ATMs accept automated deposits, most via recycling; Western Europe (Germany + Belgium historically held ~82% of the region's recyclers) is a recycling stronghold, and the pooled networks are now explicitly retro-fitting deposit/recycling into their estates (Batopin targets ~half its CASH points with a deposit function by 2026). The economic logic is the cash-handling cost base (5-10% of bank operating costs) that recycling and pooling jointly attack.

4. **Independent ATM deployers (IADs) fill the gap banks vacate.** As banks pull terminals, IADs (Euronet, Cardtronics/NCR Atleos) expand: Euronet's IAD network now spans 32 European countries (Belgium added 2024) and 53,272 ATMs globally. In Germany the decline of bank ATMs is explicitly being backfilled by independents like Euronet — usually fee-charging, making the cost of cash visible to consumers.

**Regulatory backstop under the whole picture:** the EU Single Currency Package (Council position 19/12/2025) obliges euro-area Member States to guarantee "sufficient and effective access to cash" with ATM geographic standards, and to generally ban non-acceptance of cash — a hard floor against fleet attrition. National central banks (Dutch DNB "5 km" rule, Bundesbank, UK LINK's "1 km" pledge, 184 recommended banking hubs) reinforce it. A second, newer backstop: **payment-system resilience / cyber-security fears** have made Sweden and Norway *pause* their cashless drive — cash and ATMs as a war/cyber fallback.

**Confidence:** High on direction (regulators, multiple analysts, vendors, and the pooled-utility evidence all agree). Medium on exact magnitudes (RBR/Datos country-level series are gated; vendor counts are interested-party figures).

**CEE/SEE read-through:** Printec's home markets are 3-7 years behind this curve — still flat-to-growing in installed base, with IAD-led growth (Poland) — so the Western-Europe story is a *forward order book*: pooling consolidation, recycler retrofit, IAD partnerships, and managed-services/ATMaaS contracts are what arrives next in the footprint.

## Key drivers

- **Bank-led ATM removal across Western Europe, branch rationalisation the cause** — Datos/RBR: Western Europe led branch rationalisation; the region is grouped among those whose base "will continue falling," contributing to ~4% global decline by end of decade. ## Printec implication

Western Europe is the **demand blueprint for what Printec sells next in CEE/SEE**, and it pulls demand decisively toward four lines:

- **Pooling / shared-ATM-utility enablement + ATM-as-a-Service.** The single biggest structural shift is banks de-branding and consolidating into shared operators (Geldmaat, Batopin, Bankomat) run as a managed service. Printec should position as the **integrator/operator partner for emerging CEE/SEE ATM-pooling consortia** — exactly the role Auriga plays for Batopin — bundling NCR Atleos hardware with managed services, monitoring, cash forecasting and security. This is the highest-margin, stickiest opportunity and the clearest forward indicator.
- **Recyclers / intelligent-deposit, not dispense-only.** The surviving and pooled estate is being re-equipped with deposit/recycling (Batopin retrofitting ~half its estate by 2026). Lead with recycling units (NCR Atleos SelfServ 80-series) into both bank fleets and pooled utilities — the cash-cost case (5-10% of opex; up to ~35% saved via pooling) sells itself.
- **IAD partnership / white-label deployment.** As banks withdraw, independents (Euronet, etc.) backfill. Printec can supply, deploy and service IAD/white-label estates in its markets and act as the local operator — capturing the demand banks shed rather than fighting the decline.
- **Managed services + security + compliance wrap.** Fewer machines, each more functional and outsourced, plus the EU access-to-cash regulation and resilience/cyber concerns, all pull toward the services and security layer (cash forecasting, replenishment optimisation, anti-explosive-attack protection, regulatory reporting) — recurring revenue, not one-off boxes.

Timing advantage: the CEE/SEE estate is still flat-to-growing, so Printec has a multi-year runway to land the pooling/recycler/IAD/managed-services positions *before* the Western-Europe decline curve arrives locally.

Note: vendor figures (Euronet, Tietoevry, NCR Atleos) are interested parties; any payment/security-related deployment specifics require human review and testing before being treated as production-ready, per Printec compliance (DORA, PCI DSS, ISO 27001).
