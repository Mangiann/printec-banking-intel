# Greece & Cyprus 3-5yr (Printec home markets, euro-area)

## Direction & summary (2027-2031)

Greece and Cyprus are the **two fastest-converging payment markets in the euro area** — economies that were ~80-88% cash-at-POS as recently as 2016 and are now digitalising faster than any other euro-area members. The ECB SPACE source data is unambiguous: between SPACE 2022 and SPACE 2024 **Cyprus recorded the single largest fall in cash share of POS transactions in the euro area (-11.0pp by number, to 48.5%)**, and **Greece the joint-second-largest by number alongside Spain (-8.1pp, to 53.9%)** ([ECB SPACE 2024 charts workbook, Chart 6](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx)). On value, cash is already a minority in both (GR 42.3%, CY 43.1% of POS value in 2024). The structural arc to 2031 is **continued rapid cash-share erosion toward — and likely past — the euro-area average, but NOT cash disappearance**: both central banks and the Eurosystem explicitly back preserving cash access and acceptance, and the digital euro is being designed to complement, not replace, cash.

The decisive feature for Printec is that this is **policy-engineered digitalisation, not organic only**. In Greece a triad of state interventions — universal POS mandates tied to the AADE/myDATA real-time tax interconnection (2024), mandatory IRIS instant payments for all card-accepting businesses (from 01/12/2025), and a nationwide ATM-fee ban/cap (11/08/2025) — is compressing into ~3 years a shift that took a decade elsewhere. Greece had the **fastest growth in instant-payment access in the euro area, nearly doubling from 33.0% to 62.4% of consumers (2022→2024)** ([ECB SPACE 2024 workbook, Chart 44](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx)); IRIS hit ~122m transactions worth ~€11bn in 2025 ([ProtoThema, 02/12/2025](https://en.protothema.gr/2025/12/02/dias-iris-available-everywhere-120-million-transactions-expected-by-2025/)). Cyprus reached ~96% of cashless payments going digital and the highest e-commerce share growth in the bloc (~1% of payments in 2019 to ~26% in 2024) ([Cyprus Mail, 19/06/2026](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/06/19/digital-payments-move-from-convenience-layer-to-financial-infrastructure-as-europes-regulatory-landscape-shifts)).

**Net for 2027-2031:** card/instant/online payment value keeps compounding (Greek card value +9% to €112bn in 2024, transaction count +14% to 2.51bn); branch networks keep shrinking (Greece -45% branches since 2015; Cyprus -370 branches and -4,634 jobs over 2015-2025); the ATM installed base **stagnates-to-declines** (Cyprus ~398 ATMs, essentially flat-to-down from 416 in 2022; Greece ATM *count* rose ~17% since 2015 via non-branch machines but the fee ban now compresses independent-ATM economics from 2025). Confidence: **high** on the directional convergence and on instant-payment dominance; **medium** on the ATM-fleet trajectory (the Greek fee cap is a structural shock to the standalone-ATM model that pushes toward fewer, smarter, recycling/managed-service units). The digital euro (pilot from 2027, possible issuance ~2029) lands inside the window and adds a new acceptance-infrastructure demand layer, but does not collapse cash within the horizon.

## Key drivers

- **Cyprus = euro area's single fastest cash-share drop.** Cash at POS fell from 87.9% (2016) → 82.7% (2019) → 59.4% (2022) → 48.5% (2024) by number — the largest 2022-24 fall in the bloc (-11.0pp) ([ECB SPACE 2024 workbook, Chart 6](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx)).
- **Greece = joint-second fastest, from a near-universal-cash base.** Cash at POS by number 88.5% (2016) → 79.7% (2019) → 62.0% (2022) → 53.9% (2024); by value already a minority at 42.3% (2024) ([ECB SPACE 2024 workbook, Chart 6](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx)).
- **Greece has the euro area's fastest-rising instant-payment access — the IRIS effect.** Consumers with access to instant payments leapt 33.0%→62.4% (2022→2024), the biggest jump of any member state; IRIS processed ~57.3m transactions / €6.1bn in 2024 (+137% YoY) and ~122m / ~€11bn in 2025 (+72.8% YoY), ~4.25m active users, ~27% of all DIAS credit-transfer volume and ~40% of Greek credit transfers ([ECB SPACE 2024 workbook, Chart 44](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx); [ProtoThema, 02/12/2025](https://en.protothema.gr/2025/12/02/dias-iris-available-everywhere-120-million-transactions-expected-by-2025/); [Greek City Times, 18/11/2025](https://greekcitytimes.com/2025/11/18/iris-payments-greece-december-2025-new-rules/)).
- **State mandates are forcing the pace in Greece.** IRIS became mandatory for all card-accepting businesses from 01/12/2025 (fines from €1,500); POS-cash-register interconnection with AADE/myDATA (2024) tied acceptance to real-time tax reporting; the universal card-acceptance expansion alone is estimated to have added ~€400m VAT in 2024 ([Greek City Times, 18/11/2025](https://greekcitytimes.com/2025/11/18/iris-payments-greece-december-2025-new-rules/); [IOBE, 2025](https://iobe.gr/en/2025/digital-payments-in-greece-one-decade-of-rapid-expansion/)).
- **Greek card payments still compounding double-digit.** Card transactions +14% to 2.511bn (2023: 2.2bn); value +9% to €112bn (2023: €102bn); debit = 93% of transactions ([Athens Times / TtE, 2024](https://athens-times.com/tte-payment-card-transactions-reach-e112-billion-in-2024/)).
- **ATM-fee ban reshapes the Greek standalone-ATM model (direct hit to independent operators).** From 11/08/2025, interbank DIAS withdrawals are free, balance enquiries free, and independent third-party ATM fees capped at €1.50 — triggered after a bank swapped hundreds of branded ATMs for third-party machines charging >€2 ([Euronews, 11/08/2025](https://www.euronews.com/business/2025/08/11/greek-ban-on-atm-charges-and-limits-on-other-banking-fees-comes-into-force-today)).
- **Branch networks collapsing in both markets — self-service fills the gap.** Greece: -45% branches since 2015 (and similar staff cut) while ATM count grew ~17%, skewed to non-branch machines; Cyprus: -370 branches and -4,634 jobs (2015-2025), branch network flat at 193 in 2025 ([Balkan Insight, 12/09/2025](https://balkaninsight.com/2025/09/12/greeks-grow-angry-at-bank-charges/bi/); [Sigmalive, 2025](https://en.sigmalive.com/cyprus-banks-cut-4634-jobs-and-close-370-branches-in-ten-years/)).
- **Cyprus ATM fleet is flat-to-declining; cards surge.** ATMs ~398 (Dec 2024) vs 416 (Jun 2022); payment cards in circulation passed 2m for the first time — 2,031,569 (Dec 2024) vs 1,718,939 (end-2022), +18% ([Cyprus Mail, 05/11/2025](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/11/05/cyprus-atm-network-stagnates)).
- **Cyprus is structurally card-led and online-heavy.** Cards = ~72-73% of non-cash transaction volume; online = 29% of card transactions by count but 57% by value (2024); contactless >50% of card payments since 2022; cash preference down to 17.7% (2024, from 23.5% in 2022) ([Cyprus Mail, 05/11/2025](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/11/05/cyprus-atm-network-stagnates); [ECB SPACE 2024 workbook, Chart 23](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx)).
- **Both still defend cash access — a regulatory floor under Printec's cash line.** GR/CY cash-access "difficult" perception stayed low and roughly flat (GR 15.9%→14.2%, CY 10.4%→11.0%, 2022→2024), and the Eurosystem/CBC back the EU legal-tender/cash-access package; CBC explicitly is "not considering moving to a totally cashless society" ([ECB SPACE 2024 workbook, Chart 34](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/ecb_surveys/space/shared/pdf/ecb.space2024_charts.en.xlsx); [Cyprus Mail, 06/11/2025](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/11/06/central-bank-of-cyprus-outlines-roadmap-for-digital-euro-rollout)).
- **Digital euro lands inside the window; Cyprus is an active mover.** ECB closed preparation phase Oct 2025; pilot from 2027, full readiness target 2029, possible first issuance ~2029-2030; legislation expected 2026 with Cyprus contributing during its H1-2026 EU Council Presidency; PwC pegs euro-area sector cost at ~€18bn vs ECB's ~€4-5.7bn ([Cyprus Mail, 06/11/2025](https://cyprus-mail.com/2025/11/06/central-bank-of-cyprus-outlines-roadmap-for-digital-euro-rollout); [Cyprus Mail, 20/04/2026](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/04/20/digital-euro-implementation-costs-estimated-at-e18-billion)).
- **PSD3 / Payment Services Regulation tightens the rails 2027-2028.** Provisional agreement Nov 2025, final texts H1 2026, applicability from late 2027/early 2028 — directly applicable across the single market, raising compliance/SCA/fraud obligations for PSPs operating in CY/GR ([Cyprus Mail, 19/06/2026](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/06/19/digital-payments-move-from-convenience-layer-to-financial-infrastructure-as-europes-regulatory-landscape-shifts)).
- **Low fraud / high digital trust in Cyprus.** Card fraud ~0.015% of transaction value in 2024 — among the EU's lowest ([Cyprus Mail, 19/06/2026](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/06/19/digital-payments-move-from-convenience-layer-to-financial-infrastructure-as-europes-regulatory-landscape-shifts)).

## Printec implication

Greece and Cyprus are Printec's **most-digitalising home markets** — the leading edge of the same curve that will sweep its 17-country CEE/SEE footprint. The data pulls demand decisively toward Printec's **digital/transaction-software and self-service lines, and away from raw dispense-only ATM volume**:

- **POS/payments, SoftPOS and acquiring is the immediate growth pull.** Greece's POS+myDATA mandate, the universal card-acceptance push, double-digit card growth (+14% transactions, +112bn value), and IRIS becoming compulsory for every business create sustained demand for POS estate, terminal-software upgrades (every card-accepting business had to become IRIS-ready by 01/12/2025), and acquiring/integration services. Cyprus's card-led, online-heavy mix (29% of card transactions online) extends the same pull into e-commerce/payment-gateway and SoftPOS.
- **Instant payments + IRIS-style A2A is a software/integration franchise.** Greece's instant-payment access nearly doubling and IRIS scaling to ~122m transactions is the standout regional signal — Printec's payment-software, request-to-pay and merchant-integration capabilities map directly onto mandated instant-payment readiness, with PSD3/PSR (applicable ~2027/28) layering on compliance work.
- **Cash automation shifts from "more ATMs" to "fewer, smarter, recycling + managed" units.** Both fleets are flat-to-declining (Cyprus ~398; Greece's count grew only via now-fee-capped independent machines). The Greek €1.50 fee cap and free interbank withdrawals (11/08/2025) destroy the standalone independent-ATM fee model — accelerating consolidation into multifunction cash-recyclers and **ATM-as-a-Service/managed services** (NCR Atleos partnership), where each unit does deposit + recycling + assisted-service work. This is the margin/recurring-revenue story even as machine counts fall.
- **Branch transformation / assisted self-service is the structural tailwind.** Greece -45% branches and Cyprus -370 branches with cash-access mandates intact means banks must guarantee access through self-service kiosks, video-teller/assisted devices and shared/“banking-hub” infrastructure — core Printec self-service territory.
- **Digital onboarding, eID and security/compliance ride the same wave.** Soaring digital/online payments + PSD3/SCA + the digital euro (pilot 2027, issuance ~2029, with Cyprus an active Council-Presidency mover) drive demand for remote onboarding/eID, fraud/security and compliance software — and a fresh acceptance-infrastructure upgrade cycle (terminals/ATMs made digital-euro-ready) within the outlook window.

Note (per Printec compliance framing): all figures here are public third-party data/projections; any payment-related or production-grade solution built on them must be flagged for human review and testing before being treated as production-ready, and any deployment touching cardholder/PII data handled under PCI DSS/GDPR/DORA.
