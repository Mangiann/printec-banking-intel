# Operational resilience & compliance spend (DORA, AMLA/AML, accessibility)

## Direction & summary (2027-2031)

The arc is one of structurally rising, non-discretionary spend on operational resilience and compliance across the European/CEE-SEE financial sector, shifting from one-off "get-ready" programs (2024-2026) into a permanent run-cost plateau and then continued growth through 2031. Three regulatory waves overlap and compound:

1. **DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)** — in force since 17/01/2025, moved from documentation in 2025 to active supervision and evidence-based enforcement in 2026. McKinsey finds ~70% of firms expect *permanently* higher run-costs for technology and ICT controls — i.e. this is not a transient project budget but a new baseline. The 18/11/2025 designation of 19 Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) signals the regime is now operational at the supply-chain layer.
2. **EU AML package (AMLR + AMLD6 + AMLA)** — AMLA operational from 01/07/2025; the single rulebook (AMLR) and AMLD6 apply directly from 10/07/2027, with AMLA direct supervision of ~40 systemic institutions from 2028. The indirect "trickle-down" of AMLA expectations onto nationally-supervised banks across all 27 member states (and EEA-aligned CEE markets) is the larger demand driver. Global financial-crime compliance already costs >USD 206bn/year and has risen for ~98-99% of institutions.
3. **European Accessibility Act (EAA)** — applicable from 28/06/2025, explicitly covering ATMs, payment terminals and interactive self-service terminals to EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA. Legacy ATMs get a transitional grace period (end-of-life or max 20 years from first use), which converts into a multi-year hardware/software refresh cycle precisely over the 2027-2031 window.

**Pace & confidence:** Direction is high-confidence (regulatory deadlines are fixed in law; spend trend data is corroborated across LexisNexis/Forrester, McKinsey, Gartner and ECB). Magnitude per institution is medium-confidence (vendor- and consultant-sourced ranges, wide). Net: resilience + compliance is one of the few banking-technology budget lines that is contractually mandated to grow regardless of macro conditions — a tailwind for Printec's security, compliance, onboarding and managed-services lines.

## Key drivers

- **DORA per-institution cost is large and partly permanent.** Consultants estimate EUR 5-15m just for strategy/planning/orchestration; total implementation EUR 25-150m for large institutions and EUR 1-5m for mid-sized; ~70% expect permanently higher run costs and ~40% dedicate >7 FTE to DORA — and only ~50% reached full compliance by end-2025, pushing spend into 2026+. [McKinsey – Europe's new resilience regime](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/risk-and-resilience/our-insights/europes-new-resilience-regime-the-race-to-get-ready-for-dora)
- **DORA now bites at the third-party/supply-chain layer.** On 18/11/2025 the ESAs designated 19 Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (incl. AWS, Microsoft, Google Cloud, IBM, Oracle, SWIFT, Worldline, FIS, Temenos) for direct oversight, annual oversight fees and Joint Examination Teams — institutionalising vendor-resilience scrutiny that flows down to all ICT suppliers serving banks. [Morgan Lewis – DORA CTPP list](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2025/11/dora-eu-regulators-announce-list-of-critical-ict-third-party-providers)
- **~22,000 EU regulated entities are in DORA scope**, creating a broad, recurring demand base for resilience testing, incident reporting, and ICT-risk tooling. [IBM – What is DORA](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/digital-operational-resilience-act)
- **ECB supervisory pressure is real, not theoretical.** The 2024 cyber-resilience stress test of 109 banks found gaps in business-continuity and third-party risk; a typical bank needed hundreds of hours to answer 395 questions. Operational resilience is an explicit SSM supervisory priority for 2024-2026, feeding the SREP. [ECB – cyber resilience stress test concluded](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ssm.pr240726~06d5776a02.en.html)
- **AML/financial-crime compliance is already a >USD 200bn/yr global cost and climbing.** Global cost reached USD 206.1bn in 2023 (EMEA USD 85bn / EUR 79bn; US+Canada USD 61bn; APAC USD 45bn), with ~98% of institutions reporting rising costs and labour ~72-79% of the burden. [LexisNexis – global $206bn release](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/about-us/press-room/press-release/20230926-global-financial-crime-compliance-costs) · [LexisNexis – EMEA $85bn](https://risk.lexisnexis.com/global/en/about-us/press-room/press-release/20240306-true-cost-of-compliance-emea)
- **AMLA + single rulebook (AMLR/AMLD6) from 10/07/2027 raises the floor for every obliged entity.** Direct supervision of ~40 systemic firms from 2028, plus indirect pressure on all nationally-supervised banks; scope broadens to all MiCAR-authorised crypto-asset service providers. [AMLA – about](https://www.amla.europa.eu/about-amla_en) · [Norton Rose Fulbright – AML harmonisation](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/32bd49d3/harmonisation-of-european-money-laundering-prevention)
- **EAA forces accessible self-service hardware/software.** From 28/06/2025 ATMs, payment terminals and interactive self-service terminals must meet EN 301 549 (tactile keypads, audio/text-to-speech via headphone jack, screen-blanking for privacy, alternative PIN entry); legacy terminals may run only to end-of-life or max 20 years, driving a refresh wave through 2027-2031. [Bird & Bird – EAA financial services](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-accessibility-act-focus-on-financial-services) · [European Commission – EAA](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/european-accessibility-act-eaa_en)
- **The digital euro adds a new resilience/availability mandate.** ECB's design requires distributed processing across ≥3 regions, offline functionality (usable without internet, phone signal or a working ATM), and cross-PSP continuity; preparation phase closed Oct 2025, pilots from 2027, possible issuance ~2029 — pulling banks/PSPs into new resilience and offline-capability investment exactly in the outlook window. [ECB – digital euro resilience & inclusion speech](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/key/date/2025/html/ecb.sp250904~70ab593276.en.html)
- **Underlying tooling markets are compounding double-digit.** RegTech ~16-23% CAGR to 2030; AML software ~15-18% CAGR to ~USD 7-10bn by 2030; global information-security spend USD 213bn (2025) → USD 240-244bn (2026), driven partly by rising regulatory pressure. [Mordor – RegTech market](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/global-regtech-industry) · [MarketsandMarkets – AML market](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/anti-money-laundering-solutions-market-95490454.html) · [Gartner – $213bn infosec 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-29-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-end-user-spending-on-information-security-to-total-213-billion-us-dollars-in-2025)

## Printec implication

This theme pulls demand squarely toward Printec's highest-margin, "sticky" lines and is largely macro-immune (legally mandated):

- **Security & compliance products / managed services** are the primary beneficiary. DORA's permanent run-cost reality + AMLA's trickle-down + the ECB's evidence-based supervision push CEE/SEE banks (most of them mid-sized — squarely in the EUR 1-5m DORA band) toward outsourcing resilience testing, incident reporting, ICT third-party risk and AML screening rather than building in-house. Printec's managed-services + compliance positioning lets it absorb spend the banks cannot staff themselves (labour is 72-79% of the AML cost burden).
- **DORA CTPP regime is a double-edged but net-positive signal:** as banks' suppliers face direct oversight, banks will favour vendors who can evidence resilience, sub-contracting controls and incident reporting — Printec should pre-position its NCR Atleos-based ATM/managed-services stack with DORA-aligned attestations to win/retain bank contracts.
- **EAA = a hardware + software refresh super-cycle for ATMs/self-service.** Non-compliant legacy fleets must be retired at end-of-life; new deployments must meet EN 301 549 (audio guidance, tactile keypads, accessible PIN entry, privacy screen-blanking). This directly drives Printec's ATM/cash-recycling and self-service product replacement and upgrade pipeline across 2027-2031.
- **Onboarding/KYC** benefits from AMLR's harmonised single rulebook and broadened obliged-entity scope (incl. crypto/CASPs) — a tailwind for Printec's onboarding line as banks standardise customer due diligence to one EU standard.
- **Digital euro** adds a forward option: offline-capable, highly-available payment endpoints (terminals, ATMs, PSP integration) align with Printec's POS/payments and self-service infrastructure if/when issuance approaches ~2029.

Flag (per Printec policy): regulatory-interpretation and any payment-/security-sensitive implementation should be reviewed with the Compliance team / DPO before commitments; cost ranges are interested-party (consultant/vendor) estimates requiring human validation against local CEE/SEE supervisory practice.
