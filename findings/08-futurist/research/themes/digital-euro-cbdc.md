# Digital euro & CBDCs — programme timeline, acceptance/wallet readiness, impact on cash/ATMs

## Direction & summary (2027-2031)

The 2027-2031 window is when the digital euro moves from a multi-year ECB design exercise into a live, legislated reality — and when the global CBDC wave (China's e-CNY at scale; ~3 launched, ~41 pilots) becomes a structural feature of the payments landscape rather than a research topic. The arc is now reasonably well-defined and confidence is rising materially: as of 23/06/2026 the European Parliament's ECON committee approved the digital euro framework and ordered the immediate start of trilogue negotiations, ending three years of central-bank/commercial-bank deadlock; the Council adopted its negotiating position in December 2025. The ECB closed its preparation phase on 29/10/2025 and is targeting a Q1 2026 call for expression of interest, a ~12-month pilot in H2 2027, and potential first issuance in 2029. The design that is emerging — online + offline, cash-like offline privacy, a holding cap of up to ~EUR 3,000 with a "waterfall" sweep to bank accounts, mandatory merchant acceptance and mandatory bank distribution — is deliberately built to complement cash, not replace it.

Pace and confidence: the legislative gate is the binding constraint and it has now meaningfully opened (committee approval + trilogue mandate), so a 2029 issuance is plausible but not certain — trilogues can slip, and "first issuance 2029" is a target, not a commitment. For Printec's footprint, the practical horizon is: legislation 2026-2027, pilot 2027, integration build-out by banks/PSPs 2027-2029, early issuance 2029, and gradual scale-up 2029-2031. Critically, the cash story underneath continues independently of the digital euro: euro-area POS cash share fell from 59% (2022) to 52% (2024) by transaction count, ATMs are declining ~3%/yr (≈249,300 at end-H1 2025), yet 62% of consumers still consider cash important and 87% are satisfied with cash access — so a long cash tail and an ECB mandate to preserve cash access both persist. The most important non-obvious signal: in China, banks have already wired CBDC into >3,000 ATMs for two-way digital-yuan↔cash conversion, demonstrating that a CBDC can repurpose the ATM/self-service estate as a public-money on/off-ramp rather than simply euthanise it.

Confidence: HIGH on the programme timeline and design direction (ECB primary sources + EU legislative record); MEDIUM on exact issuance dates and on the net effect on ATM volumes (depends on holding limits, offline uptake, and whether banks deploy CBDC cash-in/cash-out at the machine).

## Key drivers

- Digital euro legislation cleared its decisive hurdle: on 23/06/2026 the European Parliament ECON committee approved the framework and mandated immediate trilogues, after the Council adopted its negotiating position in December 2025 — the binding gate for issuance is now opening. [CoinDesk – EP clears last step for digital euro](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/06/23/european-parliament-clears-last-step-for-a-digital-euro-to-break-u-s-payment-grip) · [EP Legislative Train – Digital euro](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-an-economy-that-works-for-people/file-digital-euro)
- ECB programme timeline is explicit: preparation phase ran 01/11/2023–29/10/2025; Q1 2026 call for expression of interest; ~12-month pilot in H2 2027 (initial transactions possibly mid-2027); potential first issuance during 2029. [ECB – Preparation phase closing report](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/progress/html/ecb.deprp202510.en.html) · [ECB – Eurosystem moving to next phase](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2025/html/ecb.pr251030~8c5b5beef0.en.html)
- The digital euro is designed to complement, not replace, cash — online + offline, with cash-like privacy offline (P2P device-to-device, no central recording) and a holding cap of up to ~EUR 3,000 with a "waterfall" auto-sweep to commercial bank accounts; merchants and businesses get a zero holding limit to prevent disintermediation. [ECB – Preparation phase closing report](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/progress/html/ecb.deprp202510.en.html) · [Finadium – offline functionality & holding limits](https://finadium.com/ecbs-digital-euro-being-designed-for-privacy-with-off-line-functionality-and-holding-limits/)
- Distribution & acceptance model pulls demand toward banks/PSPs and merchants: PSPs (banks) handle onboarding/KYC/AML and act as the consumer interface; basic services free to users; legal-tender status implies mandatory acceptance by merchants who already take digital payments and mandatory distribution by banks. [EC Finance – digital euro FAQ](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/digital-finance/digital-euro/frequently-asked-questions-digital-euro-and-legal-tender-cash_en) · [ECB – FAQs on the digital euro](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/faqs/html/ecb.faq_digital_euro.en.html)
- Pilot scope (H2 2027) tests P2P (online + offline) and person-to-business (physical POS + e-commerce) with selected PSPs and merchants on ECB/NCB premises — i.e. real acceptance-device and wallet integration testing begins 2027. [ECB – Eurosystem invites PSPs to digital euro pilot](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/intro/news/html/ecb.mipnews251128.en.html)
- Euro-area cash use is declining structurally but retains a long tail: POS cash share 59% (2022) → 52% (2024) by transaction count; cash 39% by value; yet 62% still consider cash an important option and 87% are satisfied with cash access — underpinning an ECB mandate to safeguard cash alongside the digital euro. [ECB – SPACE 2024 press release](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2024/html/ecb.pr241219~172b929461.en.html)
- Euro-area ATM estate is shrinking ~3%/yr: ≈249,300 ATMs at end-H1 2025, -2.9% YoY; 34% accept contactless — a network being rationalised and upgraded, not yet eliminated. [ECB – Payments statistics H1 2025](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/stats/paysec/html/ecb.pis2025h1~36edd636c8.en.html)
- Global cash decline is broad-based: McKinsey puts cash at 46% of worldwide payments in 2025 (down from 50% in 2023), declining ~4%/yr, with instant/A2A and wallets accelerating the shift. [McKinsey – 2025 Global Payments Report](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/financial-services/our-insights/global-payments-report)
- The global CBDC backdrop is now structural: 146 countries/unions (~98% of GDP) exploring, 77 in advanced stages, 3 launched (Bahamas, Jamaica, Nigeria), ~41 pilots; every G20 country except the US is exploring one. [Atlantic Council – CBDC Tracker](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/cbdctracker/)
- BIS survey: 91% of 93 central banks exploring retail CBDCs, with cybersecurity, operational resilience and privacy the top cited challenges — pointing to security/compliance/managed-services demand around any rollout. [Atlantic Council – CBDC Tracker (citing BIS 2025 survey)](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/cbdctracker/)
- Crucial precedent for the ATM channel: China's e-CNY is already integrated into >3,000 Beijing ATMs (ICBC) for two-way digital-yuan↔cash conversion, and e-CNY had processed ~16.7tn RMB (~USD 2.3tn) by end-2025 — evidence a CBDC can repurpose the self-service estate as a public-money on/off-ramp. [Forkast – 3,000+ Beijing ATMs support digital yuan](https://forkast.news/china-atms-cbdc-digitalyuan/) · [Atlantic Council – CBDC Tracker (e-CNY volumes)](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/cbdctracker/)
- Programme economics signal a large, multi-year build: ECB development cost ~EUR 1.3bn to first issuance, ~EUR 320m/yr operating from 2029, and an estimated EUR 4–5.8bn of banking-sector investment — much of it integration, onboarding and acceptance infrastructure. [ECB – Preparation phase closing report](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/euro/digital_euro/progress/html/ecb.deprp202510.en.html)

## Printec implication

The digital euro and the broader CBDC wave pull Printec demand in three concrete directions, all toward existing product lines rather than away from them:

1) Onboarding / identity / compliance (strongest pull). Because banks/PSPs are the mandatory distribution channel and must perform KYC/AML and digital-euro onboarding for every customer — including dedicated, free, assisted onboarding for the digitally excluded — there is a clear pipeline for Printec's onboarding, eKYC and self-service enrolment capabilities. Member-state "vulnerable population" onboarding mandates map directly onto branch self-service kiosks and assisted-onboarding workflows.

2) ATMs / cash-recycling repurposed as public-money on/off-ramps (defensive + upside). The China e-CNY precedent (>3,000 ATMs doing two-way CBDC↔cash conversion) is the template: a digital euro with a EUR 3,000 holding limit and offline wallets will need physical top-up and cash-out points. Printec's NCR Atleos ATM/recycler estate and managed-services position it to deliver digital-euro cash-in/cash-out at the machine — turning a CBDC from a pure ATM threat into a software/hardware upgrade cycle across 17 CEE/SEE markets. The ECB's explicit mandate to preserve cash access protects the underlying ATM-as-a-service business through the window.

3) POS/payments + security/managed services. Mandatory merchant acceptance of the digital euro at any merchant already taking digital payments implies acceptance-software and POS-integration work; BIS's flagging of cybersecurity/operational resilience as the top CBDC concern aligns with Printec's security and compliance/managed-services offerings. Note the timing: real integration spend (the EUR 4–5.8bn banking-sector estimate) lands 2027-2029, so Printec should be positioning capability and NCR Atleos co-propositions in 2026-2027 to capture the build-out.

Caveat (per Printec compliance posture): any digital-euro/CBDC integration code or design Printec produces is payment- and security-sensitive and must be flagged for human review and testing before deployment; it is not production-ready on the strength of this outlook.
