## 2. The reading — what this run actually says

**The famous deadlines slipped; the quiet ones bind.** PSD3/PSR is not adopted and its cliff moved right by ~6–9 months. FIDA has no agreed text at all — the OEIL file lacks the committee-approval event that PSD3/PSR has, which is the decisive negative. The digital euro trilogue produced nothing over the summer. Meanwhile a second tier hardened into law: the AI Act Omnibus was *enacted* (2 Dec 2027, unconditional), AMLR applies 10 Jul 2027, EUDI acceptance binds 24 Dec 2027, non-euro instant and Verification of Payee bind 9 Jan / 9 Jul 2027, the European Accessibility Act removes every non-accessible payment terminal by 28 Jun 2030, AFIR puts card readers on every ≥50 kW charger by 1 Jan 2027, and PCI PIN's ban on clear-text key injection extended to processors' own estates on **1 Jan 2026 — already in force**.

**And the buyer keeps moving away from the bank.** This run surfaced, with dated evidence, at least eight buyer types that are not a bank's channels team: national supervisors and central banks (the AMLA eligibility data collection; TIPS Clone operators), CSDs and treasury functions (Pontes onboarding), regional telcos and colocation operators (DORA obligations passed through by contract from designated CTPPs), charge-point operators (AFIR), civil registries and professional chambers (eIDAS Art. 45e authentic-source verification), insurers (AI Act Annex III 5(c), on the same 2 Dec 2027 clock with less mature model risk), mobile network operators (offline digital euro), and passporting EMIs/PSPs with no local establishment (AMLA's Central Contact Point review).

**The honest counterweight.** Three of this run's strongest signals cut against the growth narrative and belong in any board reading: open-banking third-party providers are **shrinking** (554 → 552 → 547 across three quarters, withdrawals outrunning authorisations 15:10); the SEPA Payment Account Access scheme has **zero bank supply-side participants after four years**, so premium-API monetisation is not happening on the voluntary track; and the MiCA-compliant stablecoin market is **~99% USDC** — every compliant euro token combined is under 1%.

## 3. Global themes — the perimeters (lead)

### 3.1 Identity becomes acceptance infrastructure — _strong growth_ · High
Identity verification USD 14.34bn (2025) → 29.32bn by 2030, 15.4% CAGR ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/identity-verification.asp)); digital signature USD 13.4bn → 70.24bn by 2030, 39.2% ([MnM](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/digital-signature.asp)). The binding dates are **24 Dec 2026** (issuance) and **24 Dec 2027** (acceptance by medium and large relying parties), with exposure of **EUR 5m or 1% of turnover** ([Reg. 2024/1183](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1183)). Three implementing acts landed 15 Jul 2026 (CIR [2026/1730](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1730/oj/eng) on relying-party registration and the intermediary association, [2026/1731](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1731/oj/eng) on the wallet trust mark, [2026/1735](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2026/1735/oj/eng) on (Q)EAA verification against authentic sources) and **ARF v3.0.0** shipped 23 Jul 2026 with a new Relying Party Services concept. Germany will launch a stripped-down baseline wallet on 2 Jan 2027 (no QES, no pseudonymous login, no payments) and the Commission internally accepts a phased rollout. Deepfakes are now **6.5% of all fraud attempts, from 0.1% three years earlier**, and **42.5% of financial-sector fraud attempts are AI-driven** while only **22% of organisations have implemented AI-driven fraud prevention** ([Signicat](https://www.signicat.com/the-battle-against-ai-driven-identity-fraud)). **CEE/SEE play:** acceptance orchestration for medium and large relying parties across 27 registers, plus the Art. 45e authentic-source hub — not another horizontal eKYC tool, whose unit price is heading from EUR 70–100 to EUR 3–8 per customer ([Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/eudi-wallet-2026-deadline-rollout-eic-2026)).

### 3.2 Real-time payments become a supervised data layer — _strong growth_ · High
SEPA instant is **25% of euro-area credit transfers by number, 8% by value** (H2 2025) and euro-area SCT Inst volumes reached **10.1bn in 2025, +73%** ([ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/stats/paysec/html/ecb.pis2025h2~23986fb4a6.en.html)). TIPS settled **2.47bn transactions in 2025, +82.5%**, but growth is decelerating (+99.8% YoY in Jan 2026 → +86.8% in May) and turnover printed its first month-on-month decline in May 2026. **Verification of Payee already runs at close to one billion requests per month**, with reachability named as the open weakness ([EPC](https://www.europeanpaymentscouncil.eu/news-insights/news/rvm-msg-reviews-vop-progress-and-advances-vop-20-discussions)). Non-euro PSPs bind 9 Jan 2027 (receive) and 9 Jul 2027 (send + VoP); **Bulgaria owes all four obligations on 1 Jan 2027** under IPR Art. 16(9), a year after euro adoption — six months earlier than the July file assumed, and no BNB or BORICA acknowledgement of that date could be found. **CEE/SEE play:** Verification-of-Payee-as-a-service for markets with no routing and verification mechanism (DIAS is the working template), plus the ISO 20022 address-remediation work, which is a customer-master-data project sold to a buyer who has never bought from a payments integrator.

### 3.3 Financial crime: the supervisor becomes the buyer — _strong growth_ · High
Fraud detection USD 32.0bn (2025) → 65.68bn (2030), 15.5% ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/fraud-detection-prevention.asp)); AML solutions USD 4.05bn (2026) → 9.27bn (2031), 18.01% ([Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/anti-money-laundering-solutions-market)); global RegTech USD 20.67bn → 44.11bn (2030). EEA payment fraud hit **EUR 4.2bn in 2024, +17%**, with fraudulent credit transfers **EUR 2.5bn, +24%** and "manipulation of the payer" now **74% of credit-transfer fraud by value** ([EBA/ECB Joint Report, pp.10, 19](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-12/1709846a-84d9-47cf-86a0-b155efb34d66/EBA%20and%20ECB%20Report%20on%20Payment%20Fraud.pdf)). AMLR applies 10 Jul 2027; AMLA scales **120 → 233 → 432 staff** and becomes **fee-funded with EUR 65,155,000 of supervisory fees in 2028** ([SPD 2026-2028, pp.27, 75](https://www.amla.europa.eu/document/download/27549516-d110-4e91-b1ed-d3552b8f9661_en?filename=AMLA+SPD+2026-2028.pdf)). Art. 75 data-sharing partnerships become lawful the same day, but the EDPB-AMLA Joint Guidelines only reach consultation in **H1 2027** — so bankable utility revenue is a **2029–30** event, not 2027. **CEE/SEE play:** the Jan–Mar 2027 eligibility data pipeline, sold to national supervisors; the Central Contact Point service for passporting EMIs; grey-list remediation for the BiH and Bulgarian FIUs.

### 3.4 AI in banking: the financial supervisor is the AI Act auditor — _strong growth_ · Medium
Global banking AI spend USD 31.3bn (2024) → 53.1bn (2026) → 90.2bn (2028) — **but only 2024 is observed; Statista generates the rest at a flat 30.3% CAGR** ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1557311/global-banking-sector-ai-spending-forecast)). The AI-governance platform market is **USD 492m (2026) → >1bn (2030)** — a *decelerating* curve, which is the tell that **2026–2028 is the buying window**. The mechanism the July file lacked: **AI Act Art. 74(6) makes the national financial supervisory authority the market-surveillance authority** for high-risk AI in regulated financial institutions, and **Art. 74(7) requires it to report to the ECB without delay** ([artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/74/)). **Romania has already made this concrete: ASF and BNR are the market-surveillance authorities for financial institutions' high-risk AI** ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/artificial-intelligence-2026/romania)). An AI Act finding on a credit model is therefore prudential supervisory information, not a privacy fine. EBA's mapping found **no significant contradictions** with banking law and lined AI Act obligations up against CRR/CRD Arts. 174–176 and DORA Arts. 17–19 ([A&O Shearman](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/eba-factsheet-on-implications-of-eu-ai-act-for-banking-and-payments-sector)). **CEE/SEE play:** instrument the existing IRB model-governance evidence chain to emit Art. 12 logs, Art. 14 oversight records and Art. 17 QMS artefacts continuously — one evidence pipeline, two regulators — sold to the CRO/model-risk function. Adjacent: **insurers under Annex III 5(c)**, same date, less mature model risk, no IRB heritage to reuse.

### 3.5 Operational resilience: supervised, but not yet fined — _growth_ · High
The first ESAs report logged **3,383 major ICT incidents in 2025**, **29% third-party-attributable**, only **10% cyber**; system failure was **51%** ([JC 2026 16, pp.13–15](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/JC_2026_16_ESAs_2025_report_on_major_ICT-related_incidents.pdf)). The ECB's SSM priorities 2026–2028 put operational resilience as **Priority 2** with two on-site-inspection campaigns. **But no DORA fine has landed anywhere**, and the July file's "first fines expected H2 2026" is still unfulfilled — every "fines are coming" claim traces to vendor content. The most useful and least-used finding is the cost field: **~40% of major incidents reported zero direct or indirect cost and 15% left the field blank** (p.17) — a reporting-maturity artefact, and a sales argument rather than a comfort. **CEE/SEE play:** incident-reporting-as-a-service on retainer for institutions below the SSM-significant threshold, now running two incompatible clocks once CRA reporting starts 11 Sep 2026. Sell operational and contractual risk, not fine risk — Bulgaria's penalty ceiling is EUR 50,000.

### 3.6 Post-quantum crypto is a hardware-refresh event on Swift's clock — _growth_ · Medium
HSM market **USD 1.74bn (2025) → 2.04bn (2026) → 4.80bn (2032), 15.3% CAGR**, with BFSI at 37.8% and quantum cryptography the fastest-growing technology segment ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/hardware-security-modules-market-162277475.html)). The dated trigger is not the EU's aspirational 2030: it is **SwiftNet 8.0, PQC-enabled in 2027 with a 15-month institution migration window** ([The Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/07/post-quantum-cryptography-timelines/)). Two corrections matter. First, **the end-2026 EU inventory obligation binds Member States, not banks** — the entity-level hook is **PCI DSS Req. 12.3.3** (in force since 31 Mar 2025) plus DORA documentation, with NIS2-as-amended (COM(2026) 13) routing PQC into national strategies on a 12-month transposition. Second, **EMVCo is more relaxed than the market narrative** — its own text carries no RSA→ECC→PQC "telescoping" guidance and no issuer timelines. Meanwhile Thales shipped Luna 8 on 4 Aug 2026 with **payShield 11K support only "planned for a future release" — no date**. **CEE/SEE play:** cryptographic bill-of-materials discovery sold to the newly-created Head of Cryptography role, then a compressed **2028–2030** crypto-agile refresh. Note the immediately actionable adjacent fact: **PCI PIN Req. 32-9 extended the clear-text key-injection ban to processors' own estates on 1 Jan 2026** — already in force, and almost nobody is selling against it.

### 3.7 Tokenization: a euro market that is 99% dollar — _flat to growth_ · Low

The structural fact of this cycle: **the MiCA-compliant stablecoin category is ~USD 74.2bn, of which USDC alone is ~USD 73.5bn (~99%)** — every MiCA-compliant euro token combined is under 1% ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/categories/mica-compliant-stablecoin)). USDT was delisted for EEA retail on 1 Jul 2026 and the vacuum went to USDC, not to euro tokens; Schnabel conceded the point publicly at Jackson Hole on 28 Aug. **Qivalis remains unlicensed and unlaunched** — slippage into 2027 is now the base case, and the July file's "targeting H2 2026" should not be carried as on-track. What *is* dated: **Pontes goes live 21 Sep 2026** with Clearstream in testing, extending to 24/7 multi-currency by mid-2028 ([Ledger Insights](https://www.ledgerinsights.com/ecb-targets-21-september-go-live-for-project-pontes-a-primer-on-tokenized-central-bank-money/)). Analyst dispersion is 15x at the same horizon (McKinsey USD 2trn vs BCG 9.4trn, both 2030) — that is disagreement about definitions, not a forecast range. **CEE/SEE play:** Pontes onboarding for CSDs and bank treasury/settlement functions (CSD Prague is the region's only DLT Pilot Regime licensee), and banking-the-CASPs services for correspondent banks — a motion requiring **zero belief in tokenization succeeding**.

### 3.8 Open finance and the non-bank cohort: the cliff moved right — _growth_ · Medium
Europe's embedded-finance revenue pool remains EUR 20–30bn (2023) → **>EUR 100bn by 2030** on McKinsey's July-2024 work; **no 2026 update exists from any of McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Capgemini, Roland Berger or Oliver Wyman**, and the frequently-recycled Bain "USD 7trn by 2026" is a four-year-old US figure. The recomputed calendar puts PSD3/PSR in the OJ around **Feb 2027 (base)**, re-authorisation applications due **~Dec 2028**, and VoP-for-all-credit-transfers liability at **~Jun 2029**. **CCD2 applies 20 Nov 2026 with ten Member States having notified no transposition measures at all — including Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Poland and Romania** ([EUR-Lex NIM](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/NIM/?uri=CELEX:32023L2225)). The MiCA half of the cohort is *stronger* than in July: grandfathering closed 1 Jul 2026 and authorised CASPs rose from 199 to **333** between April and August — while **Poland has 1, Romania 0, Hungary 0 and Greece 0** ([Outrun](https://outrun.at/micar-dashboard)). **CEE/SEE play:** the compliance/authorisation officer at a re-licensing EMI or PI, and the first-wave CASP cohort in the four large markets whose regulators have not yet opened.

### 3.9 Core modernization: fragmentation creates the integration market — _growth_ · Medium
Core banking is **USD 17.19bn (2025) → 29.01bn (2031), 9.12%** on Mordor and **19.30bn (2025) → 80.65bn (2034), 17.4%** on Fortune Business Insights (Europe USD 6.05bn in 2026) — a 2x spread that is not reconcilable from public data, so publish the corridor. What all sources agree on is the mix: **services grow 12.12% CAGR against solutions**, cloud is 29.8% of deployment growing 16.7–21.4%, and **incremental modernization is already 48.7% of the modernization market**. The July file's "40% → 70–80% sidecar by 2028" must be **dropped**: the 40% is a 2023 sponsored IDC FutureScape prediction and the 70–80% has no traceable source at all. The 2026 event is **fragmentation** — Finastra's three divestitures, Temenos buying an orchestration layer rather than more core, Thought Machine at ~USD 100m ARR with no IPO before 2028. Evidence for the pattern in-region is concrete: **NBG completed a >EUR 1bn, five-year Finacle core replacement collapsing ~40 legacy applications into one** and cutting total applications from ~1,000 to 250; **Komercní banka completed retail migration to Temenos on private cloud on 15 May 2026** (1.73m KB+ customers, Celent Model Bank Award); **Raiffeisen BiH went live on Temenos Core on Red Hat OpenShift**. **CEE/SEE play:** the coexistence and orchestration layer, reached through the **core vendor's partner channel** rather than the bank's tender office — the route both German Mambu migrations took via Innovance.

### 3.10 Acceptance: regulation, not technology, drives the terminal estate — _strong growth_ · High
Global SoftPOS transaction value USD 23.9bn (2025) → **540bn by 2030** ([Juniper](https://www.juniperresearch.com/press/soft-pos-transactions-to-accelerate-by-2150/)), software USD 365m (2024) → 534.1m (2026) → 1,243.9m (2030). But the installed reality is early and must be stated honestly: **Tatra banka's SoftPOS is ~1% of its card-present volume** (~60,000 app transactions/week against >6m via terminals); Romania has ~25,000 SoftPOS points against **645,630** terminals; and **Ukraine — the region's leader and the only market where a central bank publishes the series — has 69,783 tap-to-phone devices, 11% of its estate**. SoftPOS wins the long tail, taking an expected 15–20% of *new micro-merchant onboarding* by 2027; it does not displace the installed base before 2031. The estate itself is driven by mandates: **Romania's Law 239/2025 abolished the acceptance turnover threshold entirely from 1 Jan 2026**, forcing ~120,000 micro-enterprises to acquire acceptance; **Slovakia's Act 384/2025 applies from 1 May 2026**; **Greece mandated instant-payment acceptance at all points of sale from 1 Dec 2025** across ~1.2m terminals. The result is a sharp CEE split — Romania **+21.4%**, Slovakia **+18.5%**, Bulgaria **+17.3%** compound annual terminal growth 2021–25 against Croatia +7.3%, Czechia +2.5%, Hungary +2.1%. **CEE/SEE play:** vendor-agnostic terminal management across a hybrid estate, the EAA-driven replacement re-tender, and unattended acceptance for charge-point operators.

### 3.11 Cash and self-service — the LEGACY BASE — _mixed_ · High
Euro-area ATMs located in-country fall from **277,612 (2021) to 245,619 (2025)**, about −2%/yr, and CEE is genuinely bifurcated rather than uniformly growing: Greece **+29.4%**, Hungary +14.6%, Romania +9.9% against Croatia **−18.7%**, Slovakia −13.9%, Slovenia −12.3% (2021→2025, [ECB PTN](https://data-api.ecb.europa.eu/service/data/PTN/A..W2.2222._T.PN?startPeriod=2022&format=csvdata)). Of Greece's 7,421 machines only 6,132 dispense cash — the growth is in non-withdrawal self-service. Meanwhile **the cash stock keeps growing**: euro area +2.4–2.9%, Romania +5.8%, Hungary +5.7%, Czechia +2.8%, Ukraine **+12.9%**, North Macedonia +12.3%, Bosnia +8.6% — while withdrawal volumes fall. That divergence is the whole thesis: a stable-to-growing cash stock served by fewer, smarter, deposit-capable machines. Vendor results confirm where the money went — **NCR Atleos ATMaaS +24% to USD 77m**, Brink's **14 consecutive quarters of mid-teens+ AMS/DRS growth**, Diebold shipping a **record number of teller cash recyclers**, and **Glory Europe +27.1%** explicitly on recyclers sold to postal operators replacing closed bank branches. Note the accessibility asymmetry: the EAA lets Member States run **self-service terminals to end of economic life, up to 20 years**, while non-accessible POS terminals must go by 28 Jun 2030. **CEE/SEE play:** recyclers, deposit automation and managed-services annuity — the base to optimise, never the spine.

## 5. Per-region outlook 2027–2031

**Three speeds plus one off-axis, grouped by what forces the spend rather than by geography** — because speed is set by the funding instrument and the compliance calendar, not by GDP per capita.

**Speed 1 — deadline-driven, inside the perimeter (Cyprus, Croatia, Greece, Slovakia, Slovenia, Bulgaria).** No discretion about timing: euro-area IPR and VoP already binding (Bulgaria's whole IPR package lands 1 Jan 2027), EUDI due 24 Dec 2026, digital-euro pilot 2027. RRF absorption ranges 53–84%. Sub-split matters: *funded-and-done* (Czechia 77%) against *cliff-edge* (Slovenia had only 5 of 60 milestones fulfilled for its seventh request, with EUR 272m riding on the now-passed 31 Aug 2026 deadline; Cyprus at 48% of 222 milestones). **Greece is the footprint's frontrunner and its own paradox**: highest card share at 71.5% yet **99% SME cash acceptance**, the largest RRF plan (EUR 35.9bn), a completed EUR 1bn core replacement, a national AI law, an RVM already operating, universal instant acceptance since Dec 2025, and ~EUR 4bn of announced systemic-four technology spend.

**Speed 2 — EU, euro-deferred (Romania, Czechia, Hungary), and they diverge sharply on funding.** Romania has the largest absolute runway and the steepest catch-up (50% → 78% grant absorption after payment request #5 on 14 Aug 2026, targeting 97.2%), modern rails already (SENT instant went from 58,515 transactions in 2019 to **138.6m in 2024**, ~46% of lei volume) and a macro constraint rather than a technical one (**GDP −0.5% in 2026, inflation 8.2%**, recovery in 2027). Its identity position is the worst in the EU — **eID use fell to 10.09%, the only Member State to go backwards**, with 57% of non-users saying they simply do not have one, only 436,674 eIDs issued against a 5m PNRR milestone, and ~EUR 264m of penalty exposure. Czechia is disciplined (77% absorbed) and is one of only two footprint countries with a national PQC strategy. **Hungary is the outlier and should be shown as a distinct risk case: 9% disbursed, the only Member State with zero result-based RRF payments**, despite 68% of milestones achieved — a political blockage that, with the deadline passed, removes ~EUR 9.5bn of public co-funding from the 2027–31 period.

**Speed 3 — accession-financed, pre-SEPA (Serbia, Albania, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Kosovo, BiH).** The binding constraint is absorption and reform conditionality, not demand: only **EUR 673.6m of ~EUR 5.9bn released by May 2026 (~12%)**, and the grace expiry has begun clawing money back — EUR 330m in play, **BiH already down EUR 108.4m with zero payments received**. But the payments perimeter is arriving anyway and on donor money: TIPS Clone live in Montenegro and BiH since 20 Jul 2026, Albania/Kosovo/North Macedonia in the Nov 2026 window, **Serbia operationally in SEPA since 5 May 2026** (SCT only — SCT Inst still to come, which is the trigger to watch for VoP). Montenegro's cross-border cost fell from **EUR 73.40 to EUR 6.15**, and CBCG/ECB put the combined SEPA + TIPS Clone benefit at ~2.3% of GDP annually. North Macedonia is the only Western Balkans country whose latest tranche is explicitly digital, and makes **IBAN mandatory for domestic payments from 1 Nov 2026**. Kosovo is the sharpest exclusion case — unilaterally euroised, still paying full correspondent-banking cost, its SEPA application blocked at the Constitutional Court, and **2.5:1 cash-to-card by value, the largest acceptance headroom in the set**.

**Off-axis — Ukraine, flagged amber.** Software- and identity-led and not ceasefire-gated for digital work: SEP 4.x is ISO 20022-native and 24/7, instant since Dec 2024 with **35.9m instant transfers in 2025**, TrackSEP mandatory since 1 Apr 2026, and Ukraine is **the first non-EU country whose trust-services system is formally recognised at EU level**, now connected via the Third-Countries AdES List of Trusted Lists. It also has the fastest cash growth in the footprint (**+12.9%**) and the only official SoftPOS series (69,783 devices, +34.3%). **Caveat: bank.gov.ua, EBRD and Commission sources were all unreachable this cycle, so the macro/reconstruction baseline stands unrefreshed and should be re-run before board use.**

**Mature markets as leading indicators.** Four things Western Europe shows now that CEE/SEE reaches by 2029–31: cash *acceptance* has stopped falling (90% → 92%) so dual-rail economics are permanent; **mobile acceptance is the fastest-moving merchant variable anywhere (36% → 68% in two years)**; the acceptance estate grows while the cash estate shrinks; and instant has overtaken classic credit transfer in growth terms (+73% against −7%). On identity and CBDC the gap is only about two years, not four — fewer than a third of Member States are on track for the wallet date.
