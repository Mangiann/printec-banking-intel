## Headline

**Nothing has been adopted and nothing has been published in the Official Journal since 02/08/2026.** Checked live today, the European Parliament Legislative Observatory files for both PSD3 (2023/0209(COD)) and the PSR (2023/0210(COD)) still read *"Awaiting Council's 1st reading position"*. The last key event on both is 05/05/2026 (ECON approval of the agreed text). The only forward entry on either file is a forecast: **"Indicative plenary sitting date, 1st reading — 14/12/2026"**. Entry into force therefore cannot be confirmed, and neither 21-month clock has started: both run from OJ publication + 20 days, and the dates in the agreed texts are still `[OP please insert the date]` placeholders.

This is a material slip. The ECON chair had pressed for Parliament adoption *by September 2026 at the latest*; the EP Legislative Train (last updated 20/06/2026) still says only "close to adoption". A 14/12/2026 plenary pushes entry into force to roughly Q1 2027, PSR general application to roughly Q4 2028, and the PSR Verification-of-Payee carve-out to roughly Q2 2029.

## Three corrections to the baseline

1. **"PSR Arts.7/38" conflates two instruments.** ATM fee display is **PSR Art.7**. Independent-ATM-deployer registration is **PSD3 Art.38**. **PSR Art.38** is something else entirely — open-banking dedicated-interface availability and performance.
2. **The PSR clock is not a single 21-month clock.** Art.112 sets three tiers: 21 months generally; **27 months** for Arts.50 and 57 (Verification of Payee and its liability regime); and **immediate effect on entry into force** for Arts.85a and 108a.
3. **PSD3 Art.38 registration carries a DORA hook.** Registering ATM deployers must evidence Chapter II of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 digital operational resilience — expressly including *"the undertakings to which it outsources the whole or part of its operations"*.

### 2. PSR final compromise text, Article 112 (Entry into force and application) — EU (AT,BG,HR,CY,CZ,GR,HU,RO,SK,SI)
- **Signal:** CORRECTION/EXPANSION of the baseline's flat '21-month clock'. The PSR final compromise text (Council doc 8221/26, Interinstitutional File 2023/0210(COD), 17/04/2026) Article 112 sets a THREE-TIER clock, verbatim: the Regulation 'shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal'; 'It shall apply from [21 months after the date of entry into force]'; 'However, Articles 50 and 57 shall apply from [27 months after the date of entry into force]'; and 'Articles 85a and 108a shall apply from the date of entry into force'. Article 50 is the payee name/unique-identifier matching (Verification of Payee, VoP) obligation and Article 57 is the PSP liability regime for getting that matching wrong; recital text confirms the extra 6 months is 'thus granting payment service providers enough time to take the necessary steps to adjust their internal systems'. Article 85a (an SCA derogation for recurring credit transfers set up under an agreement) and Article 108a (crypto/e-money-token transitional) bite immediately on entry into force.
- **Source:** Council of the EU, doc ST 8221/26 — final compromise text of the Payment Services Regulation — https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8221-2026-INIT/en/pdf (17/04/2026, primary-tier)
- **Implies:** Two separate budget waves, not one. Wave 1 (EIF+21m) = ATM fee display (Art.7), SCA accessibility (Art.88), impersonation-fraud prevention and refund (Art.59), open-banking interface SLAs (Art.38). Wave 2 (EIF+27m) = VoP-for-all-credit-transfers and the associated liability engine. Printec should sell the two waves as separate programmes; the Art.85a immediate-effect item means the SCA rules engine must be touched the day the Regulation enters into force, before any transposition.

### 4. PSR Article 50 + Article 57 — Verification of Payee extended to ALL credit transfers — EU footprint, with sharpest effect in non-euro markets: CZ, HU, RO, BG, plus HR/GR/CY/SI/SK/AT
- **Signal:** NEW and under-reported in the baseline. PSR Art.50 makes Articles 5c(1)-(7) and 5b(2) of Regulation (EU) 260/2012 (the SEPA Regulation as amended by the Instant Payments Regulation (EU) 2024/886) apply 'mutatis mutandis to ALL credit transfers, including those that fall outside the scope of Regulation (EU) 260/2012'. Where the payee account is not identified by an IBAN, references to the payment account identifier are to be read as 'the unique identifier used to unambiguously identify the payment account of the payee'. Art.57 then creates a hard liability: if a PSP fails to comply with Art.50 and that failure results in a defectively executed transaction, the payer's PSP must 'without delay refund the payer the amount transferred' and restore the account, with a right of redress against the payee's PSP or the payment initiation service provider. Both apply at EIF+27 months (Art.112).

### 5. PSR Article 59 — impersonation ('spoofing') fraud: standalone prevention duty + 15-business-day refund — EU footprint (all 10 EU markets)
- **Signal:** Verified verbatim and materially deeper than the baseline. Art.59(-1) is a standalone PREVENTION obligation: PSPs 'shall have adequate prevention and robust technical safeguards in place to prevent cases where fraudsters replicate and misuse the payment service provider's communication channels for misleading payment service users into making fraudulent transactions.' Art.59(1) then requires a FULL refund to a consumer manipulated by a third party pretending to be the consumer's PSP over channels attributed to that PSP, conditional on the consumer notifying the PSP without undue delay and reporting the fraud to the police. Art.59(2) sets a hard 15-BUSINESS-DAY clock from notification+police report to either refund or issue a justified refusal naming the ADR/complaint bodies. Art.59(3)-(4) reverse the burden of proof onto the PSP to show consumer fraud or gross negligence, and require the PSP to invite the consumer's account of events and factor it in — with an express rule that consumer silence 'shall not in itself' support a gross-negligence finding.
- **Source:** Council of the EU, doc ST 8221/26 (PSR final compromise), Article 59 — https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8221-2026-INIT/en/pdf (17/04/2026, primary-tier)
- **Implies:** Three concrete builds for footprint banks: (a) anti-spoofing controls over the bank's own outbound channels (SMS sender-ID, caller-ID, e-mail authentication) — a prevention duty no PSD2 control satisfies today; (b) a claims workflow that provably meets 15 business days with reasoned refusals; (c) evidence capture strong enough to discharge a reversed burden of proof. Printec lane: INETCO real-time transaction monitoring, FICO and IMTF Siron for behavioural/scam detection and case management, Namirial eKYC for identity assurance in the claims flow. Budget commit 9-12 months before EIF+21m.
- **Likelihood:** High — agreed text, unchanged since 23/04/2026 publication of the compromise · **Confidence:** Medium · **Opp size:** L · **New:** True
- **Follow-up:** Model the 15-business-day SLA against each customer's current disputes platform; identify which footprint banks have no outbound-channel spoofing control at all.

### 7. PSR Article 88 — accessibility of strong customer authentication (SCA) — EU footprint (all 10 EU markets)
- **Signal:** Verified verbatim; the operative bite is Art.88(2), which the baseline did not capture. Art.88(1): PSPs must ensure that ALL customers 'including persons with disabilities, older persons, with low digital skills and those who do not have access to digital channels or payment instruments' have at their disposal at least one means, adapted to their situation, to perform SCA FREE OF CHARGE, with support provided where the user requests help; the duty does not extend to providing devices. This sits alongside, not instead of, the European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882).

### 10. PSD3 Article 38 — registration regime for independent ATM deployers (IADs) — EU footprint (all 10 EU markets); largest IAD estates in GR, RO, HR, HU, CZ
- **Signal:** BASELINE CORRECTION plus new detail. The baseline attributed both ATM fee display AND independent-deployer registration to 'PSR Arts.7/38'. The registration article is in PSD3, not PSR: PSD3 Art.38(1) provides that 'ATM deployers shall not be subject to authorisation but shall register, before taking up activity, with the competent authority of the Member State where the cash withdrawal services are intended to be provided.' Art.38(2) requires the Art.3(3) application dossier (points (a),(b),(e)-(h),(j)-(l),(n),(p),(q)) including a description of audit arrangements and organisational arrangements ensuring 'continuity and reliability in the performance of the payment service', and — critically — the security control and mitigation measures 'shall indicate how the person registering will ensure a high level of digital operational resilience in accordance with CHAPTER II of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 (DORA), in particular in relation to technical security and data protection, including for the software and ICT systems used by the person registering OR THE UNDERTAKINGS TO WHICH IT OUTSOURCES the whole or part of its operations.' Art.38(2a) lets the competent authority refuse or revoke registration for non-compliance, with reasons. Art.38(4): registered deployers 'shall be treated as payment institutions'.
- **Source:** Council of the EU, doc ST 8222/26 (PSD3 final compromise), Article 38 — https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-8222-2026-INIT/en/pdf (17/04/2026, primary-tier)
- **Implies:** Independent ATM deployers in the footprint (Euronet is the dominant IAD across Greece, Romania, Croatia, Hungary and the Czech Republic) must, for the first time, register with each national competent authority and evidence DORA Chapter II ICT risk management — INCLUDING for outsourced operations. That makes the deployer's outsourced service partner part of the regulatory dossier. Printec lane, and a genuinely new buyer segment: ATM/device telemetry and monitoring as the DORA Chapter II evidence layer, physical ATM security, managed and field services, x-core for standardised control and reporting. Budget commit 9-12 months before EIF+21m, because registration must be in place BEFORE continuing activity.
- **Likelihood:** High · **Confidence:** Medium · **Opp size:** M · **New:** True
- **Follow-up:** Build a footprint IAD target list (Euronet plus national IADs in RO/GR/HR/HU/CZ) and a DORA-Chapter-II evidence pack for ATM estates; confirm the transitional treatment of IADs already operating at the 21-month date.

### 11. PSR Article 38 — open-banking dedicated-interface availability and performance — EU footprint (all 10 EU markets)
- **Signal:** NEW versus baseline (which did not distinguish PSR Art.38 from PSD3 Art.38). PSR Art.38(1): unavailability of the dedicated open-banking interface 'shall be presumed to have arisen when five consecutive requests ... receive server error responses or no response ... within 30 seconds'. Art.38(2): planned unavailability must be notified to PISPs/AISPs at least one month in advance and 'shall normally occur between 00:00 and 06:00'. Art.38(2a): unplanned outages require timely notice of remediation measures and estimated time to resolve, with 'an optimal recovery time'. Art.38(2b): the dedicated interface must provide 'availability and performance that are equal AT LEAST to those of the interface that the account servicing payment service provider uses for authentication and communication with its users' — i.e. parity with the bank's own app/web channel. Art.38(5) mandates EBA regulatory technical standards on quarterly availability/performance statistics and on the 'optimal recovery time', to be submitted to the Commission nine months after entry into force.
