## What changed since last run

The biggest new development is that instant payments went live regionally: Banca d'Italia's TIPS Clone platform launched on 20 July 2026 with the central banks of Bosnia & Herzegovina, Montenegro and Kosovo, IPS BiH has already grown from three to five banks, all eleven Montenegrin banks are live with customer how-to videos, and CBCG published its first official IPS volumes for July 2026 — with North Macedonia now onboarding alongside a MIPS ISO 20022 rebuild. Second, CBBiH formally applied to join SEPA on 6 August 2026, only 25 days old at time of writing, while BiH was simultaneously placed on the FATF grey list at the 19 June 2026 plenary, giving FBA-supervised banks a hard AML and ICT/cyber compliance runway for calendar 2026. Third, the hardware estates are expanding faster than expected almost everywhere: Albania's ATM fleet jumped to 1,414 at end-Q2-2026 (+29.6% in six months) with POS at 38,662 (+57.9% over eighteen months), Serbia posted its highest y/y ATM growth in the whole published NBS series (3,412, +6.76%), and BiH now has a bank-by-bank device count from FBA plus a national 1,868-ATM figure with recycler split from CBBiH. Fourth, cash volumes are not retreating: deposit value exceeds withdrawal value in Serbia and is growing faster, Kosovo's ATM cash figures are flat-to-up, BiH cash in circulation rose 8.56% to 7.46bn KM, and Ukraine's M0 keeps rising despite a 96%-by-count cashless card share — with the new 2000 UAH banknote entering circulation on 4 September 2026 requiring reconfiguration of 38,685 self-service devices.

autumn 2026), which should firm up the ◇ 2025-S2 provisional reads below and add 2026-S1. **Next run should
re-check from mid-October.**

**How to read the cells.** **Bold** = the latest **firm** (non-provisional) observation, with its reference
year and the year-on-year change against the same period a year earlier. **◇** = the latest **provisional**
ECB observation (OBS_STATUS "P" — an early read the ECB may still revise): shown so the early signal is
visible, but per the brief it is **Low** confidence and stays out of the firm trend line. Several 2025-S2
provisional reads move sharply — Greece ATMs +18.5%, Slovakia ATMs −14.7%, Greece POS −12.7%, Hungary POS
−9.9% — in a way that looks like a change in the reporting population rather than a real estate movement, so
**do not act on a ◇ figure alone**. Units: ATMs / POS = terminals; branches = offices; cash = the **number**
of card cash withdrawals in **millions of transactions during that year's latest reported half-year**
(ECB `PAY`/CW1), i.e. a half-year volume, not an annual total — use it for direction, not as an absolute
annual cash figure.

**⚠ Series breaks in the ECB feed (y/y suppressed — not a real market movement).** **Slovakia cash withdrawals**: 2023=85.41 vs 2024=36.52 (-57%). The ECB series itself jumps here (a reporting-population or methodology change), so the year-on-year is not a market signal — the level, not the change, is the usable figure. Flagged for the Orchestrator rather than silently averaged away.

**Reading of the EU structural picture (analysis, computed from the scripted numbers above — no hand-keyed figures).**
Branch networks are contracting in **9 of the 10** EU footprint markets on the firm
(non-provisional) series: Slovenia 365 (2025, -5.6%/yr), Bulgaria 4,542 (2025, -5.5%/yr), Hungary 1,300 (2025, -4.9%/yr), Romania 3,288 (2025, -3.7%/yr), Czechia 1,272 (2025, -3.5%/yr), Slovakia 850 (2025, -3.4%/yr), Greece 1,352 (2025, -2.2%/yr), Austria 3,046 (2025, -2.2%/yr), Cyprus 193 (2025, -1.3%/yr). The one market holding essentially flat: Croatia 792 (2025, -0.4%/yr).
Meanwhile ATM fleets are broadly **flat rather than collapsing** — within ±2%/yr in Greece 6,260 (2024, +1.3%/yr), Romania 10,242 (2024, +0.5%/yr), Bulgaria 5,122 (2024, -0.7%/yr), Czechia 5,034 (2024, -1.6%/yr), Austria 13,388 (2024, -2.0%/yr), Slovenia 1,284 (2024, +0.3%/yr), Cyprus 398 (2024, +0.8%/yr) — contracting only in Croatia 3,978 (2024, -2.5%/yr), Slovakia 3,277 (2024, -2.4%/yr) — and actually EXPANDING in Hungary 5,140 (2024, +4.5%/yr).
That combination is the brief's classic **"branches falling + ATMs steady"** pattern: the cash access point is
moving out of the branch and onto self-service, which is the demand case for **offsite ATMs, deposit/recycling
automation, in-branch teller cash recyclers and kiosks, and managed/field services** (a *cash recycler* is a
machine that re-dispenses the notes customers deposited, so the branch or ATM needs replenishing far less often).
The clear divergence is **POS**, growing hard almost everywhere on the firm series — Romania 529,862 (2024, +20.3%/yr), Slovakia 171,839 (2024, +17.9%/yr), Bulgaria 173,231 (2024, +16.4%/yr), Greece 1,324,916 (2024, +11.9%/yr), Slovenia 47,235 (2024, +10.3%/yr), Hungary 305,266 (2024, +9.0%/yr), Croatia 144,948 (2024, +7.4%/yr), Czechia 268,142 (2024, +5.9%/yr), Cyprus 151,399 (2024, +5.4%/yr) —
a direct acquiring / terminal-supply / TMS / softPOS opportunity (*softPOS* = card acceptance on an ordinary
smartphone, with no dedicated terminal).
