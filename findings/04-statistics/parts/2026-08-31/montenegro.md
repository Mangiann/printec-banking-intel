### 1. Central Bank of Montenegro (CBCG) — 20/07/2026 (primary, Medium, L)
CBCG's own TIPS Clone page confirms EU-style instant payments went live in domestic Montenegrin payments on 20/07/2026, built with Banca d'Italia under the ECB umbrella with World Bank support. NEW detail vs baseline: the infrastructure was financed by an EUR 8 MILLION WORLD BANK LOAN (not EU grant); per-transaction cap is EUR 3,000 (person-to-person and business, domestic only); payments to state accounts for public revenues (taxes, contributions, customs, excise, fees, fines) are EXCLUDED for now; 24/7/365. Regulated price cap: for instant payments up to EUR 200 initiated via mobile or internet banking the bank may charge no more than 5 euro-cents, and the same 5-cent cap applies on the RECEIVING side; above EUR 200 the fee cannot exceed the bank's ordinary credit-transfer fee. Phase-2 roadmap explicitly named by CBCG: QR-code payments, payments by mobile PHONE NUMBER (alias/proxy directory) and Request-to-Pay.

**Implies:** A 5-cent regulated ceiling on sub-EUR-200 instant payments makes cash and teller transactions structurally uncompetitive and forces banks to push volume to mobile/internet channels - directly driving Printec digital-channel work, and creating an urgent need for real-time fraud/transaction monitoring (INETCO, FICO) because irrevocable few-second payments cannot be clawed back (CBCG itself states instant payments 'as a rule cannot be revoked'). The named phase-2 items - QR, phone-number alias, Request-to-Pay - are discrete build projects per bank.
**Follow-up:** Ask each Montenegrin bank which fraud/transaction-monitoring engine screens TIPS Clone traffic in real time; pitch INETCO/FICO plus e-channel work for QR + alias phase 2.

### 8. CBCG Council - Decision capping instant credit-transfer fees — 17/07/2026 (primary, Medium, XL)
REGULATORY TRIGGER: on 17/07/2026 the CBCG Council adopted a set of rules completing the legal, operational and TARIFF framework for instant payments, including a Decision limiting the fee on domestic instant credit transfers up to EUR 200 made via mobile or internet banking to a maximum of 5 euro-cents (same cap on the receiving side). CBCG states the previous AVERAGE fee for such payments was 32 cents - a ~6x price cut (PM Milojko Spajic used the same 'about six times cheaper' framing). CBCG estimates savings of ~EUR 1 million a year at current electronic volumes, rising to ~EUR 2.8 million a year as users migrate to electronic instant payments; longer term CBCG puts the combined SEPA + instant-payments economic benefit at ~2.3% of GDP annually. Transactions below the EUR 3,000 instant cap represent almost 80% of all transactions. Go-live was conditional on Parliament passing amendments to the Law on Payment Transactions (Zakon o platnom prometu), which was in parliamentary procedure as of 03/07/2026.

**Implies:** A regulator-imposed 5-cent ceiling destroys fee income on small payments, so banks can only protect margin by cutting cost-to-serve: that means moving transactions off tellers onto self-service (kiosks, deposit/recycling ATMs), automating cash handling, and outsourcing device operations to managed services. It is the strongest commercial argument Printec has in this market right now.
**Follow-up:** Build a cost-to-serve model per Montenegrin bank showing teller-transaction cost vs kiosk/TCR cost under the 5-cent cap.
