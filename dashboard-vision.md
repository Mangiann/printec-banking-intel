# Dashboard Vision — where we want to take this

**Author:** Kostas Karakasiliotis · **Drafted:** 2026-07-01
**Status:** direction / north star — not a build spec yet.

---

## The shift

We've been looking at the dashboard from the wrong angle. Today it behaves like a **deal list** — a set of opportunities and signals. But we are **analysts**, and what we should be delivering is a **read of the market**: what is actually happening in banking, where it's heading, and — only then — where the opportunities for Printec sit inside that bigger story.

> **From:** a list of signals and opportunities.
> **To:** an analyst's lens on the whole banking market that *resolves down* to Printec's opportunities.

---

## The vision in one line

A tool that first answers *"what on earth is going on in banking — near-term and long-term?"*, lets the reader zoom from the whole world down to our footprint, and connects everything into a reasoned picture with hypotheses and suggestions — not just data.

---

## Core principles

### 1. Big picture first
Lead with the **large-scale image of the market** — the major forces and **trends, short-term and long-term** — before any single deal. The reader should grasp the state of banking at a glance, then dig in.

### 2. Two tiers of data, by geography
- **Depth (deal-level) — footprint only.** Tenders, disclosures, and other granular, opportunity-grade data are collected **only for Printec's footprint**, where we can actually act.
- **Breadth (trends only) — the rest of the world.** Everywhere else we track **trends and what others are doing** — not deal-level detail. The point is to **spot what to copy** and to **read the future** for our footprint before it arrives.

### 3. Zoom by geography
Move fluidly across scales: **World → Americas → Europe → SEE → Balkans → Printec footprint → Asia → India → China → …** Each level shows the right resolution — trends at the wide levels, concrete opportunities at the footprint level.

### 4. A different kind of story
Present it as a story, not a table. Ideally a **3D globe** the reader can **zoom into** — from global trends, progressively down to regional patterns, and eventually to specific opportunities in the footprint.

### 5. An information network, not isolated facts
Weave all the data into a **connected network** so the system can form **logical hypotheses and suggestions** for the reader — linking a trend somewhere in the world to a likely move in our footprint, and explaining the reasoning. The dashboard should help the reader *think*, not just *look*.

---

## The prerequisite we must solve first

None of this is trustworthy unless the underlying research is **genuinely thorough and complete — no matter how long the agents take to run.** Speed is not the constraint; completeness is.

And we have **not yet identified all the sources of trend information.** Known gaps already include:
- **YouTube** — transcripts of videos discussing banking/tech trends.
- **Blogs** and other informal-but-leading commentary.
- Almost certainly more we haven't catalogued.

So before the storytelling and the globe: **fix sourcing** — make trend-source discovery a first-class, deliberately exhaustive part of the system (distinct from the footprint entity/tender census).

---

## What the reader ultimately gets

1. **"What's going on in banking?"** — the trends, near- and long-term, at world scale.
2. **Zoom** from world to region to footprint, at the right level of detail for each.
3. **Depth where it matters** — real tenders/disclosures/opportunities in the footprint.
4. **Connections and reasoning** — hypotheses and suggestions drawn from the whole network of data, so the reader sees not just *what* is happening but *what it means for us* and *what to do next.*
