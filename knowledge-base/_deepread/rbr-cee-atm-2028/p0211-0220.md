### p.215 — Section 2 (cont.): Banking structure text
578 financial institutions in Poland end-2022: 30 commercial banks, 511 co-operative banks, 37 credit institutions/branches (per KNF/SKOK). Number of commercial banks remained unchanged 2021 vs 2022; co-operative banks declined by 19 institutions; one new commercial bank started (Velobank, from Volkswagen Bank, which withdrew from Polish market Dec 2017 / re-entered Sept 2022). Poland co-operative banks belong to one of two IPS (Institutional Protection Schemes): BPS IPS and SGB IPS, with BPS IPS the larger (comprising 41% of co-op banks). 4% of co-op institutions operate independently of the IPS groups. Velobank, Bank Millennium acquired Nordea/SGB IPS and Bank Pełnomocnik examples noted; restructuring after CDO in 2021. VeloBank (was Getin Noble) new bank/management since Nov 2022. The three largest banks by branches PKO BP (PLN 471bn assets / USD 621bn), Bank Pekao (PLN 281bn / USD 65bn), Santander (PLN 264bn / USD 63bn). ING Bank Śląski PLN 218bn / USD 65bn (figures approximate from text). Total assets PLN 2.10 trillion (USD 48bn implied). Increase in assets in 2022.

### p.216 — Section 2 (cont.): branch-network changes text (sparse page)
Branch networks of three smallest banks — KBS, Citi Handlowy and BGK — remained unchanged. Only three banks increased branch numbers: SGB IPS opened 16 new outlets; Crédit Agricole grew by 41; VeloBank grew by 46. Largest share of bank branches in Poland 2022 belonged to BPS IPS at 15% of outlets (unchanged from 2021). PKO BP had the second-largest branch network; SGB IPS third. No banks other than these three had more than 1,000 bank outlets each; PKO BP was the only individual bank with such a large number of branches.

### p.217 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (3.1 ATM networks and sharing)
History/text page. First ATM in Poland installed 1987; growth from mid-1990s. By 2001 ~1,750 ATMs (approx). Euronet and Planet Cash named as the two major IADs. Euronet joined the market ~1998; reached ~4,470 sites by 2010 narrative. In 2014 the installed base peaked, then began to decline. IADs (Euronet, Planet Cash) and a few banks began to consolidate / remove terminals. In 2005 manufacturer Diebold entered the Polish ATM market as an IAD, branded Cash4You, after acquiring a share of Euronet; Cash4You terminals later passed to Planet Cash. IADs Euronet and CardCard (sic) operated in the market; bank ATM deployment in 2017/2018 noted respectively. Partnership/preferred-charge agreements among Euronet, Planet Cash and the partner banks. With ATM fees on the rise, customers have benefited from frequent ATM use. Due to rapid expansion of IADs, as well as the many bank mergers in the 2010s, the Polish ATM market is highly concentrated, with the top five deployers representing 91% of the installed base. The largest ATM deployment in 2022 added ~100 net terminals from the market. Bank Pekao removed the largest number of terminals (-147); Bank Polska Kasa Opieki and SGB IPS were the only other deployers with over 1,000 terminals installed. ATM market contracted to shrink in 2022, with over 100 terminals removed from the market. Bank Pekao removed the largest number of terminals (-147). Four banks saw their ATM expansion increase in 2022, with Planet Cash growing its fleet by as many as 125 of its outlets. PKO BP also saw a significant fall in branch numbers, having shut down ~10% of its outlets.
