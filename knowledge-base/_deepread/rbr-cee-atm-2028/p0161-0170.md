Body text: Hungarian installed base contracted in 2022 to 4,780 ATMs deployed across the country at end of year — a contraction of 3% from end-2021, a slightly bigger decrease than the ~2% between 2020 and 2021. ### p.166 — Banking structure continued (Hungary Page 3)
Body text only (no figure):
- TakarékBank was the second largest bank by branch numbers in 2022 after shuttering over half its branches: network of 353 branches across Hungary, accounting for 23% of all bank branches.
- OTP Bank was the second largest bank by [overall] measure, operating across 351 branches (23% of Hungary's branch network). OTP preceded by state-owned National Savings Bank, established 1949. Now part of wider OTP Group, present in 11 countries, acquired 23 banks since early 2000s. OTP acquired Uzbekistan's Ipoteka Bank on 12 December 2022 and completed acquisition of Slovenia's Nova KBM on 6 February 2023.
- K&H Bank third largest by branch network: 185 branches, or 12% of Hungary's network. Founded by the Hungarian state in 1987; later privatised, now owned by Belgian KBC Group. Renovated/set up 11 branches in H1 2022.
- MKB Bank fourth largest; branch network expanded by 71 branches in 2022 as a result of the merger with former Budapest Bank.
- Erste Bank had the fifth largest branch network with 97 branches in 2022. Owned by Austrian parent Erste Group. Completed acquisition of Commerzbank Zrt (Hungarian subsidiary of Germany's Commerzbank) on 30 November 2022; customer migration followed from 5 December.
- Sopron Bank operates mostly in western Hungary and was previously part of Austria's GRAWE Banking Group; became subsidiary of Hungarian-owned Magföll Bank in February 2022 following the approval of the central bank. The business of Sopron Bank were taken over by Magföll Bank and renamed.
- In March 2022, MNB revoked the licence of Russian-owned Sberbank in Hungary, owing to the bank's significant liquidity problems following international sanctions against Russia and the insolvency of Sberbank. MKB Bank took over Sberbank's credit portfolio.

### p.167 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services / 3.1 ATM networks and sharing (Hungary Page 4)
Body text only (no figure):
- OTP Bank installed the first ATM in Hungary in 1990, which operated offline. Other banks then began piloting in-house ATMs, with K&H Bank installing its first terminal in 1993. ATMs that accepted internationally branded cards had emerged by end of 1993.
- A sharing network launched in 1995; initially named Giro Bankkártya Rt (GBC), rebranded as SIA Central Europe in 2011 by SIA Group (its Italian parent).
- Until 1997, SIA Central Europe ran the ATMs of Erste Bank and was responsible for its clearing and settlement system until 2009; transactions at Erste Bank's ATMs now routed directly through Mastercard and Visa. In 2010 TakarékBank moved its ATMs from SIA Central Europe to First Data. UniCredit Bank did the same in 2015, leading number of ATMs in SIA network to fall sharply. In 2018, SIA Group bought First Data's CEE processing business. SIA managed ATMs of internationally owned banks K&H Bank, Sberbank and KDB Bank until recent years.
- SIA Central Europe signed an agreement to merge with competitor Nexi in 2021, following Nexi's merger with Nordic payment group Nets, as part of a larger plan for the three companies to become Nexi Group. SIA Central Europe no longer operates as an ATM sharing network; in December 2021 the company installed its first proprietary ATM under the brand SIA Hungary (referred to as SIA in this report); SIA expanded in 2022.
- Most Hungarian ATM deployers have signed bilateral agreements allowing cardholders to use the majority of domestic ATMs; first such agreement between TakarékBank and Erste Bank.
- IAD ATMs account for 16% of terminals in Hungary; largest IAD an ATM provider, Euronet. Euronet acted as an IAD in Hungary from 1994 to 2006; between 2006 and 2013, Euronet did not operate as IAD; from 2013 it returned (re-entered) the ATM business. ATM Point and SIA Hungary entered the IAD business more recently. As part of the ongoing merger between MKB Bank and Takarék Group, which also operates the ATMs of Magyar Bankholding, deploying terminals as a single IAD — Bankholding deploying ~over 800 terminals as a single IAD (text on consolidation).
- In March 2022 MNB revoked the licence of Russian-owned Sberbank in Hungary; the IAD is a subsidiary of payment solutions provider Paycorp; it had 141 ATMs in Hungary at end-2022.

Body text: Hungarian installed base contracted slightly in 2022; total number of ATMs fell from 4,919 (end-2021) to 4,780 (end-2022) — a decline of 139 terminals, or 3%. Decline largely due to merger of MKB Bank and Budapest Bank in 2022, which absorbed the latter's ATMs (the new MKB Bank subsequently rationalising its fleet; TakarékBank also removed 44 ATMs; the bank has been rationalising its ATM fleet since 2020 following the bank's merger with smaller co-operative banks). Body text: ATMs fell in number over the past five years from 5,067 (2018) to 4,780 (2022) — a decline of 6%, decrease of ~6%; the decline reflects fewest reductions in the banks at Magyar Bankholding group (TakarékBank, MKB Bank and Budapest Bank, all fleet reductions). Although bank-deployed ATMs fell in number, the total number of IAD terminals grew in 2022, with ATM Point and SIA Hungary increasing their fleets by 10 machines and 64 machines respectively. IAD ATMs account for an increasingly significant share of the Hungarian market. Such machines represented 12% of the Hungarian installed base at end-2018, a proportion which increased to 16% by end-2022. At the end of 2022 there were an average of 480 ATMs per million people, down from 507 in 2021. ATM density in Hungary was a result of the 3% drop in ATM numbers. The number of ATMs relative to bank branches increased, from 269 ATMs per 100 bank branches at end-2021 to 317 at end-2022. Branch numbers fell at a faster rate than ATM numbers during the year; IAD sector contributed somewhat to this increase; the number of IAD ATMs in Hungary grew by 10% from end-2018 to end-2022. Hungary has a relatively low density of ATMs relative to the size of its economy but remains above the regional average for geographical density of ATMs.

Body text: In 2022, the most common location for ATMs in Hungary was off-site (64% of installed base).
