Body text:
- End-2022 ATMs = 3,183, a fall of ~1% (per text) / -0.6% (per table) between end-2021 and end-2022; minor decrease driven by small fleet rationalisations by most banks. Two IADs both expanded their fleets in 2022.
- ATM density: 564 ATMs per million people and 65 ATMs per 1,000 km².
- 25 banks in the Slovak banking sector, majority foreign-owned; one fewer than end-2021 because Commerzbank ceased Slovak operations in June 2022. Largest commercial bank by assets = SLSP with EUR 24bn (USD 25bn), 190 branches and 748 ATMs at end-2022.
- 68% of Slovak ATMs are off-site (shopping centres, transport hubs); 22% are through-the-wall; 9% inside bank branches (mostly lobby). PIN change available at all ATMs; 86% offer mobile phone top-ups. ATMs offering automated note deposit grew from 19% (end-2021) to 20% (end-2022).
- 2022: 81 million cash withdrawals in Slovakia, +13% on 2021; total value EUR 20bn (USD 21bn), up from EUR 19bn (USD 19bn) in 2021.
- End-2022 manufacturer shares: Diebold Nixdorf largest with 59% of all ATMs, NCR second with 26%; Triton 11% and OKI 4%.
- Forecast: between end-2022 and end-2028 the Slovak ATM base is forecast to grow steadily, to surpass 3,300 ATMs by end of forecast period; IAD sector to account for much of the growth as bank ATM numbers set to decrease despite new installations by smaller banks.

### p.357 — Slovakia Section 2 (continued) (Slovakia Page 3)
Body text only:
- Most private national banks foreign-owned (entities/holding groups in Austria, Italy, Czech Republic, Belgium). Foreign-owned banks widespread after privatisation of state-owned entities in the 1990s. End-2022: three foreign-owned banks controlled 60% of all banking assets — SLSP, Tatra Banka, VUB; former two owned by Austrian financial groups, VUB part of Italian Intesa Sanpaolo group.
- End-2022 Slovak banking assets totalled EUR 114bn (USD 122bn), +7% vs EUR 107bn (USD 121bn) prior year.
- SLSP largest commercial bank by assets and branches: EUR 24bn (USD 25bn) assets, 190 branches; founded 1825, gained universal banking licence 1990; privatised 2000, became member of Austrian Erste Bank Group in 2001.
- VUB second largest by assets at end-2022: EUR 23bn (USD 25bn), 155 branches; followed by Tatra Banka EUR 22bn (USD 23bn) and 72 branches. VUB was state-owned before 2001 privatisation/acquisition by Intesa Sanpaolo (now HQ Italy). Tatra Banka founded 1991 as first private bank in Slovakia, part of Austria's Raiffeisen Bank International Group; Raiffeisen banka also joined Slovak market under own brand in 2012 to attract new customers. Raiffeisen Group previously owned Zuno Bank (opened 2012 as internet bank, closed 2017).
- J&T Group present in Slovak banking, HQ Czech Republic; J&T Finance Group owns both 365.bank (formerly Poštová banka) and J&T Banka. Since 2020 J&T Finance Group rebranding Poštová banka as 365.bank; in 2021 most former Poštová banka branches renamed 365.bank (except inside Slovak Post offices where Poštová banka name retained for basic services).
- Slovenská záručná a rozvojová banka: established 1991, Slovakia's only state-owned bank at end-2022, owned by Ministry of Finance; focuses on SME development. Three building societies present at end-2022: Wüstenrot stavebná sporiteľňa, Prvá stavebná sporiteľňa, and ČSOB stavebná sporiteľňa (shares branches with ČSOB as sister company).
- 2022: numerous Slovak banks assisted Ukrainian refugees — portable ATMs at border crossings and waived ATM withdrawal fees for customers with Ukrainian bank cards.

### p.358 — Slovakia Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (3.1) (Slovakia Page 4)
Body text only:
- Five largest ATM deployers accounted for 78% of all ATM installations at end-2022. SLSP installed the first ATM in Slovakia (Bratislava, 1988) prior to dissolution of Czechoslovakia.
- End-2022: no ATM networks present in Slovakia; all ATMs owned directly by deployers. A network operated 1994-2017, originally the Authorisation Centre of the Slovak Republic, through which some ATMs were shared; founded by a group of banks including VUB and Tatra Banka; by 1997 it included all ATMs except SLSP's own fleet.
- In 1997 NBS transferred transaction authorisations from the Authorisation Centre to the Bank Card Association of the Slovak Republic to make the two networks interoperable; later renamed Transacty Slovakia, acquired first by EuroProcessing International in 2003, then by First Data in 2005. Early 2000s: a group of banks set up own in-house processing centres, cutting First Data's market share. In 2010 VUB transferred management of its ATM fleet to Intesa Sanpaolo (ISP Card), its parent company.
- Late 2018: SIA Central Europe acquired the card-processing business of First Data in central and eastern Europe, operating as front-end switch for four Slovak banks (Prima Banka, Poštová banka, UniCredit, OTP Banka) and providing clearing/settlement services to them. Since then, no shared ATM network in Slovakia.
- Slovakia's four largest banks by assets (SLSP, VUB, Tatra Banka, ČSOB) all process own transactions and clear/settle via in-house centres or parent banks. ČSOB ATMs connected to Italy's SiNSYS processing centre; VUB's transactions via parent Intesa Sanpaolo. SLSP and Tatra Banka both process in-house; Tatra Banka also deploys ATMs on behalf of parent Raiffeisen, including in latter's branches.
- Interchange/fees: on-us transactions typically charged EUR 0.20 (USD 0.23); not-on-us EUR 2.50 (USD 2.96).
- Only two IADs in Slovak market at end-2022 = ATM Point and Euronet, combined 12% of installed base. Both entered the market in 2017, marking return of IADs to Slovakia; Euronet previously deployed ATMs until 2008, First Data active 2009-2011. Surcharging not permitted in Slovakia, but deployers can charge for international withdrawals.

Body: Between end-2021 and end-2022 the Slovak installed base declined 0.6%, only the third time the market has declined in its history; due to banks rationalising fleets as a cost-cutting measure, plus a notable shift to digital/app-based solutions from changing consumer behaviour after COVID-19. Tatra Banka was one of the only bank deployers to increase ATM numbers in 2022, adding eight new installations as part of a strategy to install ATMs at selected high-traffic locations. VUB removed 21 ATMs despite branch numbers growing, part of a nationwide cost-saving/digitalisation strategy (customers moving to app and internet banking; lower cash demand favouring card/mobile payments). Various Slovak banks assisted Ukrainian refugees by placing portable ATMs at border crossings; banks such as SLSP abolished ATM withdrawal fees for customers with Ukrainian bank cards.
