# Pages 111-120

Country chapter: **CROATIA** (RBR / NCR Atleos — CEE ATM Market and Forecasts to 2028). Pages 111-120 = Croatia pages 2-11. Currency context: pre-euro figures in HRK/kuna; Croatia adopted the euro on 01/01/2023.

### p.111 — Croatia: market context (prose, Croatia p.2)
- ATM functionality currently limited, but banks increasingly deploying automated deposit ATMs. 18% of bank ATMs accepted note deposit at end-2022; up three percentage points from the previous year, but most deposits continue to be made at the branch counter. NFC withdrawal and cardless cash withdrawal by code have been available since 2017, mostly at the terminals of large banks. Dynamic currency conversion (DCC) typically offered at the ATMs of IADs and large banks.
- Anti-skimming devices a feature of all Croatian ATMs, while built-in or integrated security cameras can be found at 36% of ATMs. Note degradation becoming an increasingly common security feature, with legislation passed in 2021 requiring all old-style ATMs to install the feature by May 2024.
- Entire installed base comes from two ATM manufacturers, with Diebold Nixdorf making up over two-thirds of ATMs in service at end-2022; NCR accounting for the remainder.
- In 2022, number of ATM cash withdrawals increased by 5% to reach 98 million (figure remains below the pre-pandemic level, with many cardholders continuing to steer away from cash or making larger withdrawals than usual per ATM visit). Value of withdrawals increased by 7% to reach EUR 12 billion (USD 13 billion), slightly exceeding the pre-pandemic level as the Croatian economy rebounded and tourism recovered to a certain extent. Number and value of withdrawals expected to continue to increase as tourism industry grows.
- Light of regulatory changes / cash payment uptake: installed base expected to contract over the forecast period as the high density of ATMs becomes increasingly unsustainable. Equipping all old-style ATMs with note-degradation solution will take considerable investment from deployers. Since the euro adoption took place on 01/01/2023, visitors from the Eurozone are no longer needing DCC, which could pose a challenge to IADs as they previously gained a significant proportion of their revenue from this offering. Although banks expected to deploy more deposit ATMs in an effort to steer cash transactions away from the branch counter, the overall number of bank terminals is still expected to decline over the forecast period.

### p.113 — Section 2 (cont.): banking structure prose (Croatia p.4)
- Third-largest bank by assets: Erste Bank, with EUR 13 billion (USD 14 billion) at end-2022, an increase of 15% from a year earlier. It is owned by Erste Group Bank of Austria, which entered the Croatian market in the late 1990s.
- With EUR 8 billion (USD 8 billion) in total assets, OTP Banka was the fourth-largest bank by this measure at end-2022, up 9% from end-2021. OTP Banka acquired Splitska Banka from Société Générale in 2017.
- Raiffeisenbank, owned by an Austrian banking group, is the fifth-largest bank by assets. At end-2022, the bank had total assets of EUR 7 billion (USD 7 billion), representing an increase of 13% compared to the previous year.
- At end-2022, the total assets of the five largest banks accounted for 96%, up eight percentage points from a year earlier.
- Croatia Banka (formerly Small Business Bank, which was acquired by the state after running into financial difficulties in 1999) was put on sale in 2020. Future ownership of the bank remains unclear, with Croatian media reporting that four bids were made for the bank but agreements between the buyers were stalled.
- Sberbank entered the Croatian market in 2012, after buying Volksbank International, and the assets of the Croatian subsidiary totalled HRK 11 billion (USD 2 billion) by end-2020. In November 2021, Russia's Sberbank announced it was selling its subsidiaries in five CEE countries, including Croatia, to focus on its key markets. The subsidiaries were initially due to be sold to companies belonging to PPF Group, founded by Serbian entrepreneur Miodrag Kostić. However, following the European Central Bank's warning that Sberbank Croatia was likely to fail due to liquidity issues caused by Russia's invasion of Ukraine, it was sold to Croatian state-owned bank HPB in March 2022. The bank was renamed Nova Hrvatska Banka.
- Bank branches in Croatia have been declining in number since 2010, owing to consolidation in the sector and the growing use of self-service for simpler transactions. In 2022, the number of branches fell to 2% (or 18 units), to 862. Half of the five largest banks closed branches in 2022, with PBZ shutting down the largest number — seven outlets. This left the bank with 150 branches at end-2022, although still the largest by number. The country is most frequently visited by tourists. Based on central bank data, July was the peak month for ATM numbers in 2022, with 6,413 terminals in operation.
- In order to cut costs and deploy ATMs in more value-adding roles, banks are encouraging migration of simpler transactions from branches to self-service channels. Since 2018, Erste Bank deploys 24-hour zones at a number of its branches; allowing 24-hour access to ATMs and other self-service services. Raiffeisenbank has similarly been remodelling its branches with a focus on digitisation, advisory services and customer experience, opening 13 redesigned branches on 1st January 2022. Addiko Bank introduced a "virtual branch" in 2019, enabling customers to open a current account and secure a loan of up to a certain amount without calling a physical branch.

### p.114 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services — Figure 3.1 Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022 (Croatia p.5)
- 3.1 ATM networks and sharing: Croatia's first ATM was deployed by Rijecka Banka (later acquired by Erste Bank) in 1984; Zaba was the first bank to instal terminals with deposit functionality in 1994.
- After recognising the disadvantages of operating separate networks, member banks of the Croatian Banking Association decided to develop a single, shared network. In 1994, 23 banks came together to form Mudu Bankarske Usluge (MBU), which set up the first ATM network and acquired the first sharing infrastructure of the MBNET network. Before MBU could go live the same year, Zaba and OTP Banka had left over fees.
- MBU started expanding into other CEE markets in 2006, before being acquired by Erste Group shortly afterwards. MBU was renamed Erste Group Card Processor (EGCP) in 2014; the ATM network continues to operate under the MBNET brand. EGCP takes part in ATM and EFTPOS terminal management and monitoring, transaction processing and switching, card issuing, and mobile payment and e-commerce solutions.
- **Figure 3.1: MBNET sharing-agreement members (2018-2022, CAGR):** 1,085 -> 1,054 -> 1,036 -> 1,040 -> 989; CAGR -2.3% (Sources: Croatian ATM deployers and RBR analysis)
- MBNET had 12 ATM-deploying members in Croatia at end-2022, one less than a year earlier (due to J&T Banka leaving the network). Customers of these 12 banks can withdraw cash at other members' ATMs for free. At end-2022, the membership had a second-largest member with 75 ATMs in its fleet at end-2022.
- Two Croatian banks are members of MBNET despite not deploying ATMs (Croatia Banka, which joined in 2016, is the newest member, although its 11 ATMs went out of service at end-2022; Banka Kovanica, which is also a member of MBNET, shut down its four ATMs in 1st August 2022). The network also has members of two debit card schemes.
- Since 2016, HPB has been deploying ATMs in the Domestic Sharing network. SIA bought a part of First Data (old Italy 2016) and SIA bought a part of First Data's business. These ATMs offer DCC and are mostly located on the Adriatic coast, which is frequently visited by tourists. SIA owns ATM hardware, while HPB has an acquiring licence for the terminals. Ukraine's UPC acts as a processor for Raiffeisenbank in several European markets, including Croatia.

- Prose: Several IADs have entered the market in recent years, with six deploying ATMs at end-2022. The IAD sector saw significant expansion until 2021, with many merchants keen to earn additional revenue by renting space for ATMs. IADs, which gain a large share of their revenue from offering DCC to tourists, were drawn to Croatia due to its increasing popularity as a holiday destination; however, the adoption of the euro is expected to make the business case for IAD ATMs more challenging.

### p.116 — Section 3 (cont.): IAD landscape, services prose (Croatia p.7)
- Euronet was the first IAD in Croatia, having entered the market in 1997. It went on to sell its fleet to Raiffeisenbank in 1999, before re-entering in 2012. It has become the largest ATM deployer in the country, with over 400 terminals in operation at end-2022.
- UK-based IAD NoteMachine has been in the Croatian market since 2017 and had 156 ATMs installed at end-2022, although only 9% were in operation due to winterisation. Its ATMs are mostly found at convenience stores, cafes, camp sites and bus stations that are popular among tourists.
- NoteMachine exited the Croatian market in January 2023, and its assets were acquired by Poland's PayTm in March 2023.
- Three IADs entered the market in 2018: Croatian companies Auro Domus and PayOut, as well as Polish ITCARD, which deploys ATMs under the Planet Cash brand.
- Auro Domus, the second-largest IAD in Croatia, is a gold and silver retailer and wholesaler that deploys ATMs in its stores and other locations. It had 120 ATMs at end-2022, although only 17% were in service. PayOut had 76 ATMs at end-2022, and only 9% of which were active, unchanged from a year earlier.
- Planet Cash markets itself as the only IAD in Croatia oriented towards ATM deployment. At end-2022, it had 101 active ATMs, found in locations such as shopping malls and petrol stations.
- In 2019, PayItem began installing ATMs in Croatia under the MoneyGet brand and has since expanded its installed base to about 100 terminals, 14% of which were operational at end-2022. PayItem is owned by Aexto, an electronic-money institution that processes payments for several mid-tier merchants and cash-machine operators.
- Auro Domus launched a separate ATM business under the MoneyGet brand in 2019, offering DCC and ATM deployment with a focus on tourist locations. It is one of several IADs that have a strong presence in the Adriatic coast region and the tourist routes that connect it.
- The other IADs are smaller, with a focus on tourist locations.
- In order to instal ATMs in Croatia, IADs are required to partner with a bank. Euronet partners with Raiffeisenbank; MoneyGet partners with Zaba; and NoteMachine, Planet Cash, Auro Domus and PayOut partner with PBZ.
- Due to complaints from local residents about the high number of ATMs in towns popular among tourists, local authorities have been taking action to limit their numbers. In 2019, Dubrovnik banned the installation of ATMs at building facades in the historic city centre. Many cities along the Adriatic coast, which is frequently visited by tourists, have also taken action to limit ATM installations. Although ATM installations have similar restrictions, deployers may take a different approach to limit such installations.

- Prose: While the total installed base stood at 6,423 terminals at end-2022, only 4,754 ATMs were in service at the time due to winterisation. The installed base has contracted slightly over the past couple of years, following double-digit growth between end-2016 and end-2019, when the IAD sector was expanding quickly.
- The number of ATMs that were in service at the end of each year is shown in Figure 4.1. The number dropped in 2020 due to increased winterisation of ATMs during the pandemic, and has been declining steadily since then. The number of ATMs in service typically peaks in the third quarter, when the country is most frequently visited by tourists. This was followed by two years of growth, before the number of bank ATMs began declining again in 2019. In 2022, it declined by 54 terminals.
- Almost all banks either reduced their ATM fleet in 2022 or kept it stable, with OTP Banka removing the largest number of terminals. HPB, Addiko Bank and KentBank were the only banks that saw their ATM fleet increase in 2022.

- Prose: At end-2022, 78% of the ATMs in service were located away from bank branches. The proportion of off-site ATMs is even higher in the summer, when IAD ATMs are typically in full service. As IADs only deploy ATMs off-site, the share of off-site ATMs is highest among the IADs, but the expansion of the IAD sector and bank branch closures have driven the proportion of off-site ATMs up. Off-site ATMs enable banks to have a physical presence without the cost of operating a branch. The six largest bank deployers each had between 70% and 90% of their ATMs off-site. Smaller banks, on the other hand, tend to deploy most of their ATMs at branches, and some of them have no off-site terminals at all. There was an average of 1.3 ATMs per branch at end-2022, unchanged from end-2021. 73% of branch ATMs were installed through the exterior wall of the branch (TTW) at end-2022, up one percentage point from the previous year. While both ATMs located TTW and off-site are accessible 24/7, lobbies provide more security, as well as shelter from adverse weather conditions. As such, the lobby is considered a particularly convenient location for deposit ATMs. There are only four ATMs located in halls. There were four drive-through ATMs in Croatia at end-2022, with Erste Bank deploying three of them.
