Body text: ATM installed base in Romania to increase at an average of less than 1% each year between 2023 and 2028, to reach ~10,150 by end of 2028. ### p.280 — Russia, Section 1: Market overview — Figure 1.1 Key metrics (Russia Page 1)

Body discussion: Russia's installed base contracted by 5% in 2022, falling to a total of 114,343 ATMs. This marked the third consecutive year of decline for the Russian ATM market — a trend primarily driven by weakening cash usage in the country and a reduced business case for ATM deployment. The decrease in 2022 was worsened by Russia's invasion of Ukraine and the consequent economic and financial environment of international sanctions, implemented against the country as a response to its war in Ukraine. Major western ATM manufacturers have suspended operations in Russia, cutting off the country from a key source of ATM supplies and complicating the process of replacements and new installations. Branch numbers also declined, totalling 28,947 by end of 2022; a decline of 6% from the previous year. As more services are shifted to remote and digital forms of banking, banks are increasingly closing outlets with lower levels of foot traffic countrywide. As the country's branch numbers fell, the proportion of ATMs located off-site grew to 61% (from 60% the previous year). These are typically found in high-footfall public locations or attached to company premises as part of salary card projects. Banks are adding value to their reduced ATM fleets by enhancing the functionality of their machines. Banking services like bill payments and credit transfers are widely available across the installed base, while some banks are rolling out more alternative services, including the purchase of financial products, or, more rarely, grocery and taxi orders. The proportion of ATMs accepting note deposits is also growing, with 79% of ATMs featuring this capability at end-2022, of which 89% also recycled notes. Cash withdrawal numbers fell by 8%, totalling 2.1 billion in 2022. New customer habits, ingrained during the COVID-19 pandemic, meant more Russians than ever before performed their everyday spending with cards and mobile wallets, necessitating fewer ATM visits. The value of ATM cash withdrawals in 2022 rose, however, as inflation drove up prices and some consumers panicked by the war took out higher-value cash sums as a contingency measure.
