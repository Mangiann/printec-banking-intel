### p.483 — Estonia, Section 3.3 (cont.) + 3.4 Number and value of cash withdrawals
Body: around 90% of the installed base was located away from bank branches. As banks have closed many branches, the proportion of off-site ATMs has increased steadily in recent years. There were 1.1 branch ATMs present at the end of 2022, up from 1.2 at end-2021. While branches rely less on tellers to provide cash services than before, they also have fewer ATMs, as the overall demand to withdraw and deposit cash has fallen. Most branch ATMs are in lobbies. This location provides access outside branch opening hours as well as security and shelter from adverse weather conditions. Whilst lobbies remain the most common branch location for deployers, the number of ATMs in lobbies decreased by the greatest percentage (almost 20%) when compared to all other locations from end-2021 to end-2022. All branch ATMs of the joint fleet of Nordea and Danske Bank were previously installed through the exterior wall of the bank branch, but Luminor/LHV, which took over this fleet, deploy more terminals in lobbies. Both TTW and hall ATMs are now uncommon in Estonia.

- Annual number of cash withdrawals (million): 2018 = 35; 2019 = 33; 2020 = 27; 2021 = 25; 2022 = 23; CAGR = -9.0%.
- Average cash withdrawals per ATM per month: 2018 = 3,878; 2019 = 3,720; 2020 = 3,139; 2021 = 2,990; 2022 = 3,055; CAGR = -5.8%.
Source: Eesti Pank and RBR analysis.
Body: number of cash withdrawals at Estonian ATMs has steadied in 2022, indicating the easing of the impact of COVID. The pandemic caused the number of withdrawals to plummet by ~18% in 2020 and it accelerated the trend of consumers withdrawing larger sums at once in order to visit ATMs less frequently, which has now become a habit for many consumers.

Body: installed base expected to continue contracting over the forecast period. Similar discussions for a national ATM pool in the early 2010s were unsuccessful as Swedbank chose not to participate in the initiative.

Body: number of commercial banks operating in Georgia increased from 14 to 15 in 2022, with the National Bank of Georgia (NBG) issuing Lithuanian fintech Paysera a licence to establish Paysera Bank in November 2022; it is the country's first digital bank. While BasisBank acquired the Georgian operations of Russia's state-owned VTB Bank during the year, VTB Bank continues to hold a banking licence from NBG. The banking sector in Georgia has seen a number of changes in recent years. Between 2014 and 2020, four banks — Caucasus Development Bank, Progress Bank, Capital Bank and International Bank of Azerbaijan — lost their banking licences. Bank Republic completed its merger with TBC Bank in 2017, and Credo Bank, which had previously been operating as a microfinance organisation, was granted a banking licence in 2017. In 2021, Credo Bank acquired FINCA Bank. NBG is the central bank of Georgia; it supervises the financial sector, seeking to maintain both the stability and the transparency of the financial system, as well as to protect the rights of both consumers and investors. 14 of the country's 15 commercial banks are foreign-owned, primarily including foreign groups have bought stakes in Georgian banks, such as Russia's VTB Bank, France's Société Générale, Ukraine's PrivatBank and China's Huating Group. Turkey's IsBank and Azerbaijan's PASHA Bank began operations in 2012 and 2013 respectively. European Financial Group, based in the Netherlands, acquired a 75% stake in Liberty Bank in October 2017. Russian state-owned VTB Bank transferred its consumer loans and deposit portfolio to BasisBank (owned by the Chinese Huating Group) in February 2022, following the imposition of sanctions on the bank after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Although the COVID-19 pandemic and the associated economic downturn had a negative impact on the economy during 2020, the Georgian banking sector has since recovered well. Commercial bank assets totalled GEL 70 billion (USD 26 billion) end of end-2022, up 16% on the previous year. By some measure, the largest banks by assets are TBC Bank, which held EUR 29 billion (USD 10.7 billion) end-2022, and Bank of Georgia, with EUR 28.9 billion (USD 10.7 billion). Together, these two banks accounted for 82% of the sector's total assets. (Georgia: Page 1.)
