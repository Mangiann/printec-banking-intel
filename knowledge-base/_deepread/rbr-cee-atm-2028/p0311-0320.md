### p.320 (Russia p.41) — Section 7: Market forecasts — 7.1 Forecast installed base — Figure 7.1: Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028
Forecast table (CAGR over 2023-2028 period):
- ATMs (installed base): 110,000 (2023); 108,000 (2024); 106,000 (2025); 105,000 (2026); 104,000 (2027); 103,000 (2028); CAGR -1.3%.
- Absolute growth: -4,343 (2023); -2,000 (2024); -2,000 (2025); -1,000 (2026); -1,000 (2027); -1,000 (2028); N/A.
- Percentage growth: -3.8% (2023); -1.8% (2024); -1.9% (2025); -0.9% (2026); -1.0% (2027); -1.0% (2028); N/A.
- ATMs per million people: 760 (2023); 746 (2024); 732 (2025); 726 (2026); 719 (2027); 712 (2028); CAGR -1.3%.
Text: The Russian installed base is forecast to contract at an average annual rate of ~1% between 2023 and 2028, falling to 103,000 by end of the period — equal to a density of 712 ATMs per million people (based on 2022 population figures), down from 790 at end-2022.
The Russian ATM market is adjusting to conditions triggered by the conflict with Ukraine, including international sanctions on its banking sector and a more limited import environment. Deployers have been cut off from supplies of western-made ATMs (which accounted for the majority of the installed base), raising questions about replacements and new installations; sourcing ATMs elsewhere has become urgent.
Owing to its existing Hyosung Innovue-focused strategy, Sberbank was able to ramp up removals of other (western) ATMs in 2022 and reduce reliance on western manufacturers; it is likely to keep consolidating its fleet in the early part of the forecast period; as the biggest deployer by far, its activities will drive the market direction. Other smaller banks likely to take a more cautious approach and make smaller-scale reductions while availability of replacements remains in doubt.
Import-substitution / industrialisation policy was much discussed in Russia in 2022 after the sanctions; CBRF advised ATM deployers to pursue hardware from the Asian market in the immediate aftermath; the banking-automation industry has been abuzz with talk of the potential of Russian-made ATMs that could eventually supply the majority of the market. By end-2022, several existing Russian firms operating as ATM resellers and SAT manufacturers were testing their own ATM prototypes, with major deployers reportedly interested in rolling these out starting in 2023.
