### p.121 — Croatia, §4.4 Functionality and hardware features (Croatia: Page 12)
- Highest number of installed ATMs in Croatia is in Split-Dalmatia County, a popular tourist destination on the Adriatic coast. In August 2022 the region had ~1,160 ATMs in service, making up 18% of the country total. It surpassed the city of Zagreb (the country's capital) for highest number of ATMs in 2019. Other ATM-heavy counties: Primorje-Gorski Kotar, Istria, Osijek-Baranja, Vukovar-Srijem, Krapina-Zagorje, Koprivnica-Krizevci and Bjelovar-Bilogora.
- **Figure 4.4: ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022** (source: Croatian ATM deployers and RBR analysis). Table of "Type of deposit" by ATMs vs SATs:
  - Automated note deposit: ATMs 840, SATs 63
  - of which: note recycling: ATMs 110, SATs N/A
  - Automated cheque deposit: ATMs 0, SATs 0
  - Total: ATMs 840, SATs 63
- PIN change is available at all bank ATMs and some SATs (Self-service Terminals). Most bank ATMs and some IAD terminals offered mobile phone top-up until recently, when a new regulation required vending machines and ATMs to print a receipt with each operation — banks/IADs found it uneconomical to upgrade their ATMs for printing the required receipt, so most have stopped offering this from 1 January 2021.
- Banks increasingly deploying automated-deposit ATMs. Approx 20% of bank ATMs accepted note deposit at end-2022, with the proportion as high as 40% for PBZ. All other large banks also deploy automated deposit ATMs. At Raiffeisenbank's deposit ATMs ~20% recycling note deposits at end-2022, unchanged from a year earlier.
- ~840 ATMs accepted note deposit at end-2022, of which ~110 (13%) were recycling ATMs.
- Approx two thirds (66%) of all ATMs accepting cash deposits were located at bank branches (~67% of recycling and ~63% of all deposit terminals), and ~13% (DCC-type figure) at off-premise. Approx 5% of the bank's own application/network used at the branch counter.
- ATMs made up ~90% of cash withdrawals in 2022, up from ~89% in 2021 and ~87% in 2020. ATM cash withdrawals accounted for ~60% of the value of all cash withdrawals from ATMs at the branch counter in 2022.

### p.122 — Croatia, §4.4 continued: NFC / contactless & security (Croatia: Page 13)
- In 2017, Erste Bank introduced contactless NFC cash withdrawal at some of its ATMs in cooperation with Avenco, making it the first bank in Croatia to offer this service. By end-2022, NFC withdrawal was available at 87% of its fleet, up seven percentage points from a year earlier. Zaba rolled out contactless including NFC withdrawal without a card on a mobile phone at some of its ATMs in 2018. PBZ, Raiffeisenbank and HPB have also started offering NFC withdrawal recently. Although this feature is mostly limited to the terminals of large banks, smaller-deployer Podravska Banka replaced all its ATMs with new terminals in August 2022, equipped with NFC and contactless capability, the bank using a card slot.
- HPB became the first bank to offer cardless cash withdrawal at its ATMs in 2017, allowing customers to enter a code from the bank's mobile application; Via HPB's eCash service, it is also possible to transfer money to other people, with the recipient receiving a code and an SMS that allows them to withdraw cash at one of the bank's ATMs.
- Functionality of IAD ATMs tends to be more limited than bank terminals. Planet Cash, however, can deploy ATMs with advanced features such as recycling deposit at its ATMs in 2017, allowing customers of interested businesses.
- ATMs deployed by IADs or large banks typically offer DCC, which in the case of HPB this service is limited to the terminal it deploys in partnership with SA. Among smaller banks, IKB has introduced DCC. Among large banks, DCC support is increasing.
- ATMs, including those deployed by OTP Banka, also offer foreign-exchange rate information.
- Anti-skimming devices are installed at all Croatian ATMs, either built-in or integrated security cameras can be found at 36% of machines. All ATMs of PBZ and HPB have security cameras, while around 40% of OTP Banka ATMs, and the bank's fleet was from Diebold Nixdorf at end-2022, up from 57% at end-2021 and 56% at end-2020.
- Until recently, note degradation had been a less common security, adopted by only a small number of deployers. For example, OTP Banka has the feature at 11 ATMs, while Agram Banka and KentBank has it across their entire fleet. In May 2021, the central bank introduced a directive under the Protection of Monetary Institutions introduced a requirement to install all-time ATMs with a note-degradation solution — a measure intended to reduce the number of crimes to attack ATMs for cash. Attacks that involve explosives or gas can lead to short-term damaging the machine and the cash they hold. ATMs equipped with a note-degradation solution will protect the cash, deterring attacks. As well as 24/7 video surveillance and anti-burglary systems that notify of attempts to enter the safe.

### p.124 — Croatia, §4.5 continued: Value of cash withdrawals (Croatia: Page 15)
- Cash withdrawals can also be made in the form of cashback, although this is rare. Customers of OTP Banka and Nova Hrvatska Banka can withdraw cash from Tisak kiosks and Tisak Media stores for a fee of EUR 0.66, while customers of other banks are required to pay a non-network ATM fee. HPB's customers can withdraw cash at POS terminals belonging to Croatian Post and Fina.
- Just over three quarters of all ATM transactions were cash withdrawals in 2022. The share of withdrawals has increased in recent years as IADs, which permit fewer other transaction types, account for a growing share of the installed base.

  - Cash withdrawal value as a proportion of GDP: 2018 = 20.0%; 2019 = 19.9%; 2020 = 20.7%; 2021 = 19.3%; 2022 = 17.8%; CAGR = -2.8%.
- In 2022, the value of cash withdrawals increased by 7% to EUR 12 billion (USD 13 billion), surpassing the pre-pandemic level. In 2020, the value of withdrawals declined by 3% as COVID-19 drove more people to replace cash with digital payment methods. Consumers increasingly shopped online as a result of lockdowns, or paid via card in store to avoid handling cash for hygiene reasons.
- The average withdrawal value increased by 2% between 2021 and 2022, reaching EUR 123 (USD 129). Even when discounting inflation, the average has been increasing in recent years. Customers are increasingly withdrawing larger amounts at ATMs instead of at the branch counter, resulting in an increase in the average ATM withdrawal value. The growth of the average value of a cash withdrawal was extended to the pandemic, as people withdrew larger sums for this reason. The average value of a cash withdrawal is expected to grow by approximately 3.2% year-on-year between 2023 and 2028.

- In 2022, the majority of Croatian ATMs were supplied by Diebold Nixdorf, which accounted for 69% of the terminals in service at end-2022. NCR accounted for the remaining 31%, although its share of the total installed base is slightly higher. This is because most IAD ATMs and a high proportion of these are out of service for winter months.
- NCR's end-year market share has fallen since 2020, largely because IADs winterised more terminals than usual during the pandemic. Also, Erste Bank relies more on Diebold Nixdorf ATMs: the latter's 62% of the bank's fleet was from Diebold Nixdorf at end-2022, up from 57% at end-2021 and 56% at end-2020.
- All ATMs in the fleets of Zaba, HPB and MoneyGet are supplied by Diebold Nixdorf. MoneyGet's deployer Payten partners with this manufacturer; also facilitating the delivery of Diebold Nixdorf ATMs to other deployers in the market. The most commonly requested Diebold Nixdorf models are ProCash 2050 and CINEO 2550. Raiffeisenbank deploys Diebold Nixdorf recycling ATMs, including models CINEO 4060 and CINEO 4560, which are delivered to the bank and installed in cooperation with Payten.
- Some deployers add a multi-vendor approach. IAD NoteMachine, for example, had nearly 60% of its ATMs from Diebold Nixdorf and end 2022, while the rest were from NCR.
- Jadranska Banka previously had several of its ATMs supplied by Swedish manufacturer BANQIT, but none remain following the bank's merger into HPB in 2019.

### p.126 — Croatia, §6 Market forecasts; §6.1 Forecast installed base (Croatia: Page 17)
- **Figure 6.1: Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028** (source: RBR analysis). Table:

  - Absolute growth: 2023 = -54; 2024 = -50; 2025 = -50; 2026 = -50; 2027 = -50; 2028 = -50.
  - Percentage growth: 2023 = -1.1%; 2024 = -1.1%; 2025 = -1.1%; 2026 = -1.1%; 2027 = -1.1%; 2028 = -1.1%.
  - ATMs per million people: 2023 = 1,166; 2024 = 1,154; 2025 = 1,141; 2026 = 1,129; 2027 = 1,117; 2028 = 1,104; CAGR = -1.1%.
- The number of ATMs in service is forecast to shrink to 4,450 at end-2028. The number of bank ATMs is predicted to continue declining over the forecast period, while the IAD installed base is expected to be more stable. Although some IADs plan to expand their fleets, others may remove terminals or exit the market altogether as conditions are becoming more challenging due to the level of market saturation, new security requirements and the adoption of the euro.
- Legislation was adopted in 2021 that requires all off-site ATMs to be equipped with a note-degradation solution by May 2024. This requires considerable investment as few deployers have used such technology before; the technology, with not all large banks, let alone small ones, expected to roll it out over the forecast period. NFC withdrawal is anticipated to be introduced more widely, although the overall functionality of ATMs is expected to remain limited.
- Croatia's adoption of the euro, which took place on 1 January 2023, is also predicted to have a negative effect on the installed base, at least in the short term. Now that the euro is Croatia's national currency, visitors from the Eurozone no longer require DCC, which had previously been a considerable revenue source, particularly for IADs. However, growth in the number of tourists from outside the Eurozone could help reduce the loss in revenue. IADs may also introduce new ATM services that could appeal to tourists, or build sales, in an attempt to offset this loss.

### p.128 — Croatia, §6.3 Forecast ATM cash withdrawal number and value (Croatia: Page 19)

Largely for this reason, the average value of a cash withdrawal is expected to grow by approximately 3.2% year-on-year between 2023 and 2028.
