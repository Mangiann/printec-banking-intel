### p.46 — Section 3 forecast text (full text page; closing of installed-base discussion)
Body: CEE installed base expected to decline further at a slow rate. Forecast: numbers will, however, remain below levels in 2022 and earlier. Half of the 12 primary CEE markets are expected to see some rise in ATM numbers between 2023-2028; the rest will fall. IADs (independent ATM deployers) are not currently present in Kazakhstan, but several banks there are pursuing an ambitious expansion strategy to increase their share of the market in multiple areas: digital payments, e-commerce; also cash withdrawals and other ATM transactions; expecting large-scale ATM-network additions to their ATM networks in the near future. The government of Kazakhstan is also actively encouraging banks to extend access to banking services as part of financial-inclusion initiatives. Recycling-ATM expectations and a discussion of cash-recycling growth in several markets; the fastest decline forecast in Bulgaria, where the installed base is projected to contract by an average of over 1% of the total each year. Russia is the largest market; expected to fall by 2% over the forecast period; will likely lead numbers in absolute terms.

### p.49 — Section 3: Figure 3.7 chart (number of cash withdrawals)
Body: proportion of ATM shipments to CEE intended to be replacements for obsolete/damaged terminals expected to rise steadily over the forecast period, from 84% in 2023 to 91% in 2028. The share of replaced each year expected to be substantially higher than the number of new installations. Majority of shipments to all primary countries are expected for replacements; replacements expected to account for at least 75% of shipments received over the forecast period in most CEE primary markets. Poland and Russia (both 98%) and Bulgaria and Serbia (both 96%) are expected to see particularly high proportions of replacement shipments; share of replacements in Ukraine and Kazakhstan (77% and 75%) forecast to be lower. In non-primary markets, share of shipments comprised by replacements expected to rise significantly over the forecast period — from 52% in 2023 to 68% in 2028. While much of the ATM growth in non-primary market is now organic and characterised by replacement of new installations, by the end of forecast period an increasing number of ATMs installed during the mid-to-late 2010s will be due to be updated and replaced.

**Figure 3.7: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2028** (dual axis). Left axis = Annual number, million [bars], scale 0-7,500. Right axis = Average number [line], scale 0-2,500. Bars: 2018-2019 highest (above 6,000), 2020 ~5,200, 2021 ~5,000, 2022 ~4,500; forecast bars (2023-2028, lighter) decline gently from ~4,300 down to ~4,000. Average-number line is roughly flat/slightly declining around 1,700-1,750 across the forecast. Note: statistics include primary markets only.
