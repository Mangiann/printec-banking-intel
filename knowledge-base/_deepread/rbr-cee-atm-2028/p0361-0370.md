### p.361 — Slovakia Page 7 — Section 4.2: Density measures
Figure 4.2: Density measures, 2022 — table comparing Slovakia vs Rank vs CEE average:

- ATMs per 100 bank branches: Slovakia 322, Rank 13, CEE 341
- ATMs per 100 bank branches and post offices: Slovakia 131, Rank 14, CEE 147

- ATMs per USD billion GDP: Slovakia 28, Rank 20, CEE 47
Narrative: Slovakia had 564 ATMs per million people at end-2022, down from 588 at end-2021. There were 322 ATMs per 100 bank branches, compared to 307 at end-2021. The density grew due to the 5% decline in branch numbers between end-2021 and end-2022, when ATM numbers declined by almost 1%. Branch numbers fell due to closures by banks as part of an optimisation process, which has been driven by the increasing shift to digital and remote forms of banking, a trend accelerated by COVID-19. Many deployers are also reducing their ATM fleets; this is happening at a slower rate — banks recognise that ATMs can be deployed strategically to fill the gap left behind by branch closures, while being cheaper to install and maintain than a branch. Slovakia had a particularly high density of ATMs per 1,000 km² for the CEE region, at 65 — unchanged from 2021.

Narrative: 68% of ATMs in Slovakia were located off-site at end-2022, an increase on the end-2021 figure. The majority of Slovak ATMs continue to be located off-site; deployers opt for these locations usually due to the profitability potential of the high-footfall areas where they are placed, such as shopping centres. All Slovak ATM deployers, aside from Prima Banka, had the majority of their fleets in off-site locations at end-2022 of all the banks. 363 banks had the highest proportion of its estate placed off-site, accounting for 77% of all its installations. This compares to 69% of SLSP's estate and 62% of Tatra Banka's. The proportion of ATMs located off-site in Slovakia has increased since 2017 when the two IADs entered the market; as all IAD ATMs are off-site by their very nature, their expansion has pushed up the share of off-site as a location overall. The second most common location is through-the-wall (TTW), accounting for 22% of ATMs at end-2022, below end-2021 levels. This location often offers some advantages as a placement for ATMs — they are usually accessible 24/7 but remain within reach of branch resources and staff. TTW ATMs accounted for half of Prima Bank's fleet at end-2022. The share of TTW ATMs has declined from 31% in 2017 due to the decline in bank branch numbers in Slovakia over this period.

### p.363 — Slovakia Page 9 — (cont. locations) + Section 4.4: Functionality and hardware features
Narrative: The remainder of Slovak ATMs are found in bank halls and lobbies, which accounted for 3% and 6% of ATMs at end-2022 respectively. Between end-2021 and end-2022, SLSP maintained its proportion of lobby ATMs at 8%. While the bank further contracted its branch network in 2022, it is strategically developing self-service lobby zones in the branches it retained and installing additional ATMs in these areas. Self-service zones allow banks to move transactions away from the counter; lessen wait times and offer customers extended access to ATMs outside of branch opening hours. ATMs in such zones are often considered more secure than off-site or TTW machines, being easier to service and monitor as they are in close proximity to branch personnel.

Narrative: Automated deposit functionality was available at 20% of ATMs at end-2022 (up from 19% at end-2021). SLSP increased its base of ATMs with this functionality between end-2021 and end-2022 (from 148 to 183). Of all ATMs with automated deposit functionality at end-2022, 79% were note-recycling. Banks offer note recyclers as they reduce cash-in-transit (CIT) costs. Both SLSP and Tatra Banka offer note recyclers, providing a strong incentive for deployers to offer note recycling. Banks' deployment is seen as an attractive option to those deployers embarking on branch rationalisation programmes as it is an effective way of shifting in-branch services to self-service, while also saving on CIT expenses. Both SLSP and Tatra Banka made significant additions to their recycler bases in 2022; with recyclers now accounting for 24% and 62% of their fleets respectively, up from 20% and 59%. All Slovak ATMs offer PIN change as a service. Mobile phone top-ups remain widely offered and are available at 67% of all ATMs, with several statements can be printed at 94% of ATMs, and 71% of their fleets respectively, up from 20% and 59%. In terms of hardware, most Slovak ATMs have integrated security cameras and anti-skimming devices. Tatra Banka ATMs have note degradation technology too.

### p.364 — Slovakia Page 10 — Section 4.5: Number and value of cash withdrawals
Figure 4.5: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022 — bar (annual number, million [left]) + line (average cash withdrawals per ATM per month [right]):
- Annual number of cash withdrawals (million): 2018 98 / 2019 94 / 2020 75 / 2021 72 / 2022 81 / CAGR -4.4%
- Average cash withdrawals per ATM per month: 2018 2,813 / 2019 2,383 / 2020 1,983 / 2021 1,863 / 2022 2,110 / CAGR -6.9%
- Cash withdrawal as a share of total transactions: 2018 75% / 2019 76% / 2020 76% / 2021 76% / 2022 75% / CAGR -1.4%
Narrative: The number of cash withdrawals grew significantly in 2022 but remained below the levels seen prior to the pandemic. This was caused by high levels of inflation and also a post-COVID-19 recovery after two years of lockdowns, some of which saw all non-essential businesses close their premises — this resulted in a reduction in footfall to locations with ATMs, as well as decreased travel and cross-border withdrawals across the country. Cash remains the most widely used payment method in Slovakia, although withdrawals were down in 2022 than in 2019 before the COVID-19 pandemic, which can be explained by already existing uptick in the usage of electronic payment methods during the pandemic. The pandemic has seen an increased tendency among consumers to increased e-commerce spending and card usage, especially contactless. These new habits have lessened consumer demand for cash to a certain extent and inhibited growth in cash withdrawal numbers. Since February 2022, the Single Euro Payments Area (SEPA) Instant Credit Transfer scheme has been in use in Slovakia. Transfers made via this scheme now account for a significant share of non-cash payments in the country and the scheme enables customers to instantly make cross-border payments. Cash withdrawals accounted for 75% of all ATM transactions in 2021, down from 76% the previous year. This fall is due to the growing number of ATMs with automated deposit functionality in Slovakia — as an increasing number of self-service cash deposit transactions in addition to the fall in total withdrawals. In spring 2022, ATMs in Slovakia near the Ukrainian border recorded extremely high withdrawal numbers owing to spike in activity by Ukrainian refugees.

Narrative: The annual value of cash withdrawals grew in 2022, by 27%. Value continued to rebound to exceed the pre-pandemic 2019 figure of EUR 16 billion (USD 19 billion). This followed a pandemic-induced drop of 11% in 2020 as economic uncertainty and lockdowns caused a reduction in both the number and value of cash withdrawals in Slovakia. Both the average per-ATM and the annual value grew significantly in 2022, due to consumers making withdrawals of higher sums than they did prior to the COVID-19 pandemic. Lockdowns in 2021 and 2022 meant that customers visited ATMs less often but withdrew higher amounts when they did. The trend of increasing average withdrawal value continued into 2022, with the average value of a withdrawal going up to 13% to reach EUR 252 (USD 265). The total value of cash withdrawals equated to 19% of national GDP in 2022, exceeding 2018 and 2019 levels after declining by a small measure in 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic.

SLSP added a few OKI machines in 2022. As a result of this, the manufacturer accounted for 15% of SLSP's ATM fleet.

Figure 6.1: Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028 — table:

- ATMs per million people: 2023 567 / 2024 571 / 2025 576 / 2026 580 / 2027 585 / 2028 589 / CAGR 0.8%
Narrative: There are projected to be 3,325 ATMs in Slovakia by end-2028, representing a 4% increase on the 2022 figure of 3,183. As a result, there would be an average of 589 ATMs per million people at end-2028, up from 564 at end-2022 (based on 2022 population figures). The majority of this growth will come from deployment by IADs rather than by banks. IADs are expected to add around 200 new ATMs between end-2022 and end-2028; IADs have continuously grown their ATM fleets in Slovakia after re-entering the market in 2017. Partnerships with retailers have allowed them to install ATMs in stores with high customer traffic; in the coming years, there is the potential for IADs to grow further into more rural parts of the country. It is expected that the number of bank ATMs will gradually decline over the forecast period. Many banks believe that the Slovak ATM market is already saturated, and they are likely to make minor reductions to their ATM fleets as cashless payment methods further reduce the demand for cash transactions in the future. The costs of ATM installation and maintenance act as an inhibitor to growth, in addition to lower interchange fees on some card types reducing the profitability of ATMs. Some banks may make small additions to their fleets over the forecast period to expand their customer base; some deployers will remove single-function ATMs and replace them with multi-function ones in order to make their estates as competitive as possible. For Banka, a Czech bank which was already present in the Slovak retail banking sector deployed its first ATM in Slovakia starting from the second half of 2022. Branch numbers are forecast to fall by 925 by end-2028, a decline of 7% compared to end-2022. Banks are reducing branch numbers mainly as part of branch rationalisation strategies linked to cost-saving plans. Banks are also migrating more services away from branches to digital and remote forms of banking, a trend accelerated by the COVID-19 pandemic. ATMs can remain a useful, strategic tool for banks reducing branch numbers; off-site ATMs can serve a usually a branch presence.

### p.369 — Slovakia Page 15 — 6.3: Forecast ATM cash withdrawal number and value (number)
Figure 6.4: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2028 — bar (annual number, million [left], y-axis 0 to 120) + line (average number per ATM, right, y-axis 0 to 3,300). Historical bars 2018-2022 in darker shade, forecast 2023-2028 lighter. Withdrawal numbers decline gradually over forecast period after the 2022 partial recovery; average withdrawals per ATM also trend downward. Source: NBS, Slovak ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
Narrative: Many banks are expected to rationalise their ATM networks over the forecast period but the IADs, as well as some other banks, are expected to increase their ATM fleets between 2023 and 2028. This means that the shipments for new installations will be greater than the absolute growth in the Slovak installed base. Growth in the installed base is projected to be slow throughout the forecast period, with shipment numbers also decreasing from 2026 onwards. Between 2023 and 2028, the number of ATM cash withdrawals in Slovakia is forecast to decline by an average annual rate of 4%, falling to 63 million by the outer years of the forecast period. This decrease will be driven by the increasing uptake of electronic payments, including mobile and app-based payments. Nonetheless, cash will retain relevance in Slovakia due to usage by older customers, the small unbanked population, as well as the sizeable grey economy present in the country. The average number of cash withdrawals per ATM per month is also forecast to decrease over the forecast period. By 2028, the average number of withdrawals per ATM per month is projected to fall to around 1,830 compared to 2,110 in 2022.
