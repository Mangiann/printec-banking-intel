Source: RBR analysis. Note: ranking shares of off-site ATMs and cash withdrawals per ATM exclude primary markets (Fig 1.1 footnote).
Narrative key points: After four years of expansion, Serbia's installed base shrank by 1% in 2022 to 3,071 ATMs. At 425 ATMs per million people, Serbia's ATM-to-population density is notably lower than the regional CEE average of 573. ~Half of Serbian ATMs are deployed through the exterior wall (TTW) of the bank branch; TTW installations have been declining as a result of branch closures and a steady increase in branch ATMs being deployed in extended-access lobby areas. Off-site share (37%) is well below CEE average. Automated deposit functionality uptake is strong (NFC and QR-code readers in 16% and 13% of ATMs respectively; terms of security cameras while 50% have anti-skimming devices). Cash demand strong: cash withdrawals up 5% in 2022; average number of withdrawals per ATM per month exceeded 2,700 for the first time in 2022. Diebold Nixdorf is the largest ATM manufacturer in Serbia with a 70% share of the installed base; NCR accounts for 29% of ATMs; BANQIT also retains a presence. Growth in ATM installations expected to resume in 2023, with almost 3,400 ATMs forecast to be installed by end-2028. Number and value of ATM withdrawals forecast to grow by 11% and 50% respectively between 2022 and 2028.

Narrative: Serbian banking regulated by the National Bank of Serbia (NBS). 18 commercial banks at end-2022, down by one from end-2021 following AIK Banka's acquisition of Russian-owned Sberbank. Foreign-owned banks account for the majority of the sector: of the 18, two are state-owned (Poštanska štedionica, Srpska banka), two are domestic privately-owned (AIK Banka, ALTA banka). After the disintegration of Yugoslavia and formation of a new government in 2000, sector underwent radical change with mass privatisation in the early 2000s and entry of international banking groups; current largest five banks by assets.

### p.335 — Section 2 (cont.): Banking structure narrative (M&A history) — Serbia: Page 3
Prose page detailing Serbian banking-sector consolidation since 2016 (no figures table):
- Direktna Banka created when Slovenia's Nova Kreditna Banka Maribor acquired KBM Banka in 2016; subsequently acquiring Findomestic Banka (ex-BNP Paribas Group) in 2017, then Piraeus Bank (2018); in 2021 Greece's Eurobank acquired 70% of shares, the bank and the two institutions merged to form Eurobank Direktna later in 2022.
- 2017: Greek-owned Alpha Bank acquired by AIK Banka and temporarily rebranded as JuBanka, then merged into AIK Banka's existing network. Nov 2021 AIK Banka agreed to buy Russia's Sberbank's Serbian operations; deal finalised March 2022 and Sberbank promptly renamed Naša AIK Banka, barely a week after Russia's invasion of Ukraine and imposition of sanctions on Russian financial institutions by European and North American governments. Naša AIK Banka ceased to exist on 1st December 2022 when fully merged into AIK Banka.
- 2017: Hungary's OTP Group purchased 100% of shares of Vojvođanska banka from National Bank of Greece; OTP continued to operate it as a distinct entity until 2021 when the two were merged. 2019 the Hungarian banking group completed acquisition of France's Société Générale Serbian subsidiary, merging operations with OTP banka during 2022.
- 2019: mobile operator Telenor sold its Serbian digital challenger bank to PPF Group, a Czech private equity fund; bank subsequently renamed Mobi Banka.
- 2020: Nova Kreditna Banka Maribor acquired KBM Banka (existing subsidiary in Serbia) — entered a share purchase agreement with Serbian Government for acquisition of 83% stake in Komercijalna Banka (completed Dec 2020). April 2022 Komercijalna banka and NLB Banka fully integrated; merged entity named NLB Komercijalna banka; reduced Serbian top-five share to ~15% to 6%.
- April 2021: Serbia's competition authority approved integration of lender MTS Banka (fully owned by state-owned Telekom Srbija, into state-run postal savings bank Poštanska štedionica).
- Aug 2021: Raiffeisen Bank announced acquisition of Crédit Agricole Serbia; bank renamed RBA banka in Sept 2022 as part of the merger process with Raiffeisen's existing Serbian operations. The two banks expected to complete merger in Q2 2023.
- March 2023: AIK Banka signed an agreement to buy Eurobank Direktna (domestically-owned bank among the country's top-five banks for the first time in several years; merger expected later in 2023).
- Consolidation: number of bank branches has fallen in recent years; 5% reduction in outlets in 2022. OTP banka closed the largest number of branches in 2022. Eurobank Direktna and NLB Komercijalna banka also reduced branch numbers in 2022 following separate mergers in 2021 & 2022.
- Poštanska štedionica expanded its branch network by 16% in 2022 as part of its strategy to increase its physical footprint; took 17 branches formerly operated by Sberbank that were added to AIK Banka's network.

### p.336 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (Figure 3.1 Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022)
First ATM in Serbian region of former Yugoslavia installed 1982 by Beobanka, one of the leading banks in the country at the time. Was soon followed by units installed by Riječka banka (now Croatia's Erste Bank) and Novosadska banka (now Serbia's Erste Bank). The Sarajevo Winter Olympic Games in 1984 provided further impetus for ATM growth in the country.
Until the 1990s the ATM sector was dominated by a single domestic card scheme, YUBA. Few banks ran proprietary acceptance networks until the 1990s. National card scheme **DinaCard** launched in 2001 (Diners Club was the only internationally-carried brand present in Serbia during this period; Visa and Mastercard returned to Serbia in 2002).
**Figure 3.1: Domestic sharing agreements (DinaCard members), 2018-2022:**
- 2018: 2,848
- 2019: 2,953
- 2020: 3,042
- 2021: 3,097
- 2022: 3,071
- CAGR: 1.9%
(Source: Serbian ATM deployers, NBS and RBR analysis.)
At end-2022 there were 17 members in the DinaCard network, down by one from 2021 following Sberbank's exit from the Serbian market. Several independent processors active in Serbia as part of the DinaCard system, incl. EuroPlanet, Chip Card, Fiserv, and Erste Group Card Processor. Jan 2022, DinaCard and Discover signed a strategic agreement giving Discover, Diners Club International, PULSE and Network Alliance cardholders the ability to use their cards on the DinaCard payment network, including POS, e-commerce platforms and ATMs. Euronet withdrew its ATMs from Serbia in 2014 (had only been in market for a year; maintained presence through its part ownership of domestic processing centre, EuroPlanet). With the exception of a small number of ATM sharing agreements, most Serbian card issuers charge customers an "off-us" fee for cash withdrawals at other banks' ATMs, typically ranging from 1% to 2%.

Narrative: AIK Banka is Serbia's largest ATM deployer, with its number of ATMs increasing to over 400 in 2022 as a result of its acquisition of Sberbank's network. Second and third largest deployers Banca Intesa and Poštanska štedionica also continued to expand their estates in 2022, each adding ~20 machines. By contrast, Raiffeisen Group, Eurobank Direktna and OTP banka reduced their ATM estates in 2022 as part of network rationalisation following mergers between 2021 and 2022. MoneyGet — owned by the Payten group and part of Asseco Group — is the only IAD operating in Serbia; has seen its estate more than double in size since 2018. The IAD has an agreement with Alta Banka to deploy some ATMs at the bank's branches and multiple off-site locations; Alta Banka's customers can make fee-free withdrawals at all MoneyGet's ATMs. Erste Bank, Poštanska štedionica and OTP banka have entered "on-us" agreements that let their customers use MoneyGet ATMs without incurring disloyalty fees.

### p.339 — Section 4: Current ATM market / 4.1 Market size and growth (Figure 4.1 Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022) — Serbia: Page 7
Combined column (ATMs, left axis 0-5,000) + line (growth %, right axis -2% to 8%) chart. Data table:
- ATMs: 2018 = 2,848; 2019 = 2,953; 2020 = 3,042; 2021 = 3,097; 2022 = 3,071; CAGR = 1.9%
- Absolute growth: 163 / 105 / 89 / 55 / -26; CAGR N/A
- Percentage growth: 6.1% / 3.7% / 3.0% / 1.8% / -0.8%; CAGR N/A
Chart takeaway: ATM count plateaued ~3,000-3,100 over 2018-2022; growth-rate line declines steadily from ~6% (2018) to slightly negative (-0.8%) in 2022 (first contraction). (Source: Serbian ATM deployers, NBS and RBR analysis.)
Narrative: Following a decline in 2017, ATM number in Serbia increased by over 400 between 2018 and 2021 as many larger deployers continued to expand estates despite COVID-19 disruption. In 2022 the installed base contracted slightly, with net reduction of 26 units; decline mainly the result of consolidation in banking sector following several major mergers and acquisitions. Raiffeisen Group recorded the largest decrease, with Raiffeisen banka and RBA banka removing 66 machines between them in 2022. While full integration of RBA banka into Raiffeisen banka's network not scheduled to complete until Q2 2023, customers of both banks had "on-us" access to all ATMs in 2022 enabling rationalisation of branches and removal of ATMs from sites with multiple installations. Eurobank Direktna and OTP banka also reduced sizes of respective ATM estates following mergers in 2021. AIK Banka's growth was result of acquiring Sberbank's former estate. Banca Intesa and Poštanska štedionica continued increasing demand from customers. NLB Komercijalna banka added around 80 ATMs to estate since 2018; bank is upgrading all installations to multifunctional ATMs (customers can deposit as well as withdraw cash and carry out numerous additional transactions). MoneyGet — only IAD in Serbian market — continued to expand its estate in 2022, having doubled its number of ATMs since 2018.

(Source: NBS, Serbian ATM deployers, UN, CIA, IMF and RBR analysis.)
Density narrative: At end-2022 there were 425 ATMs per million people in Serbia, increasing slightly from 424 at end-2021 as Serbia's population declined more rapidly than ATM installations. Serbia has one of the lowest densities of ATMs to population among CEE primary markets, indicating significant potential for growth. Serbia's ATM-to-branch density increased in 2022 to 219 ATMs per 100 bank branches, compared with 209 the previous year, a result of widespread rationalisation of branch networks. Serbia continues to rank below the CEE average by this measure. Serbia's density of ATMs relative to size of its economy slightly above the CEE average of 47 ATMs per USD billion GDP.
