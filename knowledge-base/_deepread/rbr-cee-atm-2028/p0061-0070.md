Country chapter: **Belarus** (RBR / NCR Atleos — Central and Eastern Europe ATM Market and Forecasts to 2028). Pages 61-70 cover the end of Section 2 (banking sector) and Sections 3 (ATM networks, sharing and services) and 4 (current ATM market) for Belarus.

### p.61
Belarus banking-sector narrative (continued). Key facts:
- In 2022 the number of bank branches in Belarus fell 14% (1.4%) to 1,910. Total number of bank branches dropped over 100 every year between 2014 and 2020 as banks have prioritised digitalisation of banking activities, instead of investing in physical (branch) network.
- 2022 was a stagnation year: GDP fell 4.7% (sanctions/Russia-war fallout).
- 205 branches were shut as Belagroprombank and BNB Bank each lost the largest contraction in 2022 with rural ones; Belarusbank and VTB Bank opened the fewest new branches.
- Since 2014 ATM networks have remained relatively stable while bank branches contracted; in 2016 the Belarusian Rouble was redenominated (1:10,000) — replaced the old rouble at a rate of 1 new rouble = 10,000 old; the NBRB became the Bank of Dabrabyt.
- In 2009, SBS was established for the number of banks operating in the country (was 27,924). In 2020 the Belarusian Inter-bank Settlement Centre (now Bank Dabrabyt), BelSwissBank (now BSB Bank), RRB-Bank (now CBD-Bank) and Technobank; SBS provided online and mobile banking services to the customers of its partner banks, enabling cardholders to use payment terminals. This includes credit cards, debit cards, prepaid cards and other payment methods.
- April 2020: the Belarusian government sold state-owned Paritetbank to a private Russian businessperson.
- June 2020: Belgazprombank's long-serving CEO who had resigned in May 2020 to run for president was placed under investigation; bank's assets were seized and its operating director was arrested; mid-2020 banking turmoil amid political/electoral crisis.
- November 2021: MTBank rebranded from Minsk-Transit Bank to MTBank.
- January 2022: Alfa-bank acquired Frondbank, with the latter ceasing operations in Belarus later that year.
- March 2022: post-Russia-invasion sanctions/payment-scheme exits were noted: Visa and Mastercard suspended operations in Russia; some Belarusian banks were cut off from SWIFT; Belarus banks largely shifted to the Russian Mir scheme and the domestic BELKART system, and to Russian Mir.
- July 2022: AbsolutBank changed its legal status, becoming a non-bank credit and financial institution.

### p.62
**Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services** begins.
**3.1: ATM networks and sharing**
- In 1996 the first ATM in the country was installed by Bank BelVEB (previously Belvnesheconombank).
- There are currently two ATM networks in Belarus: **BPC** and **SBS**. BPC is the larger network, with 71% of Belarusian ATMs connected, including those of the four largest deployers (Belarusbank, Belagroprombank, Belinvestbank & Sberbank). It was established in 2002.
- In 2009, SBS was established (was 27,924 — number reference) for online and mobile banking and payment terminals; current deployers connect their networks to interconnect — settlement and switching of card payments and internet providers.
- Customers can change their PIN codes, view account statements, block their cards, and pay bills at the ATMs of their own bank's network; cardholders of one network cannot easily withdraw at the other.
- BSB Bank is the only bank in Belarus to be a member of both networks and joined BPC in 2019 (SBS members and a former member of BPC); SBS gained 12% of the installed base, with 508 ATMs.
- There are no IADs (independent ATM deployers) in Belarus as they are not licensed to operate in the country.

### p.65
Body text on deployer structure + **3.3: ATM-as-a-Service**.
- **Belarusbank** was Belarus' largest ATM deployer, accounting for **a third of the total market** despite a decline of 22 since end-2021. It had over double the ATMs of the second-largest deployer, **Belagroprombank** (579 installations at end-2022, a decline of 38). **Alfa-bank** had the largest expansion of its ATM fleet in 2022, adding 5 new terminals.
- Third and fourth largest deployers were **Sberbank (470)** and **Belinvestbank (460)**, followed by **Priorbank** (a contraction of 16 units, to 341 ATMs).
- **3.3 ATM-as-a-Service:** In 2020 Bank BelVEB separated its processing division (ATM-network development) into a separate company, **BelVEB AIT LLC**, which had all ATMs transferred to its balance sheet; legally and for customers the fleet remained under Bank BelVEB. In 2022 the company was renamed **SmartProcessing Ltd.** after an ownership/beneficiary change, but it continues to service the Bank BelVEB network as a third-party company. Other banks such as Belagroprombank also engage in ATM-as-a-Service operations.

Body text: After a 3% drop in installed base in 2018, the ATM market grew by 1% with growth continuing in 2020 and 2021 (by 2% and 1% respectively). Cashless-payment growth and a trend (accelerated by COVID-19) toward fewer cash transactions also reduced demand for ATMs; banks paused purchasing as cash demand fell. Additionally the devaluation/fluctuation of the local currency over the past two years has played a part in the reduced deployers' purchasing power, as they may buy hardware abroad in foreign currency.

Body text: 71% of ATMs are located off-site — remaining unchanged from the previous year (vs 74% in 2018). Common off-site locations include supermarkets, shopping centres, offices, university buildings and transport hubs. Off-site has been the preferred location due to prevalence of salary-card projects, whereby companies can partner with banks to issue their employees salary cards. Off-site locations are useful in the Belarusian market as the installed base is also affected by the constantly falling branch numbers in other markets. Technobank had the highest proportion of off-site ATM fleets located off-site, with 88% of their terminals not based in branches. ### p.69
Continuation of 4.3 + **4.4: Functionality and hardware features.**
- ATMs situated in branch lobbies have seen the strongest growth at an average annual rate of **23%** between end-2018 and end-2022 (driven by developing sophistication of ATMs and rising transaction values; lobby locations seen as safer/more secure while offering extended-hours or 24-hour access). At end-2022 the share of ATMs in lobbies dropped one percentage point, while off-site rose by a similar proportion.
- At end-2022 **3% of ATMs were located in the bank hall**, unchanged from the previous year. **Belgazprombank had the highest proportion of hall ATMs, accounting for 12% of its estate, while Belarusbank reported zero hall ATMs.** Belarusbank has the only drive-through ATM in Belarus.

Body text: In 2022 the vast majority of Belarusian ATMs provided mobile phone top-ups and printed statements, with the vast majority also offering transfers to another account at the same bank (89%) and PIN change (96%). 38% of ATMs in the country offered multi-currency dispensing, increasing by 17 percentage points as Belagroprombank and Priorbank now offer this service across their whole fleets. 47% of ATMs provided cardless cash withdrawal using a mobile-app/QR code (most rolled out 2021). 13% of ATMs provided speech capability for the visually impaired in 2021; available at both Belagroprombank and Priorbank ATMs among others. Paid-for advertising for a third party was available in a small facility — to <10% of Belarusian ATMs, an increase of one percentage point from 2021. In terms of hardware and security, all ATMs have built-in or integrated security cameras, and 78% have anti-skimming devices.

### p.70
Functionality narrative (continued).
- **NFC cash withdrawal** decreased by one percentage point from 11% (2021) to 10% (2022), due to contraction of various deployers in the installed base rather than deployers removing the facility. Belagroprombank has made it a requirement that all future ATMs purchased should have NFC readers; Priorbank had NFC readers installed at a small proportion of its terminals in 2022, but the functionality is not due to be in use until 2023-2024, so the share of ATMs offering NFC cash withdrawals remains lower than the share with NFC hardware.
- In 2022 the share of ATMs accepting **automated deposits** increased by one percentage point to **9% of the installed base**; in absolute terms the number of automated-deposit ATMs grew by **4% in 2022**. Belagroprombank (the only deployer that used non-recycling automated-deposit ATMs) has now removed all non-recyclers; as of 2022 all major automated-deposit terminal deployers have note recycling across their whole fleets.
- **Belarusbank is the largest deployer of stand-alone deposit terminals (SATs)** in 2022, constituting **59%** of the country's total of **1,671 devices**. Belagroprombank, Priorbank, Bank BelVEB and Belgazprombank all deploy SATs that accepted note deposits. The number of SATs increased by **194 in 2022**, mainly driven by **Belagroprombank adding 218 machines**.
