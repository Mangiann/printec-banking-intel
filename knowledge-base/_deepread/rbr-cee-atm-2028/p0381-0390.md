### p.381 — Ukraine: cards & wartime banking context (Ukraine Page 5)
Body text only, no chart/table. Key facts:
- March 2022: Sberbank, followed by VTB Bank, announced their withdrawal from the European banking market in the face of Western sanctions over the Ukraine war.
- Since February 2022, over **8 million** citizens have fled Ukraine; NBU has urged those leaving Ukraine to keep their money in Ukrainian bank accounts.
- Banks continued to facilitate critical operations during the crisis, launching the Power Banking joint banking initiative.
- NBU consumer-credit / spending stat: **40%** of all bank branches (excluding closed outlets and those located in occupied areas, plus around the same number of branches near the front line) still functioned; lending continued.
- NBU confirmed electronic payments and ATMs are still working, with cash continuing to be delivered in all areas of Ukraine except active combat zones. NBU increased the limit on cash withdrawals in foreign currency accounts, from **UAH 30,000 (USD 1,020)** to **UAH 100,000 (USD 3,340)**.
- Additionally, in December 2022 the NBU asked banks to introduce "national ATM roaming," allowing card holders to withdraw cash from any bank's ATM.
- Several banks have been liquidated or declared insolvent since the invasion of martial law (June 2022). Mega Bank was declared insolvent after the bank's noncompliance with NBU requirements to eliminate breaches of Ukrainian laws within the term set by the NBU. The central bank stated that these were not caused by the imposition of martial law.
- October 2022: NBU revoked the licence of and liquidated Sich Bank after its failure to fulfil its liabilities to the NBU, owing to the prescribed crime in the refinancing loan agreement due to the lack of funding. In February 2023, Forward Bank was declared insolvent due to the bank's failure to bring its activities in compliance with the applicable laws and NBU regulations after declaring the bank a problem bank.

### p.382 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (Ukraine Page 6)
Opens **Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services**, sub-section **3.1: ATM networks and sharing**.
Body text:
- In 1996, Ukraine's first ATMs were installed, and the market developed slowly, reaching **300 terminals by 2000**.
- The following years saw rapid expansion as banks began issuing payment cards through salary card projects (agreements between a bank and an employer to provide banking services for all company employees). This boosted demand for ATMs/cards, which were often located on the premises of companies with large workforces.
- Growth in the market gathered momentum in the early 2000s, peaking at **52% growth in 2007**, which saw 7,187 new terminals installed in one year. There was a significant slowdown in expansion after the global financial crisis began, with a net annual increase of only **5,374** in the previous year (2009; compared to 5,374 the previous year). Many deployers began removing terminals as part of branch network rationalisation, while few continued to expand their fleets. However, the installed base grew by an average of 800 ATMs per year between 2009 and 2013.
- In 2013, Ukraine was rocked by political unrest and economic turmoil, which has had a significant impact on the ATM market. There was a substantial contraction in the installed base in 2014, as it lost 5,392 terminals to reach an installed base of 24,880, representing an 18% drop from a year earlier. This was mostly driven by the loss of machines in regions of Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk, which were no longer under the control of the Ukrainian government. Over the following years, despite the dampening effect on the country's economy, the installed base grew again, recovering to 32,030 ATMs by 2018 — though this remained below the 2013 peak. Historically, most deployers have viewed ATMs as a cheaper alternative to branch membership or branch networks for delivering banking to their own fleets. However, due to the ongoing war the central bank has asked banks to introduce national ATM roaming, which will cancel arbitrary fees for ATM withdrawals by cardholders issued by other banks. In order to deal with the difficulties of providing banking transactions, as well as informing clients about the mode of operation of the banks and their branches.

### p.384 — ATMoSPHERA history & Euronet IAD share (Ukraine Page 8)
Body text only:
- In 2006, UPC launched ATMoSPHERA to offer lower "off-us" withdrawal fees to its small and medium-sized members. It initially had 10 members, but all banks using UPC processing services, with the exception of Raiffeisenbank Aval, had joined by 2013. The political and economic crisis in 2013 prompted a drop in the number of members, falling from **24 in 2014 to 18 in 2015**.
- In 2016, plans were announced to disband the ATMoSPHERA network, because members were no longer routing Mastercard debit transactions through the network due to the lower ATM interchange fees of such cards. Network membership was no longer considered cost-efficient by banks since Visa adopted a similar ATM interchange fee structure for most debit products. Despite this, ATMoSPHERA reversed its original decision later the same year and announced it would continue to operate, albeit on a smaller scale following the exit of Alfa-Bank and Prominvestbank. Ukrgasbank replaced Raiffeisenbank Aval as the settlement bank. There were **six ATM deployers in the network at end-2022, with nearly 600 ATMs**.
- The only IAD in Ukraine is Euronet, accounting for **3% of the installed base at end-2022**, unchanged from the previous year. In March 2022, Euronet announced the waiver of fees on the company's ATMs for Ukrainian cardholders and on money transfers to Ukraine, following the invasion of Russia.

### p.388 — Off-site vs on-site locations narrative (Ukraine Page 12)
Body text only:
- The majority of ATMs have traditionally been located off-site in Ukraine. Salary card projects have historically driven this, as large employers are able to request the installation of ATMs on their premises to allow employees convenient access to their salaries. This arrangement is mutually beneficial for employers and banks, as the rental fees tend to be lower than those for other off-site locations. Other common off-site locations are often those with high footfall, such as shopping malls, supermarkets, and transport hubs.
- Since 2018, the proportion of off-site Ukrainian ATMs has increased by seven percentage points to comprise **71%** of the installed base at end-2022. PrivatBank is the largest deployer of off-site ATMs; this location accounting for 74% of its total fleet, while Oschadbank and Ukrgasbank have 77% and 74% of their fleet in off-site locations respectively.
- The most common branch location is through the exterior wall (TTW), which provides customers with extended ATM access. However, the share of terminals deployed in this location has steadily fell between 2018 and 2021. In 2022, however, this share declining by seven percentage points to account for 16% of the ATM market. In 2022 the proportion of TTW ATMs fell by four percentage points, as it is most affected by infrastructure damage. Oschadbank has the largest proportion of TTW ATMs, with 21% of its fleet in this location.
- The proportion of ATMs installed in the lobby increased by two percentage points in 2022 to 7% of the total installed base. During 2022 several deployers sought to optimise their branch networks by closing unprofitable branches and relocating equipment. Some deployers also moved their machines into more secure locations, namely inside branches, in order to limit damage to the infrastructure, driving this increase in lobby ATMs.
- OTP Bank has the highest proportion of lobby ATMs with **41%** of its fleet installed there, while Oschadbank has the highest number of ATMs in lobbies, totalling 474. Most Ukrainian deployers have a number of terminals located in the branch hall, and this location accounted for 6% of the installed base at end-2022. PrivatBank has reported the largest number of ATMs in bank halls, with 336 terminals in this location, 5% of its fleet.
