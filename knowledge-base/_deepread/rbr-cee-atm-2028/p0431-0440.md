Body text: At end-2022 the Armenian banking sector comprised 17 commercial banks, unchanged from the previous three years. The installed base stood at ~1,475 ATMs after a growth of ~2% since end-2021 as deployers expanded their fleets. The country's financial sector is regulated and overseen by the Central Bank of Armenia (CBA); the only such authority in Armenia since the mid-2000s. Branch numbers have declined over the last few years, declining in 2021 by only one, followed by a sharper fall of 35 the following year, leaving 519 bank branches in Armenia at the end of 2022. VTB Bank saw the largest fall, shuttering 19 outlets as sanctions impacted the Russian-owned subsidiary and consequently money transfer operations. Unibank shuttered ten branches while Armswissbank closed five. Meanwhile, Ardshinbank opened two outlets and both Ameriabank and IDBank opened one. The five largest banks by branch networks in the country, Ardshinbank, ACBA-Credit Agricole Bank, VTB Bank, Unibank and Armbusinessbank collectively accounted for 55% of the national total at the end of 2022.
Further body text: notes the CBA has implemented a number of changes to strengthen the financial sector and to ensure stability; minimum capital requirement raised several times, with the first deadline in 2009 requiring all banks to boost their recapitalisation from AMD 5 billion (USD 9.x million) to AMD 5 billion (USD 11 million). The last deadline was at the end of 2017, with the level set at AMD 30 billion (USD 62 million). A number of small acquisitions between banks took place as a result of the more than tenfold increase in the minimum capital requirement, and the number of banks in the country totalled less than 20 by the end of 2016.
Regulatory changes and banking sector consolidation caused a notable exit of foreign investors from the sector. The number of banks with foreign-held shares declined from 18 to ten between 2015 and 2022. The number of locally-incorporated banks where foreign investors held shares of more than 50% declined over this period too.
In 2016, Russian-owned Areximbank and Kazakh-owned BTA Bank were sold to local Ardshinbank and Armeconombank respectively. Armenian Development Bank was the only locally incorporated bank to be sold that year, merging with AraratBank in late 2016. In the same year, German ProCredit Bank was among the banks to cease operations in 2016, following a sale to Inecobank. The deal was financed through the European Bank of Reconstruction and Development, which provided AMD 10 billion (USD 20 million) against the 23% stake in Inecobank. Footer: Armenia: Page 1.

### p.439 — Armenia, Section 2 (ATM networks, sharing and services) 2.1
Body text: In 2000 the central bank and ten local banks collectively established Armenian Card (ArCa), aiming to support and develop a national payment system which also now functions as the national shared ATM network. All of Armenia's ATMs are connected to ArCa, the national switch and processor of the ArCa domestic payment scheme. ArCa provides processing services to Armenia's retail and medium-sized banks as it would not be financially viable for these banks to maintain their own processing facilities. There were 15 member deployers of ArCa by the end of 2022, unchanged from the previous year. Currently VTB Bank and Mellat Bank are the only banks in Armenia who are not participants of ArCa.
In 2017 ArCa was connected to Russian National Payment Card System (NSPK), the operator of Russia's largest domestic payment scheme, Mir. This enabled the acceptance of both ArCa and Mir payment schemes in both countries. Armenian banks then began offering co-badged ArCa-Mir cards, which allow cardholders to make international funds transfers between Russia and Armenia at ATMs.
As of September 2022, several Armenian banks have suspended the acceptance of Russian Mir cards, as well as money withdrawals for their holders, after the US tightened sanctions against Russia.
