### p.191 — Kazakhstan, banking sector (Page 4)
Body text only. Key facts:
- Alfa-Bank (Kazakhstani arm of Russian Alfa-Bank group) acquired by Bank CenterCredit and renamed Eco Center Bank; fully absorbed, all branches and ATM fleets merged. All Russian shareholders in Home Credit Bank divested, selling stakes to top managers of Czech PPF Group.
- As of early 2023, VTB Bank in Kazakhstan remains under Russian ownership, operating normally.
- Total banking assets rose from KZT 37.6 trillion (USD 86.3 bn) end-2021 to KZT 44.6 trillion (USD 96.3 bn) end-2022.
- Largest bank by assets: Halyk Bank KZT 13.9 tn (USD 30.0 bn); then Kaspi Bank KZT 5.1 tn (USD 11.0 bn); Bank CenterCredit KZT 4.3 tn (USD 9.4 bn). Bank CenterCredit entered top three for first time in 2022 via Alfa-Bank absorption. Top three banks = 52% of all Kazakhstani banking assets.
- Branches: Halyk Bank has by far the most, 597 at end-2022; Bank CenterCredit next with 183. Total branches falling since 2015; in 2022 fell to 1,697, down 2% year-on-year.

### p.192 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (Page 5)
Body text only. Key facts:
- First ATM in Kazakhstan installed 1994 by Alem Bank, also placed in the bank's head office in Almaty. Alem Bank also launched Kazakhstan's first processing centre that year. ATM numbers slowly grew, owing to low payment-card penetration; situation began to change with the launch of domestic card scheme AlemCard and AltynCard in mid-1990s (cited over the decade).
- Payment-card numbers grew rapidly, driving ATMs up between 2003 and 2021.
- ATM market doubled between 2003 and 2007. Market saw growth every deployer in 2015; banks consolidated and reduced surcharging; ambitious expansion strategies (Halyk Bank) drove growth.
- 2017–2018 Halyk Bank started culling the two ATM fleets after the merger with Kazkommertsbank (Qazkom), removing ATMs from branch lobbies of both banks. From 2018 Halyk continued processing in-house.
- Following the acquisition of Qazkom in 2017, Halyk Bank started uniting the two ATM fleets, removing ATM fees for both deployer's customers (free-of-charge cash withdrawals).
- ATM transactions with biometrics introduced in 2018 (later detail in fig 4.x); cash usage high. Cash threshold mentioned: KZT 200,000 (USD 433 / 1,083). No surcharging at ATMs in Kazakhstan.

Body: ATMs installed through-the-wall (TTW) are most common consumer location, ~11% of installed base; TTW offer accessibility but perceived as less secure, can be affected by adverse weather. Kaspi Bank has rolled out QR-code withdrawals, complementing its wide system of QR-code payments. Bereke Bank only deployer known to operate ATMs with assisted self-service video, with 10 such installations.
