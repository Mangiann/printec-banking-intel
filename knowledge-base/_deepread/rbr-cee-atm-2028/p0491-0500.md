### p.492 — Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services (footer "Georgia: Page 3")
2.1 ATM networks and sharing. First ATMs installed in Georgia in the late 1990s; growth accelerated mid-2000s with non-bank ATMs and member banks joining processing/sharing arrangements from ~2007–2008. Two independent processing centres: United Financial Corporation (UFC) — the larger by members and coverage; formed 1996 by TBC Bank plus four other banks; eight bank members at end-2022. 42% of installed base connected to UFC, up from 39% at end-2021, growth driven by TBC Bank's fleet expansion. The other independent centre is Georgian Card. Two in-house processing centres operated by Cartu Bank (established 2001) and Liberty Bank (launched 2008).

### p.499 — Section 5: Market forecasts — forecast installed base (footer "Georgia: Page 10")
5.1 Forecast installed base.
**Figure 5.1: Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028 (2023 / 2024 / 2025 / 2026 / 2027 / 2028 / CAGR):**
- ATMs: 2,950 / 3,000 / 3,050 / 3,100 / 3,150 / 3,200 — CAGR 1.6%
- Absolute growth: 48 / 50 / 50 / 50 / 50 / 50 — N/A
- Percentage growth: 1.7% / 1.7% / 1.7% / 1.6% / 1.6% / 1.6% — N/A
- (Row also begins "ATMs per million people…" ~788 / 80x in 2024 etc., partially cut off.)
Installed base forecast to continue growing 2023 and 2028 at avg ~2% annually; ~2,000 ATMs per country to ~3,200 by end of forecast period, implying ~855 terminals per million people (2022 population figures). Growth driven by continued demand for cash, which has been maintained despite increasing adoption of cashless payment methods. Georgia remains a cash-heavy society, with both number and value of withdrawals continuing to rise year on year. Payment cards widely used at point of sale in the capital Tbilisi, but penetration lower in the country's regions. Banks are expected to continue investing in their ATM networks in underserved regional areas. The majority of the installed base concentrated in Tbilisi; significant potential for growth outside the capital. Growth nonetheless limited by the widening penetration of cashless payment methods, especially card and mobile payments, which have both seen strong growth in recent years. ATMs also face competition from alternative methods of withdrawing cash, such as via cashback at point of sale.
**Figure 5.2: Number of ATMs, 2003-2028** — line chart of total ATMs from 2003 (~very low) rising steadily to plateau ~3,200 by 2028 (left axis to 4,000); shows long historical ramp flattening across forecast. Sources: NBG, Georgian ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
