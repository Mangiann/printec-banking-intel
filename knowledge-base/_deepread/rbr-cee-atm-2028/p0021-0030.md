### p.21 (printed p.3) — Installed base & cash withdrawals narrative
- CEE installed base contracted for a third consecutive year in 2022, down 4% from end-2021, with just one country (Romania) of the 12 primary CEE markets seeing an increase in installed base.
- Russia's largest installed base in CEE fell by a significant margin, but also removed the most terminals in 2022 so far. Russia removed more than 4,000 ATMs in 2022, representing the largest part of the overall decrease in the CEE installed base. However, even if Russia is removed from the calculation, the region still declined by 2,000 ATMs.
- In percentage terms, Ukraine saw the most significant contraction in installed base, which shrank by 18% in 2022. The conflict in the country drove this sharp decline, with deployers making solutions for their fleets in 2022 and significant disruption to infrastructure (taking terminals out of operation).
- The next-largest percentage decrease of 5% was recorded in both Russia and Bulgaria. The Bulgarian installed base has been declining since 2020 with deployers keen to cut high terminal maintenance costs and focus on the operation of fewer, but more functional machines. Older machines are being removed in large numbers and replaced by a smaller number of more sophisticated, multifunctional models. While cash usage remains prevalent in Bulgaria, mobile payments have been particularly swift uptake among the younger generation, another contributor to its declining installed base.
- The only primary CEE market to see growth in its ATM installed base was Romania, which added 70 ATMs, an increase of 1%. The small increase marked a return to growth after several years of decline and was driven by a small number of bank and IAD deployers expanding their fleets. Despite a notable shift towards cashless payments in recent years, Romania remains largely cash-reliant with continuing a demand for self-service cash transactions.
- Much more growth was recorded among secondary and tertiary markets, with nine out of fourteen secondaries seeing increases, and each of the three tertiaries. Of the secondary markets, Uzbekistan saw the largest increase by far, with its installed base growing by 13% following the installation of 1,200 new ATMs during the year. Uzbekistan has seen considerable strong growth since the mid-2010s. The country's low penetration of ATMs relative to its population and area, coupled with a young, increasingly banked and cardholding population, provides an impetus for deployers to expand access to self-service transactions.
- Uzbekistan's neighbour Tajikistan saw the fastest-growing tertiary market, with 312 new installations in 2022 representing an increase of 22%. While Tajikistan has a much smaller installed base than Uzbekistan (1,724 ATMs vs 9,151), the conditions driving ATM penetration, an increasingly banked population with demand for cash at self-service terminals.

### p.23 (printed p.5) — Cash usage / digital displacement narrative
- Cash usage is weakening in Russia and rapid uptake of digital, particularly mobile, payment methods, reducing the business case for wide ATM deployment by banks. Largest deployer Sberbank has been removing large numbers of ATMs for several years, part of a wider digital and fleet transformation project which has seen the bank withdraw older terminals and update its network with a smaller number of new, multifunctional ATMs. Activity by Sberbank, which accounts for almost 40% of Russia's installed base, tends to drive growth or contraction in the market overall. Other major deployers with significant shares of overall ATM numbers also rationalised their fleets in 2022 in pursuit of cost reduction; the region's overall ATM numbers, but a small number of new strategies.
- The second-largest ATM market in CEE is Poland, which inventoried Ukraine in 2015. There were 21,280 ATMs in Poland at end-2022, representing a small decline of 1% from end-2021. Following a period of rapid growth after Poland joined the European Union, the Polish ATM market peaked at 23,400 units in 2016 and has since declined in each subsequent year owing to falling numbers of cash withdrawals and a slowdown in removals by deployers. Although cash remains in widespread use, the increasing popularity of card and digital payment methods, multifunctional models. While cash usage remains prevalent in Bulgaria, mobile payments have seen particularly swift uptake among the younger population, another contributor to its declining installed base.
- Ukraine remains the third largest ATM market in the region, representing 7% of the regional total at end-2022. Ukrainian deployers had collectively installed 15,591 ATMs at end-2022, down from 18,939 the previous year (-18%). This markedly sharp decline owes much of the ongoing conflict in the country which has disrupted infrastructure, weakened the economy and caused large numbers of the population to seek refuge abroad.
- Previously, the Ukrainian market saw a period of relatively slow growth following the global financial crisis in 2008, then a rapid decline of 18% in 2014 due to the withdrawal of Ukrainian banks from the annexed Crimea region and rebel-held areas in the east of the country. The Ukrainian installed base has continued to fall in subsequent years, with western multinationals withdrawing in 2022, accounting for 3% of the regional installed base — a stable share with the previous year. Plant Cash continued and continued strong demand for self-service cash transactions.
- Kazakhstan is the fourth-largest ATM market in CEE in 2022, having overtaken Romania. There were 12,391 ATMs in Kazakhstan at end-2022, accounting for 5% of the regional total. Following an increase in 2021, the Kazakh installed base saw a slight decline of less than 1% during 2022 — fleet rationalisation by larger deployers offsetting modest growth elsewhere. Demand for ATMs remained robust during the COVID-19 pandemic on bank expansion strategies and consumer ATM usage. Some major deployers made a significant number of new installations, with the small decline of overall numbers owing to rationalisation of other deployers.
- The ATM environment in Kazakhstan is somewhat mixed; the country has seen a rapid shift to digital payments, primarily via QR code, since 2019 which have replaced cash (and even card) for many types of everyday spending. The wide usage of QR code and other digital payments have reduced demand for ATM transactions and some deployers have reduced their fleet sizes significantly as a result. Some deployers continue to expand with large numbers of new installations, however. This is in pursuit of a strategy to reach new customers in regions with less banking infrastructure and to place multifunctional ATMs in all key touchpoint in their wider banking ecosystems.

### p.24 (printed p.6) — Romania, Uzbekistan & Figure 1.5: ATMs by deployer type
- The fifth-largest market in the region was Romania, with 10,044 ATMs at end-2022. The Romanian installed base increased by 1% in 2022 thanks to small numbers of new installations by bank and IAD deployers and a slowdown in removals by others. Previously, the Romanian ATM market had been in decline for several years due to fleet rationalisation by deployers in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic.
- The largest non-primary market in CEE is Uzbekistan (included in "Others" in Figure 1.4), which is also the sixth largest market in the region overall as of end-2022, with 9,151 ATMs. The Uzbek installed base has seen rapid growth in recent years, with ATM numbers rising by 15% in 2022, thanks to over 1,200 new installations by deployers expanding their presence across the country and reaching new customers.

- The number of bank-deployed ATMs in CEE declined by 4% in 2022 to reach 218,668 ATMs. This mirrored the second consecutive year of growth for IAD-deployed units in CEE, following a decline of 5% in 2020. The first year of the COVID-19 pandemic was a difficult time for many IAD deployers owing to reduced customer traffic, particularly in tourist areas where IAD ATMs are often placed. 2021 and 2022 allowed some recovery to take place thanks to more in-person cash spending and travel, although the IAD total installed base remained below 2019, pre-pandemic levels at end-2022.
- The proportion of ATMs deployed by IADs rose from 7% to 8% between end-2021 and end-2022 as IAD-deployed units grew in number while bank-deployed ones fell. IADs are present in nine of the 12 primary CEE markets. - IADs are not permitted to deploy ATMs in Russia or Belarus due to regulations limiting ATM deployment to licensed banks. Among primary markets, Kazakhstan also did not have any non-bank ATM deployment at end-2022.

### p.25 (printed p.7) — Poland & IAD analysis narrative
- Poland maintained the highest share of the region's IADs in 2022, with 76% of the region's total installed in the country. Poland also saw IAD-deployed ATM numbers return to growth in 2022 with an uplift of 1% following a decline of 3% in 2021 as large IADs Planet Cash and Euronet added to their fleets. The largest increase in the IAD market was in Poland, where IAD-deployed ATM numbers, followed by Slovakia with 7%.
- ATMs deployed by IADs represent 64% of the entire Polish installed base. No other CEE market has as high a proportion of IAD-deployed ATMs. IADs accounted for more than 10% of the installed base in the following countries: Czech Republic (17%), Hungary (16%), Croatia and Slovakia (12%).
- Euronet is the largest IAD in CEE, and is present in several markets in this region. There are other IADs present in the region such as MoneyGet in Serbia and Croatia, ATM Point in Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia, and Planet Cash in Poland and Croatia. Croatia has the largest number of IAD deployers in the region at six thanks to its popularity as a tourist destination — two of these are domestic IADs, while Czech Republic also has a domestic IAD, Pharro-Praha.
- Czech Republic had the largest absolute total of IAD ATMs in the region, representing 953 at the end of 2022. Euronet is the largest IAD present in the Czech market with 650 installations, followed by Pharro-Praha, with an estate of 218 ATMs at the end of 2022.
- Hungary and Croatia were the third and fourth largest regions by IAD ATMs at end-2022 with 762 and 580 IAD-deployed ATMs respectively. A considerable part of the revenue of the Croatian IAD sector comes from servicing tourists and offering dynamic currency conversion. Hundreds of IAD terminals are taken out of service in Croatia for winter months, however, when fewer tourists visit the country.

### p.27 (printed p.9) — Density narrative (continued)
- At end-2022 Russia had the second highest density of ATMs to population in the region, with 790 ATMs per million people. However, Russia's geographical density was the second lowest among primary markets at 7 ATMs per 1,000 km², due to its extremely large geographical area. Kazakhstan was the primary market with the lowest ATM density relative to geographical area, with only five ATMs per 1,000 km², mostly due to vast areas of mountainous terrain and uninhabited regions.
- Among primary markets, Ukraine had the lowest ATM density relative to population, with 393 ATMs per million people, having declined sharply from 435 the previous year owing to the major contraction in its installed base.
- Belarus had the lowest density of ATMs to 100 bank branches among the primary markets, at 212. If post offices are included, this figure drops to just 65 ATMs per 100 bank branches and post offices. The primary market with the highest ATM density to this measure was Kazakhstan, with 730 ATMs per 100 bank branches. Kazakhstan banks have been rationalising their branch networks in recent years but investing heavily in their self-service capabilities. Kazakhstani deployers have invested in off-site ATMs as a method of increasing their brand awareness and expanding the offer of banking services into more remote, underbanked areas.
- At end-2022 Ukraine was the market with the highest number of ATMs in relation to GDP, with 104 ATMs per USD billion GDP. Ukraine was the only primary market in CEE to have more than 100 ATMs per USD billion GDP, significantly more than Croatia which had the second highest ATM density relative to GDP at 67 ATMs per USD billion. The primary market with the lowest ATM density by this measure at end-2022 was the Czech Republic which had 19 ATMs per USD billion.

### p.29 (printed p.11) — Largest deployers narrative (continued)

Halyk Bank also has a small presence in Uzbekistan through its subsidiary Tenge Bank, which began deploying ATMs in the country in 2019.

- The 25 largest deployers collectively accounted for 57% of CEE's installed base at the end of 2022, up from 54% at end-2021. During a period of consolidation in the banking sectors of several markets, larger deployers represent a high share of the overall installed base.
