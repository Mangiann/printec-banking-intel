Body text: Bulgarian installed base declined 5% to 4,994 ATMs at end-2022, due to estate rationalisation, higher ATM fees and growing cashless payment usage (ATMs becoming less profitable). Bank branch numbers also fell (branch transformation strategies pushing customers to self-service). Off-site ATMs were the largest share of terminals at 64% end-2022 (high-footfall areas away from branches more profitable; lobby/ADT installs gained share). Number of ADTs (automated deposit terminals) rose to 768 units at end-2022; voice guidance rolled out for the first time in 2022 on some Fibank terminals. A total of 136 million cash withdrawals were made in 2022, down 9% on previous year. Value of cash withdrawals grew more, totalling BGN 45 billion (USD 24 billion) withdrawn from Bulgarian ATMs in 2022. NCR held the largest share of the market at 83%; several large deployers removed Diebold Nixdorf installations (contributing to NCR's share gain). GRG accounted for the smallest installed-base share (1%), deployed exclusively by Fibank.

### p.88 — Bulgaria, Section 2: Banking structure (body text)
- DSK Bank (owned by Hungarian OTP Group) continued to be the largest bank by branch network, with ~300 outlets, followed by CCB. These two banks were the only ones to boast over 200 branches each.
- Bulgaria grew its robust economic growth in early-to-mid 2000s, following a boom in foreign investment which further accelerated on accession to the European Union in 2007; the economic downturn that took place after the 2008 global financial crisis had a negative impact on the Bulgarian banking sector. Several banks needed to be recapitalised; confidence in the sector was further exacerbated by two bank runs on Corpbank and Fibank in 2014. Although BNB extended a line of credit to help the two banks return to normal operation, Corpbank's licence was revoked the same year.
- Bulgaria joined the European Exchange Rate Mechanism II (ERM II) in July 2020, which acts to prevent economic instability of the single market arising from fluctuations in exchange rates between euro and other EU currencies. As of early 2023, Bulgaria is preparing for adoption of the euro.
- Bulgarian banking sector saw several mergers in the late 2010s. Most recently, Raiffeisen's acquisition by KBC Group was approved in late 2021, with the bank renamed as KBC Bank and its branches rebranded in July 2022. The bank is also expected to be eventually merged with UBB, the other Bulgarian subsidiary of the KBC Group.
- In late 2022, Postbank (owned by the Greek Eurobank) reached an agreement with BNP Paribas to acquire the latter's Personal Finance subsidiary in Bulgaria, with the acquisition expected to be finalised in 2023.
- Number of bank branches in Bulgaria declined by 92 at end-2022. Banks continued to close unprofitable branches over the course of 2022, as some of them are seeing lower footfall after the outbreak of COVID-19. The decline in the branch network was less pronounced in 2022 than in the previous year, when around 270 outlets were closed.
- Banks have continued to carry out branch transformation strategies during 2022, which aim to encourage greater usage of self-service terminals for simple banking transactions, allowing branch employees to focus on value-added services.
- UniCredit Bulbank and TBI Bank saw the largest decline in their branch number, closing 16 and 15 banking outlets, respectively, during 2022. UBB and DSK Bank also contracted their branch network on a similar scale.
- Investbank and Municipal Bank were the only institutions to see a net increase in the number of bank branches, although these were very small, with each bank opening only two new branches in 2022.

### p.89 — Bulgaria, Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (3.1)
Figure 3.1: "Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022" — single network BORICA.
- BORICA installed base: 2018 = 2,880; 2019 = 2,554; 2020 = 1,992; 2021 = 1,860; 2022 = 1,749; CAGR = -11.7%.
Source: Bulgarian ATM deployers and networks and RBR analysis.
Body: UBB deployed Bulgaria's first ATM in 1995 via partnership with BORICA, the shared ATM network; UBB also first to operate terminals accepting international card-scheme cards. Installed base saw strong growth 1999-2008; from 2008 (global financial crisis) growth slowed to ~3% p.a. over next five years. In 2014 the Bulgarian ATM market experienced its first decline, terminals falling 3% — driven by bank deployers Corpbank and MK Unionbank leaving the market, and Euronet (Euronet was the only IAD operating in the market and decided to leave in 2014 after six years' presence, though it returned in 2018 with a smaller fleet than in 2014). In mid-to-late 2010s the installed base remained in fluctuation, slow growth in some years and contracting in others.
- BORICA was the only ATM sharing network at end-2022. Company formed 1995, initially owned by BNB until 2005 when 25 local banks acquired over half its shares. Early 2000s: several banks withdrew their terminals to set up their own ATM processing centres, transferring machines by 2008 — included largest bank deployers DSK Bank, UBB, Postbank and Fibank. BORICA also saw smaller deployers join: Ziraat Bank joined in 2011; Texim Bank and Bulgarian American Credit Bank followed in 2012.
- KBC Bank (formerly Raiffeisenbank) was the most recent bank to leave BORICA (2020), connecting its terminals to UPC, the Ukrainian ATM processing centre of the Raiffeisen Bank Group; after KBC acquired the bank, terminals no longer processed by UPC at end-2022.
- There were 12 BORICA members at end-2022, unchanged from prior year. Number of ATMs belonging to the network has been declining as the installed base in the market contracts steadily; its share of the installed base remained unchanged at end-2022 vs end-2021 at 35%. Mergers contributed to declining BORICA ATM numbers (e.g. CI Bank's absorption into UBB meant its former ATMs now connected to UBB's own processing network). Other banks formerly part of BORICA included Piraeus Bank and Société Générale Expressbank, which were also acquired by larger banks in 2019.
- BORICA does not enforce a standardised ATM fees agreement; instead banks choose the level of disloyalty fees they impose on cardholders for using another bank's terminal.
