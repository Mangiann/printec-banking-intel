### p.31 — Branch / off-site ATM trends (Regional Analysis, p.13)
Body text only, no figure on this page.
- Branch-based ATMs are falling faster than off-site ATMs; growth in off-site ATM installed base is the key driver — off-site grew while branch numbers fell to 2022.
- The proportion of IADs (independent ATM deployers) in the region is rising. Off-site ATMs sometimes used to broaden access to services in locations where bank branches have closed, although declining demand for cash can also lead to off-site terminals being withdrawn from lower-footfall locations once they become commercially unviable. IADs are particularly tied to deploy ATMs at locations other than bank branches, although in some markets such as Poland and Romania IADs may also deploy branch ATMs on behalf of a bank.
- 36% of ATMs deployed in CEE are located at bank branches, with the most common branch location being the lobby; branch lobbies account for 17% of the regional installed base at end-2022.
- The biggest single market is **Poland** (branch ATMs ~19%); the most successful drive-through ATM deployments are also in Poland.
- Share of ATMs installed through the external wall of the bank branch (TTW) is declining in CEE, falling from 15% at end-2018 to 11% by end-2022. While TTW ATMs are usually accessible outside of branch opening hours and therefore convenient to customers, they are seen to offer less in terms of security when compared to the lobby and can also be limited in terms of functionality, depending on what machines can be integrated with the building's size and structure.
- Bank branch halls accounted for just 8% of the regional installed base at end-2022, a share stable with end-2021. While hall ATMs are secure and offer more opportunities for customer service, they are typically only accessible during branch opening hours, making them less attractive for many deployers. Drive-through ATMs at branch premises in CEE but have grown in number by an average of 9% each year between 2018 and 2022. Of these, most are attached to bank branches, with a smaller proportion off-site, typically at fuel stations.

### p.33 — Section 1.5 Functionality and hardware features (Regional Analysis, p.15)
Body text only, no figure.
- Drive-through ATMs are present in six of the 12 primary CEE markets at end-2022, but only in very small numbers. No markets had more than 1% of their ATMs in this location.
- **1.5: Functionality and hardware features** — across the CEE region, banks are investing more in their self-service capabilities and adding new additional services and functionalities to their ATMs.
- **PIN change** is the most widely available additional service across the CEE regional installed base; available at over 99% of all primary enhanced market ATMs. Only two other services are supported at more than 90%: printed statements (93%) and mobile phone top-ups (90%).
- Transfers are commonly supported throughout the region: 87% of ATMs allow users to make a transfer to an account at the same bank, while 83% offer this service to accounts outside the bank. Over three quarters allow the user to make a bill payment via a credit transfer to a pre-arranged payee. A smaller proportion (44%) offer bill payments by scanning a barcode or QR code on the bill.
- **NFC** cash withdrawals are becoming more common, supported at 60% of the installed base by the end of 2022. Connecting the largest ATM markets in CEE, at end-2022 NFC withdrawals were possible at 67% of ATMs in Russia, 34% in Poland, and 17% in Ukraine. Other cardless withdrawals (usually facilitated via a one-time PIN to the user's phone or a QR code on the ATM screen or smartphone) are also gaining traction, available at over half of all ATMs in CEE. In Kazakhstan, cardless withdrawals are supported at almost 80% of the installed base, with Halyk Bank supporting OTP withdrawals across its whole fleet.
- Assisted self-service is extremely rare in the region, with no in-person assisted self-service ATMs installed in the three largest markets and only one ATM offering video assisted self-service, which is deployed in Russia.

Body text: 112,514 ATMs offered automated note deposit at end-2022, accounting for 48% of the CEE installed base. Note acceptance at ATMs is becoming more common as deposers invest in their service channels and move cash deposit services away from the bank counter, introducing more multifunctional ATMs.
- Russia accounts for the majority of automated deposit ATMs in CEE, with 84,281 such installations in 2022, equivalent to almost three quarters of the entire Russian installed base. The prevalence of automated note deposit ATMs is strengthening considerably in Kazakhstan, where 57% of ATMs in 2022 offered this service, up from 50% the year before. This is largely owing to fast field expansion by Kaspi Bank, which is adding large numbers of ATMs and deposit-capable machines.
- Automated note deposit ATMs account for 20% or less of the installed base in the following primary markets: Slovakia (20%), Ukraine (17%), Croatia (18%), Bulgaria (13%), Serbia (13%) and Belarus (9%).
- Cash recycling capability is a central priority for many deployers in CEE looking to enhance the efficiency and profitability of their fleets thanks to the savings it can offer, including on CIT services. By end-2022, there were 92,475 cash recycling ATMs in the region, equivalent to 39% of the CEE installed base. Russia accounted for 91% of these, having increased its recycler total significantly in 2022 thanks to progression in a fleet update by Sberbank.
- Poland also has considerable numbers of recycling ATMs with 7,278 (71% of its automated deposit ATMs), while Kazakhstan has 4,121, equivalent to 59% of its base of automated note deposit ATMs. Several countries had small numbers of automated note deposit ATMs, but a strong majority of these were recyclers including Hungary 99%, Belarus, 79% in Slovakia and 73% in Romania.
- Recycling remains uncommon in Serbia, but the number of recyclers grew from 28 to 40 between end-2021 and end-2022. Bulgaria was the primary market with the smallest number of recyclers with just eight, but this marked growth from just a single recycling ATM in deployment the previous year.
- Stand-alone terminals (SATs) offering automated deposit functionality have historically been prominent in CEE and have been used in many markets in the region as an economical way of providing customers with automated note deposits. SATs were often manufactured locally with single note acceptors, making them cheaper to procure than multifunctional ATMs. SATs' position in the market has been weakening in recent years, however, with many deployers removing SATs in large numbers and providing investment in their ATMs.

### p.37 — Touch screens, video & assisted self-service, integrated security cameras (Regional Analysis, p.19)
Body text only, no figure.
- Touch screens are relatively uncommon in CEE despite being present at an ostensibly high 67% of terminals. Russia inflates the overall regional figure by accounting for 95% of touch screens across enhanced markets, with Ukraine representing 3% and Poland the remaining 2%. Across the primary markets in general, Speech capability for the visually impaired is supported at 41% of the CEE installed base but, once again, the vast majority of these (93%) are found in Russia and few are available in the rest of CEE. Many markets, such as Romania, do not offer this facility at all. Poland (at 1% installed base) supports it at only a handful of ATMs, although the use at this location is growing fast.
- Video cameras for assisted self-service are scarce in the region, with just one ATM offering this facility in Russia and none elsewhere.
- In terms of security, anti-skimming devices are the most commonly used feature. Croatia is the only market to have 100% of its installed base equipped with anti-skimming devices, but they are also widespread in Slovakia (82%), Bulgaria (80%), Belarus (78%) and Ukraine (77%). All other primary markets have at least half of their installed base equipped with this feature.
- In CEE, built-in or integrated security cameras are also common — the primary markets with the highest proportion of ATMs with cameras are Belarus (100%), Kazakhstan (80%), Russia (77%) and Ukraine (73%). Croatia has the lowest proportion with 36% of its installed base. Apart from Poland with 49%, over half of all ATMs in all other primary markets are equipped with built-in or integrated security cameras.
- Note degradation is not very common; however several markets have employed this feature. Croatia has 18% of ATMs installed with note degradation at end-2022, while in Poland it is on the present in Slovakia (11%), Czech Republic (9%), Hungary (5%), in Poland (2%) and Romania (negligible).
- Biometric identification is even rarer, with only Poland, Russia, and Kazakhstan installing this technology. Many deployers in Kazakhstan in particular are interested in this feature, while in Poland it is on the wane with the introduction of BLIK cash withdrawals. Fingerprint identification is the most commonly used form of biometric identification in these markets, although Russia's Sberbank has implemented facial recognition on some of its terminals.

Body text: The total number of ATM cash withdrawals made in CEE has declined every year since 2018. The most pronounced decline over this period was in 2020 when the number of cash withdrawals fell by a sharp 16%, a result of the COVID-19 pandemic and lockdowns across the CEE region limiting customer traffic at ATMs.
- The decline in withdrawals slowed by 4%, but in 2022 withdrawals fell by 9% to a total of 4,516. Weakening demand for cash in CEE is the main factor behind the decline, with the shift to cashless and mobile payments advancing further in 2022. New customer habits to spend less in cash and make fewer cash withdrawals were accelerated during the COVID-19 pandemic and have become further entrenched since, driving down withdrawal levels in 2022. This trend is reflected in the average number of cash withdrawals made per ATM per month which has also been falling since 2018 as customer footfall lessens.
- Ukraine contributed the most to the decline in 2022, with 263 million fewer cash withdrawals in 2022 than in 2021. This 34% drop was a result of martial law restricting citizens' ability to leave their homes, as well as new legislation that enabled cash withdrawals at retail stores like petrol stations and supermarkets, reducing the need for separate trips to ATMs. Destruction of infrastructure, contributing to the reduction in cash withdrawals, as well as the government's promotion of cashless payments.
- The next largest proportional decline was in Belarus, which saw cash withdrawals fall by 14%, followed by Russia with an 8% decrease. In both cases, the main factor was increasing usage of cashless payments, particularly mobile wallets, among consumers.
