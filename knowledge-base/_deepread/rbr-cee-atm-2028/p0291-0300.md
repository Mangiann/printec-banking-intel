Body: Off-site locations account for a growing majority of ATMs in Russia, with the proportion of ATMs placed in third-party sites like supermarkets, shopping centres and transport hubs increasing from 60% to 61% between end-2021 and end-2022. Salary card projects are widespread across Russia, meaning there are many ATMs present on company premises or in office complexes as employers partner with a specific bank to provide salary payments, cards and other banking services to their staff.
Rapid expansion by Tinkoff Bank, a branchless, digital bank (whose network of which is off-site, has been driving off-site deployment in Russia in recent years). Its addition of 1,500 ATMs in 2021 and 500 in 2022 swelled overall off-site ATM numbers. Post-Bank has a presence at its fleet off-site (with 86%), while Otkritie Bank has 73%.
Russian deployers generally favour off-site deployment for its accessibility and profitability benefits, as ATMs in high-footfall locations typically have higher usage and are convenient for customers. Off-site ATMs also allow deployers to fill the gap left in service provision by bank branch closures. While the majority (56%) of Sberbank ATMs are also off-site, this is a relatively low proportion in comparison to other deployers, mostly owing to Sberbank's branch transformation and rebranding programme which has seen it develop dedicated self-service zones in branches which offer multiple services to support a wider "Sber ecosystem", including online order collections and refreshments.

### p.293 — Russia: Locations (text continued)
The lobby is the second most common location for ATMs in Russia, representing 24% of all installations at end-2022, a stable level with the previous year. While branch numbers are falling in Russia, the proportion of lobby ATMs has remained resilient because of bank branch transformation and optimisation programmes which see lobby areas re-designed and fitted with multi-functional terminals. As stated, Sberbank has been leading the way on such strategies and has the largest proportion of lobby ATMs, accounting for 32% of its fleet. Gazprombank has 31% of its ATM base placed in its branch lobbies. Some deployers also favour the lobby area as it offers the accessibility advantages of off-site ATMs (lobbies are commonly open after bank closing hours), while providing enhanced security to both bank and customers, as well as a warm environment to perform transactions during Russia's cold winters (unlike through-the-wall ATMs).
The hall is less commonly used for ATM installations, accounting for 14% of the installed base at end-2022. This location offers some advantages, namely security and opportunities for assistance and cross-selling by bank staff. Its main limitation, however, is that hall ATMs are typically only accessible during branch opening hours; Post-Bank is the main deployer of ATMs in the hall in Russia, with 98% of its fleet in this location at end-2022. No other bank comes close to this level of hall deployment, but a quarter of Promsvyazbank's ATM network is in the bank hall.
Through-the-wall (TTW) is the least used location for ATMs in Russia, with just 2% of the installed base in such locations. While TTW ATMs are highly accessible, they are also perceived as less secure than other branch ATMs, can necessitate structural changes to buildings and can be impacted by harsh weather conditions. Russian Agricultural Bank is the only deployer with a meaningful proportion of TTW ATMs, with 17%.
There are no drive-through ATMs known to be deployed in Russia.

### p.295 — Russia: Automated deposit / recycling functionality
**Figure 4.5: ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022** (Type of deposit | ATMs | SATs):

- of which: note recycling: 74,953 | N/A
- Automated cheque deposit: 0 | 0
- Total: 84,281 | 65.3%
(Source: CBRF, Russian ATM deployers and RBR analysis)

Body: PIN-change and mobile phone top-ups are universally available across the Russian installed base. It is possible to make bill payments (via a credit transfer to a pre-arranged payee) at 94% of all ATMs. Some ATMs equipped with QR code and barcode readers — such as those deployed by Post-Bank — can also offer bill payments through reading a code on a printed bill, and this is currently possible at 58% of all ATMs.
Transfers to an account at the same bank are supported at 94% of all ATMs, while transfers to an account with a different bank can be made at 93%. Bank statements can also be provided at 93% of the installed base.
Contactless cash withdrawals via NFC, in Russia first developed in 2015, when Alfa-Bank rolled out this feature for all Mastercard cards, followed by Visa cards and smartphones in 2017. Other banks followed suit and NFC withdrawals were available at 67% of all ATMs at end-2022, up from 65% the previous year. Sberbank, Tinkoff Bank and Raiffeisenbank all offer such withdrawals across their whole fleets.
Post-Bank deploys a number of ATMs which offer withdrawal, as well as other functions such as deposits and balance enquiry, via a QR code on the customer's phone. Tinkoff Bank also supports QR code withdrawals, displayed on a customer's phone through their Tinkoff Bank app.
Financial products including loans, insurance and savings accounts are also on offer at many Russian ATMs, sold on 29% of machines at end-2022. Deployers such as VTB Bank, Post-Bank and Alfa-Bank offer a range of products across their whole fleets. Third-party products are less commonly available, at just 5% of machines, although Sberbank has enabled services including taxi and food orders at some of its new-generation ATMs.
The proportion of ATMs offering automated deposits grew from 65% of the installed base at end-2021 to 74% by end-2022, with a total of 84,281 ATMs offering this functionality. Of these, 74,953 also recycle banknotes, equal to 66% of all ATMs in Russia. Automated deposit functionality has formed a core part of deployer self-service and cost-effectiveness strategies in recent years, allowing banks to shift services away from the counter.
Recycling is the next step in this strategy, providing even more cost efficiency possibilities for deployers, including savings on CIT. Post-Bank and Tinkoff Bank deploy only recycling ATMs, while other deployers have been increasing numbers in recent years — recyclers made up 96% of Sberbank's fleet at end-2022, up from 72% the year before. As well as adding new recyclers, the bank removed a large number of older, non-recycling ATMs, which also pushed up the proportion of recycling-capable machines.

### p.296 — Russia: Hardware and security features (Section 4.4 continued)
Body: Russia is the largest market in the world for stand-alone deposit terminals (SATs), but SATs' position in the self-service environment is weakening every year, falling to 65.3% units at end-2022 from 75,332 (-13%) at end-2021. Banks originally deployed SATs in large numbers as a cheaper alternative to deposit ATMs and a useful tool for reducing queuing times at the bank counter. Deployers are now modernising their fleets and prioritising the installation of more recycling ATMs to benefit from true efficiency advantages this ATM type can offer. There is no longer a strong business case for SATs, which are being removed by almost every deployer in large numbers each year.

QR code readers are available on all Sberbank, Otkritie Bank, Tinkoff Bank and Post-Bank ATMs. Tinkoff Bank and Post-Bank both support QR code withdrawals.
A large majority (85%) of Russian ATMs have touch screens, including all Sberbank, Post-Bank and Tinkoff Bank ATMs. 51% of ATMs had speech capability for visually impaired users at end-2022, up from 49% in 2021. One feature made available to improving access to financial services for customers with disabilities were published.
There is a single ATM in Russia offering assisted self-service via video, part of a pilot programme run by VTB Bank. The service has not yet been rolled out to other VTB Bank ATMs or by other deployers to their fleets.
Biometric identification is enabled at 23% of all ATMs at end-2022, with around half of the bank's machines equipped. Sberbank is enabling face biometric ID at a number of its ATMs and aims to roll out across its fleet. There is some debate around customer adoption of and trust in such methods. Anti-skimming devices are present on 80% of all ATMs and 77% have an integrated security camera; VTB Bank deploys cameras across its whole fleet.
In terms of security features, most ATMs are equipped with anti-skimming devices but note degradation is not known to be currently used by Russian deployers.

### p.297 — Russia: Number and value of cash withdrawals (Section 4.5)
**Figure 4.7: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022** (line/bar chart, left axis Annual number, million [left axis 0-3,500]; line = Average per ATM per month [right axis 0-2,500]). 
Table (2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | CAGR):

- Average cash withdrawals per ATM per month: 2,092 | 1,968 | 1,627 | 1,587 | 1,518 | -7.7%

(Source: CBRF, Russian ATM deployers and RBR analysis)

Body: The number of cash withdrawals has fallen every year in Russia since 2017. The main factor in this trend is increasing usage of cashless payment methods among Russian consumers, who are some of the most active users of mobile wallets globally. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated this trend, with withdrawal numbers falling by a particularly sharp 18% in 2020, the peak of the pandemic. This decline has slowed but continued, with the total dropping by 7% in 2021 and 8% in 2022.
Customer habits of withdrawing less cash and performing more of their in-person spending with cards or smartphones are now ingrained, although cash usage is stronger outside of major cities. Russian and international media reported a rush of activity at ATMs in February and March 2022 as Russians panicked by the crisis withdrew savings from ATMs en masse. This burst of activity was short-lived, however, and did not drive up the whole-year ATM withdrawal figures.
The average number of cash withdrawals per ATM per month is also declining each year, falling to 1,518 in 2022 from 1,587 the year before. Of all deployers, Sberbank typically sees the highest level of monthly ATM usage owing to its large client profile of pensioners and other recipient of state benefits, a segment more likely to access their funds via regular cash withdrawals than ATMs.
Cash withdrawals as a proportion of ATM transactions stayed stable at 61% in 2021, although it has generally been declining in recent years as banks enhance the functionality of their ATMs and customers carry out an increasing number of deposits and transfers at ATMs.

Body: Despite the long-term decline in the number of cash withdrawals, the value of withdrawals has grown consistently in recent years, with the exception of 2020 when the impact of COVID-19 on customer activity was enough to push the total value of cash withdrawals into decline.
In 2022, the value of cash withdrawals rose by 5% to reach RUB 31.1 trillion (USD 451 billion). High inflation, with the Russian inflation rate standing at 14% in 2022, played a major role in this rise. Changing customer habits which see Russians use ATMs less often, but offset their reduced usage by taking out higher sums at each transaction, was also a factor. This trend is evident in the rising average value of a cash withdrawal, which increased by 15% to RUB 14,514 (USD 211) in 2022. Some customers also took out unusually large sums at specific points of instability and panic during 2022, including the outbreak of war in February 2022, the wave of sanctions in March and the announcement of partial mobilisation in September, activity which also contributed to the rising average withdrawal value.
Cash withdrawal value as a proportion of GDP continued its long-term decline in 2022, demonstrating the increasing shift to cashless payment methods in the country.

### p.300 — Section 5: Manufacturer market shares (Russia, text continued; end of Russia chapter)
Body: Thanks to deliveries to Sberbank, Hyosung Innovue overtook NCR in 2021 to become the largest supplier present in the market and its lead was strengthened significantly in 2022. The most common Hyosung Innovue model deployed by Sberbank is the MX8600.
Other deployers of Hyosung Innovue ATMs in Russia include VTB Bank, with over 2,000 installations, and Raiffeisenbank with nearly 400.
NCR remained the second largest manufacturer present in the market, but its share fell from 32% at end-2021 to 26% at end-2022. NCR's share has been falling steadily in Russia for several years due to removals by Sberbank and other banks investing in more ATMs from rivals. NCR ATMs were deployed by most Russian banks including VTB Bank, Post-Bank, Raiffeisenbank, Russian Agricultural Bank and Gazprombank. The most common western models deployed in Russia were SelfServ 22, 25 and 32.
Diebold Nixdorf retained the third largest share of the market, but this fell from 28% to 25% with the removal of over 5,000 Diebold Nixdorf ATMs from Russia. The manufacturer's exit from the market accelerated its long-term share decline. VTB Bank is the largest deployer of Diebold Nixdorf ATMs, but reduced its base by several hundred in 2022. Diebold Nixdorf ATMs are also deployed by Sberbank, Post-Bank and other banks. The most common Diebold Nixdorf models deployed by Sberbank, the CS 4060 and CS 4060.
OKI, the fourth largest manufacturer, saw its share increase from 4% to 6% by the end of 2022, and was one of the suppliers the CBRF recommended banks to use following the exit of NCR and Diebold Nixdorf. The G7 and G8 models are the most commonly reported OKI ATMs in deployment in Russia. The largest deployer of OKI ATMs are Otkritie Bank and Tinkoff Bank, each with 1,400 OKI units, and Alfa-Bank, with 1,336.
GRG's presence on the market remained small, with just 1% of the installed base, but its supplier reach increased in the ATM sector in 2022 owing to Russia's enduring close ties with China and the sanctions placed on western suppliers. GRG models used by banks include the H68NL and H68NL.
New City, a Russian manufacturer, remains a small player on the market, with under 200 ATMs in operation (all deployed by Sberbank) and a negligible share of the installed base. The supplier is set, however, to ramp up its ATM manufacturing activities, part of a wider strategy to reduce reliance on imports and reinvigorate Russian-made technology.
SFOUR, Saga and BTE, all Russian SAT manufacturers or importers of ATMs from overseas, have also declared their intention to manufacture their own ATMs and provide a domestic supply of terminals to the market. While these activities were in their early stages in 2022, banks are likely to start making Russian-supplied ATM installations in 2023.
