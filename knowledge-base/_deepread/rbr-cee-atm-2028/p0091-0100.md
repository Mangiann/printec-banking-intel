Source: Bulgarian ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
Text: number of automated note deposit terminals (ADTs) increased by 2% from end-2021, to stand at 768 in 2022. Although dispense-only ATM installations are declining, ADTs are seeing growth each year as deployers aim to optimise their terminals and equip them with a wider range of facilities. This is particularly important as banks are choosing to transform branches into places where employees focus on value-added services and encourage customers to turn to self-service channels, be it ATMs or digital banking. Note recycling remains a rare feature on ATMs (8 / ~1% of installed base). UniCredit Bulbank boasted the largest number of deposit ATMs in the market, with a total of 171 machines. Note recycling is a known deployer of recycling terminals (Bulgaria had no recycling functionality). One ATM commonly found facilitates buying/loading of bus/transport-card top-ups; ATMs belonging to Fibank and KBC Bank, two of the largest deployers in the market. Anti-skimming devices and built-in cameras are common across Bulgarian ATMs, installed on 80% and 71% of terminals respectively. Touch screens present on just under one fifth of the installed base, with many recent new installations equipped with this feature, particularly note deposit terminals. Footer: Bulgaria: Page 10.

### p.96 — Section 4.5 Number and value of cash withdrawals; Figure 4.5: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022

- Growth in number of cash withdrawals: 89% | 90% | 90% | 90% | 89% | (neg.)
Source: Bulgarian Central Bank (BCB) and RBR analysis.
Text: number of cash withdrawals made by Bulgarian ATMs increased by 9% in 2022, to stand at a total of 136 million. This growth was higher than in the previous year, as Bulgarian consumers returned to making regular and more frequent cash withdrawals at ATMs in 2022. Cash remains a widely used payment method in the market, which drives continued growth in the number of cash withdrawals. The average number of cash withdrawals made at an ATM per month stood at 2,216, which was the highest average in the past five years. This average increased aside from more frequent ATM usage compared to 2021; the ATM installed base fell at a slower pace in 2022 than in the previous year. Cash withdrawals have represented a similar proportion of ATM transactions between 2018 and 2022, accounting for ~89% in 2022. Bulgarian consumers typically associate ATMs with cash withdrawals and these terminals usually have a limited range of facilities available to customers. Nevertheless, automated note deposit is an increasingly prevalent facility found on Bulgarian ATMs, and the share of note deposits has been increasing, reaching around 7% in 2022. Text: Bulgarian installed base expected to decline by 2% each year on average between 2023 and 2028, down to 4,400 ATMs by the end of the forecast period. With large-scale estate rationalisations having taken place in 2022, particularly by large deployers, it is expected they will continue to evaluate the profitability of their ATMs and remove those that are obsolete or do not see enough customer traffic. The IAD operating in the market is one of the few deployers expected to grow its ATM fleet as it has stated its intention to expand into rural areas which are typically lacking in ATM infrastructure; this expansion is not set to be large-scale. Also discusses development of digital banking channels and rationalisation of merged banks' branch networks. Footer: Bulgaria: Page 14.
