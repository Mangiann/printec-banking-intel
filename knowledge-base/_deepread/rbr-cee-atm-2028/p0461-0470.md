Body text: BiH is split into two federal entities — Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina (capital Sarajevo) and Republika Srpska (capital Banja Luka). 15 banks operate in BiH at end-2022, with 12 headquartered in the Federation and three in Republika Srpska; most offices concentrated in the two capitals. Three banks have subsidiaries in both entities: NLB Bank, UniCredit Bank, Addiko Bank. Russia's Sberbank previously operated in both entities before exiting in 2022 (Nova Banka in Republika Srpska + ASA Banka in Federation acquired its assets). Total banking-sector assets grew ~4% in 2022 to BAM 37.6bn (USD 21.3bn); ~74% held by Federation, remainder by Republika Srpska. UniCredit Bank = 17% of total assets (highest single share / largest); Raiffeisen Bank BAM 6.5bn (USD 3.7bn) = 13% with BAM 5.1bn (USD 2.9bn); Nova Banka Banja Luka BAM 2.8bn (USD 1.6bn) = 7% (third largest by total assets).

### p.467 — Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services; Figure 2.1 (page 4)
Section 2.1 ATM networks and sharing. BiH ATM market began early 2000s but sharing networks only established over a decade later. First branded ATM sharing network, BH Mreža Bankomata, formed in 2013 — consisted of Bosna Bank International, Privredna Banka Sarajevo, Union Banka Sarajevo and ASA Banka; later joined by Vakufska Banka Sarajevo in 2016 (membership lasted until its 2021 acquisition by ASA Banka). The Bamcard processing centre provides card-transaction infrastructure; Visa debit cardholders from member banks can make withdrawals with no surcharge from the network's ATMs.
Figure 2.1 — Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022 (BH Mreža Bankomata members count): 2018 = 136; 2019 = 138; 2020 = 149; 2021 = 152; 2022 = 207; CAGR = 11.1%.
(Sources: BH Mreža Bankomata, Banking Agency of the Federation of BiH, Banking Agency of Republika Srpska and RBR analysis.)

Section 3: Current ATM market — 3.1 Market size and growth.
Figure 3.1 — Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022 (combo: bars = ATM installed base [left axis, 0-1,800], line with diamonds = annual % growth [right axis, -6% to 6%]):
- ATMs: 2018 = 1,584; 2019 = 1,635; 2020 = 1,670; 2021 = 1,636; 2022 = 1,546; CAGR = -0.6%
- Absolute growth: 2018 = 36; 2019 = 51; 2020 = 35; 2021 = -34; 2022 = -90; CAGR = N/A
- Percentage growth: 2018 = 2.3%; 2019 = 3.2%; 2020 = 2.1%; 2021 = -2.0%; 2022 = -5.5%; CAGR = N/A
Chart take-away: installed base peaked at 1,670 in 2020 then declined; growth line rises from ~2.3% (2018) to peak ~3.2% (2019), eases to ~2.1% (2020), turns negative -2.0% (2021) and falls to -5.5% (2022).
Commentary: Number of ATMs grew at an average ~3% per year between end-2018 and end-2020. Market has declined every year since, contracting 2% in 2021 and ~6% in 2022. In 2022 only a minority of deployers expanded their estate and many reduced deployment numbers. Union Banka Sarajevo had the largest absolute increase — 135 newly deployed terminals; followed by ASA Banka with 60 (second consecutive year of acquisition-driven significant increase for ASA Banka). UniCredit Bank Mostar had the most substantial reduction in absolute terms, removing 183 machines to mitigate costs. Addiko Bank Banja Luka was the second most active bank in consolidating its fleet, removing 37 of its terminals.
