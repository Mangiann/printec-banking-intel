### p.517 — Latvia, Banking Structure (narrative continued)
Country = Latvia (footer "Latvia: Page 2"). Prose only, no figures/tables.
- Luminor Bank: Latvian subsidiary of Luminor banking group (HQ Estonia); ~3rd-largest
  bank by assets in Latvia at end-2022. Group emerged 2017 from merger of Baltic units
  of Nordea and DNB; Blackstone-led private equity group bought a majority stake;
  Nordea/DNB reduced ~20% stake; in Sept 2022 Blackstone completed acquisition of
  Nordea's ~20% stake. Latvian banking industry has faced consolidation/branch closures.
  PrivatBank, Baltic International Bank and SEB references; SEB at risk of losing
  banking licence in 2019. Estonian authorities ordered Danske Bank's Estonia branch to
  cease operations (Feb 2019 — money-laundering scandal). Rietumu Banka established 1992.
  In 2021 the FCMC approved peer-to-peer/marketplace banking operators; international
  scandals affected confidence. Notes general international banking decline; ~level
  international bank shut down in early 2020.

### p.519 — Latvia, Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services
Country = Latvia (footer "Latvia: Page 4"). Sources: Latvian banks/ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
- **Section 2.1 narrative:** Latvia's first ATMs installed 1994 (Saules Banka, now
  Rietumu Banka) — first ATMs that could process transactions on internationally branded
  cards, deployed following year. No single shared ATM network; the two largest deployers
  Swedbank and SEB banka have a sharing agreement which, as of end-2022, covered **64%**
  of the installed base; customers of the two banks can withdraw cash from each other's
  ATMs up to monthly limits. Rietumu Banka previously part of this agreement, withdrew 2016.
  Agreement previously included DNB and Nordea (separate arrangement with Citadele);
  terminated 2017 after DNB+Nordea merged into Luminor.
- **Figure 2.1: ATMs by deployer, 2021 and 2022** (Deployer | 2021 | 2022 | Change):

  | Deployer | 2021 | 2022 | Change |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Swedbank | 363 | 362 | -1 |
  | SEB banka | 211 | 210 | -1 |
  | Luminor Bank | 174 | 173 | -1 |
  | Citadele banka | 148 | 147 | -1 |
  | BlueOrange Bank | 5 | 4 | -1 |
  | **Total** | **901** | **896** | **-5** |

- Further narrative: market is highly concentrated, with only five deployers. Swedbank
  the largest, then SEB banka, Luminor, Citadele, BlueOrange. Most ATMs accept payment
  cards previously issued by the bank that owns the terminal as well as cards on
  international networks; BlueOrange Bank, smallest deployer, had four ATMs in 2022 — the
  bank established 1992 and originally called Baltic International Bank. Notes ATM-network/
  recycling and automated note-deposit/bill-payment functionality being introduced.

### p.520 — Latvia, Section 3: Current ATM market (market size & growth)
Country = Latvia (footer "Latvia: Page 5"). Sources: Latvijas Banka, Latvian ATM
deployers and RBR analysis.
- **Figure 3.1: Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022** (combo chart: blue bars = ATMs
  [left axis], black diamond line = growth [right axis]). Left axis 0-1,200; right axis
  0% down to -8%. Data table below the chart:

  | | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | CAGR |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | ATMs | 991 | 930 | 906 | 901 | 896 | -2.5% |
  | Absolute growth | -24 | -61 | -24 | -5 | -5 | N/A |
  | Percentage growth | -2.4% | -6.2% | -2.6% | -0.6% | -0.6% | N/A |

  Chart confirms: steady decline in installed base every year; sharpest drop in 2019
  (growth ~-6.2%), then decline rate eases to ~-0.6% in 2021 and 2022.
- Narrative: between end-2021 and end-2022 the number of ATMs fell from 901 to 896. Number
  of ATMs has fallen partly due to a contraction in the number of banks from liquidations/
  mergers, with some former deployers ceasing operations over the period; installed base
  has been generally declining each year since 2011. The decline has been more substantial
  in 2019 (with the exception of 2012 and 2013 when there was some limited expansion). The
  installed base affected by the number of ATMs operated by Luminor Bank and Citadele banka
  (removing ~49 and ~21 ATMs respectively). Banks rationalising fleets in response to cost-
  saving/efficiency measures; reducing branch footprint; rolling out higher-functionality
  machines (recycling, automated note-deposit, bill-payment) on fewer terminals.
