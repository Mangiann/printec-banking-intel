### p.581 — Turkmenistan, Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services
- **2.1 ATM networks and sharing.** In 2001 the Central Bank of Turkmenistan launched Altyn Asyr ("Golden Age"), a national payment system / domestic card scheme designed for full interoperability of transactions between banks and the two existing proprietary bank payment systems, namely Turkmenbart (operated by Turkmenistan State Commercial Bank) and Millikart (State Bank for Foreign Economic Affairs). Altyn Asyr incorporates a single processing centre, managed by state-owned Halkbank. Altyn Asyr cards make up the majority of cards issued in Turkmenistan and are enabled for domestic use only. Several banks in the country issue Visa and Mastercard cards, but these are generally blocked from international use.
- **Figure 2.1: Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022** (sole deployer Altyn Asyr): 2018: 2,176 / 2019: 2,198 / 2020: 2,162 / 2021: 2,158 / 2022: 2,157; CAGR -0.3% (all ATMs participate in the national Altyn Asyr sharing arrangement).
- **Figure 2.2: Participation in domestic sharing agreements by deployer, 2022** — deployers listed each marked participating (×): Dayhanbank; Turkmenistan State Commercial Bank; Turkmenbashi State Commercial Bank; Halkbank; Senagat Bank; State Bank for Foreign Economic Affairs; Rysgal Bank; Turkmen-Turkish Bank; Central Bank of Turkmenistan. Total participants = 9 (all deployers participate).
- Note: most banks deployed ATMs in the mid-2000s; ATM growth accelerated in the mid-2010s with the installed base trebling in size in 2016. The expansion of ATMs over this period was driven by a government mandate to migrate all salary/benefit payments of public-sector and state employees from cash to card.

### p.582 — Turkmenistan, Section 2 (cont.): ATMs by deployer
- ATM numbers accelerated rapidly between the mid-2010s, with the installed base trebling in size in 2016. Expansion of ATMs over this period was driven by a government mandate to migrate salary, benefit and welfare payments and student grants from cash to card. Cash withdrawals from ATMs spiked initially as state workers shifted from cash to card.
- The country's capital city of Ashgabat is home to the highest number of ATMs in the country, with 449 at the end of 2022. It is closely followed by the velayat (region) of Mary and Lebap, in which 443 and 459 machines are located respectively. These two provinces are of great economic significance, due to the abundance of natural gas and cotton production found there.

  - Chart shows a flat/slightly declining installed base — peaked in 2019 (~2,198), dipped to ~2,157-2,162 thereafter.
- Narrative: ATM deployment grew rapidly in the mid-2010s; growth slowed and the installed base contracted for the first time in 2020. In 2022 the market contracted for the third successive year, with 21 machines removed by deployers. Decreases in ATM numbers can be attributed to the ongoing economic turbulence and cash shortages in Turkmenistan. As a result of low cash stocks, it is highly likely that the number of operational ATMs is lower than the official installed base.
- Cash shortages: long queues; cash withdrawals limited to ~USD 18 equivalent per transaction in 2017 (mentioned across the page narrative). COVID-19 / pandemic limited cross-border activity. Cash demand remained high owing to low levels of trust in financial institutions.

### p.584 — Turkmenistan, Section 3.2: Density measures
- Narrative: Turkmen cardholders are encouraged to make payments by card rather than withdrawing large sums (monthly benefits or entire salary) at ATMs in one transaction; card acceptance, while increasing, remains low and is generally limited to large supermarkets and high-end retailers; card payments are commonly refused even where EFTPOS terminals are officially present.
- **Figure 3.2: Density measures, 2022** (measure / Turkmenistan / Rank / CEE):
  - ATMs per million people: 332 / rank 22 / CEE 573
  - ATMs per 100 bank branches: 1,327 / rank 1 / CEE 341
  - ATMs per 100 bank branches and post offices: 696 / rank 1 / CEE 147
  - ATMs per 1,000 km²: 4 / rank 26 / CEE 10
  - ATMs per USD billion GDP: 27 / rank 21 / CEE 47
- Turkmenistan has a low ATM density relative to population (332 per million, end-2022). Owing to the large uninhabited (mostly desert) landmass there are just 4 ATMs per 1,000 km². Highest ratio of ATMs to bank branches in the region (1,327 per 100 branches vs CEE average 341) due to the small number of branches.

Modest single-digit annual growth expected to ~350 per million.

- Narrative: At end-2022 the Uzbek banking sector is regulated by the Central Bank of Uzbekistan (CBU), made up of 31 commercial banks (compared with 33 in 2021) before Turkiston Bank and Hi-Tech Bank closed down. On 7 October 2022 the CBU revoked the licences of both Turkiston Bank and Hi-Tech Bank, after the process of liquidation began. The Turkiston InterBond (Economic Court) ruled Turkiston Bank and Hi-Tech Bank bankrupt on 15 February 2023 and 17 March 2023 respectively.
- While the government has a majority stake in almost all of the biggest banks, the banking sector is expected to be further privatised in the future, in an attempt to increase transparency and attract foreign investment.
- In September 2017 a currency reform took place: the Uzbekistani som was no longer tethered to its US dollar peg, and a floating exchange rate was adopted instead. This led to a sharp increase in the exchange rate, with the rate rising from ~UZS 4,210 per US dollar in 2016 to ~UZS 8,100+ per US dollar in 2022. As a result of the weak exchange rate and the prevalence of small-denomination notes, it is common for Uzbekistani citizens to carry large sums of cash. A UZS 100,000 (equivalent to USD 9) banknote was introduced in 2019.
- In 2021 several banks introduced new measures to address queuing at ATMs, including a requirement to collect tokens for ATM usage from branches before being able to make withdrawals. Cash shortages continued into 2022, with queues at ATMs in larger cities — and particularly Ashgabat (as printed; refers to large urban centres) — remaining problematic.
- Since the CBU's 2018 relaxation of regulatory requirements for foreign banks entering the market, several new players have emerged. Halyk Bank, the largest bank in Kazakhstan, opened a subsidiary called Tenge Bank in Uzbekistan in 2019. Georgia's TBC Bank entered Uzbekistan in 2020, becoming the country's first digital bank. Another digital bank, domestic Anor Bank, entered the market later that same year. Kapitalbank's mobile application, Apelsin, which was launched in 2019, obtained a licence from the central bank in November 2021, allowing it to operate as a digital bank in its own right.

- Narrative: At end-2022 Uzbekistan had a total of 2,375 bank branches, comprising 832 full-service branches and 1,534 "mini-banks" and banking service centres that offer a narrower range of services. Branch numbers increased ~13% in 2022, accounting for 271 of the country's total branches; Xalq Bank added the greatest number of outlets over the year (opening ~30 new outlets in 2022). Agrobank, which has the biggest branch network, expanded its operations by opening 30 new outlets in 2022. By assets, Uzbekistan's banks are dominated by a small number of large state banks: the four largest by branch network are Agrobank, Xalq Bank, Mikrokreditbank and NBU. Despite having four times the branch network of NBU, Agrobank's assets were valued at UZS 51 trillion (equivalent to USD 5 billion) by the end of 2022, making it the fourth-largest bank in that regard.
- The banking sector's total assets reached UZS 557 trillion (equivalent to USD 49 billion) by end of 2022, indicating a growth of 25% from the previous year's level. The National Bank of Uzbekistan (NBU), which is the fourth-largest bank in terms of branch network but the largest lender in the country, retained its position as the bank with the highest assets, totalling UZS 111 billion (as printed) ... Kapitalbank holds the largest institution by this measure, holding assets worth UZS 44 trillion (USD 4 billion) at end-2022.
