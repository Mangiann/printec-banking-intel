Body text key facts:
- ATMs in Kazakhstan decreased for the second year running in 2022 (negligibly) to 12,391 units; small-scale reductions by some deployers partly in response to declining cash demand, but some major deployers still made significant fleet additions.
- Branch numbers fell faster than ATMs, declining by 2%; branches have fallen every year since 2015 due to digitisation; majority of off-site ATMs growing, installed in office buildings and shopping centres.
- Basic services (PIN change, printed statements) near-universal; cardless transactions for withdrawals being rolled out (QR code / one-time PIN); banks increasingly adding automated note-deposit and recycling-capable models for CIT savings.
- Number of cash withdrawals in 2022 declined by 4% to total 260 million; total value of withdrawals rose 8%, driven by high inflation and larger ticket withdrawals.
- Manufacturer shares: Diebold Nixdorf largest at 54% of all installations; NCR 21%; GRG rose from 17% to 20%; Hyosung Innovue remaining 4%.
- ATM installed base expected to return to growth 2023-2028, growing at avg annual rate under 1% to reach 13,000 ATMs by end of period.

### p.190 — Kazakhstan, Section 2 banking structure history (continued, narrative only)
No new figures/tables; banking sector M&A history:
- Post-2008 crisis: sovereign wealth fund Samruk Kazyna bailed out four of country's largest banks. 2009: two new banks — Russia's VTB Bank and domestic Kassa Nova Bank (later renamed Freedom Finance Bank after 2020 sale by ForteBank to US-based Freedom Holding Corp's Freedom Finance group). 2010: Al Hilal Bank (Islamic bank, Abu Dhabi) entered.
- 2010s M&A: UniCredit Group sold majority stake in ATFBank to KazNitrogenGaz (2013); Halyk Bank acquired HSBC's local subsidiary (became Altyn Bank, 2014); 2014 Samruk Kazyna sold 80% of TemirBank and 16% of Alliance Bank for KZT 37 billion (USD 210 million) to a private investor — both subsequently merged into ForteBank (completed 2015).
- 2016: Eurasian Bank absorbed EU Bank (formerly BankPositif, Turkish). 2017: South Korea's Kookmin Bank sold 42% stake in Bank CenterCredit.
- 2018: bank count fell by four; NBRK removed licenses of Qazaq Banki, Bank Astana, Eximbank; Halyk Bank completed merger with Qazkom (acquired 2017) — enabled by Qazkom's large NPL portfolio / recapitalisation need.
- 2018: China CITIC Bank bought majority stake in Altyn Bank (Halyk retained 40%). 2019: USD 3.4 billion bailout of Tsesnabank (bad agricultural loans); avoided liquidation after First Heartland Securities acquired 99.5% of ordinary shares and rebranded it Jusan Bank.
- 2019-2020: Al Hilal Bank became part of UAE's Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank Group (May 2019); Tengri Bank stripped of license Sept 2020 (liquidation completed early 2021), partly owned (42%) by India's Punjab National Bank, lost license due to large NPLs.
- 2021: four banks ceased operations — ATFBank (merged with Jusan Bank after end-2020 acquisition); AsiaCredit Bank, Capital Bank, and National Bank of Pakistan all liquidated in 2021 (first two had licenses removed for insolvency/non-compliance, third voluntary liquidation).
- 2022: upheaval as Russian-finance-group subsidiaries changed ownership to avoid sanctions; Sberbank's Kazakhstani subsidiary acquired by Baiterek Holding (government-owned investment company) and renamed Bereke Bank (September 2022).
