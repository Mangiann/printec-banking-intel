### p.531 — Lithuania, Section 1 (Branches), Figure 1.2: Branches by bank, 2021 and 2022
Body text: In June 2021 a group of banks/credit institutions, with the Bank of Lithuania and the Association of Lithuanian Banks, signed a Memorandum of Understanding for Ensuring Access to Cash. Commitment: by 1 July 2022, ≥1 cash withdrawal point (ATM or equivalent) within 10km of ≥90% of population, or within 20km of 99% of population; ensure sufficient cash supply; cash access ≥12 hours/day. Result: localities with cash withdrawal points rose from 91 to 191 by end-2022, after 100 new ATMs installed by Worldline Lietuva under the BANKOMATAS.LT brand during 2022. Cash also withdrawable at over 2,000 POS locations (participating retailers + Perlas Finance terminals).

### p.532 — Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services; Figure 2.1: Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022
Body: First ATMs in Lithuania deployed by Vilniaus Bankas (now SEB Bankas) in 1995, with Hansabankas (now Swedbank) following in 1996. In 2003 a bilateral sharing agreement was established between SEB Bankas and DNB's customers to use SEB Bankas' ATMs free, while customers of SEB Bankas were charged lower fees for using DNB's ATMs. At end-2022 SEB Bankas had a similar arrangement with Luminor Bank, which was formed in 2017 from the merger of the Baltic banking operations of DNB and Nordea. In 2006, Nordea and Danske Bank agreed to create a cross-border ATM network. This was developed and operated by First Data and became known as the Joint 5 Network after a further three banks joined (Citadele Bank, Šiauliu Bankas and Ukio Bankas). By end-2018, the network had three members (Ukio Bankas was integrated into Šiauliu Bankas in 2013, while Luminor was formed when DNB and Nordea merged in 2017). The number of ATMs in the network fell sharply in 2019, as Luminor Bank and Šiauliu Bankas (and Citadele Bank) began co-operating with a new IAD known as Medus. By the end of 2020 the Joint 5 Network had ceased to exist.
**Figure 2.1 (Joint 5 Network ATMs): 2018 = 368; 2019 = 121; 2020 = 0; 2021 = 0; 2022 = 0; CAGR = -100.0%.** Source: Lithuanian ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
Further body: Medus is operated by Worldline Lietuva (subsidiary of France's Worldline). While the IAD's own branding is most prominent, Medus ATMs also carry branding of partner banks Šiauliu Bankas and Citadele Bank, and cardholders of these banks can transact without incurring charges. In 2021 Medus made a similar agreement with Luminor Bank to integrate Luminor Bank ATMs into the Medus network, while Luminor's credit-union customers can also make cash withdrawals at Medus ATMs without charge. By end-2022 Medus had 136 ATMs around the country, all offering both cash withdrawal and deposit. Worldline Lietuva installed 100 ATMs in previously unserved locations under the IAD BANKOMATAS.LT brand between Jan and Sep 2022; installations delayed (intended end-2021); IAD's own branding most prominent on BANKOMATAS.LT ATMs alongside logos of supporting banks (Swedbank, SEB Bankas, Luminor Bank, Šiauliu Bankas, Citadele Bank and LKU Credit Union Group); cardholders of those banks make withdrawals on same terms as their own bank's ATMs. (footer: Lithuania Page 3)

### p.534 — Section 3: Current ATM market; 3.1 Market size and growth; Figure 3.1: Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022
Figure 3.1 chart: blue bars = installed ATMs (left axis 0-1,200); black line+diamond = growth % (right axis -6% to 12%). Bars decline 2018-2021 then jump in 2022; growth line dips to its low in 2020 (~-4.5%) then rises sharply to ~10% in 2022.
**Figure 3.1 data table — ATMs: 2018 = 1,112; 2019 = 1,087; 2020 = 1,038; 2021 = 1,021; 2022 = 1,123; CAGR = 0.2%. Absolute growth: -19; -25; -49; -17; +102. Percentage growth: -1.7%; -2.2%; -4.5%; -1.6%; +10.0%.**
Body: Lithuania's installed ATM base returned to growth in 2022 as 102 net additional terminals were added, owing to the market following the implementation of the June 2021 Memorandum of Understanding for Ensuring Access to Cash. This followed a continuous decline in the installed base 2017-2021 (and in 2018), caused by factors including the consolidation of the banking sector, the rise of alternative payments, and a general drive to decrease the costs associated with cash provision. Most former deployers present at end-2022, three removed ATMs during the year. Luminor Bank and Medus removed the most, at three and two ATMs respectively, as part of the ongoing rationalisation of their sharing agreement, which allows Luminor Bank's customers to use Medus machines without incurring charges. The target deployer, Swedbank, deployed one additional terminal in response to the commitment it made in 2021, along with other banks, to increase access to cash. Likewise, Euronet's ATMs were removed in Lithuania in 2021 as signatories of the Memorandum committed to maintaining their existing ATMs except in places where services are provided by a joint network. ### p.536 — Lithuania, 3.4 Number and value of cash withdrawals; Figure 3.4: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022
Figure 3.4 chart: blue bars = annual number of cash withdrawals (million, left axis 0-60); orange/red line = average number per ATM (right axis, scale to ~6,000). Bars dip 2020 then recover; per-ATM line follows similar trend.
**Figure 3.4 data table:** Annual number of cash withdrawals (million): 2018 = 39; 2019 = 57; 2020 = 47; 2021 = 44; 2022 = 58; CAGR = -0.1% (the 2018 column reads "39" with subsequent values 57/47/44/58 — note 2018 figure appears low relative to others). Average number per ATM (rows shown): 4,405 / 4,326 / 3,709 / 3,525 / 4,547; CAGR ≈ 0.8%. *(Figures partly faint; treat number-of-withdrawals 2018 value with caution.)*
Body: A sizeable majority of ATMs in Lithuania are installed away from branches, with off-site terminals accounting for 83% of the installed base at end-2022, a two-percentage-point increase on 2021. There were also two drive-through ATMs, which both belonged to SEB Bankas. The number of off-site terminals rose by 105 in the year in absolute terms following installation of BANKOMATAS.LT and Euronet machines in Lithuania. Branch-based ATMs tend to be placed either in lobbies or through the exterior wall (TTW), in order to provide extended-hours access for customers; only a small number of ATMs are located in branch halls. Citadele Bank was the only deployer to have an entirely branch-based ATM fleet at end-2022, while Swedbank removing seven machines from branch locations.
The number of cash withdrawals decreased at an average rate of less than 1% per year between end-2018 and end-2022, but grew by 34% in 2022 alone. The follows a sharp decline due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which continued to affect cash spending. Growth in 2022 was caused by increased demand for cash due to a combination of recovery in the use of cash following a once-off decline, high levels of inflation affecting consumer prices, and increases in wages and social-security benefits. Geopolitical uncertainty following the Russian invasion of Ukraine also influenced demand for cash as a means of ensuring continuity in case electronic payment systems were interrupted. 2022 saw the number of withdrawals reach the 2019 level, suggesting cash is likely to remain important in the immediate future despite the growing popularity of cashless payments in Lithuania. Cash remains particularly well-used in rural areas, so initiatives to improve access to cash and distribute ATMs in under-served locations are also likely to have contributed to the growth seen in 2022. Body: The Lithuanian ATM market is supplied by two manufacturers. Diebold Nixdorf accounted for 60% of the installed base at end-2022. NCR accounted for the remaining 40% of the market at end-2022. Most of NCR's machines belong to Swedbank and Euronet, although Luminor Bank also has a small number. ### p.539 — Section 5: Market forecasts; 5.1 Forecast installed base; Figure 5.1 + Figure 5.2
**Figure 5.1: Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028 (2023/2024/2025/2026/2027/2028, CAGR):** ATMs 1,120/1,110/1,100/1,090/1,070/1,050 (-1.3%); Absolute growth -3/-10/-10/-10/-20/-20 (N/A); Percentage growth -0.3%/-0.9%/-0.9%/-0.9%/-1.8%/-1.9% (N/A); ATMs per million people 407/404/400/396/389/382 (-1.3%). Source: RBR analysis.
Body: Lithuanian ATM installed base expected to decrease by 7% between end-2022 and end-2028, to stand at 1,050 ATMs — equivalent to 382 ATMs per million people, down from 408 at end-2022 (based on end-2022 population figures). Banks are likely to continue rationalising both their branch networks and their ATM fleets, a trend which may be only partially offset by growth in the number of IAD machines, as cash usage is likely to decline after its return to pre-pandemic levels. As more of an increase in the availability and customer adoption of cashback at POS may also impact negatively on demand for ATMs. Despite the central bank's Memorandum of Understanding, which aims to expand the proportion of Lithuanians served by banking services, the installed base is forecast to fall, as deployers reduce the cost of providing cash services. To reduce service costs, deployers are expected to make greater use of networks, and increase support for deployers such as BANKOMATAS.LT. This will redistribute existing ATMs to strategic locations. Deployers are expected to increase their focus on rural areas which are currently served by neither an ATM nor a branch. It is forecast that the total number of branches will fall faster than the number of ATMs between end-2022 and end-2028; by 8%, to reach around 140 branches. This would imply a significant rise in the density of ATMs per 100 branches over time, from 645 at end-2022 to 750 by end-2028.
**Figure 5.2: Number of ATMs, 1995-2028** (line chart, left axis 0-1,600). Installed base rose from near-zero in 1995 to a peak around the mid/late-2000s (~1,400-1,600), then a long gradual decline through 2018-2021, an uptick in 2022, and a continued downward forecast trend to 2028. Sources: Bank of Lithuania, Association of Lithuanian Banks, Lithuanian ATM deployers and RBR analysis. (footer: Lithuania Page 10)

### p.540 — Lithuania, 5.2 Forecast ATM shipments; Figure 5.3: Forecast ATM shipments, 2023-2028

Body: Around 650 ATMs projected to be shipped to Lithuania between 2023 and 2028 — an average of around 110 per year. The vast majority will be replacements for existing units, with only small numbers needed for new installations. While the overall installed base is forecast to decrease over the forecast period, there will be some new installations, owing to growth in the IAD sector. The proportion of shipments that replacements constitute is forecast to increase across the forecast period, although a dip is forecast in the middle years, as replacements may fall faster than new installations around this time based on previous patterns of deployment. The number of ATMs forecast to be shipped for new installations does not match the increase in the installed base in Figure 5.1, as some deployers are expected to continue expanding their ATM estates while other deployers reduce theirs.
