Body text:
- **5,646 ATMs** at end-2022, a decrease of 18 units from the previous year. Decline largely owing to the April 2022 liquidation of **Sberbank** (Czech subsidiary of the Russian bank), whose entire 36-ATM fleet was removed.
- Seven deployers expanded ATM networks in 2022, most notably IAD **ATM Point** which added 25 new machines, and bank **ČS** with 18 — not enough to offset the Sberbank collapse and smaller-scale fleet reductions by other deployers. Market saw mild growth in 2021 (economic recovery from COVID-19 drove IAD-led expansion), following a difficult 2020 in which 59 ATMs were removed at the peak of the pandemic.
- Market highly concentrated — **top three ATM deployers = 59% of all installations**. In 2022 two bank deployers entered an ATM sharing agreement, with more banks set to join in 2023. **Four IADs** present, jointly **17% of all ATMs**. Prior to 2020, ATM numbers were on a consistent upward trend.
- Bank branches contracted by **6%** between end-2021 and end-2022, to **1,336** outlets (long-term trend of strategic shrinking + remote channels). Branch-based ATMs becoming less common; off-site ATMs account for the majority of installations.
- Cash withdrawals rose in 2022 by **5% (number)** and **9% (value)**; high inflation drove value surge, cash demand higher post-pandemic plus instability from the war in Ukraine.

### p.138 — Czech Republic, Section 1 (continued, body text)
- **Diebold Nixdorf** was the largest ATM supplier to the Czech Republic in 2021, **51%** of installed base. **NCR** next largest with **43%**; **OKI** and **Triton** supplied the remaining ATMs.
- By 2028 it is forecast that Czech ATM numbers will reach **6,000 units**. Growth set to be driven by the IAD sector, with bank deployers likely to continue consolidating fleets, particularly as interest grows in ATM sharing agreements.

Body text: Modern Czech banking sector formed after fall of communism in 1989 and dissolution of Czechoslovakia in 1992. Communist Czechoslovakia had a monobank, centrally planned. Newly independent Czech Republic underwent major reforms; the four largest banks (ČS, KB, ČSOB, IPB) all remained majority state-owned until late 1990s; three of these were bought by international finance groups based in Austria and France; IPB collapsed in 2000 following an earlier privatisation; ČSOB took on IPB's operations including assets and liabilities. 1990s saw more small/medium banks enter a two-tier system; foreign banks increased their share, holding **over 40% of assets by the late 2000s**.

### p.140 — Czech Republic, Section 2 (continued, body text)
- **Czech National Bank (CNB)** is the central bank, oversees the entire banking sector. End-2022: **44 banks and six credit unions** operating. Of the banks, **33** were subsidiaries or branches of foreign institutions and **nine** were privately-owned Czech entities; remaining **two** are state-owned (Czech Export Bank and Národní rozvojová banka — the latter financing SMEs for public economic and social development).
- **Two new banks** entered in 2022: **InBank** (Estonian digital bank) and **FCM Bank** (Maltese corporate lender). **Three banks** dissolved over the same period: first, **Equa Bank**, acquired by international Raiffeisen banking group, ceased independent operations at the start of the year and transferred operations/clients to Czech Raiffeisen Bank entity.
- **Sberbank** — war in Ukraine and sanctions precipitated its collapse (Czech subsidiary of the Russian banking giant) in Q1 2022; legally dissolved end of April 2022 after closures of branches and cessation of services; Sberbank Russia's other European subsidiaries also folded and its European banking group (parent of the individual entities) was liquidated.
- Third defunct entity in 2022 was the **Prague-based arm of MUFG Bank Europe**, a subsidiary of the international banking group of the same name.
- Czech Republic experiencing long-term decline in bank branch numbers; sector incorporating more digital/remote banking (particularly via app). In 2022 branch numbers fell for the **seventh consecutive year, contracting by 6%**. Liquidation of Sberbank removed almost 30 outlets.
- **ČS** (part of Austrian Erste Group): largest bank by branches — **394** at end-2022, unchanged YoY (stability followed a major branch rationalisation in 2021 when 59 outlets removed). In assets, ČS is **second largest** with **CZK 1.6 trillion (USD 72.7 billion)** at end-2022, **18% of total Czech banking assets**.
- **KB**: second most branches, **217**, a decrease of 24 since end-2021 (strategic branch cuts; move to cashless and digital). Majority owner France's Société Générale (bought shares early 2000s). End-2022 KB third in country by assets, **CZK 1.3 trillion (USD 57.8 billion)**.
- **ČSOB**: **181** branches, third largest by branches; removed five branches over 2022. 100% subsidiary of KBC Bank (Belgian banking/insurance KBC Group); group first bought majority stake of ČSOB from Czech government in 1999 for **CZK 40.1 billion (USD 11.2 billion)**. ČSOB leads in assets with **CZK 1.8 trillion (USD 80.0 billion)**, individually **20% of all Czech banking assets**.
