### p.452 — Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services (2.1 ATM networks and sharing)
- First ATMs installed in Azerbaijan in late 1990s; modest growth, fewer than 200 installations by early 2000s; expansion driven by payment sector (salary increases, social cards). Stable until 2010 then re-stabilised again.
- Central bank's 2015 reform programme inhibited deployment growth as banks focused on recapitalisation; installed base contracted by 10% between end-2015 and end-2017, to 2,431 ATMs.
- Growth resumed 2018, +71 terminals that year (closures at four banks). 2019: Kapital Bank +40, Rabitabank +27. 2020 modest: installed base +68 (IBA and Kapital expanded fleets by 128 and 124 machines respectively, offsetting closures).
- Azerbaijani ATM market highly concentrated, consolidating in the banking sector. IBA and Kapital Bank account for 67% of the country's ATMs at end-2022 (collective share +2 percentage points vs previous year).
- Azerbaijani Postal Service (Azerpost) is the country's only non-bank ATM deployer; began deploying ATMs in 2012, had 63 terminals by end-2022.
- AzeriCard, Azerbaijan's first processing centre, opened 1997; owned by IBA; certified by Visa, Mastercard, American Express, Diners Club, JCB and UnionPay. Provides bill payments, account transfers, processing of multi-currency payment cards, loyalty support. Introduced WAY4's Cash By Code technology in 2013 (cardless cash deposits introduced across all AzeriCard ATMs in 2017). At end-2022 AzeriCard clients = 14 banks, down from 15 in 2020 after closure of four banks (previously AzeriCard clients). Due to a contraction from Xalq Bank, AzeriCard ATMs fell again in 2022 to 1,505; partially offset by IBA which added seven terminals in 2022. IBA remains the network's largest member, accounting for 60% of all AzeriCard ATMs.
- 2nd processing centre began as a department of the National Bank of Azerbaijan in 2005, reformed as MilliKart a year later by 18 small/medium Azerbaijani banks; encompassed 519 ATMs at end-2022 (down from 534 at end-2021); 9 members at end-2022 (unchanged).

### p.457 — 3.3 Locations; 3.4 Number of cash withdrawals
Body (3.3 Locations): At end-2022 off-site locations most common — 83% of installed base = **2,509 terminals**; branch-based ATMs = 17% = **488 terminals**. Some deployers (Azerpost, Mugan Bank, Access Bank) centralise fleets in branches, but most banks (esp. largest players) prefer off-site. Off-site ATMs in high-footfall locations: petrol stations, shopping centres, workplaces, supermarkets; also urban residential areas, transport hubs, places of education.
