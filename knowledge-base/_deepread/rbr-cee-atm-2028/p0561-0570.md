### p.567 — Slovenia, Section 2 (ATM networks, sharing and services) + Figure 2.1
Country: Slovenia (footer "Slovenia: Page 3"). Source: Bankart, Slovenian ATM deployers, Banka Slovenije, RBR analysis.
- Section 2.1 text: Slovenia's first ATM installed 1990 by Ljubljanska banka (now Nova Ljubljanska banka). By end of the year, five other banks had installed ATMs. Nova Ljubljanska banka developed the basis of the present-day ATM network over the course of the 1990s and attracted many other Slovenian banks to join. A competing network, the P network, was developed by SKB banka and a few smaller institutions, forming the establishment of a processing company named Placo. Most of the machines in the P network were owned by SKB banka. The BA network accounted for around 80% of ATMs in Slovenia by 1997. The P network merged with BA that year; most of the majority of SKB banka's machines were integrated into BA. In 2005, Placo was acquired by all of which, which was then acquired by the BS processor. Activ in 2007.
- Euronet entered the market in the second half of 2019 and is the only independent ATM deployer in Slovenia.
- The Slovenian ATM network is currently managed by Bankart. Bankart was established in 1997 and is currently owned by seven Slovenian banks, including Nova Ljubljanska banka (46% share), Nova KBM (29%), SKB banka (14%) and Banka Intesa Sanpaolo (7%). The company provides ATM processing for all of Slovenia's ATM deployers except Banka Intesa Sanpaolo, BKS Bank and Euronet. Bankart handles ATM authorisations for the purpose of monitoring and fraud prevention. In addition, the company provides statistical reports on ATM transactions for banks, develops technological solutions for the network and has a call centre.

- Text: Bank customers can always make cash withdrawals at ATMs of their own bank ("on-us" withdrawals) free of charge. For "not-on-us" transactions, most banks charge a disloyalty fee. The first major institution to introduce such fees was Nova Ljubljanska banka in 2006, with the aim of covering the cost of running its ATMs. Other banks soon followed. Although Nova KBM and SKB banka previously had an agreement allowing customers of one bank to make debit-card cash withdrawals and balance enquiries free of charge at ATMs of the other, this agreement ended in mid-2020 when Nova KBM merged with Abanka. Under parent OTP Group's plans for the two banks to merge, it is expected that customers will once again be able to use the ATMs of both banks free of charge. Banks offer a range of service packages to their customers, which can include unlimited withdrawals at other banks' ATMs. Customers of other packages may still be required to pay disloyalty fees, although some banks allow their customers to make a limited number of "not-on-us" cash withdrawals before fees apply. Currently, such fees typically range from EUR 0.45 to 0.90 (USD 0.51 to 1.02) per transaction.

- Text: the Slovenian installed base of ATMs contracted slightly in 2022, with 1,419 terminals operating across the country at end of year (note: Fig 3.1 shows end-2022 = 1,415; deployer total = 1,415). Most ATMs deployed by banks, with Euronet being the only IAD (independent ATM deployer) in the market. At end-2022, the five largest deployers accounted for 82% of ATMs, and the two largest (Nova Ljubljanska banka and Nova KBM) accounting for 63% of the installed base in both years. Euronet continued to expand its fleet over 2022 despite the overall decrease in the installed base. Slovenian ATMs do not typically offer many additional features and facilities; however mobile phone top-ups, PIN change and statement printing are relatively common. The majority of terminals are equipped with anti-skimming devices, but under half have an integrated security camera or note-degradation technology. Some ATMs have NFC/contactless readers and QR-code readers. SKB banka has recently expanded the Slovenian subsidiary of Sberbank following the sanctions imposed against Russia. For example, in efforts to save customers the time it would take to visit a branch, UniCredit Bank upgraded its fleet in 2021 and at end-2022 offered cash deposits at 61 ATMs as well as having introduced bill payments.
