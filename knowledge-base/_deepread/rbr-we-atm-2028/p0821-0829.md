- Text: Maltese ATM installed base grew by 5% in 2022. Number of ATMs in Malta reached their peak in 2019 at 405 and end-2019. Represented an increase of almost 200 units from end-2016. In 2017, Euronet, an IAD, entered the market and total number of ATMs increased; the total number of ATMs did not increase materially. High growth rate of 27.3% in 2018 results from Euronet's recent entry to the market.
- The slower growth in number of ATMs from 2018 to 2019 is explained in part by Bank of Valletta's and HSBC's rationalisation of ATM numbers, which offset Euronet's growth in 2019. The significant decrease in ATM numbers seen from 2019 to 2020 results from the COVID-19 pandemic, which caused a reduction in transaction numbers. The decrease in demand for cash encouraged bank deployers to continue rationalising and Euronet to pause new deployments and temporarily remove some of its ATMs.
- In 2021, Junior ATM removals by bank deployers were balanced by Euronet, even though it was expanding at a slower rate than from 2017 to 2019. Bank of Valletta and HSBC only removed a single unit each in 2022; Euronet increased its fleet size, as did smaller bank deployers in small numbers.

### p.823 — Malta, location detail / 3.4 Number and value of cash withdrawals
- Text: The first ATMs in Malta were installed through the exterior wall (TTW) of the branch. TTW was the most common location for branch-based machines until 2012, when Bank of Valletta moved its branch machines to new 24-hour lobbies to improve customer privacy and security. Ever since, the lobby has been the most common branch location. The entirety of APS Bank's fleet was installed in lobbies, as was the majority of Bank of Valletta's and BNF Bank's at end-2022. Bank of Valletta remains the largest deployer of lobby installations.
- 72% of all ATMs in Malta are installed off-site. This is heavily influenced by the size of Euronet's fleet, which, given that it is an IAD, is wholly installed off-site and accounts for 76% of all ATMs installed off-site. All deployers except for APS Bank deploy terminals in this location. The share of ATMs installed off-site has increased by six percentage points from 2018 to 2022, owing to Euronet's increase in share from 45% of the total number of Maltese ATMs to 55% in the same period.
- The fluctuation in the numbers of ATMs installed in lobbies is due to periods of rationalisation and expansion by banks that deploy ATMs in this location. The size of the percentage increase seen from 2019 to 2020 is due to the significant decrease in the number of ATMs installed in off-site and TTW locations because of larger deployers rationalising ATM fleets.
- TTW installations are the next most common location; although their share has decreased steadily since 2012. HSBC is the largest deployer of ATMs in this location. There are two main drivers of these through-terminals installed in Malta, both by the largest deployers. There is no ATM installed in Malta's hall location.
- **Figure 3.4: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022** (dual-axis: bars = Annual number [left, millions, 0-24] / line = Average number [right, 0-7,200]).
  - Annual number of cash withdrawals (millions): 2018 = 16; 2019 = 17; 2020 = 12; 2021 = 13; 2022 = 14; CAGR = -2.6%.
  - Average cash withdrawals per ATM per month: 2018 = 3,878; 2019 = 3,577; 2020 = 2,718; 2021 = 2,993; 2022 = 3,268; CAGR = -4.2%.
  - Trend: withdrawals dipped in 2020 (COVID) then partially recovered.
- Text: In 2022, the number of cash withdrawals has continued to recover from the significant drop seen from 2019 to 2020, when COVID-19 adversely impacted cash spending both by tourists and by local residents. Nevertheless, withdrawal numbers are yet to reach the levels seen in 2019. 2022 saw a total of 14 million cash withdrawals, at an average of 3,268 withdrawals per ATM per month.

- Text: At end-2022, NCR remained the market's largest supplier, accounting for 75% of the total installed base. NCR supplies the entire fleets of Bank of Valletta, Lombard Bank, BNF Bank and APS Bank. NCR also supplies Euronet, its largest deployer. NCR's increasing market share from 2018 to 2020 is in part explained by the difference in Bank of Valletta's rate of rationalisation and HSBC's, which is supplied by Diebold Nixdorf. - Diebold Nixdorf is the other smaller supplier, providing 25% of the installed base at the end of 2022. Diebold Nixdorf's market share has decreased steadily overall as HSBC, its largest deployer, has rationalised its ATM fleet. The slight increase in market share in 2020 is due to Bank of Valletta and Euronet decreasing their number of ATMs by a relatively large amount, while HSBC reduced its by 45 total units.

- Text: Following an increase in the total number of ATMs in 2022, the ATM installed base in Malta is expected to continue to increase further. The rate of growth is expected to slow by 2028, when it is projected there will be 396 ATMs and 752 ATMs per million people based on the 2022 population.
- 2022 was the first year since 2017 when the larger deployers' rationalisation slowed; the smaller bank deployers increasing their number of ATMs. The larger deployers' rationalisation has been the main driver behind the negative growth of the ATM installed base in Malta. Euronet is expected to plateau and the growth of Bank of Valletta's installed base by 2028.
- Smaller bank deployers increased their fleets in 2022. This, in combination with the continued increase in the number of IAD ATMs installed in the 5% growth of the total number of ATMs from 2021 to 2022. Although the Central Bank of Malta found that merchants are increasingly receptive to alternative means of payment other than cash, there is still opportunity for IADs to capitalise on a demand for cash generated by tourism. The number of IAD ATMs is expected to grow slightly from 2023 to 2027, but plateau by 2028.
- Whereas the number of branches for the smaller banks is expected to remain steady, it is expected that larger banks will decrease the number of their bank branches further. As the uptake of digital banking increases, the number of all bank branches is projected to continue to decrease steadily, falling to 80 units by 2028.
