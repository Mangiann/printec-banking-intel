- Body text: ATM installed base in Portugal contracted to 13,862, the highest rate of decline on record. There were 13,862 terminals in the country at end of 2022, implying an average of 1,099 ATMs per million people. Portuguese banks continued to significantly rationalise their branch networks during the year, with total number of outlets in the country falling by 7%. There were 3,283 bank branches at end-2022, implying an average of 422 ATMs per 100 outlets. Portuguese ATMs (~70% via Multibanco network) are highly sophisticated, offering a wide range of services that remain popular with cardholders. The most common facilities include inter- and intra-bank transfers, printed statements, FX changes, mobile phone top-ups, and prepaid card reloading. 24% of terminals accept automated note deposits; 24% of these automated deposit terminals (ATDs) are recyclers. 2022 marked continued recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic in terms of both number and value of cash withdrawals. During the year, 452 million withdrawals were made (+9% vs 2021). Spain's CaixaBank took over ~100% of the shares of Portuguese bank at the end of that year [partial detail]. Withdrawals were worth a total of EUR 36 billion (USD 38 billion), representing annual growth of 6% in local currency.

### p.508 — Portugal, Section 1 continued (supplier landscape & forecast)
Portugal: Page 2.
- Widening range of suppliers to the Portuguese market. Diebold Nixdorf is the largest, with its share increasing year on year as a result of rising deployment in the Multibanco network. Glory is the next largest, although its share has fallen in recent years. NCR supplies a smaller but increasing proportion of the installed base. A number of other vendors — many of which have entered the market relatively recently — hold smaller shares.
- BPI is the most commonly used network central hardware solution for Portuguese ATMs. SBS offers a variety of software and other solutions, which are used across the Multibanco network for application software, terminal driving software, message protocol, management and monitoring software, and remote software distribution. In 2022, SBS also introduced its own dedicated customer relationship management and security solutions, which it has rolled out across the entire Multibanco network. The most common operating system is Windows 10; XP is the most common operating system, half of installed base now operates over Windows 10 [trend toward Win10].
- Portuguese deployers typically keep both cash replenishment and first-line maintenance services in-house for branch ATMs, while outsourcing them for off-site terminals. Second-line maintenance is generally outsourced, as is the proportion higher for branch-based ATMs than those located off-site.
- The total number of ATMs is forecast to continue decreasing between 2023 and 2028, at an average annual rate of 1%. There are projected to be 12,800 terminals in the country by the end of the period. The number of total cash withdrawals will see a sharp decline as projected to decline over the forecast period at an average annual rate of 1%. Conversely, continued recovery is a forecast for the value of cash withdrawals, with total value surpassing pre-COVID levels during the forecast period. 2028 is projected to see 480 million withdrawals totalling EUR 40 billion (USD 43 billion at the 2022 exchange rate). Portugal is expected to receive around 6,000 ATM shipments between 2023 and 2028, with virtually all of these being replacements.

- Body text: several major banking groups operate in Portugal — CGD, the BCP Group (including Millennium bcp and ActivoBank), Santander Group, Novo Banco, and Group BPI. As of June 2022, these banks accounted for well over two thirds of the country's total banking sector assets. The largest bank by assets was the state-owned Caixa Geral de Depósitos (CGD), with EUR 97.5 billion (USD 104.6 billion). The next largest bank by this measure was Millennium bcp — part of the wider BCP Group — which held assets worth EUR 71.9 billion (USD 77.1 billion). Millennium bcp also owns ActivoBank, which was re-launched in 2010 as a "bank of the future" to focus on technological innovation and the simplification of the customer experience. The bank's clientele predominantly comprises urban, university-educated people between the ages of 23 and 40. ActivoBank had total assets of EUR 3.2 billion (USD 3.4 billion) as of June 2022.
- The third largest Portuguese bank in terms of assets was Santander Totta, a subsidiary of Spain's Santander Group that has a significant presence in Portugal. Santander Totta held assets worth EUR 64.9 billion (USD 64.7 billion) as of June 2022. The next largest bank by this measure was Novo Banco, with assets worth EUR 45.1 billion (USD 48.3 billion). Novo Banco was formed after the 2014 collapse of the Espírito Santo business empire, which included Banco Espírito Santo (BES). The bank's healthy assets were separated to form Novo Banco, with the aim to sell it in 2015. After a number of unsuccessful bids in 2015 and 2016, the bank was eventually sold in November 2017 to US private-equity fund Lone Star. At the beginning of the fourth quarter of 2017, Novo Banco ceased to be considered a transition bank and began to operate under normal conditions, while continuing to be subject to certain measures imposed by the European competition authority. In late 2021, Novo Banco received EUR 112 million (USD 126.9 million) from the Portuguese resolution fund in order to keep its solvency ratio at levels required by regulators.
- Portugal's fifth largest bank by branch network is Novo Banco. Amongst the country's major banks, it experienced the second most significant rationalisation in 2022, closing 18 outlets, or 6% of its total network.
- Barclays' retail, private and SME operations in Portugal were sold to Spain's Bankinter in April 2016 and the former bank's presence in the country is now just one branch which continues the operations of Barclaycard, investment banking, and multinational corporate banking in Portugal.
- Banco CTT was formed as the result of the privatisation of the national postal service (CTT) in August 2015. The bank began to deploy ATMs in February 2016 and opened 51 branches in CTT post offices as well as its one headquarters branch in March 2016. At end-2022, the bank had 212 branches, the same number as a year earlier.

### p.510 — Portugal, Section 2 continued (bank network history)
Portugal: Page 4.
- Body text: The number of bank branches in operation in Portugal has fallen consistently since its peak of 6,232 in 2010, shortly before the Eurozone crisis transformed the face of banking. This total fell again in 2022; there were 3,283 outlets at the end of the year, 7% fewer than at end-2021. All but one of the country's major banks either rationalised their branch networks during the year or maintained the same footprint. The only exception — Millennium bcp — added just two more outlets. The bank that experienced the greatest rationalisation in absolute terms was Millennium bcp — which closed 26 branches, an 7% reduction in its branch network during the year (consistent with Figure 2.1: 375 to 349). The next largest such reduction was at Santander Totta (-14), then Novo Banco (-18) and Banco BPI (-19).
- At end-2022, the largest bank by branch network was Crédito Agrícola, a bank specialising in agricultural financing that is particularly active in rural areas of the country. Crédito Agrícola closed five outlets during 2022, or 1% of its network.
- The next largest bank by this measure is CGD. It removed a negligible proportion of its branch network during 2022, closing just two outlets. In addition to its 508 retail branches, CGD operates its "automated branches", which are fully self-service outlets, 24 business offices, three mobile branches and one "Caixa BI" investment branch. In the aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis, the Troika (EU, IMF, and the European Central Bank) ordered the CGD to rationalise its structure. As a result, the bank sold its healthcare unit in 2012.
- The third largest bank by branch network is Millennium bcp. The bank was bailed out by the Portuguese government after the Eurozone crisis and had to follow a restructuring plan outlined by the European Commission in the years that followed, which entailed cutting staffing costs and the rationalisation of its branch network. It was announced in 2017 that the bank no longer had to follow such a strict cost cutting policy, although it continued to trim its branch numbers and embrace fully automated branches to further reduce staffing costs in subsequent years. In 2022, however, it increased its branch total by two.
- Santander Totta is the next largest bank by number of outlets. Santander acquired Banco Internacional do Funchal (BANIF) in December 2015 and Banco Popular in April 2017. Following these acquisitions, Santander has rationalised its branch network, closing 63 branches in 2020 and a further 76 in 2021. These closures were partly a continued response to the COVID-19 pandemic, and partly a response to the greater number of transactions being carried out via digital channels instead of in person at the branch in recent years. In 2022, Santander Totta continued to rationalise its network, albeit to a lesser extent, with a reduction of 14 branches, or 4% of its network.
