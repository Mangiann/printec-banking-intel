Body text: 1,955 ATMs in Denmark at end-2022, a fall of 2% from end-2021. Installed base has contracted every year as deployers rationalise fleets via cash usage decline; decline has been more gradual recently owing to a flurry of activity from IADs expanding their fleets. Majority of ATMs are on-site (55% of terminals found in bank branches); most common location is ATMs within branches, through the exterior wall of the branch. Off-site ATMs in country increasing — IAD fleets +45% at end-2022; off-site ATMs made up 45% of machines, up from 42% a year previously. Danish ATMs typically provide wide range of functions — all ATMs can provide a printed statement; over half offer automated note deposit (numbers rising). ATMs now fitted with built-in security cameras and anti-skimming devices. 35 million cash withdrawals in Denmark in 2022, +8% YoY (recovery following lifting of COVID-19 pandemic restrictions in early 2022; withdrawals had impacted the already-dwindling demand for cash in 2020 and 2021). End-2022: only two ATM manufacturers in Danish market — NCR and Diebold Nixdorf; NCR is largest supplier with ~61% of machines (a percentage-point increase from end-2021). Footer "Denmark: Page 1".

### p.159 — Denmark, Section 2 (continued)
Text page, banking-structure narrative / M&A history (no figure):
- 2014: Jutlander Bank created by merger of Sparekassen Himmerland and Sparekassen Hobro. Same year, Santander Group acquired GE Money Bank's operations across Scandinavia to enter the Danish market. 2018: two mergers — Vestjysk Bank purchased Den Jyske Sparekasse; Spar Nord Bank acquired BankNordik's operations in the Danish market. Both deals approved by Danish regulators in early 2021.
- March 2021: Arbejdernes Landsbank purchased a majority stake in Vestjysk Bank (continue to operate as separate entities; acquisition took Arbejdernes Landsbank over the threshold to be considered a SIFI by Danmarks Nationalbank). Late 2021: Folkesparekassen merged with Middelfart Sparekasse; July 2022 Jyske Bank agreed to acquire Handelsbanken's operations in Denmark (process to move forward gradually throughout 2023).
- End-2022, Nordea was the largest bank in Denmark by assets, total DKK 4.4 trillion (USD 637 billion); also remained the largest bank in terms of branches, with 86 located across the country, although this represents a fall of 4% from previous year. Headquartered in Helsinki; Nordea formed 1997-2001 from merger of Finland's Merita Bank, Sweden's Nordbanken, Denmark's Unibank and Norway's Christiania Bank og Kreditkasse. 2009 acquired Fionia Bank from Finansiel Stabilitet. Market share in Funen region of Denmark; also active in Eastern Europe, with operations in Poland and across the Baltic states.
- Second-largest Danish bank by assets at end-2022: Danske Bank, DKK 3.8 trillion (USD 542 billion). Third-largest by branch count (68, despite 8% decrease in size of branch network in 2022). Danske Bank acquired several banks through the 1990s/2000s, some most significant purchases being those of Northern Bank in Northern Ireland and National Irish Bank in the Republic of Ireland; also acquired Sampo Bank in Finland in 2007.
- Jyske Bank remained third-largest by assets at end-2022, DKK 750 billion (USD 108 billion). The bank has an IT agreement with Bankdata, one of the largest data-processing centres in Denmark, which resulted in the migration of the bank's IT systems to Bankdata's platform in 2012. It had the second-largest branch network in the country with 72 outlets at end-2022. This number set to grow in coming years as Handelsbanken branches are gradually incorporated into the Jyske Bank network.
- Fourth-largest by assets: Nykredit Bank, DKK 237 billion (USD 34.1 billion); had 41 branches at end-2022, size of network remaining unchanged year-on-year.
- Fifth-largest bank by assets: Sydbank, DKK 233 billion (USD 25.8 billion). Branch network = 57 outlets (sixth-largest in country). Acquired numerous banks in the 2000s (incl. Egnsbank Fyn 2002, Sparekassen sparTrelleborg 2008, Tønder Bank 2012, DiBa Bank 2013).

### p.160 — Denmark, Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services
Section 3 opens. Subsection 3.1 "ATM networks and sharing". At end-2022 there were 1,955 ATMs installed in Denmark, a 2% decrease vs end-2021.
Figure 3.1: "Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022" — single-row table, columns 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | CAGR:
- Nets: 2,150 / 1,996 / 1,838 / 1,663 / 1,605 / -7.0%.
Source: Danish ATM deployers and networks and RBR analysis.
Body: ATMs not installed in Denmark until the 1980s, making it one of the later western European countries to adopt ATM technology (concerns from deployers about potential low usage and poor return on investment; in 1975 banks decided to make a formal agreement on the subject, declaring they would not be adopting ATM technology). 1981: banks relented and established the Banks' Cash Dispenser Company, Pengeinstitutternes Pengeautomatselskab (PPA). The PPA was a joint venture owned by Danish savings and commercial banks; developed Kontanten, the first shared ATM network in the country. Originally, all ATMs deployed in Denmark were located in bank halls; from 1984 there were ten off-site terminals piloted, and by 1990 banks reached an agreement to allow installation of terminals in branch lobbies and through the exterior wall of the branch. Nets Betalingsservice (Nets) is the payment service provider for all banks in Denmark, formed in 2010 following a merger between Denmark's PBS and Norway's Nordito. Bought by a consortium of Advent International, ATP and Bain Capital in 2014; Nets sold its electronic-payment (direct debit) service Betalingsservice to Mastercard in August 2019. June 2021: Nets entered into an all-share merger with Italian payment service provider Nexi, becoming part of the Nexi Group. Nets acts as a back-end resource for the three Danish data-processing centres: Bankdata, BEC and SDC. Most Danish banks use these data centres for any data-processing requirements, including the switching of ATM transactions. Nordea and Danske Bank are the only two Danish banks to use their own in-house processing centres. Nets also processes the clearing and settlement of ATM, EFTPOS and other interbank transactions. As of end-2022, all bank ATMs continued to be shared through the Nets network; ATMs belonging to IADs Euronet and Loomis are not included in the Nets network. Footer "Denmark: Page 5".
