### p.631 — Switzerland, banking sector background (Switzerland: Page 5)
Prose only, no figures/tables.
- **PostFinance** (financial services subsidiary of Swiss Post / Swiss Post): obtained banking licence in 2013, became separate legal entity from parent. Operates proprietary network of **34 branches**, plus banking services via Swiss Post's **773 post offices** and **1,254 outlets** at partner organisations. Declared "systemically relevant" by the SNB in 2015 (enhanced capital requirements). Key role in processing/clearing cashless payments.
- **Bank Cler** (until 2017 named Bank Coop) and **Migros Bank** founded by large retailers. Bank Cler formed 1927 by Swiss Coop (Verband Schweizerischer Konsumvereine), acquired 1999 by Basler Kantonalbank. Migros Bank founded 1958 by Migros Genossenschaftsbund. Both retain founder links.
- **Entris Banking Group** (historically Regional Banks Association): umbrella for regional co-operative & savings banks. **Valiant Bank** is the largest in the group — over a quarter of branches, assets of **CHF 36 billion (USD 39 billion)** at end-2022. Mid-2000s: several Entris banks left to form the **Esprit Network**; at end-2022 this group had **25 members**, all small institutions without ATMs of their own.

### p.632 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (Switzerland: Page 6)
**3.1 ATM networks and sharing.** Bancomat is the domestic shared network; co-operation started 1968. SIX (formerly Telekurs) runs switching. Telekurs merged with SIS Group in 2008, rebranding as **SIX Group**, which acquired the merchant acquiring/payment terminal business of Aduno in 2017; in 2018 **SIX Payment Services acquired by France's Worldline**.
- 84% of ATMs in Switzerland belong to the SIX-operated **Bancomat** network, which connects all ATMs of the 43 members plus 6 ATMs neighbouring in Liechtenstein. ~13% (of Switzerland) ATMs belong to PostFinance's Postomat network; remainder are proprietary/variously shared. Bancomat ATMs accept international schemes — Mastercard, Visa, UnionPay, JCB, etc.
- ATM withdrawals support 5 supported dynamic currency conversion (DCC) and mobile/box top-up.
- In 2019, Switzerland launched a major multi-bank overhaul of the Swiss ATM estate under the **ATMfutura** programme. ATMfutura project involves ~40 deployers including PostFinance; rationalises shared ATM software platform across the estate. "National Cash Scheme" (NCS) launched 2020, replacing national standards for cash transactions.

### p.635 — ATM estate ownership & ATM pooling (Switzerland: Page 9)
Prose only.
- As of end-2022, the **Raiffeisen Group operated the largest ATM estate** in Switzerland — though individual Raiffeisen banks are independent, considered a single collective entity for practical purposes. The next-largest ATM pools belong to PostFinance, UBS and Credit Suisse. Raiffeisen Group remains the only deployer with more than **1,000 ATMs**; the five largest deployers had a combined share of **60%** of the total. Most Swiss banks with ATMs cut the size of their fleets in both 2021 and 2022.
- Swiss banks typically charge their customers a disloyalty fee for "not-on-us" cash withdrawals unless they subscribe to an inclusive banking package. Typical charges can be **CHF 2 (USD 2.15)** for franc withdrawals, and **CHF 5 (USD 5.41)** for euro withdrawals at other deployers' ATMs.
- February 2021: Swiss competition authority launched an investigation following a complaint from SIX that Mastercard had refused to introduce dual badging on its debit cards to obstruct the rollout of NCS. As of March 2023, the case has yet to be resolved.
- IADs Euronet and Travelex have operated ATMs in Switzerland since 2019, when the gradual withdrawal of banks from off-site ATM deployment opened up a viable market for non-bank deployers. The two companies have located their ATMs in transport hubs and tourist destinations, and accounted for **3%** of the country's ATMs at end-2022.
- **3.2 ATM Pooling:** ATM pooling not known to have been seriously considered by banks in Switzerland, but most of the country's ATM deployers looking to cut costs and Switzerland's relatively small size. ATMfutura project may serve as a useful technological foundation for future ATM pooling projects.

- ATMfutura project has also enabled banks to rationalise their ATM estates, as a greater range of functions are now available on a "not-on-us" basis across the Bancomat network.

### p.638 — ATM locations prose (Switzerland: Page 12)
Prose only, no figures.
- Nearly two-thirds of ATMs in Switzerland are located at bank branches, though this proportion is gradually falling as branch closures continue. Nearly half of branch ATMs are installed through the exterior wall (TTW). Most automated-deposit ATMs (which make up a large proportion of the Swiss ATM estate). Many banks have a significant minority of their ATMs in lobby locations, but it is only at UBS and a small number of cantonal banks that the majority of machines are installed in lobbies. Between them, Raiffeisen and UBS account for nearly half of all lobby ATMs in Switzerland.
- The majority of Raiffeisen branches have a TTW ATM, and the size of the group's branch network makes it the largest TTW deployer in the country, accounting for **29%** of the national total. PostFinance accounts for a similar share, but among the commercial cantonal and regional banks, only Credit Suisse has 100 or more TTW machines (5% of the total).
- Unlike many European countries, Switzerland still has a substantial proportion of ATMs located in the interior of the bank branch, and the number in fact increased between 2018 and 2021, partly as a result of deployers identifying the bank hall as their preferred location for new assisted self-service terminals (ASSTs). ATMs in this location have the disadvantage of being accessible only during branch opening hours.
- Over one-third of ATMs in Switzerland are located away from bank branches, typically on high streets, in shopping centres, transportation hubs and tourist locations. The proportion of off-site ATMs has been growing in recent years as banks have continued to close branches. Banks have also been removing off-site ATMs, but at a slower pace, and since 2019 IADs have been installing ATMs in Switzerland to fill the gaps that the banks have left as they withdraw from off-site ATM deployment.
- Most medium-sized and smaller banks focus ATM deployment on their own premises, but there are some exceptions: Valiant Bank, Bank Cler, Luzerner Kantonalbank, Banque Cantonale du Valais and Basler Kantonalbank all deploy more than half of their ATMs away from their own branches.
- There are a small number of drive-through ATMs in Switzerland. Most are located away from branches, with the main deployers being Raiffeisen Group, PostFinance and St Galler Kantonalbank.

- Prose: Swiss ATMs offer customers a wide range of banking services in addition to cash withdrawal. Almost all of them allow customers to change their PIN. Mobile-phone credit top-up was phased out at Bancomat and Postomat ATMs during 2021 because of falling customer demand — the number of prepaid mobile phones in Switzerland is continuing to decline.
- The vast majority of ATMs in Switzerland offer **multi-currency dispensing**, with Swiss francs and euros the currencies typically available. This is mainly because Switzerland is a non-Eurozone country surrounded by Eurozone members (except Liechtenstein), which uses the Swiss franc), and receives large numbers of foreign visitors. **Dynamic currency conversion (DCC)** is offered at 97% of ATMs, and foreign-exchange rate information is available at over half.
- Automated note deposit possible at **38%** of Switzerland's ATMs, up from 37% in 2021. Most ATMs accepting automated deposits **recycle the deposited notes** and accept deposits in multiple currencies — typically Swiss francs and euros. **Automated cheque deposit is not offered**, as cheques are rarely used in Switzerland. Swiss banks generally offer automated deposit services via multifunction ATMs, and stand-alone deposit terminals (SATs) are uncommon. **56% of automated deposit ATMs deployed by Raiffeisen Group, UBS, and Credit Suisse.** The majority of stand-alone deposit terminals belong to the Raiffeisen banks.
- Swiss banks do not use the ATM channel to sell financial products. Some would increase transaction times. Some banks therefore deploy separate terminals for this purpose; UBS, for example, has installed separate "Multimat" machines where customers can access a wider range of services.
- PostFinance with third-party products through its ATMs, including digital top vouchers for iTunes and app downloads.
- Assisted self-service is not widely practised in Switzerland. The largest institution to offer it is Raiffeisen Group, which has deployed ~120 assisted self-service ATMs since 2018. Typically, assisted self-service is used for staff to approve high-value cash withdrawals, with customers able to carry out most other transactions independently.
