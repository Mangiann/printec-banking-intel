### p.811 — Cyprus, ATM locations (Cyprus page 9)
Body text only (no figure on this page).
- Share of ATMs located away from bank branches (off-site) reached 54% at end-2022, up six percentage points from end-2021. An acceleration of a long-term trend: banks in the area under effective control of the Republic of Cyprus have cut their branch footprint, removing branch ATMs, while IADs expanded. Between end-2018 and end-2022 the number of off-site ATMs grew at an annual average of 7%.
- In 2022 the main contributors to the falling number of branch ATMs were the two largest bank deployers — Hellenic Bank and Bank of Cyprus. Alpha Bank also closed a handful of branches. RBC Bank (all its ATMs were on branch premises) no longer operates after its banking licence was removed in December 2022.
- Virtually all bank deployers have more ATMs at branches than off-site. AstroBank, Ancoria Bank, Capital Bank and Akfinans Bank deploy exclusively on branch premises. IADs have all ATMs off-branch. Only two bank deployers have more than half their fleet off-site: Near East Bank and Albank Limited, both operating in TRNC.
- Some Turkish entities operate many off-site ATMs in TRNC; as these run under Turkey's domestic Ortak ATM sharing agreement, they are counted within Turkey data and excluded from the Cyprus figures.

Narrative: Following the 8% decline seen in 2022, the Cypriot ATM market is forecast to contract further at an annual average of 1% between 2023 and 2028, to stand at 900 machines by end-2028. Falling bank branches reduce available ATM deployment sites, but banks want to keep self-service, so there may be some added ATM deployment at bank branches (one to three extra ATMs at some). In more remote areas, branch closures may create demand for off-site ATM deployment, partly offsetting the branch decline. Inbound tourism is expected to support limited growth in the IAD sector, and there is still significant demand for cash access among much of the local population in TRNC, leading to some further bank-deployer fleet expansion. This is not expected to fully offset consolidation of ATM estates by major bank deployers in the Republic-controlled area.

### p.817 — Malta, Banking Structure (continued) (Malta page 2)
Body text only.
- 21 commercial banks operated in Malta at end-2022. Recently several had licences revoked: Pilatus Bank and Nemea Bank (2018), Satabank (2019), CommBank Europe and Yapi Kredi Bank (2021). 18 of the 21 are foreign-owned; just three are private national banks.
- Total Maltese banking assets stood at EUR 52.7 billion (USD 56.2 billion) at end-2022. Bank of Valletta was the largest by assets at EUR 14.5 billion (USD 15.5 billion). HSBC second at EUR 7.4 billion (USD 7.9 billion). MeDirect third at EUR 4.6 billion (USD 5.0 billion).
- Total bank branches unchanged in 2022, remaining at 103. Bank of Valletta closed four branches (continuing rationalisation since 2018); HSBC also rationalised 2018-2021 but the rate has slowed; both banks' branch numbers stable. Lombard Bank opened four branches in 2022.

### p.818 — Malta, Section 2: ATM networks, sharing and services (Malta page 3)
Section 2.1 ATM networks and sharing. Contains two tables (small render).
- Narrative: Malta's two ATM networks have machines installed through the exterior wall (TTW) of Bank of Valletta branches (in 1989). Mid-Med Bank (now HSBC) installed six TTW ATMs that same year; although these remained offline until 1990, APS Bank and Lombard Bank began deploying in 1994 and 1995 respectively. Euronet is the sole IAD operating in Malta; it entered the market in 2017, becoming the second-largest deployer, and grew to 207 ATMs in 2019, representing ~51% of Malta's installed base.
**Figure 2.1: Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022** — columns 2018/2019/2020/2021/2022/CAGR. Rows: Cashlink ~116 / ~104 / ~91 / ~86 / ~85, CAGR ~-7.5%; Quikcash ~90 / ~81 / ~74 / ~64 / ~66, CAGR ~-7.5% (approx; small render). The two ATM networks operating in Malta were first introduced in 1995 and both have two member banks; networks are interlinked. All ATMs in Malta are shared via Visa and Mastercard.
- Bank of Valletta and APS Bank share their ATMs via the Cashlink network. The Quikcash network is managed by HSBC and includes Lombard Bank's ATMs. Cashlink network is larger than Quikcash, but both have shrunk as banks rationalised ATM numbers.
**Figure 2.2: Participation in domestic sharing agreements by deployer, 2022** — Deployer / Cashlink / Quikcash. Euronet — neither; Bank of Valletta — Cashlink (+); HSBC — Quikcash (+); Lombard Bank — Quikcash (+); BNF Bank — neither; APS Bank — Cashlink (+). Total participants: Cashlink 2, Quikcash 2.

| Deployer | 2021 | 2022 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|

| HSBC | 49 | 48 | -1 |
| Lombard Bank | 15 | 18 | +3 |
| BNF Bank | 13 | 15 | +2 |

Narrative: Five banks operate ATMs in Malta, plus Euronet (an IAD). Euronet grew its fleet 2020-2021 and again in 2022; after installing its first ATMs in 2017 it expanded strongly to 207 ATMs by end-2019, then contracted in 2020 (pandemic, reduced transactions; removed/paused some), and has been restoring/growing steadily since. Since 2018 Bank of Valletta's fleet fell 26 units (100 at end-2018 to 74 at end-2022) and HSBC's fell from 79 to 48 over the same period; the decline has slowed greatly — in 2022 both fell by just one unit (-1% Bank of Valletta, -2% HSBC). BNF Bank's fleet was unchanged 2019-2021 then grew by two units in 2022; Lombard Bank grew by three units in 2022.
