### p.122 — Belgium, Section 4.5: Number and value of cash withdrawals (Figure 4.7)
Figure 4.7: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022. Sources: NBB, Belgian ATM deployers and RBR analysis. (Left axis = number, millions [bell axis]; right axis = average withdrawal value, EUR.)
Table rows 2018 / 2019 / 2020 / 2021 / 2022 / CAGR:
- Annual number of cash withdrawals (millions): 275 / 268 / 187 / 157 / 139 — CAGR -15.7%
- Average cash withdrawals per ATM per month: 2,920 / 2,917 / 1,986 / 2,049 / 2,008 — CAGR -7.6%
- Cash withdrawals as a share of ATM transactions: 59% / 58% / 58% / 61% / 65% — CAGR 2.3%

Body text: number of cash withdrawals fell steadily since 2013; as in much of the region, decline driven by increasing widespread use of cashless payment methods, with contactless and mobile payments — particularly Bancontact's app Payconiq — reducing demand for cash as consumers increasingly pay digitally both in store and online in Belgium. Between 2018 and 2019, number of cash withdrawals declined at an average of 3%, followed by a 37% fall precipitated by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021, as customers were encouraged to use cashless payment methods at retailers. Online and mobile banking were also used to avoid going into branch to reduce face-to-face contact. In 2021, despite physical restrictions being lifted, people maintained COVID-19 payment habits, with annual cash withdrawals declining by a further 7%; 2022 saw a sharper decline of 12%, as the four major deployers began removing machines in preparation for Batopin expansion. Proportion of cash withdrawals making up ATM transactions stayed stable in 2022, with both ING and KBC Bank reporting lower shares of ATM transactions as cash withdrawals than the average for Belgium, whereas bpost reported a higher share than the 2021 average. Batopin reported a higher-than-average share of ATM transactions as cash withdrawals, which could indicate that people are more frequently using alternative means for banking activities, such as online and mobile channels. This then reduces demand for such functionalities at ATMs, leading to higher usage of ATMs solely for cash withdrawal.

Body text: Between 2018 and 2019, total value of cash withdrawals from Belgian ATMs declined by 9%; 2020 saw a sharper drop of 22% to EUR 29 billion (USD 33 billion), as a result of customers limiting their visits in order to keep social distancing orders. Decline continued in 2021 to EUR 28 billion (USD 33 billion) and in 2022 cash withdrawals fell further to reach a value of EUR 24 billion (USD 25 billion). As a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, the average value of a cash withdrawal rose by 24% in 2020 to EUR 173 (USD 197) as customers made fewer, higher-value cash withdrawals, continuing to rise by 3% in 2021 to an average of EUR 178 (USD 210). In 2022, the average value resumed pre-pandemic decline to reach EUR 170 (USD 179). Average value of withdrawals per ATM also maintained pandemic trends of EUR 4 million (USD 4 million). Regarding individual deployers, KBC Bank again reported one of the highest withdrawal values in 2022 of EUR 199 (USD 210), up from EUR 196 (USD 231) in 2021. By contrast, the ATMs of Belfius saw much lower sums withdrawn per transaction, with an average withdrawal value of EUR 142 (USD 149) in 2022.

Body text: As in previous years, there were just two ATM suppliers to the Belgian market in 2022, Diebold Nixdorf and NCR. Diebold Nixdorf had the higher share of the installed base, providing 68% of Belgium's ATMs, while NCR models constituted the remaining 32%. Diebold Nixdorf's share increased by three percentage points during 2022, while NCR's decreased by the same proportion. This rise in Diebold Nixdorf's share was partly driven by Batopin's addition of 372 new machines from this manufacturer. Belfius and BNP Paribas Fortis were the largest deployers of Diebold Nixdorf ATMs, with both fleets exclusively comprising Diebold Nixdorf models. Collectively, both deployers constituted 47% of all Diebold Nixdorf ATMs in Belgium. Argenta, bpost, AXA and Crelan also exclusively deployed Diebold Nixdorf ATMs. This was also true of other deployers in the joint venture JofiCo, whereby the manufacturer provided hardware and services for the banks' fleets. The most commonly reported Diebold Nixdorf models in the Belgian market were the CS 56Z, DN Series 450 and CS 4060, with all three models collectively accounting for 43% of total terminals from this vendor. Two of the largest deployers, ING and KBC Group, deployed exclusively NCR ATMs, accounting for 80% of the vendor's total in the country. The most commonly reported model in Belgium was NCR's SelfServ 84, constituting 76% of total NCR models in the country. SelfServ 88 was the second most common NCR model installed across Belgium, accounting for 7% of the manufacturer's total and exclusively deployed by Batopin. In terms of IADs, Travelex deployed NCR ATMs only, while all YourCash ATMs were Diebold Nixdorf models. Loomis is known to deploy both NCR and Diebold Nixdorf machines.
