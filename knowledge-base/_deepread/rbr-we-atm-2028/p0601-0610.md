Source: RBR analysis.
Narrative: At end-2022 Sweden had 2,305 ATMs; the ATM count has declined over the past ten years and fell 7.3% in 2022. With 218 ATMs per million people (down from 238 at end-2021), Sweden has one of the lowest ATM densities in the region. Off-site ATMs are the most profitable location type, with 77% of ATMs deployed off-site; second most common is through-the-wall (TTW) at 11% of ATMs. PIN change offered at 72% of ATMs; 77% could print statements; other additional facilities remain low. Owing to growing popularity of digital wallets and card payments, the number and total value of cash withdrawals decreased in 2022, from SEK 79 billion (USD 9 billion) in 2021 to SEK 78 billion (USD 8 billion) — i.e. from 58 million withdrawals (value SEK 79bn) in 2021 to 57 million withdrawals (value SEK 78bn) in 2022. Diebold Nixdorf is the largest supplier with 93% of the market; the remainder is NCR terminals. The Swedish ATM market is predicted to continue decreasing between end-2022 and end-2028 at an annual average of 2%; by end-2028 the installed base is projected to be 2,025 ATMs. (Footer: Sweden: Page 1.)

Narrative: At end-2022 there were 121 banks operating in Sweden, comprising 42 Swedish commercial banks, 33 foreign-owned banks, 45 savings banks and two co-operatives. Sveriges Riksbank is the (independent) central bank responsible for monetary policy. The "Big Four" largest banks are Swedbank Group, Handelsbanken, Nordea Bank and SEB. Swedbank Group (Swedbank + Savings Banks) had the largest branch network (279 outlets) at end-2022. Handelsbanken closed 162 branches between 2021 and 2022 (ending at 208) and has maintained "home market status" in the Netherlands since 2013. SEB has significant international presence across Scandinavia, the Baltic states and Germany; in 2011 SEB acquired DnB NOR's Retail customer mortgage portfolio and opened a branch in Hong Kong. The Nordea Group is by far the largest foreign bank in Sweden (since October 2018, when Nordea moved its corporate HQ from Stockholm to Helsinki, Finland, to position itself within the Eurozone's banking union). Two retailers — ICA and KF (Kooperativa Förbundet, the Swedish Co-op) — operate direct banking subsidiaries; ICA Banken (operating since 2001) operates through its retail outlets and is the third largest ATM deployer in the market.
