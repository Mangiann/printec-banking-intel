### p.391 — Italy: ATM deployers (Section 2 continued, body text)
Covers major Italian ATM deployers and ownership/networks:
- **Gruppo BPER Banca**: fourth-largest bank in Italy; total assets EUR 152 billion (USD 163 billion); branch network of 1,913 outlets at end-2022. In 2022 BPER acquired Banca Carige.
- **Gruppo Monte dei Paschi di Siena (MPS)**: ranks fifth in terms of assets, with EUR 132 billion (USD 142 billion) as of June 2022. Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena is the parent company of the group, which is composed of a number of financial institutions in Italy and other European countries. Gruppo MPS had a total of 1,360 branches in Italy at end-2022.
- **Banca Nazionale del Lavoro (BNL)**: privatised in 1998; French bank BNP Paribas acquired it in 2006. BNL had a network of 660 branches at end of 2022.
- **Crédit Agricole Italia**: total assets EUR 103 billion (USD 96 billion) and 1,148 branches in Italy as of June 2022. The group is part of the French bank Crédit Agricole, which in March 2007 acquired controlling shares of Cariparma and Banca Popolare FriulAdria — both previously part of Gruppo Intesa Sanpaolo — in order to create a new banking group outside France. Gruppo Crédit Agricole Merged with Crédit Agricole Italia in April 2022.
- **Iccrea Banca**: parent bank of the Iccrea Cooperative Banking Group (BCCs), comprising ~150 cooperative credit banks (BCCs), operating as a central banking institution for BCCs and providing them with banking products and services. At end of 2022, the group had 2,470 branches, ranking second in the country.
- **Cassa Centrale Banca**: parent company of Gruppo Casse Rurali Trentine — also operates as a central banking institution for Casse Rurali (rural banks) and BCCs in the Italian region of Trento. Provides banking and acquiring services to its member banks and manages their ATM networks. At the end of 2022, the group had 1,474 branches.
- **Italian post office (Poste Italiane)**: with 12,756 outlets as of June 2022 — plays an important role in the Italian payments system through its financial services arm Banco Posta.

### p.392 — Italy: Section 3 "ATM networks, sharing and services" (3.1 ATM networks and sharing)
- First ATM in Italy installed in 1978, on the national shared ATM network, Bancomat, set up by the Italian Banking Association ABI (Associazione Bancaria Italiana). The Bancomat network had great difficulty gaining customer acceptance during the 1980s, with a series of well-publicised fraud cases undermining public confidence in the security of the system. Banks decided to shut down their network branch opening hours as a result. The network won the early acceptance battle.
- Pelé / Nazionale Interbancaria (PIN): national interbank network developed by SIA (Società Interbancaria per l'Automazione) in 1984. The PIN is an electronic system connecting all the banks' Centri Operazione Dati (data centres) and provides a specialised and secure infrastructure for the transfer of interbank data. The main objective is to ensure system helped resolve public confidence in network during the 1990s.
- In January 2007, SIA and Società per i Servizi Bancari (SSB), an issuer and acquirer processor, merged to create a new entity, SIA-SSB Group. This company manages the national interbank network to which all banks are connected. SIA-SSB changed its name to SIA in 2017.
- Three main processing centres (omni-application) — SIA, Nexi and Iccrea Banca — offer ATM and EFTPOS services to their respective members.
- **Bancomat** is the national ATM network and connected 36,342 ATM at the end of 2022 (a 3% decrease since end-2021) and comprise for 83% of the country's total installations.
- **QuPaMultibanca** is a sub-network within Bancomat, and it was set up by ICBPI – Istituto Centrale delle Banche Popolari Italiane (now Nexi), and offers its member additional ATM facilities, such bill payments and account transfers. QuPaMultibanca members place its logo on their ATMs next to the Bancomat marque, to show that additional facilities via QuPaMultibanca are available for the deployers. 14 banks were part of the QuPaMultibanca agreement at the end of 2022, with a total of 14,710 ATMs. Gruppo UniCredit, Gruppo MPS and BPER Banca are the largest deployers in QuPaMultibanca, accounting together for 63% of the network's ATMs.
- Postamat belongs to Banco Posta. The Italian Bureau of Poste Italiane and BPER Banca are the only deployers of off-site ATMs; Banco Posta's ATMs are not absorbing all Creval's ATMs.

### p.393 — Italy: domestic sharing participation (body + Figure 3.2)
- **Bancomat** (formerly Consorzio Bancomat) manages the Bancomat and PagoBancomat domestic payment card schemes and is the owner of the related trademarks. It also promotes the use of payment cards in Italy. The PagoBancomat logo was introduced in 1995 to draw attention to the payment function of debit cards. 439 banks were members of the Bancomat scheme at the end of 2022.
- SIA oversees ATM transactions between the three main processing centres: SIA, Nexi and Iccrea Banca. SIA is responsible for switching and processing ATM transactions for all Banco Posta's ATMs and several banks within Bancomat.
- Nexi is responsible for processing ATM transactions for its bank members within QuPaMultibanca, a sub-network within Bancomat. Iccrea Banca is responsible for processing ATM transactions for the BCCs within Bancomat.

### p.397 — Italy: 4.3 Locations (body text continued)
- **TTW** (exterior wall of bank branch) is the most common ATM location in Italy as it offers convenient access to cash. Between 2018 and 2021 the number of TTW ATMs decreased, but in 2022 the number of this location increased by 1%, totalling 22,833. TTW account for more than half of the ATMs in Italy.
- **Bank lobby** is the second most common ATM location, accounting for 26% of the total in 2022. Commonly used for automated deposit ATMs. Favoured lobby location for Gruppo Intesa Sanpaolo (50% of its ATMs in this location), Gruppo UniCredit (34%), and Banca Popolare di Sondrio (81%). Decrease of 622 units (-5%) from end-2021, owing to some big banks such as Gruppo UniCredit rationalising this location.
- **Hall** location ranks third. Gruppo Intesa Sanpaolo owns the highest number of hall ATMs in the country, accounting alone for 37% of ATMs in this location. Gruppo UniCredit owns 33% of Italy's hall ATMs. Together these banks account for 70% of hall ATMs in Italy.
- **11%** of Italian ATMs are in non-branch (off-site) locations, down from 12% in 2021. Gruppo Intesa Sanpaolo is the largest bank to deploy off-site ATMs, with a share of 20% of all off-site ATMs in the country at end-2022. All ATMs belonging to the IAD Euronet are located off-site and they account for 25% of ATMs in this location.
- There were **two drive-through ATMs** at the end of 2022, deployed by Gruppo BPER Banca.
