### p.163 — Denmark: Page 8. Section 3.2 ATM Pooling
**3.2: ATM Pooling.** Kontanten was formed in 2018 when three banks — Handelsbanken, Nykredit Bank and Spar Nord Bank — entered into an agreement with Nokas, an IAD. The network is unrelated to the PPA network of the same name established in the 1980s. Nokas manages the shared network, with all ATMs having shared branding.
Since launch, member-bank count has grown every year. In 2022 the number of members rose to **25 — up from 18 at end-2021** — with several former SDC members migrating their terminals to Kontanten. New members: Broager Sparekasse, Fanø Sparekasse, Frøs Sparekasse, Lån & Spar Bank, Rise Sparekasse, Sparekassen Balling, Sparekassen Bredebro and Sparekassen Djursland. Following these additions, Kontanten's fleet grew by **20% to reach 317 machines.**

Body: At end-2022 there were **1,955 ATMs** deployed across Denmark — a 2% decrease from end-2021, in line with recent trends; the number of ATMs in Denmark falling each year since its peak of **3,067 machines in 2008.** ATM numbers in Denmark fell in the immediate aftermath of the 2008 financial crisis as bank branches closed and bank mergers/acquisitions led to consolidation of formerly competing terminals. Decline developed into a long-term trend on use of cashless and digital payment methods continuing to grow, reducing demand for cash. The domestic MobilePay has become far more prevalent in the market and have further reduced the need for cash among Danes. COVID-19 pandemic also contributed to the fall — consumers more likely to opt for seemingly more hygienic payment methods such as contactless payment cards. There were two lockdowns in Denmark which reduced demand for cash, with one major ATM deployer reporting a fall of 40% in cash demand during the initial months of the pandemic.

Body: 50% of ATMs were located within the bank branch, down from 57% at end-2021. Majority of those located in the lobby of the branch (with installed through the exterior wall (TTW)). There is just one drive-through ATM in Denmark, belonging to Ringkøbing Landbobank, after Kontanten uninstalled the three terminals they had previously operated.

### p.167 — Denmark: Page 12. Section 4.4 Functionality / hardware features
**4.4: Functionality and hardware features. Figure 4.4: ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022** (columns ATMs | SATs):
- Automated note deposit: **939** ATMs | 0 SATs.
  - of which: note recycling: **701** | N/A.
- Automated cheque deposit: **0** | 0.
- **Total: 939** ATMs | 0 SATs.
Body: All ATMs in Denmark are able to offer printed statements. Over half of ATMs are now capable of multi-currency dispensing, whilst 16% accept deposits in multiple currencies. Furthermore, 18% of ATMs are equipped with dynamic currency conversion (DCC). An increasing number of ATMs now offer auto guidance for customers who are visually impaired — at the end of 2022, 42% of ATMs had this function. At end-2022, **48% of ATMs offered automated note deposit**, showing a one percentage point decrease compared to end-2021. 36% of terminals in Denmark are currently capable of note recycling. This proportion is set to increase over the forecast period, with deployers replacing their older machines with cash recyclers. No Danish ATMs offer automated cheque deposit, due to the fact that cheques have not been in circulation in the country since January 2017. Anti-skimming devices and integrated security cameras feature in the vast majority of ATMs in Denmark, and are present in 85% and 84% of machines respectively. Almost half of terminals are equipped with note degradation technology, and the most common method of note degradation is ink staining. At the end of 2022, 11% of ATMs were enabled with a touch screen.

Body: Total number of cash withdrawals grew by 6% in 2022 to reach 35 million, representing a change from the trend of increasingly fewer cash withdrawals seen in recent years. This is largely due to lifting of lockdown restrictions in February 2022, which allowed businesses to open as normal for the vast majority of the year. In 2020 and 2021 the number of cash withdrawals fell sharply, with both years being heavily impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic, and consequent lower demand for cash. Consumers turned towards more hygienic payment methods throughout the pandemic, such as contactless cards and digital wallets such as Apple Pay, Google Pay and MobilePay. Furthermore, the closure of businesses (including bars and restaurants) in which cash would be frequently used for payment affected the number of withdrawals made from Danish ATMs. The number of monthly cash withdrawals per ATM in Denmark increased to beyond the level seen in both 2020 and 2021. As the installed base of Danish ATMs shrunk and demand for cash grew following lifting of pandemic restrictions, each individual ATM saw greater use with an average of 1,496 withdrawals per month. This represents an increase of 10% from the previous year.

Body: Total value of cash withdrawals from Danish ATMs rose to DKK 62 billion (USD 9 billion) in 2022, an increase of 4% from the previous year. This was due to the removal of lockdown restrictions and the consequent increase in opportunities for in-person transactions and cash usage. Increase in the value of cash withdrawals was not as sharp as the rise in the number of withdrawals, meaning the average value of a cash withdrawal fell by 3% to DKK 1,749 (USD 247). In recent years this number has been relatively high, with many Danes routinely withdrawing large amounts of cash in order to avoid negative interest rates affecting their deposits. However, in July 2022, Danmarks Nationalbank took the decision to raise interest rates from -0.6% to -0.1% in response to the economic fallout of the pandemic and the Russian invasion of Ukraine. This is likely to have made the practice of regular high value withdrawals less common in Denmark, consequently driving the average value of a withdrawal down. As well as this, both 2020 and 2021 saw sharp increases in the average value of a cash withdrawal, with COVID-19 restrictions reducing the frequency with which Danish consumers were able to leave their homes. As a result, 2020 and 2021 saw lockdowns no longer imposed in Denmark, it was possible to make smaller, more frequent ATM withdrawals.

Diebold Nixdorf has been the largest manufacturer by market share in Denmark until 2019, but in recent years an increasing number of NCR terminals have been deployed, with NCR/Euronet expanding its fleet aggressively in 2021. Nordea is the most significant deployer of Diebold Nixdorf terminals with 318. Among the other larger deployers of Diebold Nixdorf machines are Kontanten and Sydbank, with 138 and 98 respectively. The most prevalent model produced by Diebold Nixdorf is the CS 4560 (CINEO 4560), of which there are 334. There are also over 100 units of both the ProCash 2150xe and CS 4060 (CINEO 4060).
