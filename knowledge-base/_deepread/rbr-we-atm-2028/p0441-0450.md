**Body text takeaways:** The Dutch ATM installed base grew for first time in eight years to **5,117 terminals** in the country at end-2022, up **4% from end-2021**. This was primarily driven by the absence of the lockdown restrictions seen in 2020 and 2021, which encouraged deployers to either maintain or expand the size of their fleets. Geldmaat (the country's largest ATM deployer, owned by major Dutch banks via the **Dutch ATM pooling project**) contributed to this growth. As bank branches closed throughout the year, the share of ATMs located off-site increased to **96%** in 2022. All remaining branch-based ATMs are located through the exterior wall (TTW). The **density of ATMs per 100 bank branches** in the Netherlands increased sharply in 2022, as banks shuttered a significant number of their outlets. Most notably, **ABN AMRO** closed 42 of its branches, leaving 27 in its network. ATM functionality tends to be limited in the Netherlands, although **15%** of ATMs (cash and cardless cash withdrawal) — via the display of a QR code on the ATM screen are both possible at over 80% of the installed base. An increasing share of ATMs offer automated note deposit facilities, with all such ATMs recycling the deposited notes. All Geldmaat ATMs are protected by built-in/integrated security cameras, anti-skimming devices and use note degradation technology, in response to the explosive attacks which were frequently carried out on Dutch ATMs in recent years. There was a total of **130 million cash withdrawals** made at Dutch ATMs in 2022 — representing an 8% increase from the previous year. The total value of withdrawals grew by **13%**, with EUR 26 billion (USD 23 billion) withdrawn throughout the year. This increase in ATM usage was driven primarily by the lack of pandemic-induced restrictions on freedom of movement, meaning Dutch consumers had more opportunities to make in-person payments. The average value of a cash withdrawal increased in the Netherlands, predominantly due to the high rates of inflation seen in the country.

### p.446 — Netherlands Section 1 (continued) (footer "Netherlands: Page 2")
Body text on manufacturers, software and forecast:
- **NCR** is the largest supplier of ATMs to the Netherlands, accounting for **53%** of the country total at end-2021. **Diebold Nixdorf** accounts for most of the rest of the installed base, while **Triton** is also present.
- Most ATMs run from IBM network central hardware, with the remainder running from HP servers. All ATMs run over Windows operating systems. **Geldmaat's usage of Tetonyx** solutions makes this supplier's **Mocra** solution by far the most common terminal driving software, application software, message protocol, management/monitoring software and cash forecasting and management software. All ATMs can have software updates distributed remotely. It is extremely uncommon for ATMs to use dedicated customer relationship management software or security software.
- Nearly all ATMs see cash replenishment and first line maintenance performed by third parties, with **Brink's** the largest provider of such services. While second line maintenance is outsourced for the vast majority of terminals, most commonly to ATM suppliers, **In2Retail** performs the function in-house for its entire fleet. Helpdesk services are kept in-house for all deployers.
- The **installed base of ATMs in the Netherlands is expected to contract** at an annual average rate of **3%** over the forecast period to reach **4,350 at the end of 2028**. This decline in ATM numbers is expected to be caused by the ongoing reduction in demand for cash as well as the declining availability of profitable ATM locations. The size of the **Dutch ATM installed base will fall gradually**, with authorities keen to ensure adequate cash provision within five kilometres of virtually all Dutch residents. The average **number of cash withdrawals per ATM** is also forecast to fall, although the average cash withdrawal **value** is forecast to rise further, continuing the trend of preceding years.

**Body takeaways:** At end-2022, there were a total of **71 banks** operating in the Netherlands, down from 74 at end of 2021, with Amsterdam Trade Bank declared bankrupt in April 2022. There are 22 domestically owned banks and 10 subsidiaries of foreign banks as well as 39 foreign banks which operate branches within the Netherlands. The **European Central Bank (ECB)** supervises the activities of the largest Dutch bank, which are categorised as being significant under Eurozone rules. Smaller banks in the Netherlands are overseen by the Dutch central bank, **De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB)**. This institution shares the responsibility of regulating the Dutch financial sector with the **Dutch Authority for the Financial Markets**. The total value of Dutch banking assets at the end of 2022 was EUR 2.6 trillion (USD 2.8 trillion), down 1% from end-2021. The Netherlands has a large banking sector relative to its overall economy, with around three times as large as the Netherlands' GDP. The ratio has taken the global financial crisis of 2007-2008 — the banking sector has been as large as banking 4 times GDP. The largest bank by assets is ING, with EUR 968 billion (USD 1 trillion) at end-2022, followed by Rabobank with EUR 629 billion (USD 674 billion) and ABN AMRO with EUR 379 billion (USD 407 billion). The market is fairly concentrated, with the five largest banks accounting for 83% of total banking sector assets at the end of 2022, down from 84% at end-2021. A merger between major **Nationale-Nederlanden** and national postal bank **NPB Postbank** saw the establishment of the ING group bank in 1991. The postal bank continued to operate as a separate entity until 2009, when it was integrated with ING's business. **Rabobank** was formed into its current guise in 2016, when 106 local cooperative credit institutions merged with their central institution. ABN AMRO, meanwhile, was established when ABN and Amro Bank merged, in 1991. During the global financial crisis of 2007 and 2008, the Dutch government invested nearly EUR 40 billion (USD 57 billion) to ensure the survival of its domestic financial sector. A levy on banks was subsequently introduced in 2012. The largest bank, ING, was the recipient of EUR 10 billion (USD 14 billion) in state support, which it had repaid by 2014.

### p.448 — Netherlands Section 2 (continued) (footer "Netherlands: Page 4")
Body text on banking history:
- **ABN AMRO** was nationalised at the height of the crisis in 2008, and subsequently merged with the Dutch business of Fortis Bank in 2010. Another entity to be nationalised was the banking and insurance group **SNS REAAL**, with this move made in 2013 to save it from insolvency. The banks which were part of SNS REAAL merged into de Volksbank in 2017, although the latter continues to operate under four different brand names — SNS, ASN Bank, RegioBank and BLG Wonen. ABN AMRO and de Volksbank are owned by the government through NLFI, a government institution established to exercise shareholder rights within the financial sector, with the ultimate goal of re-privatising them.
- The **number of bank branches** in the Netherlands has fallen every year since 2005. This decrease has accelerated in recent years, with total branch numbers more than halving from the end of 2019 to end-2022. At the end of the year, there were 580 branches in the country, down from 726 at the end of 2021. This represents a decrease of 20%, and marks the third consecutive year in which branch number have fallen by over 20% year-on-year.
- Although branch numbers had been falling prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, the reduced footfall across the Netherlands' branch network precipitated by lockdowns put further financial strain on banks looking to reduce their physical footprint. As a result, some branches which had initially been closed temporarily due to pandemic restrictions were not reopened. The pandemic trend of branch closures can be primarily attributed to Dutch banks' success in migrating many cash-based transactions to self-service. The digitalisation of banking in the Netherlands has led to a greater share of customers no longer needing to visit bank branches, with an increasing rate of banking services accessible online.
- The three largest banks in the country (by assets) all intend to reduce the size of their branch networks, and have invested in ensuring alternative channels are available for customers to carry out banking services. These three banks shuttered 94 branches between them during 2022. **Rabobank** closed just two of its outlets to leave 144, making it the largest of the three institutions by physical footprint. **ING** closed a third of its branches, with 50 remaining open. Since 2019, **ING's** branches have been changing to adopt a "home-like" branch concept, with all branches due to conform to the new concept during 2023. The bank has also introduced pop-up stores in shopping centres, where employees help customers with digital banking. Additionally, **ING** operates a number of service points in outlets of the Primera, Read Shop and Bruna retail chains. ABN AMRO shuttered by far the most branches of any bank during 2022. At the end of the year its network consisted of just 27 outlets, having closed 71% of the 94 branches operating at end-2021.
- **De Volksbank** operates the most branches in the country, with 203 open at the end of 2022. These branches are operated under the SNS Bank brand. Since the conversion of many branches to SNS Shops in 2009, there were consistent increases in the size of the bank's physical footprint. These locations are generally a more relaxed, retail-focused atmosphere than traditional branches, offering banking services and financial advice without the need to book an appointment beforehand. The RegioBank brand — which is also part of de Volksbank — does not operate any full branches, but has a present at over 500 locations.

### p.449 — Netherlands Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (footer "Netherlands: Page 5")
**3.1 ATM networks and sharing.** ATMs were first installed in the Netherlands in 1983, and banks initially agreed not to extend ATM services beyond basic functionality. Banks also agreed that the installation of ATMs through the exterior wall of bank branches (TTW) and in off-site locations would be limited unless there was consultation with competitor banks. Growth in the installed base was relatively steady until the market peaked at around 9,500 machines in 2008. Since then, there has been a long-term decline in the size of the Dutch installed base, with 2011 and 2014 the only exceptions to this trend (owing to expansion in the IAD sector).
- An ATM sharing network was established by the four largest banks — ABN, Amro Bank, NMB Bank and Rabobank — in 1985, under the trading name. For several years, Postbank was the only deployer to operate outside of the network. Postbank subsequently merged with NMB Bank in 1989 and integrated into ING in 1990, leading to the creation of a new network in 1991, which allowed Postbank's ATMs to be used by ING and Postbank customers and vice versa. An interbank arrangement enables the Dutch Banking Association, announced in December 2019 that all bank and Geldmaat ATMs would have to cease operating between 11pm and 7am with immediate effect, with successful ATM attacks more difficult to achieve if the ATM is deactivated. The fact that less than 2% of cash withdrawals from external ATMs were being made at night meant that it was not considered to have a damaging impact on cash access, and a total of 25 explosive attacks in 2020 represented a marked improvement from 71 in 2019.

### p.450 — Netherlands Section 3 (continued) (footer "Netherlands: Page 6")
Body text on Geldmaat / deployers / attacks:
- **Geldmaat** — a joint venture set up by ING, Rabobank and ABN AMRO — the largest ATM deployer in the Netherlands, with **4,205 terminals** at the end of 2022. The vast majority of Geldmaat ATMs are installed away from bank branches, but the most of regular explosive attacks on Dutch ATMs has placed the off-site ATM sector under severe pressure. While the majority of such attacks do not result in the successful theft of cash, it threatens the safety of those living near targeted ATM installations, as well as driving insurance costs up.
- In 2019, there were **71 explosive attacks on ATMs**, up sharply from 42 in 2018. Criminal gangs involved in explosive attacks have moved targets as ATM deployers have responded, including by targeting ATMs across the border in Germany. In 2019, criminals were having success in breaking a certain type of ATM deployed by ABN AMRO, with more than 50 attacks reported. This prompted the deployment of around 500 ABN AMRO and Geldmaat (ex-ABN AMRO) machines in December 2019. Some were subsequently brought back online, while others were replaced with other models.
- The primary focus of explosive attacks in 2020 was de Volksbank, with the bank deciding to temporarily decommission more than 200 of its ATMs located in HEMA and Spar stores in May 2020. In light of continued attacks, the deployer elected to uninstall its other 1,800 ATMs through partner banks — by end-2020, it no longer deployed any ATMs.
- In an effort to thwart explosive attacks, the **Dutch Banking Association** announced in December 2019 that all bank and Geldmaat ATMs would have to cease operating between 11pm and 7am, with successful ATM attacks more difficult to achieve if the ATM is deactivated. The fact that less than 2% of cash withdrawals from external ATMs were being made at night meant that it was not considered to have a damaging impact on cash access, and a total of 25 explosive attacks in 2020 represented a marked improvement from 71 in 2019.
- As of early 2023, **Geldmaat was still committed to retaining the measure** for the foreseeable future in the case of ATMs located outside, with exceptions for ATMs in areas of night entertainment open until 2am, rather than 11pm, and making ATMs operational from 6am rather than 7am.
- In 2022, Geldmaat also began opening "stores", in which several ATMs are located within closed premises. These are seen as safer and less vulnerable to explosive attacks. As of early 2023, 16 such stores had been opened. The stores also provide longer opening hours than ATMs deployed outside, with a typical outlet remaining open from 7am until 10pm. Geldmaat also deploys some ATMs in supermarkets and bookshops.
- Note degradation technology, which makes notes unusable in the event of an attack by gluing them together, has been used in the Netherlands since 2021. Ink staining had previously been more common, but the **technology of choice at Geldmaat ATMs** has changed in recent years.
- The number of explosive attacks on Dutch ATMs dropped sharply during 2022 to just nine. New security measures have played a significant role in deterring criminals from carrying out such attacks, with many crossing the border to Germany in order to attempt explosive attacks on ATMs.
