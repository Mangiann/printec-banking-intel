### p.693 — Turkey, Section 4.3 Locations (cont.) (Turkey: Page 12)
Body text only (no figure):
- Most common branch-based location for branch-based ATMs is through the exterior wall (TTW), with vast majority of ATMs deployed by smaller banks such as HSBC, Anadolubank, Fibabanka and ICBC found at this location. Total number of TTW installations fell by 1,300 in 2022 to reach 20,780. The majority of this decline was accounted for by Akbank and İşbank, which collectively removed over 1,000 terminals. Akbank increased the size of its fleet in total, but moved many of its off-site terminals and added further assisted self-service ATMs within bank halls.
- Overall proportion of ATMs located in the bank halls increased once again in 2022, with 5% of all installations found at this location at end of year. İşbank-based ATMs are seen as an important aspect of banks' efforts to transform their branches and digitalise processes for customers, by allowing greater scope for self-service transactions. As well as this, indoor ATMs are considered to offer greater security than those located TTW or off-site. Terminals found in bank halls tend to offer greater functionality, such as assisted self-service, touch-screens, QR-code readers and NFC readers.
- Kuveyt Türk deploys a more significant share of ATMs in the bank hall (44%) when compared to the other deployers in Turkey. This includes the majority of its ASSTs, known as Self Point, which function as free-standing 'retail branches' for the bank. Akbank — which deploys roughly half of all bank hall ATMs in Turkey — also deploys its Giga Branch ("Extra Teller") ATMs in bank halls, with the aim of digitalising an increasing number of transactions. 13% of DenizBank's ATMs are deployed in bank halls, and it has reported a preference for installing note recyclers at this location, as there is a more even split of deposits and withdrawals made within the bank hall.
- Just 1% of terminals in Turkey are located in the bank lobby, with roughly 650 machines found at this location at the end of 2022. Over half of all Turkish ASSTs located in the bank lobby are deployed by Ziraat Bank.
- Ziraat Bank remained the only drive-through ATM in Turkey in 2020. Garanti BBVA, however, deployed its first in the same year, which is now the only such terminal in the country.

### p.695 — Turkey, Section 4.4 Functionality (cont.) (Turkey: Page 14)
**Figure 4.5: ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022** (Sources: Turkish ATM deployers and RBR analysis). Table columns ATMs / SATs:

Text: Turkish ATMs typically offer a wide range of functionalities. All terminals allow cardholders to transfer funds to other accounts at the same bank, whilst 81% offer transfers to those of different banks. Nearly all ATMs in Turkey offer the option to change PIN or receive a printed statement, and 78% of machines allow funds to be added to a prepaid card.
Almost a third of Turkish ATMs dispense multiple currencies; Akbank offers this functionality at all of its terminals. Besides Turkish lira, it is most common for ATMs to offer withdrawals of US dollars and euros. Whilst not as prevalent, an increasing number of machines are beginning to dispense pound sterling.
99% of the Turkish installed ATM base allow cardless cash withdrawals. Many deployers facilitate cardless transactions through a QR code displayed on the terminal's screen, whilst others offer this feature using Bluetooth or a one-time password sent to the customer's phone. DenizBank and Yapı Kredi are among the deployers to offer cardless transactions via QR code, whilst customers may use Bluetooth to withdraw cash at İşbank ATMs. The vast majority of ATMs allow other cardless transactions such as cash deposits, transfers, credit card repayments and loan repayments.
Contactless cash withdrawals using NFC readers are available at a far smaller share of Turkish ATMs, with İşbank and Yapı Kredi both offering the feature at the end of 2022. This equated to 18% of the total installed base in the country. There are many terminals across the country that are fitted with NFC hardware, but are yet to be enabled for contactless transactions. For most deployers, QR codes are sufficient for customers wishing to make a cardless withdrawal. It is also presented a far more cost-effective option, as no hardware installation is required.
The number of ATMs which accept automated note deposit increased slightly to 49,732 by the end of the year. This represents 96% of installed base. The small number of ATMs which had offered automated cheque deposit were removed in 2022, with thousands of machines transforming rather than this method.
The proportion of ATMs in Turkey which were note recyclers at the end of 2022 had risen to 63%, up from 61% & end-2021. This equates to 66% of all Turkish ATMs which accept automated note deposit.
There are several banks in the country which deploy assisted self-service terminals. Both Kuveyt Türk and TEB deploy ASSTs with remote (video) assistance, whilst Odea Bank and Akbank deploy ASSTs with in-person assistance. Akbank's Giga Branch ("Extra Teller") ATMs are deployed at its branches as part of its digital transformation project. The bank expanded the size of its ASST fleet during 2022, as it introduced 390 further machines.

Text: Just over a quarter of Turkish ATMs are equipped with a QR code reader. However, a far greater share of machines offer cardless transactions using a QR code. In most cases, a QR code is displayed on the terminal screen, which is then scanned by the customer in order to make a transaction.
The proportion of ATMs with a touch screen rose to 50% by the end of 2022, up from 46% at end-2021. All terminals deployed by Akbank, QNB Finansbank and HSBC are equipped with a touch screen. This is also the case for the vast majority of DenizBank's, Yapı Kredi's and Garanti BBVA machines.
There was a significant rise in the share of ATMs with speech capability for visually-impaired customers to 79% at the end of 2022. This figure has consistently increased recent years, following legislation passed by the Turkish government requiring banks to improve ATM access for their disabled customers.
Ziraat Bank remained the only deployer whose ATMs featured biometric identification at the end of 2022. 20% of its ATMs are equipped with palm-vein identification technology, which enables customers to make cardless transactions. Fingerprint identification technology had been offered at İşbank ATMs in the past, but was removed due to reported issues with the hardware.
Anti-skimming devices are present in virtually all ATMs in Turkey, whilst two thirds are fitted with integrated security cameras. It is very rare for Turkish ATMs to be equipped with note degradation technology. At end of 2022, only 45 machines had this feature.

### p.697 — Turkey, Section 4.5 Number & value of cash withdrawals (Turkey: Page 16)
**Figure 4.7: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018–2022** (Sources: BKM, Turkish ATM deployers and RBR analysis). Line chart: "Annual number, million (left)" vs "Average cash withdrawals (right)". Table:
- **Annual number of cash withdrawals (million)**: 2018 1,502 / 2019 1,395 / 2020 1,323 / 2021 1,388 / 2022 1,629 — CAGR **2.2%**
- **Average cash withdrawals per ATM per month**: 2018 2,409 / 2019 2,193 / 2020 2,087 / 2021 2,202 / 2022 2,406 — CAGR **0.0%**
- **Cash withdrawals as share of ATM transactions**: 2018 50% / 2019 51% / 2020 50% / 2021 54% / 2022 55% — CAGR **2.1%**
Text: There were a total of 1.6 billion cash withdrawals made from ATMs in Turkey in 2022, up 18% from the previous year. This can be attributed to a number of factors. Firstly, only partial recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic — which had seen a 17% fall in the number of cash withdrawals in 2020 — took place in 2021, with the total figure remaining far below the level seen before the pandemic. In 2022, restrictions on freedom of movement were no longer in place for the entirety of the year, meaning Turkish cardholders were able to make more frequent cash withdrawals.
As well as this, soaring inflation rates in Turkey and the depreciation of the Turkish lira have forced consumers to make more frequent cash withdrawals, with some banks imposing daily cash withdrawal limits. For example, the country's largest ATM deployer, Ziraat Bank, had a limit of TRY 1,500 (USD 80); as a result of this, cardholders are required to make a greater number of withdrawals at ATMs in order to make the same purchases as before, due to rising prices.
Due to the shrinking installed ATM base as well as the increasing number of cash withdrawals, there was a sharp increase in the average number of monthly cash withdrawals per ATM, which rose from 2,202 at end-2021 to 2,406 at end of 2022.
The larger ATM deployers in Turkey, such as Ziraat Bank, İşbank and Yapı Kredi, tend to see a higher average number of transactions, due to the wider reach provided by their networks, as well as the higher share of off-site ATMs within their fleets. Comparatively, the ATMs of smaller deployers — which have far fewer ATMs deployed off-site — do not see as many individual withdrawals. Odea Bank — with a network of 69 ATMs — saw just 439 monthly withdrawals at each of its machines on average, compared to averages of 2,000 or more at the eight largest deployers.

### p.698 — Turkey, Section 4.5 Value of cash withdrawals (Turkey: Page 17)
Text: Cash withdrawals made up 60% of all ATM transactions in Turkey in 2022, up from 54% the previous year, with inflation increasing demand for cash. The majority of banks reported that over 50% of transactions are accounted for by withdrawals. This share reflects the need to withdraw cash more frequently in a period of rising prices, as well as an increasing number of customers preferring to use alternative channels, such as online banking, to carry out non-withdrawal transactions.

Between 2018 and 2022, the average value of a cash withdrawal in Turkey more than doubled from TRY 542 (USD 115) to TRY 1,189 (USD 72). This represents an average annual growth rate of 22% over the period in local currency. However, this represents an average annual growth rate of 11% in terms of US dollars, as inflation rates have remained high and there has been a currency devaluation against the US dollar during that period.
