### p.805 — Branch-network narrative (text)
Chapter page 3. Three of the largest five banks by branch network — Bank of Cyprus, Hellenic Bank and Alpha Bank — all substantially cut their branch footprint in 2022, collectively shuttering almost 50 branches. RCB Bank lost its banking licence during the year. Despite no bank-branch closures in TRNC in 2022, the net effect was a **13% decline** in Cypriot bank branch numbers, to **just below 400 outlets**. There were **over 700 outlets** across the island as recently as end-2016. Banks streamlining to cut costs and reflect rising electronic transactions.

Text: JCC network supervised by Central Bank of Cyprus; Card-Plus meets needs of TRNC deployers. Hellenic Bank is largest deployer in JCC; Near East Bank largest in Card-Plus. In TRNC, Card-Plus connects most of the eight TRNC deployers (incl. state-owned Kıbrıs Vakıflar). The number of ATMs operating outside the two networks rose by ~3% in 2022; Kıbrıs Vakıflar was the largest ATM deployer at end-2022 — accounts for 31% of the end-2022 (off-network) total.

### p.809 — Section 3: Current ATM market (3.1); Figure 3.1 Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022
Chapter page 7. **Figure 3.1** dual-axis chart: bars = ATMs (left axis 0–1,200), line+diamonds = year-on-year growth (right axis -9% to +9%). Source: JCC, Card-Plus, Cypriot ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
Table beneath the chart (ATMs | Absolute growth | Percentage growth) across 2018-2022 + CAGR:
- ATMs: 2018 **961**; 2019 **1,047**; 2020 **1,031** (approx; chart bar just above 1,000); 2021 **1,044**; 2022 **963**. CAGR row shown ~ **0.1% / N/A**.
- Absolute growth: 2019 **+65**; 2020 **≈-16**; 2021 **+13**; 2022 **-81** (the visible printed values include 65 and -81; intermediate small).
- Percentage growth: 2018 **7.3%** (vs prior); 2019 **8.9%**; 2020 **-1.5%**; 2021 **1.3%**; 2022 **-7.7%**.
Chart confirms: bars roughly flat ~960-1,050 across the period; growth line peaks ~+9% (2019), falls to ~-1.5% (2020), recovers to ~+1.3% (2021), then drops sharply to ~-7.7% (2022).
Text: Cyprus had a rebound in growth during 2021; the ATM market contracted again in 2022 with an **8% decline** in the installed base, standing at **963 ATMs** (lowest total since 2018). Fall largely due to banks reducing on-site/branch ATMs (Hellenic Bank, Bank of Cyprus, removal of branch ATMs), partly offset by IAD (Euronet) growth. Other entities: removed ATMs from market in 2022 include Alpha Bank, RCB Bank (after losing licence Dec 2022), BLOM Bank (left in Aug 2022). Note that the 763 ATMs figure on p.803 (Fig 1.1) and the 963 installed-base figure differ in scope (printed as-is).

Text: Cyprus has a very high density of **769 ATMs per million people** at end-2022, down from **839** a year earlier as the installed base contracted; tourism is an important contributor and good ATM provision caters to inbound tourists plus locals. Density of ATMs per 100 bank branches **rose** during 2022, as both declined but the IAD-sector expansion meant the fall in ATMs was not as great as the fall in bank branches.
