Prose only, no figures. Cash replenishment and first line maintenance are done in-house for most bank branch ATMs, but outsourced for all off-site ATMs. Second line maintenance is universally contracted to third parties; help-desk services mostly in-house.
Forecast statement: the Spanish installed base is forecast to **contract through the forecast period to a total of 41,000 ATMs by end-2028**. More bank branches expected to close, to an anticipated total of **14,400 branches by end-2028**.

### p.563 — Section 2 prose ("Spain: Page 4")
No figures. Until the 2008 crisis, retail banking dominated by savings banks (cajas de ahorros); crisis triggered restructuring, many cajas acquired commercial-bank status. 2014: 32 cajas rurales combined to create Banco de Crédito Cooperativo (BCC); BCC does not operate own branches but centralises/standardises operations for ~19 entities of Grupo Cooperativo Cajamar (formed 2009); BCC operates 6 mobile offices as travelling vehicles serving villages with <1,500 inhabitants.
La Caixa = first savings bank converted to commercial bank, operated as CaixaBank since 2011; a year on from acquiring state-owned Bankia, CaixaBank is Spain's largest domestic bank with assets worth EUR 592 billion (USD 635 billion), **4,191 branches and 11,605 ATMs** (all decreases from 2021). Little/no M&A in 2022; branch numbers still fell **7%, dropping to 17,945 outlets at end-2022, 1,367 fewer than 2021**. Unicaja and Ibercaja each cut branch networks ~10%; Santander, BBVA and Banco Sabadell combined closed >100 outlets. Digital banking transition continues to reduce branches.

### p.564 — Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services ("Spain: Page 5")
First ATM in Spain 1979; installed base surpassed 10,000 in 1989 and 10,000+... peaked at **61,714 ATMs in 2008**, falling since.
**3.1 ATM networks and sharing:** ServiRed, Euro 6000 and Telebanco 4B were the three shared networks/card payment operators until they merged Feb 2018 to form **SistemaPay** (Sistema de Tarjetas y Medios de Pago) — runs the national card payment system, unifying the country toward a single EU-style network. SistemaPay is not an ATM network per se. As of end-2022, **Euro 6000 is the only remaining branded sharing agreement, with members collectively deploying 7,800 ATMs at end-2022**. Former ServiRed and Telebanco 4B brands negotiate ATM sharing bilaterally. ServiRed (ATM fleets of CaixaBank, BBVA, Bankia, Banco Sabadell) was largest single network pre-SistemaPay; at end-2017 ServiRed = 58% of installed base, Euro 6000 = 34%, Telebanco 4B = 17% (Telebanco 4B shared ATMs mostly Grupo Santander).
**Figure 3.1: Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022** (Euro 6000 ATMs): 2018=8,700; 2019=8,425; 2020=8,282; 2021=8,000; 2022=7,800; **CAGR −2.7%**. Source: Spanish ATM deployers and networks and RBR analysis.

### p.566 — Section 3 prose ("Spain: Page 7")
No figures. Cash withdrawals at ATMs deployed by a cardholder's own bank are free; "off-site" transactions often incur a fee. Formation of SistemaPay coincided with a revolution in direct-access fee-charging practices, now applicable to ATM transactions with foreign cards. Cardholders are now no longer able to be charged both an ATM access fee by the ATM owner and a withdrawal fee by their own bank (regulatory requirement since 2016). Cardholder pays the card-issuer disablement fee... For off-site ATMs in Spain, deployer-set fees are common between banks and networks. Banco Sabadell customers charged various banks within the Euro 6000 network (incl. ABANCA, Ibercaja, Kutxabank) for use of EUR 0.65 (USD 0.71). Mentions a pre-existing agreement between CaixaBank, Banco Sabadell and Euro 6000 (expired Oct 2022); a similar agreement between Bankia and ING scheduled to expire 31 January 2023.

### p.568 — Section 3 prose ("Spain: Page 9")
No figures. Largest banks in Spain all reduced ATM fleets in 2022; the largest total reduction was CaixaBank (−1,403 ATMs, vs −1,051 at Santander). The Santander figure represents the steepest single-bank ATM contraction at ~22% of fleet. Banco Santander/ Euro Automatic Cash dynamics: Euro Automatic Cash (the off-site/IAD arm partly owned by Santander) grew. Of the second-largest deployer Grupo Santander's ~6,756 ATMs, decreased 9% (8%?) overall. ATM numbers peaked... The IAD Euronet has the second-largest single ATM fleet in Spain (3,500), behind only Spanish banks; grew in 2022. IT services provided by Rural Services Informáticos (RSI), a processing centre established 1986 for many Spain's cajas rurales and other smaller banks; RSI also manages the ATMs deployed by its members. Cashzone added ~250 terminals (growth ~25% in 2022).
