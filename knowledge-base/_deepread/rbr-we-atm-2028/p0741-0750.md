### p.749 — UK, Section 2 Banking structure
Narrative on ATM network history and consolidation. Key points: In the UK, building societies play a similar role to small savings banks in other European markets; these organisations are mutuals owned by their customers and have a focus on taking deposits and offering mortgage loans to individual customers. Typically building societies have a regional focus, with a branch network based in a specific area. The main exception is Nationwide Building Society, which is the largest institution in this sector by a large margin and which has a network of branches across the country. Discusses LINK ATM network; Supermarket Tesco launched its banking subsidiary Tesco Bank in 2019 in partnership with RBS Group (now NatWest Group) with a number of in-store branches, offering mortgages and savings accounts followed by current accounts. In 2010 Metro Bank was granted a banking licence, the first new high street bank in over 150 years; more recently a number of digital-only banks including Starling, Atom, Tandem and Monzo have entered the UK market. Virgin Money is the largest institution not to fall into any of the above categories. Tymothore Bank and Clydesdale Bank were owned by NAB Group of Australia until February 2016, when they were spun off as CYBG, which acquired Virgin Money, with plans announced to rebrand the whole group under the Virgin Money name. Discusses Irish retail banks (Bank of Ireland, AIB, Ulster Bank/Danske Bank), CYBG, Post Office. The UK banking sector was severely impacted by the 2008 financial crisis. Following the near collapse of Northern Rock in 2008, the British government intervened to nationalise it; Northern Rock was sold to Virgin Money UK in 2012, which was itself acquired by CYBG in late 2018. RBS Group (now NatWest Group) and Lloyds Banking Group received bailouts. In 2008, when government investment was needed, RBS was the world's largest bank by total assets; the government took a majority stake. Lloyds Banking Group acquired HBOS in 2009 to form the most widely used current account/retail banking group; while 43% of retail bank current accounts are accounted for by just five commercial retail banking groups. The possibility of acquiring TSB but was rebuffed. (Footer "UK: Page 4".)

### p.750 — UK, Section 2 Banking structure
Narrative continuation:
- RBS Group (now NatWest Group) announced the sale of 318 branches to Williams & Glyn, a former brand of the Royal Bank of Scotland. Group intended to finalise the sale by end-2016; major UK banks including Santander and CYBG (now Virgin Money) considered possible bids.
- Negotiations with Santander collapsed in September 2016 (sides could not agree a price for the new bank). Given the failure to divest by February 2017, the UK government approached the European Commission with an amendment to the bailout deal — measures worth **GBP 750 million (USD 966 million)** and required RBS Group to provide funding for challenger banks and fintechs entering the UK market.
- UK banks closed a total of **745 branches during 2022, an 8% decline from end-2021**. Number of bank branches has declined each year since 2016 as banks rationalise networks in response to declining customer footfall and the drive for greater cost efficiencies.
- This trend accelerated from 2020 onwards as COVID-19 drove a dramatic decline in customers attending branches (health concerns, legal travel restrictions); the pandemic also drove uptake of digital services among customer groups such as the elderly who had historically relied on traditional in-person branch services. (Footer "UK: Page 5".)
