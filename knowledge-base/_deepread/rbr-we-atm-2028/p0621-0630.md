### p.630 — Switzerland, Section 2 (continued), banking-structure narrative
Full-page body text (no figures). Footer: Switzerland: Page 4. Key points:
- Switzerland's banking sector is one of the oldest and most developed in the world, with a wide range of institutions serving both retail and commercial customers. Retail banking sector includes large multinational banks, cantonal banks, regional banks, savings banks, and cooperative Raiffeisen banks. Stock exchange banks, private banks and foreign-owned banks not involved in retail have operations in the country. With the exception of insurance (which requires a separate licence), all Swiss banks are permitted to offer the full range of financial services.
- Swiss banks and other financial services providers regulated by the Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA); Swiss Bankers Association helps banks manage their self-regulation through issuing directives/guidelines, while auditors and other self-regulatory organisations also assist in ensuring banks comply with regulatory requirements.
- Swiss National Bank (SNB) is the central bank of Switzerland and responsible for setting monetary policy. SNB is the sole issuer of Swiss banknotes and coins, and plays a major role in supervising/promoting the cashless payments sector.
- UBS classified as a globally systemically important bank, active in wealth, investment and asset management, as well as traditional retail banking, with operations across the globe; world's largest private bank. Switzerland's largest bank by assets, with CHF ~1.1 trillion (USD ~1.2 trillion) and the second largest network of branches at end-2022 (UBS operates the second largest branch network in Switzerland after Raiffeisen).
- Credit Suisse was Switzerland's second largest bank, with CHF ~531 billion (USD ~575 billion) in assets at end-2022. Credit Suisse experienced a difficult period in 2022 and faced financial difficulty / loss of confidence; world's third largest network of branches at end of 2022 (third largest in Switzerland after Raiffeisen and UBS).
- In March 2023, following the failure of three American banks with exposure to the technology and cryptocurrency sectors, Credit Suisse underwent a run on deposits and required intervention to avoid a collapse. The Swiss government was unwilling to nationalise the bank, and instead facilitated a takeover by UBS; its only domestic rival. On 19th March 2023, UBS agreed to purchase Credit Suisse for CHF 3 billion (USD 3.2 billion), a consideration which significantly undervalued the bank. The deal will create one of the largest banks in the world, and is set to take several years to complete.
- Switzerland's 24 cantonal banks traditionally focus on offering retail banking services within their respective cantons, although a few do have some operations further afield. All cantonal banks belong to the Association of Cantonal Banks (Verband Schweizer Kantonalbanken), with most having a guarantee from their respective cantons. Zurcher Kantonalbank is the second largest cantonal bank by assets, with CHF 200 billion (USD 215 billion) in assets at end-2022. The next largest cantonal bank by assets is Basler Kantonalbank, Banque Cantonale Vaudoise and Banque Cantonale de Geneve.
- The largest branch network is operated by the Raiffeisen Group, a collective of small co-operative banks, with a single central institution. While the banks in the group have traditionally focused on serving areas in rural and small-town Switzerland, since the millennium some have also established an urban presence. The group has seen a process of consolidation in recent years: at the end of 2022 it had 220 member banks, down from more than 250 in the early 2000s. It was announced in mid-2021 that Raiffeisen Group's directly operated branches in major cities would be released to form new urban member co-operatives.
