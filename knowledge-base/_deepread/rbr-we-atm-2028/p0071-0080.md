### p.71 — Section 4.5 "Number and value of cash withdrawals"; Figure 4.7 "Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2022"
Combo chart: bars = annual number of cash withdrawals (million, left axis 0-350); line = average cash withdrawals per ATM (thousand, right axis 0-3,000).
Table "Number of cash withdrawals":
- Annual number of cash withdrawals (million): 2018 = 273; 2019 = 277; 2020 = 211; 2021 = 182; 2022 = 192. CAGR = -9.6%.
- Average cash withdrawals per ATM (per ATM, thousand): 2018 = 31; 2019 = 31; 2020 = 23; 2021 = 20; 2022 = 22. CAGR ≈ -9.0%.
- Cash withdrawals as a share of ATM transactions: 2018 = 88%; 2019 = 87%; 2020 = 87%; 2021 = 87%; 2022 = 87%. CAGR = -0.2%.
Body text: Austria had 182 million cash withdrawals in 2022, up by 1% from 2021. Number of withdrawals had risen each year until 2019, but the COVID-19 pandemic increased usage of contactless cards for hygiene reasons, which in turn reduced demand for cash significantly in 2020. Pandemic-related lockdowns also meant that people were spending far less money at places that were usually frequented more regularly. This reduced demand for cash had continued into 2021 and has rebounded only slightly in 2022. Cashless competition for cash from contactless and mobile payments rose for low-value, cash-heavy society; deployers expect the number to decline towards the long-term average. Average withdrawal numbers vary across different institutions/sectors; the majority of cash withdrawals were monthly averages of between 1,500 and 2,100 (savings banks lower; the 3-Banken group banks higher). Both the cooperative banks of the Raiffeisen group and Volksbank Wien, and BAWAG PSK, have lower average values than the savings banks.

- Average value per ATM: EUR mn — 2018 = 5; 2019 = 5; 2020 = 4; 2021 = 4; 2022 = 5. CAGR = -3.4%. USD mn — 2018 = 6; 2019 = 6; 2020 = 5; 2021 = 5; 2022 = 5. CAGR = -6.2%.
- Cash withdrawal value as a proportion of GDP: 2018 = 11.8%; 2019 = 11.4%; 2020 = 10.4%; 2021 = 9.1%; 2022 = 8.7%. CAGR = -6.1%.
Body text: Average value of ATM cash withdrawals in Austria had been growing until 2020, when there was a substantial fall owing to COVID-19. The decline continued in 2021, followed by a limited rebound in 2022 to EUR 41 billion (USD 43 billion). This is 3% higher than 2020 but remains 11% below 2019 as more and more customers regard cashless payments as their primary payment method. The average value of cash withdrawal increased at an average rate of 8% from 2018 to 2022, and by 5% during 2022, because customers increasingly prefer to make fewer but more high-value cash withdrawals. The average value of a cash withdrawal stood at EUR 226 (USD 237) in 2022. Average withdrawal values vary between sectors and specific deployers. Members of the 3-Banken group tend to be at the upper end of the range, contrasting with the lower average withdrawal values at savings banks. The cooperative banks of the Raiffeisen group and Volksbank Wien, and BAWAG PSK, have lower average values than the savings banks.

### p.73 — Section 5 "Manufacturer market shares"; Figure 5.1 "Manufacturer market shares, 2018-2022"
Stacked bar chart (share of installed ATMs) split among Diebold Nixdorf, NCR, KEBA. Approximate 2022 stack shares from chart labels: Diebold Nixdorf ~64%, NCR ~21%, KEBA ~16% (chart segment labels show values like 64% bottom, 21% mid, 16% top for 2022; earlier years similar with Diebold Nixdorf ~64-65%).
Table "Manufacturer market shares" (units = ATMs installed base by manufacturer):
- Diebold Nixdorf: 2018 = 5,925; 2019 = 5,939; 2020 = 6,083; 2021 = 5,815; 2022 = 5,585. CAGR = -1.5%.
- NCR: 2018 = 1,503; 2019 = 1,676; 2020 = 1,562; 2021 = 1,909; 2022 = 1,921. CAGR = 6.3%.
- KEBA: 2018 = 1,345; 2019 = 1,372; 2020 = 1,424; 2021 = 1,441; 2022 = 1,427. CAGR = 1.5%.
- Total: 2018 = 8,773; 2019 = 8,987; 2020 = 9,069; 2021 = 9,165; 2022 = 8,933. CAGR = 0.5%.
Body text: Diebold Nixdorf is the largest of the three ATM vendors present in the Austrian market, with a 63% market share. Most deployers use machines from this vendor. Diebold Nixdorf makes up 97% of Raiffeisen group's, 89% of Volksbank Wien's and 58% of Bank Austria's ATMs. The number of Diebold Nixdorf machines has decreased since 2017 as several banks have replaced ATMs with models from other suppliers. The most common Diebold Nixdorf models are the Opteva 522 and 562, and the ProCash 2000. NCR had a 22% market share at end-2022; the manufacturer's share of the market increased between 2018 and 2021 owing to the rapid growth of the IAD Fiserv, but declined during 2022. Almost all of Fiserv's machines are manufactured by NCR. Bank Austria had recently installed around 1,200 ATMs, all NCR models. Euronet's ATM fleet are also supplied by NCR exclusively. NCR has a smaller presence in the bank sector: Volksbank Wien, BAWAG PSK and the 3-Banken group members all have some NCR terminals, but the Raiffeisen and savings banks have none. The most common NCR models are the SelfServ 6622 and the SelfServ 6626. KEBA has a 16% share of its domestic market. Its main customers are the savings banks, which account for 74% of its Austrian installations. The 3-Banken group banks account for a further 13%, and KEBA has also sold cash recyclers to the Raiffeisen banks and Volksbank Wien. The most widely deployed KEBA models are the KePlus Rxxe, representing 39% of the KEBA installed base, and the KePlus Xxxe, representing 32%.
