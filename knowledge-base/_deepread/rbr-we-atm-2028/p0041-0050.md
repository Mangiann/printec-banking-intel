### p.42 — Manufacturer share commentary continued (printed p.22)
Pure text page (no figures). Country-level supplier detail:
- KEBA: ~3% share of western Europe's ATMs in 2021, up from 2% in 2021 text; has just under 1,500 ATMs installed in its home country Austria, also its largest market (~1,400 ATMs). Has just over 7,000 KEBA terminals in neighbouring Germany. KEBA maintained a small presence in Switzerland until 2020; however no longer has a presence there. Exclusively supplies cash-recycling deposit ATMs.
- Glory: ~2% share of regional installed base in 2021. There are ~3,500 of its machines in Portugal, where it has a 23% market share, and ~3,500 (roughly 2,500) machines in Italy where it has a 6% share. Also has a small presence in Switzerland, France and Spain.
- Several other ATM manufacturers have a presence in western Europe collectively: Sigma, GRG, Triton, Hyosung Innovue, ARCA, Hitachi Channel Solutions, PREPIA and OKI. Together account for ~5% of the market. Virtually all of Sigma's ATMs are installed in its home market Italy; it has now begun supplying ATMs in Portugal too.
- Chinese manufacturer GRG has ~7% of the Turkish installed base and a much smaller presence in Germany, Portugal and the UK. Triton's retail-grade ATMs are concentrated in the UK, where they are bought by independent deployers (IADs).
- Hyosung Innovue is present in the UK, the largest of its market; as well as Italy, Germany, Portugal and France. ARCA has supplied a small number of ATMs to deployers in Italy and Portugal, while Hitachi Channel Solutions and OKI have a presence in Turkey, and PREPIA in Switzerland.

### p.43 — Section 3: Market forecasts (printed p.23)
Section 3 opener. Figure 3.1 "Number of ATMs, 1995-2028" — column chart (dark bars historic to 2022, light bars forecast 2023-2028). Y-axis 0-450,000. The installed base rises from ~167,000 in 1995, climbs steadily through the 2000s, peaks at ~400,000 around 2018-2020 (highest bar ~405,000), then declines: ~395k (2021), ~390k (2022), then falls each forecast year to ~316,000 by 2028 (the light forecast bars step down from ~345k in 2023 to ~316k in 2028).

Body text: number of ATMs in western Europe projected to decline at an average rate of ~2% each year over the forecast period, ending with a total of ~316,000 ATMs by 2028. All primary markets except Austria are expected to see a decline of their installed bases. Austria's base is forecast to remain broadly flat. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated this trend. Ongoing switch to cashless payment methods in recent years, and the rise in demand for ATM services over the forecast period, is resulting in region-wide ATM-estate rationalisation. The UK will see one of the most significant numbers of terminals removed between 2022 and 2028, with the installed base falling by 8,740 over the course of the period. By the end of the forecast period, the UK installed base is expected to fall by ~92 terminals between 2022 and 2028. France, the second-largest market, is forecast to remove a significant number of terminals too. Spain, supplying 40% of the region's ATMs in 2022, will see the region's ATM pool decline at a slower rate. France retail-grade ATMs are now begun supplying ATMs in Portugal. The next-largest supplier of ATMs in western Europe is in the Spanish market, where just over 12,000 of its machines make up ~27% of Spain's installed base. In 2022, its share of the western European market as a whole was up from 4% in 2018.

### p.45 — Section 3 commentary + Figure 3.3 ATM shipments (printed p.25)
Body text: Several primary markets will also see significant declines of their installed bases, including Italy, Belgium and Turkey, collectively removing over 5,000 between end-2022 and end-2028. Banks in those markets widely promote various digitisation initiatives; additionally, as they are well served by ATM services and customers' changing payment preferences, ATM deployment has become less profitable than it was in the past. Turkey is expected to remain the region's largest market by end-2028 — despite ongoing ATM-pooling projects and sharing agreements — with ~16% of all ATMs in western Europe (i.e. ~50,700 in 2028). Belgium, the Belgian ATM-pooling project will be the main driver behind the decline in the installed base, as the initiative seeks to rationalise and combine the ATM estates of multiple deployers, removing the surplus of ATMs collectively over the forecast period.

Figure 3.3 "Forecast ATM shipments, 2023-2028" — column chart, y-axis 0-35,000. Bars roughly flat around 27,000-28,000 units per year across the forecast period (matching the Fig 3.4 totals: ~27,658 in 2023 down to ~26,826 in 2028).

Body text: around 167,000 terminals will be shipped to western Europe over the forecast period, which equates to an average of ~27,800 ATMs per year. Spain and Germany will receive the most shipments, representing 19% and 16% of the regional total respectively over the forecast period. Other markets with significant shares of shipments sent to western Europe will be France (15%), Italy (14%) and Turkey (11%).

### p.48 — Figure 3.7: Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2028 (printed p.28)

Body text: despite the recovery seen in both 2021 and 2022, the annual number of cash withdrawals in western Europe is projected to decline gradually each year from 2023 onwards, with a projected 9.4 billion cash withdrawals in 2028. As consumers in the region use payment cards and other cashless payments more often, demand for cash from ATMs will fall.

### p.50 — Cash-withdrawal density commentary + Figure 3.9: Value of cash withdrawals (printed p.30)
Body text: Germany is projected to see an average of 3,563 monthly withdrawals per ATM in 2028, the highest density of any primary market. In 2022 there were 3,336 monthly withdrawals per ATM in the country on average. This figure is set to rise despite a fall in the number of cash withdrawals, as the German installed base of ATMs contracts at a faster rate. Norway and Denmark will continue to have the lowest number of cash withdrawals per ATM per month in 2028, with 905 and 1,071 withdrawals on average, respectively.
