### p.660 — Switzerland, Section 7 Market forecasts: forecast installed base 2023-2028 (Fig 7.1)
Section 7 opens; 7.1 Forecast installed base. Figure 7.1: Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028 (Source: RBR analysis).
- Table — ATMs: 2023 6,200; 2024 6,000; 2025 5,800; 2026 5,600; 2027 5,400; 2028 5,300 (CAGR -3.1%).
- Absolute growth: -280 / -200 / -200 / -200 / -200 / -100 (N/A).
- Percentage growth: -4.3% / -3.2% / -3.3% / -3.4% / -3.6% / -1.9% (N/A).
- ATMs per million people: 709 / 686 / 664 / 641 / 618 / 606 (CAGR -3.1%).
- Text: Swiss installed base forecast to contract by 18% between 2022 and 2028, reaching 5,300 in 2028 — implying a fall in ATM density from 741 machines per million people in 2022 to 606 in 2028 (based on 2022 population data). Density of ATMs relative to bank branches projected to fall from over 250 ATMs per 100 branches to below 240.
- Main FLM/decline driver: declining use of cash as customers increasingly rely on cashless methods (contactless cards, mobile wallets) for small, everyday payments. Falling usage levels make running a network of ATMs uneconomical; particular as fixed costs (e.g. site rental, security, insurance) stay the same or even rise. Inflation in Switzerland did not reach levels seen in many European countries in 2022, but still rose sharply.
- ATMfutura pooling project likely to further limit ATM growth: enables banks to offer a range of ATM functions on a "not-on-us" basis; allows mobile banks to remove ATMs that are in close proximity to others; a longer-term prospect is a pooling arrangement (formalising a joint venture by groups of banks), likely reducing ATM numbers.
- Non-bank IAD sector relatively new in Switzerland but expected to grow; proportion of ATMs deployed by non-banks forecast to roughly **double by 2028, to 7% of the total**. ATMs in Switzerland will continue to be primarily deployed by banks; non-bank deployers expand their business and banks continue to cut numbers of ATMs deployed away from branches.
- Commercial banks tend to reduce/right-size networks on commercial grounds; cantonal banks are more constrained (obligations to maintain a level of access to cash in non-urban areas), so cantonal banks therefore less likely to make significant cuts to their ATM networks over the next two years. The co-operative sector that comprises the Raiffeisen banks expects to cut nearly one quarter of its ATMs between 2023 and 2028. UBS–Credit Suisse merger announced early 2023 likely to lead to removal of significant numbers of machines.
