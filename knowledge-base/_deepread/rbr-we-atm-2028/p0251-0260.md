### p.255 — Germany banking structure (continued, dense text)
There are around **370 savings banks** represented by the **Deutsche Sparkassen- und Giroverband (DSGV)**; savings banks constitute the largest of the three retail banking sectors, with ~**7,800 branches** as of mid-2022. Co-operative banks have largely consolidated, scaling back branch and ATM estates. Commercial banking dominated by **Deutsche Bank** (also bought Postbank), **Commerzbank** and **HVB** (UniCredit subsidiary). Discusses cooperation among the savings/co-operative sectors. Mentions the cooperative banking sector with ~4,760 branches; group of large commercial banks ("GroßBanken"). The largest commercial banks outside the "big five" are Targobank and Santander. Deutsche Bank closed a large proportion of its retail branches in Germany in recent years; there were ~400 at end-2022. Group has ~700 retail branches under Postbank brand and ~400 under Deutsche Bank brand. Direct banks ING-DiBa, DKB and Comdirect operate online with few/no branches.

### p.258 — Germany ATM sharing / IAD history (dense text)
During the mid-1990s a number of banks, including BfG (now Santander Bank) and internet banks, began aggressively competing for sight and savings deposits, including offering accounts that did not require the account holder to pay any fees. These banks did not operate a significant number of branches or ATMs but as they were party to sharing agreements, their customers had access to physical banks' infrastructure. It was in light of this trend that the DSGV cancelled its connection to the H&V in 1997. Following pressure from the Federal Cartel Office (BKA) and consumer lobbies, the banking industry finally reached an agreement within the framework of the DK, and in 2011 direct surcharging was introduced to replace the regime of interchange and disloyalty fees. Having rejected the EUR 5 (USD 5.66) surcharge cap proposed by the DK, the Federal Cartel Office continues to monitor surcharge levels. ATM fees range from EUR 2 to EUR 6 (USD 2.20 to USD 6.60). The BKA indicates that at ATMs in its sector, the average fee has decreased from EUR 4 (about USD 8.40 in 2011) to EUR 4.20/EUR 4.25 (this new regime was introduced). Notes that withdrawals at the market's ATM networks, and banking sector has an organisation registered with the DK which fulfils the function of a central switch (Kopfstelle). Bulleted list of central-switch / network functions: Bank-Verlag (commercial banks); Finanz Informatik (FI, for savings banks); VR-Payment (for co-operative banks); Pago/Pagoday (subsidiary of Deutsche Bank, formerly VÖB-ZVD, for public banks); Fiserv (the only-sector independent central switch). Most banks use their sector's designated central switch (Kopfstelle), but a few hire processing of cross-sector mergers and acquisitions: cross-sector processing has driven down switching/processing costs and overheads of small special projects. FI (Finanz Informatik) headquartered in Frankfurt am Main, processes ATM transactions on behalf of all savings banks; also owned by FI subsidiary. It is owned by Sparkassen-Finanzgruppe (SFG). VR-Payment owned by the DZ Bank in Frankfurt. The co-operative banks' ATMs may be managed as a national data centre, Sopra Financial Technology (formerly Sparda EH). Since 2018, four Sparda banks have migrated to Atruvia, with the remaining seven expected to follow during 2023. Germany has six non-bank independent ATM deployers (IADs): Euronet, NCR, InterCard, NoteMachine, Reisebank and moneyserv. IADs must be sponsored by a bank in order to access ATMs, although their card. Euronet and Reisebank have escaped this constraint by obtaining banking licences themselves.

### p.259 — Germany IADs (filler / two paragraphs)
- **Euronet** is Germany's oldest and largest IAD, active since **1997**. In **January 2021 NCR** (US ATM manufacturer, second largest supplier of ATM hardware in Germany) acquired the **Cardtronics** corporation, including its estate of **over 1,300 ATMs** in Germany. Cardtronics had itself established significant German market share via acquisition of **Payzone's ATM business in 2013**. NCR continues to use Cardtronics' **Cardpoint** brand at its German ATMs.
- **Reisebank** ("travel bank") has most of its ATMs in airports, railway stations and other transport hubs; also operates bureaux de change and precious-metal deposit services; active in Germany since the late 1990s; despite its name, not a retail bank.
- **moneyserv**, a subsidiary of Cash & Call, is Germany's newest IAD, having built an estate of around **100 ATMs** since **2020**.
