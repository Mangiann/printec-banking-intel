Body: ATMs installed in western Europe = 12% of global total at end-2022, a figure that has remained broadly similar over several years. Region remains third largest after Asia-Pacific and North America, and is set to remain in third position over the next years, although its proportion of the total is forecast to fall to 11% by 2028. At end-2022 the three largest markets in western Europe were Turkey, Germany and the UK, which together account for 44% of the region's ATMs. The share of the six largest markets, which includes France, Spain and Italy, was 87% — the same as in 2021. Most countries saw their share of the regional total remain ~unchanged in 2022, but there were some exceptions; the UK fell from 15% to 14%, while Turkey became the largest country in western Europe (at ATM installed base, with its share rising from 14% to 15%). Turkey overtook UK and Germany to become western Europe's largest ATM market in 2022, despite the fact that its installed base contracted slightly. Growth in the Turkish ATM installed base has stalled since 2019, with the number of ATMs in the country falling gradually in each of the last three years. This fall in ATM numbers in Turkey has been driven by branch closures, as well as the introduction of various ATM pooling and sharing agreements. In 2021, Isbank, Akbank and ING Bank entered into a sharing agreement, leading many terminals to be removed in areas that several ATMs were deployed in close proximity to each other. A formal ATM pooling project began between the Turkish state-owned deployers — Ziraat Bank, VakifBank, Halkbank, Vakif Katilim, Ziraat Katilim, Emlak Bank, and PTT — was also announced in 2021, and began to pilot scheme in 2022. At the end of 2021 there were roughly 1,500 terminals operating under this project, which is known as Turkey's ATM Centre Project (TAM).

### p.23 — Germany / UK / largest markets; Figure 1.3: Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022
Germany remains the second largest ATM market in the region, with 51,260 ATMs at end-2022. This market has seen falling ATM numbers every year since 2018, contracted by 5% in 2022. Long-term rationalisation of ATM networks by banks. Germany, and the pandemic, has halted growth in the IAD sector — which had been expanding. There has also been an increase in the number of off-site ATMs, making off-site ATMs a less attractive proposition for deployers.
Since 2019, the UK market has contracted each year. During 2022 the number of ATMs in the UK fell by 6%. This decline was driven by the closure of bank branches and consequent removal of branch-based ATMs, as well as the removal of less profitable IAD machines. The two largest IADs in the UK market — Cashzone and NoteMachine — each withdrew more than 700 ATMs in 2022. The UK has the third largest installed base of all western European markets, having had the largest at the end of 2021.

### p.24 — Italy / Portugal / Austria / Netherlands & Greece (body text only)
Italy is the region's sixth largest ATM market, accounting for 12% of its ATMs in 2022. The market has been declining in size for nearly a decade, and contracted by 3% during 2022; this decline was largely a result of continuing branch rationalisation. No other western European market has more than 15,000 ATMs. Portugal, the region's seventh largest market, had 13,862 at end-2022, while Austria had 8,933. The only two countries in which the number of ATMs grew in 2022 were the Netherlands and Greece, at 4% and 1% respectively.

### p.26 — IADs by deployer type; Figure 1.5: ATMs by deployer type, 2021 and 2022
Body: Majority of European markets saw an increase in the number of IAD ATMs during 2022, as was also the case in 2021. As COVID-19 restrictions were entirely removed in many countries, increased footfall encouraged IADs to expand their fleets. In countries such as Portugal and Spain, IAD growth is organic, while in Ireland and particularly France, existing ATMs are being transferred from bank to IAD operation. In Finland, nearly all ATMs are deployed by IADs.
Markets with the lowest shares of IAD ATMs are Turkey (none), Switzerland (where they make up 3% of the total), Italy (3%) and Belgium (4%). In 2022, the proportion of French ATMs deployed by IADs increased from 5% to 19%, as Brink's completed its acquisition of all ATMs which previously belonged to BPCE Group.
Just under half of all IAD ATMs in western Europe are found in the UK; down from 55% in 2021, after Cashzone and NoteMachine each removed more than 700 each in 2022. IADs continue to deploy more than half of all terminals found in the UK. They benefit from efficient operating models, such as merchant replenishment and use of lower-cost "retail-grade" ATM models. However, the number of IAD terminals in the UK has fallen in each year since 2019, with around 2,000 withdrawn during 2022.

### p.27 — Germany / Ireland / Finland IAD detail (body text only)
Germany had 5,290 IAD ATMs at end-2022, or 8% of all IAD ATMs in western Europe. The first IAD to begin operating in Germany was Euronet in 1997, and there are currently five. In descending order of size, they are Euronet, InterCard, NoteMachine, Reisebank and moneyserv. In Germany IADs must be sponsored by a bank, although Euronet, InterCard and Reisebank now have banking licences in their own right.
The fifth largest IAD market is Ireland, with 2,459 terminals deployed by IADs. The Irish IAD sector has seen strong growth in recent years, and Euronet is the country's largest ATM deployer. Brink's is the next largest independent ATM deployer, with 460 terminals in its fleet. It entered the market in 2020, having acquired the off-site ATMs of AIB.
In Finland, all ATMs had been deployed by non-banks since 2020. However, IAD Re:Cash lost its deal with Forex Bank in 2022. As a result, Forex Bank now deploys its seven multi-currency ATMs directly, under an ATM-as-a-service agreement with Diebold Nixdorf. There are now just two IADs operating in Finland: Automatia and Nokas. Automatia is the largest deployer in the country and was previously owned by a group of banks, but was sold to Loomis in December 2021. Nokas, the second largest, has made agreements with two Finnish retail groups to deploy ATMs at their outlets, including supermarkets and petrol stations.

### p.29 — Density measures detail (body text only)
There is significant variation between markets in terms of ATM density to population. ATM density in western Europe is not necessarily related to the maturity of a market. While Portugal is a relatively young market, for example, it has the highest density of ATMs to inhabitants, at 1,350 ATMs per million people. Norway and Sweden are two of the region's longest-established markets, but have the lowest densities of ATMs to population, with 215 and 218 ATMs per million people at the end of 2022, respectively.
Western Europe had 121,420 bank branches at the end of 2022, a decrease of 5% compared to 2021. The long-term decline in branch numbers is due to lower customer footfall and the desire by banks to rationalise their physical distribution networks, as well as some sector consolidation. On average, there were 291 ATMs per 100 bank branches at the end of 2022. This is up slightly from 289 at the end of 2021, with banks closing their branches at a slightly faster rate than deployers removed ATMs from their fleets.
The Netherlands has the highest density of ATMs to bank branches, with 882 ATMs per 100 branches at end-2022. The next highest densities are in Ireland (681 ATMs per 100 branches) and the UK (633). These countries have all seen large-scale branch closures in recent years, with a significant proportion of ATMs in each of them located off-site. Additionally, over half of all terminals in both Ireland and the UK belong to IADs. While the IAD sector in the Netherlands is comparatively small, Geldmaat — an ATM pooling project involving ING, Rabobank and ABN AMRO — deploys the vast majority of its ATMs away from bank branches, having expanded its fleet in 2022 by opening ATM "stores" with several terminals at each location. Turkey, Greece and Portugal also have more than 400 ATMs per 100 branches.
Belgium has the lowest density of ATMs by this measure, with 148 terminals per 100 branches. The Belgian installed base was contracted by 9% during 2022, as the country's banks drastically reduced the size of their fleets. Some machines were taken over by an ATM pooling initiative, Batopin, while others were removed entirely. Italy, France and Finland also have low ATM densities relative to branches. Along with Germany and Spain, these countries have atypically large branch networks compared to other countries in the region.
