### p.783 — UK §7 Market forecasts → 7.1 Forecast installed base
Footer: UK: Page 38. Section 7 begins here.
**Figure 7.1 — Forecast ATM installed base, 2023-2028** (table; columns 2023 / 2024 / 2025 / 2026 / 2027 / 2028 + CAGR):
- ATMs: 49,200 / 47,600 / 46,100 / 44,700 / 43,400 / 42,300; CAGR -3.0%
- Absolute growth: -1,836 / -1,600 / -1,500 / -1,400 / -1,300 / -1,100; CAGR N/A
- Percentage growth: -3.6% / -3.3% / -3.2% / -3.0% / -2.9% / -2.5%; CAGR N/A
- ATMs per million people: 729 / 705 / 683 / 662 / 643 / 627; CAGR -3.0%
Source: RBR analysis.
Body: The installed base will keep declining (to 42,300 by 2028, CAGR -3.0%) driven by branch rationalisation and ATM pooling. IADs continue to seek profitable sites (branch-led and IAD-led); bank-branch ATMs become more sophisticated as they replace tellers for routine cash transactions. Cardless/contactless usage grows. IADs absorb some bank ATM-rationalisation programmes as cash demand declines in favour of mobile/online payments; cash withdrawals remain a meaningful share. References to Access to Cash / banking-hub initiatives and LINK's banking-hub expansion (March 2023).

### p.784 — UK §7.2 Forecast ATM shipments
Footer: UK: Page 39.
**Figure 7.2 — Number of ATMs, 1995-2028** (bar chart; y-axis 0-75,000 in 15,000 steps). Dark-blue historical bars rise from ~10,000 (1995), pass 45,000 around 2002-2003, peak ~72,000 around 2017-2018, then decline; light-blue forecast bars 2023-2028 fall from ~49,000 to ~42,000 (matches Fig 7.1).
Body: The share of ATMs sited away from a bank branch keeps rising as branch rationalisation rolls out, driving more off-site deployment by both banks and IADs at locations where the branch has closed. Branch ATMs become more sophisticated (replacing tellers for routine/most cash transactions) but fewer in number; NFC and QR-code readers likely more widely installed by end of forecast period as deployers migrate customers to self-service for routine transactions (cash deposits, some bill payments).
**Figure 7.3 — Forecast ATM shipments, 2023-2028** (table; 2023-2028 + CAGR):
- Shipments: 3,500 / 3,460 / 3,060 / 2,420 / 1,670 / 1,060; CAGR -21.3%
- of which new installations: 25 / 25 / 20 / 20 / 20 / 20; CAGR -4.4%
- of which replacements: 3,475 / 3,435 / 3,040 / 2,400 / 1,650 / 1,040; CAGR -21.4%
- Replacements as % of shipments: 99% / 99% / 99% / 99% / 99% / 98%; CAGR -0.2%
Source: RBR analysis.
Body: ~15,200 ATMs forecast to be shipped to the UK 2023-2028, avg ~2,530/yr, almost all replacements of ATMs withdrawn due to age or damage; new installations remain a negligible proportion. UK IADs typically buy large numbers of second-hand ATMs from banks and keep them until no longer compliant with security/software standards, reducing the need for new installations.

### p.785 — UK §7.3 Forecast ATM cash withdrawal number and value
Footer: UK: Page 40.
**Figure 7.4 — Number of cash withdrawals, 2018-2028** (combo; both axes 0-3,500 in steps of 700). Bars = annual number of withdrawals; line = average withdrawals per ATM (right axis). Bars: ~2,400 (2018) and ~2,330 (2019, dark blue), drop to ~1,300 from 2020 and stay roughly flat ~1,300 across the forecast (light blue 2023-2028). The per-ATM line: ~3,050 (2018), ~3,100 (2019), falls sharply to ~1,900 (2020-2021), then recovers to ~2,400 by 2028.
Body: Average cash withdrawals per ATM forecast to rise gradually to ~2,400 because branch rationalisation cuts the ATM base faster than withdrawals fall. ATMs in high-footfall and travel markets keep recovering; branch ATMs become less used as customers shift to mobile/online banking.
**Figure 7.5 — Value of cash withdrawals, 2018-2028** (combo; left axis 0-200, right axis 0-120). Bars = Annual value GBP bn (left); line = Average value GBP (right). Bars: ~180 (2018), ~170 (2019, dark blue); ~115 (2020), ~110 (2021), ~118 (2022); forecast 2023-2028 (light blue) roughly flat ~128-132 GBP bn. Average-value line (right axis): ~75 (2018), ~72 (2019) rising steadily to ~107 by 2028.
Source: UK ATM deployers and RBR analysis.
Body: Average withdrawal value rises gradually 2022-2028 on strong early-period inflation and the move away from cash for the smallest transactions. Total value withdrawn stays relatively stable with a very gradual inflation-driven increase; overall cash demand weaker due to alternative payments.
