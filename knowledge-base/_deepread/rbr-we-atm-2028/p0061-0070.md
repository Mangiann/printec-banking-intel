Body text: Bankomat is the shared ATM network in Austria, run by PSA; the Sparkassen (savings banks) network is Erste Digital, Raiffeisen Wien's ARZ network, and the Bankomat network which encompasses deployers from various sectors. Raiffeisen Group's network was set up in 1994 to connect the group members' ATMs; it is the largest network. Raiffeisen's network grew. ARZ network is named for the Raiffeisen Wien data centre (Allgemeines Rechenzentrum). 3BEG is the smallest, connecting 3-Banken-EDV-Gesellschaft (Oberbank, BKS, BTV) lobby ATMs. GABE is the former name; Bankomat established 1980 after Austria's banks set up a national network. Bankomat network has been declining; e.g. ~1,500 ATMs in 2018, shrank to ~1,500 (text references 2021/2022 changes).

### p.62 — Austria: networks/deployers narrative (continued)
- 3BEG: IT services provider founded 1991 by the 3-Banken group members Oberbank, BKS Bank and BTV; connects the three banks' lobby ATMs. Many ATMs previously connected to Bankomat were switched to the 3BEG IT platform.
- Euronet (US IAD): entered the Austrian market in 2013; ATM estate grew from **200 in 2017 to 480 at end of 2022**.
- PayLife Bank: became an ATM deployer in 2009 after acquiring **48 off-site machines from Bank Austria**. Europay Austria rebranded as PayLife Bank in 2007 (multi-brand acquirer); purchased a Visa issuing and acquiring licence. In 2012 it spun off its debit-card issuing and ATM acquiring business into **PSA**, which operates under the Bankomatkarte brand. PayLife handles "not-on-us" transactions for all deployers.
- 2011: some Raiffeisen banks introduced disloyalty fees for "not-on-us" cash withdrawals; prior attempts faced opposition. Largest IAD Fiserv (formerly First Data) has refrained from surcharging; **Euronet started charging EUR 1.95 (USD 2.22) per cash withdrawal in 2016**.
- Concentration: share of the five largest ATM deployers was **62% at end-2022** — Raiffeisen group largest at **32%**, then Fiserv **14%**, Volksbank Wien **7%**.

- Text: ATMs fell significantly in 2022, by 3%, the strongest decline ever recorded in Austria. Rationalisation measures by banks led to the decline. Most notably, BAWAG PSK's subsidiary Easybank terminated its cooperation with Shell petrol stations and subsequently withdrew all its 129 ATMs by the end of 2022; the bank advises its customers to use BAWAG PSK's ATMs instead. ### p.66 — Austria: 4.3 Locations
- Text: At end-2022, Austria had the second highest density of ATMs per inhabitant in western Europe, with **999 ATMs per million people**, well above the regional average of 691. The density declined during 2022 (per 100 outlets). Branch numbers continued to fall in 2022, while ATM numbers declined for the first time since 2016.
- Off-site ATMs: density of ATMs to outlets for financial-services distribution is also higher than the regional average.
- Many BAWAG PSK banking outlets were co-located with post offices prior to the termination of the joint venture, reflecting PSK's origins as the postal savings bank. Many of these outlets are now used by bank99. Self-service postal outlets and in-store post services counters are also common in Austria.

- Text continues: Austria has one of the highest proportions of lobby ATMs in western Europe, with over half of all ATMs installed in such locations. The Raiffeisen group is the largest lobby ATM deployer, with 72% of its ATMs installed in lobbies. The proportion of off-site ATMs has grown an average annual growth rate of 6% between 2018 and 2022; the share of off-site ATMs has grown due the removal of ATMs installed in branches that have been closed permanently. Some banks deploy ATMs in "self-service branches" but these are counted as off-site locations.

### p.67 — Austria: 4.3 Locations narrative (continued)
- Off-site share declined by one percentage point, to **30% in 2022**, largely due to slower growth of the IADs Fiserv and Euronet, plus the withdrawal of online bank Easybank (which had deployed most of its terminals at Shell petrol stations).
- Off-site ATM proportion in Austria remains low by western European standards. Together, the co-operative and savings banks account for **59% of the country's ATMs**, but only have **24% of off-site ATMs**. **Fiserv operates 44% of Austria's non-branch ATMs**, mostly in retail outlets.
- TTW ATMs (installed through the exterior wall of the bank branch) have declined as a share of the total since 2018, due to branch closures and the sector's preference for ATMs in secure 24/7 lobbies.
- A few ATMs are located in bank halls and drive-through locations, operated by various co-operative, savings and commercial banks.

### p.69 — Austria: Figure 4.5 ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022 + narrative
**Figure 4.5** (ATMs / SATs):
- Automated note deposit: 2,287 ATMs / 275 SATs.
- of which: note recycling: 2,278 / N/A.
- Automated cheque deposit: 0 / 0.
- **Total: 2,287 ATMs / 275 SATs.**
- Source: Austrian ATM deployers and data centres, and RBR analysis.
Narrative:
- Compared to many western European countries, ATMs in Austria have a relatively limited range of facilities. Similar to neighbouring Germany, many banks prefer to install separate single-function machines, e.g. for passbook updates, statement printing, coin deposits and prepaid card top-ups, rather than combining all functions into a single machine. From the banks' perspective, the benefits of shorter queues at ATMs offset the disadvantages of procuring and maintaining extra hardware and space.
- 26% of ATMs — mainly deployed by savings banks — allow customers to make credit transfers to another account at the same bank.
- Almost all ATMs display account balance on-screen, while statements are printed at some Raiffeisen and Volksbank Wien ATMs. 73% of ATMs offer mobile phone credit top-ups.
- Dynamic currency conversion (DCC) is available at ATMs belonging to Raiffeisen Group, Fiserv, Bank Austria and several savings banks.
- The ATMs deployed by BAWAG PSK support cardless cash withdrawals via the SmartCash method, which involves the entering of a 10-digit one-time code (OTC) at the ATM for withdrawals of up to EUR 400 (USD 470). The OTC is delivered to the customer either over the counter or via a banking app.
- 26% of ATMs accept automated banknote deposit, with the overwhelming majority of them recycling the deposited notes. The Raiffeisen group, the savings banks and BAWAG PSK own particularly high numbers of cash recyclers. Relatively few banks own stand-alone deposit terminals (SATs), which accept banknote deposits without dispensing cash.
- Bill payment facilities are not available at Austrian ATMs. Instead, bill payments facilities tend to be offered at separate, dedicated terminals.
