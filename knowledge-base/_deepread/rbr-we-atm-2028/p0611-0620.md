### p.611 — Sweden, Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (Sweden Page 3)
**3.1 ATM networks and sharing.** Since early 1970s Swedish bankers' association, state-owned PK Banken, and Federation of Swedish Co-operative Banks formed a consortium to manage the Bankomat network (not confused with the pooling arrangement of the same name in earlier years, in use from 2010). In 1996 an agreement reached to allow cardholders reciprocal access to the ATMs of each network. In 2010 the formation of Bankomat, through which the majority of ATMs are shared via an ATM pooling arrangement, changed the landscape of the Swedish ATM market. The five largest banks in Sweden — Swedbank, Danske Bank, Handelsbanken, Nordea, Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken (SEB) — only terminals are not deployed by Bankomat are those deployed by ICA Banken, Forex Bank and IAOs. Banks have also been sharing ATMs since 2013; previously the savings banks operated a shared network, called Minuten, which first introduced ATMs to the regional market. In 2013, Bankomat's founders completed the migration of their ATMs to the Bankomat company. In 2014, LF Bank and Sparbanken Öresund sold their ATMs to Bankomat. At end-2022 there were three IAOs operating in the market: Nokas, Re:Cash and Euronet; Bankomat ATM withdrew from the market during 2021.

**3.2 ATM-as-a-Service.** At end-May 2022 Forex Bank (which specialises in currency exchange services in the Nordic countries) entered a management agreement with Diebold Nixdorf to manage its entire fleet in Sweden, Denmark, Finland and Norway. The management agreement, first of its kind in the region, includes hardware and software lifecycle management, security and compliance, monitoring and integrated service desk, installation, maintenance and transaction processing.

### p.615 — Sweden, 4.4 Functionality and hardware features (Sweden Page 7)
Text: Partly driven by access to cash legislation, remote ATM deployment grew between 2019 and 2020, in line with closures of bank branches and the expansion of the IAO sector and the most common location type for Swedish ATMs at the end of 2022 was off-site, accounting for 76% of the installed base. ICA Banken closed its only branch in 2019; the bank operates entirely through retail outlets therefore it deploys ATMs exclusively off-site. At end-2022 the most common location for branch-based ATMs was through the branch wall (TTW). ICA Banken operates solely through its retail outlets and therefore deploys ATMs exclusively off-site. The bank removed 24 off-site machines and planning to transfer its entire ATM network to Bankomat by 2027.

Text: Until the end of 2018, Bankomat operated 200 SATs, however in 2019 the deposit function was enabled on these terminals, converting then into recycling ATMs there 397 recyclers operating and there are no stand-alone deposit terminals (SATs) in the market at present at end 2022. In general, Swedish ATMs tend to offer a broad range of functionalities. At the end of 2022, the most common automated capability was chip-currency conversion, offered at 98% of ATMs. Foreign currency conversion and PIN change are available at 72% of ATMs respectively. Foreign exchange rate information is less common, possible at around a quarter of ATMs, while multi-currency dispensing is only offered at 9% of terminals. In terms of security features, the overwhelming majority of Swedish ATMs are equipped with anti-skimming devices and have note degradation facilities. 98% and 68% respectively. On the other hand, just 4% of terminals have a built-in security camera. At present, no ATMs in the market have QR code readers. While 82% of terminals have speech capability, just 17% of ATMs have touch screens. At end-2022, there were no terminals offering assisted self-service transactions.

Text: As cash usage continues to decline and the ongoing rationalisation of bank branches, the total number of ATMs is forecast to gradually reduce between 2022 and 2028, at an annual average of 2%, to 2,025. With branch numbers and ATM numbers both falling, the number of ATMs per 100 branches is also anticipated to decrease to 207 by 2028. The density of ATMs relative to population is expected to fall to 192 ATMs per million people (based on 2022 population numbers). The formation of Bankomat in 2010 has been a key factor contributing to the rationalisation, as banks have collaborated to find the most cost-efficient locations for ATM deployment, often removing the less profitable terminals. Although the Swedish government requires large financial institution to provide minimum level of access to cash, Swedish deployers are expected to continue rationalising their fleet which will lead to decline in the total number of ATMs. In addition, as digital wallet payments and internet banking are becoming increasingly common, banks will continue to remove underused terminals; functions such as note recycling, cardless withdrawal and automated note deposit are forecast to become more common as banks aim to maximise the value of their fleet for customers and automate more transactions when possible.
