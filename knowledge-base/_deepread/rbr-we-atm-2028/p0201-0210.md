Body text: France's first ATM was installed in 1968. Following the introduction of the ATM replacement, a complex pattern emerged of cooperation and competition between proprietary fleets and sharing agreement. In 1971, the Carte Bleue system was formed to provide a shared network of ATMs operated by the banks which issued the Carte Bleue card. These included Société Générale, Banque National de Paris (BNP Paribas), Crédit Commercial de France (now HSBC), Crédit du Nord, Crédit Lyonnais (now LCL), CIC, the Banques Populaires, and the Caisses d'Epargne. La Poste set up its own fleet of cash dispensers in 1973 at major post offices throughout France. In recent years, almost all members of the market is predominantly a result of the transfer of ATMs into the CB network. Crédit Agricole's Carte Verte network was the main competitor to the Carte Bleue network. The bank began installing ATMs in 1971 and for several years had the world's largest proprietary system. In 1984, the banks of both the Carte Bleue and Carte Verte networks, as well as a number of other financial institutions, signed a protocol to establish an interbank payment system and a national ATM network. The protocol defined the framework for the establishment of Groupement des Cartes Bancaires (CB) which was set up in 1984 to introduce a single, standard payment card for both cash withdrawals and payments. The system additionally works towards the establishment of common equipment standards and procedures. This process led to the creation of a national shared ATM network, which was ultimately established at the beginning of 1986. CB is responsible for co-ordinating the network and controls the rules for membership. ATM deployers retain control over their ATM installations and distribution strategies. "Paid-on-us" cash withdrawals are switched by CB, while deployers process "on-us" transactions themselves. 12 large French banks and payment service providers (known as Chefs de File or Membres Principaux) act as representatives of smaller deployers (Sous-Participants) at meetings of the CB membership, and they assume responsibility for the exchange of "not-on-us" transactions for those smaller deployers. These 12 organisations fund the interbank payment system, but they also charge smaller deployers for the services that they provide. Non-bank deployers (IADs) need to have a banking license and the sponsorship of a bank to join the CB network in France. T-mobile was the first IAD to enter the French market, operating ATMs outside of the CB network through its acquisition of Loomis France in 2014 (and ChangeGroup in 2015). Travelex left the French market in 2021, while the other two maintain a very small presence in France; Loomis entered the market as an IAD in 2014 with 100 ATMs deployed in a country where there are 48 per 24/7 access to cash.

**Section 3.2: ATM Pooling** — Three French banks — BNP Paribas, Crédit Mutuel and Société Générale announced plans in September 2021 to pool their ATMs. Studied over 2022, the process of implementation for the pooling project began at the start of 2023 and is expected to be completed by the end of 2025. As a result of the arrangement, customers of each bank will no longer need to pay a charge for using ATMs of the other banks. The banks expect to launch CASH SERVICES at the end of 2023; a single entity under which their ATMs will be pooled. The pooling project is to be operated by joint venture company 2SF (Société des Services Fiduciaires) which was formed for the purpose of the project.
**Section 3.3: ATM use** — In July 2019, BPCE Group and Brink's France formed a partnership, through which it was agreed that Brink's would operate and manage the ATMs of the BPCE Group, including the Banques Populaires and Caisses d'Epargne. By end-2022, the fleets of both banks had been transferred to Brink's, with the banks' branding maintained. As well as optimising costs for the group's banks, the outsourcing partnership aims to provide a broader range of services to customers through the ATMs and to harmonise the management of both ATM networks.
