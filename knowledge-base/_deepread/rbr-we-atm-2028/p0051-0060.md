Narrative (text on page): In 2028 the total annual value of cash withdrawals in Western Europe is set to stand at USD 2.0 trillion, up 31% from 2022. Around half of markets expected to see an increase in this measure over 2028 vs 2022, with Turkey and Italy seeing particularly high annual average growth (22% and 5% respectively). In Turkey the growing banked population sustains demand for cash withdrawals, driving the rise in their value (extremely high inflation rates being the main driver of cash-withdrawal value in Turkey over the forecast period). As cash withdrawals become less frequent and customers lean toward higher-value withdrawals, the average value of a cash withdrawal is projected to increase in all markets; regional average set to stand at USD 213 in 2028, up from USD 155 in 2022.

Narrative highlights: There were 8,933 ATMs installed in Austria at end-2022, making Austria the eighth-largest market in Western Europe. Unlike previous years, the market contracted, with ATM numbers having decreased by around 3% in 2022. More than half of the installed base (53%) located in bank branches, with a core of the highest shares in Western Europe. Off-site ATMs constitute 30% of the market. 73% of ATMs offer mobile phone top-ups, 44% offer PIN change, and 26% offer automated note deposits. 182 million ATM cash withdrawals were made in Austria in 2022, with combined value of EUR 41 billion (USD 43 billion). Cash withdrawals comprised 87% of ATM transactions in 2022. Diebold Nixdorf held 63% share of the ATM installed base at end of 2022; NCR was the second-largest manufacturer by market share, with 22%; this was followed by KEBA, which had a share of 16%. Nearly half of Austrian ATMs run from IBM servers, while KIX Terminal Control by SBS is the most widespread terminal driver. KXCustomer is the most commonly used application software. The most commonly used monitoring, remote distribution and ATM security software are also SBS products. Cash replenishment outsourced for 58% of ATMs in Austria. First-line maintenance mostly takes place in-house, while second-line maintenance carried out by external partners in most cases. It is forecast that the number of ATMs in Austria will rise to 9,025 by the end of 2028. Growth likely to stem from the IAD sector only, while the number of branch ATMs is expected to decline. ATM deployments expected to be mostly replacements for existing machines.

### p.60 — Austria: Section 2 Banking structure (continued, narrative only)
Bank Austria is the largest commercial bank by branch network, ~120 branches in 2022. The Italian UniCredit group owns Bank Austria as well as Schoellerbank, which had 9 branches. BKS, BTV and Oberbank operate in their respective federal states and Vienna; they form part of the 3 Banken group. As such, they hold shares in each other. The 3 Banken group had a combined total of 176 branches at end-2022, made up 6% of the national total. The Raiffeisen group is the larger of the two co-operative banking groups in Austria and consisted of 315 individual Raiffeisen banks at end-2022. The group has a total of 1,286 branches that are mostly located in small towns and rural areas. There are eight Raiffeisen Landesbanken — one in each federal state. The central wholesale bank for the group is Raiffeisen Zentralbank Österreich. According to the group, its customer base spans nearly half the Austrian population. The bank is also active in central and eastern Europe. Volksbank Wien is the other, much smaller co-operative banking group. It had been undergoing significant restructuring since 2015. The bank also administers all Volksbanken outside Vienna, formerly member banks of Volksbank AG (VBAG). The financial crisis of 2008 nearly led to its collapse in 2012, but the Austrian government prevented bankruptcy by bailing out the group. Subsequently, the group reduced its size from 40 banks to nine regional banks through various mergers. No further consolidation of the group is planned, and restructuring is complete. BAWAG PSK operated through 84 branches at end-2022. In March 2020 it decided to prioritise its retail banking operations, consequently terminating its joint venture with the Austrian postal service Österreichische Post. In March 2020, which replaced BAWAG PSK's financial services with its own subsidiary bank99 from April 2020 onwards. In Vienna, for example, the Austrian postal service reduced its number of offices by 42 and replaced them with 34 more modern outlets, stating that the same quality of service can be offered with less space required. Self-service postal outlets and in-store post service counters are also common in Austria.
