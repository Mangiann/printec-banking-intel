### p.261 — Germany: IAD sector & ATM pooling (Section 3.2)
Body text only (no table/chart). Key points:
- The IAD (independent ATM deployer) sector grew steadily until 2020, with net increases of ~300 ATMs per year during 2017, 2018 and 2019. Since 2020, COVID-19 effects hurt IAD growth: lockdowns and business closures severely affected IADs' business model during 2020 and 2021, and consumer uptake of card payments (particularly contactless and mobile wallets) reduced cash demand. These trends were already evident before 2020 but were accelerated by the pandemic.
- The bank ATM sector has been contracting since 2017. IADs have regularly taken over non-branch ATM locations from bank deployers who wished to divest those assets; several years' IAD expansion offset the shrinkage of the bank ATM sector.
- The previous period of IAD stagnation was in 2011-2014, when the sector struggled to grow following the introduction of a new surcharge-based regime which deployers initially found challenging to their business model.
- Since 2018, NCR (at the time Cardtronics) has had an agreement with Deutsche Postbank whereby the latter's debit cardholders have fee-free access to around half of NCR's ATMs, with the machines bearing Deutsche Postbank branding.
- **Section 3.2 ATM Pooling**: No German ATM deployers are known to be considering pooling their ATMs, but the market would be a suitable one for arrangements of this type, given its large size and fragmented nature. Pooling could take various forms (intra-sector e.g. between savings/co-operative banks, or involving commercial banks) to reduce the costs of running an ATM estate.

Body: Nearly two-thirds of German ATMs are located in 24-hour bank lobbies; this proportion is gradually increasing as it is the banks' preferred location, and ATMs in the bank hall and exterior wall of the branch (TTW) are typically removed first. Banks prefer to install self-service devices in the lobby because it provides 24-hour customer access (which maximises customer convenience) and offers a more secure environment than street-facing machines. Banks typically allow access to lobbies only via swiping a debit card through an external door reader. As well as ATMs, lobbies commonly contain payment-terminals, information terminals and other devices. Since November 2022, bank lobbies have been closed between 11pm and 6am in response to a wave of physical ATM attacks across western and southern Germany (many of them using explosives). This has affected both the ability of consumers to withdraw cash at night, and of businesses to deposit their takings overnight, as many lobby ATMs offer automated note deposit for this purpose. 32% of ATMs are installed away from bank branches, a proportion that has risen from 30% in 2018. Absolute numbers of off-site ATMs have fallen, however, as demand for cash has decreased. German banks have never deployed off-site ATMs in high numbers, seeing them primarily as a means to serve business customers rather than as a way to improve the service they offer to existing retail customers, to acquire new ones, or to increase revenues. The growth of lobby has over the past 25 years, which deploy ATMs and accounts for a small number of cash non-branch locations, makes it unlikely that banks will make any serious attempt to deploy non-branch ATMs in the future.

### p.266 — Germany: TTW / hall / drive-through ATM commentary (text only)
Body text continuation (no table/chart):
- Just 2% of ATMs are installed through the exterior wall of the branch (TTW). The number of TTW ATMs is falling steadily as branches close. Banks with significant numbers of TTW machines include Hamburger Sparkasse, HVB and Deutsche Postbank.
- Most new and recently refurbished branches include ATMs in 24-hour lobbies but not in the exterior wall, as the latter is considered a less secure environment in which to transact and gives banks less scope to deploy additional self-service facilities.
- Bank hall ATMs are accessible only during business hours and are now rare — the only significant deployer of hall ATMs is Deutsche Postbank.
- There are also a small number of drive-through ATMs, deployed by HVB and others.

### p.268 — Germany: functionality commentary + Figure 4.5: ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022
Body text:
- German banks have a relatively limited range of additional functions, partly because of historical preference for deploying different terminals for separate transaction types (e.g. statement printers, terminals for updating passbooks, and stand-alone bankcard and coin-deposit devices). The trend in recent years has been towards multifunction terminals — most cash deposit has largely been absorbed into the ATM channel but lobbies containing several inconsistent-functionality device... remain common.
- Mobile phone credit can be topped up at 71% of ATMs in Germany, while PIN change is offered at 44%. Deutsche Postbank, Targobank and Berliner Sparkasse are among the major banks which offer mobile top-ups; PIN change is offered at ATMs belonging to Commerzbank, Deutsche Bank, Santander and the larger savings banks.
- IAD ATMs offer even fewer facilities than bank ATMs — no machines in this sector offer PIN change, or even balance enquiry. Features that are present include dynamic currency conversion (DCC), which is one of the IADs' main revenue streams and is offered at nearly all non-bank ATMs, i.e. 10% of the national total. Euronet's ATMs also display paid-for on-screen advertising, as do some bank ATMs.
- Just 9% of ATMs print a statement — around 30,000 dedicated statement printers are still in service. Nearly half of ATMs that print statements belong to the savings banks. Bill payment and prepaid top-up are offered by some savings banks, and funds transfer by some commercial banks, but not on a wide scale.
- Although the number of bank ATMs offering additional facilities is growing, the shrinking size of the bank ATM sector relative to the IAD sector (where most ATMs have few/no additional facilities) means that the overall proportion of ATMs offering these extra functions is not necessarily increasing. The proportion of ATMs offering automated note deposit has however continued to increase each year.

Commentary: The large majority of debit and credit cards in Germany are now contactless, but contactless ATMs are still relatively rare. Nearly 6,000 ATMs (~12%) — most of them deployed by co-operative banks — have NFC readers, with the majority of these offering contactless cash withdrawal. ATMs offering QR-code-based withdrawals are rare, and this functionality is unlikely to become widespread. Nearly half of Germany's ATMs now have touch screens. Machines of this type are common at the co-operative banks, commercial banks (Commerzbank, Deutsche Bank and HVB) and the IAD Euronet. Speech capability is less common but is offered at all Commerzbank ATMs and some ATMs deployed by savings and co-operative banks. Contactless ATMs also offer audio guidance for the visually impaired. Over half of ATMs are covered by built-in or integrated security cameras, and other security features are increasingly common: 72% of ATMs now have anti-skimming devices, and 18% feature banknote degradation. In late 2022 the federal government expanded ATM deployers to protect more of their terminals with video surveillance and banknote-degradation technology, in order to combat an ongoing wave of explosive ATM attacks originating from the Netherlands, where a much higher proportion of ATMs have these security features.

Chart takeaway: withdrawal volume dipped sharply in 2020 (COVID) to 1,784m, then partially recovered (1,923m in 2021, 2,103m in 2022); per-ATM monthly average dipped in 2020 then rose to its highest in 2022 (3,336) as the ATM fleet shrank faster than total withdrawals.
Body: Germany's ATMs were used for 2.1 billion cash withdrawals during 2022, a 9% increase compared to 2021. In 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic drastically reduced commercial and consumer activity, and therefore demand for cash. This demand has rebounded in 2021 and 2022 to some extent, but is still below pre-pandemic levels. The number of withdrawals was already beginning to fall before COVID. Underlying the short-term effects of the pandemic is a longer-term shift away from cash towards cashless payments — this has been slower to happen in Germany than in most of Europe, but is now clearly visible. The average number of withdrawals per ATM per month has risen fairly steadily since 2020. This is partly a result of renewed consumer activity, but also a consequence of the large-scale rationalisation of ATM estates across Germany, which typically involves the removal of the machines that are used least often, and are therefore the most expensive for their owners to operate. Average withdrawal numbers are typically higher at ATMs belonging to commercial banks than at those belonging to co-operative and savings banks. Commerzbank — one of the largest commercial bank deployers — had an average of nearly 2,600 in 2022 (up from 2,430 in 2021). However, some large urban savings banks such as Berliner Sparkasse and Hamburger Sparkasse have very high monthly volumes of up to 8,000 withdrawals per ATM. Non-bank ATMs are used much less frequently than bank ATMs; IAD machines typically average between 300 and 400 withdrawals per month. This is because IADs surcharge all withdrawals, and German customers therefore use bank ATMs whenever possible to avoid these fees. The online bank ING-DiBa, whose physical distribution network consists solely of ATMs, has an average of around 700 withdrawals per ATM per month, while Reisebank has over 1,700.
