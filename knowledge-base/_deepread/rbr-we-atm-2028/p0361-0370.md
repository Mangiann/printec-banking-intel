### p.370 — Ireland, Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services (3.1 ATM networks and sharing)
Narrative section (no tables/charts). Key points:
- First ATMs in Ireland were installed by Bank of Ireland in 1979, followed soon after by AIB and Ulster Bank.
- Between 1984 and 2015 there were several sharing agreements between Irish banks; in 1984 Bank of Ireland and the former Northern Bank agreed to offer reciprocal cash-withdrawal facilities to each other's customers. Sharing extended across access to ATMs in 2003, which removed a part of the historic surcharging arrangements; from then customers could use ATMs belonging to other deployers.
- Irish pounds/punts from ATMs in 1999 (pre-euro) context referenced; introduction of euro banknotes and coins.
- AIB established the Irish Banks Network in 1989 to enhance ATM sharing. Through a collection of bilateral sharing agreements, cardholders of other banks were able to use AIB's, TSB Ireland's (now permanent tsb) participating in the network in 1998. In 2015, the Irish Banks Network was wound down and most banks moved to operating in Northern Ireland offered installation proposals to the DCA, based on a surcharging business model; The DCA rejected the proposal and legislation was introduced that meant an IAD could only deploy ATMs after establishing a partnership with a domestic bank. This requirement was removed in 2014 after the migration from the domestic Laser card scheme to the international schemes; IADs could now access Irish cardholders without being required to establish a partnership with a bank.
- At the end of 2022 there were ten ATM deployers operating in the Republic of Ireland. As well as the four banks (Bank of Ireland, AIB, permanent tsb and Ulster Bank — there were ten ATM deployers), the largest deployer of cash withdrawals was AIB (higher than in 2021), and the average number of cash withdrawals made per ATM per month was 9% higher.
- The total value of cash withdrawals was EUR 13 billion (-EUR 1 billion in 2021), an increase of 6% from 2021; the increase can be seen largely as a result of high inflation in 2022.
- Of the two manufacturers present in the Irish market, Diebold Nixdorf had the largest share, representing 64% of the market, with NCR accounting for the remaining 36%.
- ATM numbers are forecast to decline steadily in Ireland over the forecast period; in 2028 it is expected that there will be 3,870 terminals deployed across the country.
