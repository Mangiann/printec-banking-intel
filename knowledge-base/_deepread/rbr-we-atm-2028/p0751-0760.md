### p.751 — UK chapter, Section 3: ATM networks, sharing and services; sub-section 3.1 ATM networks and sharing
- Body text: UK's LINK network connects almost all ATMs in the UK; developed over a long period from the first ATM-sharing initiatives in the 1980s. LINK was formed by a group of building societies in 1982; the original network connected nearly 4,000 such machines (per narrative). The four "Group of Four" network sharing operations began in 1986. The first multilateral network operated by Bank of Scotland began 1988. The remaining building societies, led by Abbey National, joined with other organisations to form the LINK network. The building societies pooled their networks in 1989 and Plus Italia was merged into LINK. In 2000, following the addition of Barclays, HSBC and Lloyds, LINK effectively became a unified network. The Group of Four and MINT networks were therefore dissolved.
- Until 2013 only a few smaller banks deployed ATMs outside of the LINK network; these accounted for only a negligible share of the LINK installed base. By 2022 nearly all ATMs are connected to LINK; ~26 ATM members deploying via LINK; two-smaller non-bank ATM deployers ceased to deploy ATMs during 2022.
- **Figure 3.1: Domestic sharing agreements, 2018-2022** (number of ATMs, in thousands [left] for the LINK row):
  - LINK: 2018 = 62,374; 2019 = 60,945; 2020 = 55,326; 2021 = 53,671; 2022 = 50,428; **CAGR = -5.2%**.
- Forward text discusses LINK interchange-fee/funding model and ongoing ATM closures; the future of the LINK core model under review as interchange revenues fall while operators' costs (cash, maintenance) remain. In January 2018, the LINK board agreed a phased reduction of the interchange fee from GBP 0.25 (USD 0.34) to GBP 0.20 (USD 0.27); a further reduction to be applied 2019 onwards. In July 2018 LINK announced it was cancelling the third planned reduction and postponing the fourth to maintain ATM provision; a fifth reduction has not occurred.

### p.754 — Section 3.2: ATM-as-a-Service
- Body text only: In late 2022, Santander announced it had entered an ATM-as-a-Service agreement with manufacturer NCR to manage and operate its ATM fleet. NCR is taking over operation and management of Santander's self-service channel, including transaction processing, software, ATM monitoring and maintenance. Mostly white space.

### p.755 — Section 4: Current ATM market; 4.1 Market size and growth; Figure 4.1: Number and growth of ATMs, 2018-2022
- Combined column + line chart. Bars = ATM installed base (left axis 0-75,000); line = annual percentage growth (right axis 0% to roughly -8%/-10%). Chart visually confirms: bars declining from ~64k (2018) to ~51k (2022); growth line stays negative throughout, dipping deepest in 2020.
- Underlying data table:
  - **ATMs**: 2018 = 63,586; 2019 = 62,057; 2020 = 56,378; 2021 = 54,481; 2022 = 51,036; **CAGR = -5.3%**.
  - **Absolute growth**: 2019 = -1,529; 2020 = -5,679; 2021 = -1,897; 2022 = -3,445 (CAGR n/a).
  - **Percentage growth**: 2019 = -2.4%; 2020 = -9.2%; 2021 = -3.4%; 2022 = -6.3% (CAGR n/a).
- Body text: number of ATMs in the UK continues to decline, with an average annual fall of ~5% over the period (2018-2022). The decline has not been smooth; the largest fall (-9.2%) was in 2020, when the COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated ATM-rationalisation programmes. The number of ATMs declined by ~3,000 in 2022, to just over 51,000 ATMs at year-end. This decline driven by rationalisation of bank-ATM fleets, particularly the move away from off-premises ATMs, but also as deployers managed costs. The proportion of pay-to-use ATMs (run by IADs) declined in 2022 due to the withdrawal of significant numbers of IAD ATMs during the year; IADs account for almost 60% of the UK market over recent years, but their numbers fell significantly in 2022 (e.g. Cardtronics/Cashzone). Most major IAD deployer NoteMachine placed announced plans to introduce customer charges at more of its ATMs due to increased cost pressures.

  - ATMs per USD billion GDP: UK 17, rank 10, W.Europe 18
- Body text: UK ATM density remains high vs the Western Europe region despite ongoing reductions in the installed base; services unevenly distributed, with LINK intervening to support ATM provision in underserved communities lacking bank branches/post offices. LINK scheme lets communities without banking services apply for funding to install an ATM, and offers additional subsidy for operators in remote/economically deprived areas. The ratio of ATMs to bank branches rose again in 2022, to 633 ATMs per 100 branches, due to ongoing branch rationalisation. IADs account for almost 60% of the UK market; ATM density more than halves if IAD-deployed terminals are excluded.

- Body text: most common location for ATMs deployed in the UK is off-site, not linked to a bank branch — such terminals account for a relatively consistent proportion of the installed base at around 72% (between 2018 and 2022). The share of off-site ATMs (~5-9% by number) rose to ~74% in 2022. This is largely driven by the very significant presence of non-bank ATM deployers, which account for well over half of total UK installations. While IAD/NoteMachine deploys some branch ATMs on behalf of smaller banks/building societies (e.g. Yorkshire Building Society and Metro Bank), the majority of ATMs deployed by IADs are off-site, where self-service helps footfall (retail outlets, shopping centres, transport hubs). Some IADs such as Euronet are looking at lobby installs. Banks typically locate ATMs in branch halls or through the exterior wall (TTW), with the exception of a limited number of terminals deployed by NoteMachine. Some UK bank deployers offer cardless cash withdrawal as an emergency service, with examples including NatWest Group (allows customers to withdraw emergency cash from its ATMs without a debit card using a one-time code generated in the bank's mobile app). Barclays allows customers to withdraw cash via smartphone NFC functionality.

### p.758 — UK locations narrative (continuation of 4.3)
- Body text only: Branch lobbies are less common in the UK than in many comparable European markets; UK banks/customers express concerns around security of such spaces which are often unstaffed, particularly outside normal branch opening hours. Some deployers such as NatWest Group and Lloyds Banking Group have installed some ATMs in branch lobbies, but the more typical option in the UK is for deployers to install ATMs through the exterior wall of a bank branch (TTW). TTW is the most common branch location, and such ATMs account for **13% of the total installed base**.
- Drive-through ATMs are a novelty in the UK, with just **one such ATM deployed by Barclays**.

### p.760 — Figure 4.5: ATMs and SATs with automated deposit functionality, 2022; deposit narrative
- Table: type of deposit / ATMs / SATs (self-service terminals):
  - Automated note deposit: ATMs 5,604 / SATs 2,148 (of which: note recycling — ATMs 1,550 / SATs n/a)
  - Automated cheque deposit: ATMs 3,931 / SATs 2,118
  - **Total: ATMs 5,604 / SATs 2,708.**
- Body text: PIN services are the most commonly offered additional functionality at ATMs in the UK, with all bank-deployed ATMs as well as the majority of IAD-deployed ATMs allowing customers to change/unlock their PIN. Cashzone, PayPoint and Euronet are the only bank deployers which do not offer PIN services at all of their ATMs. DCC is commonly offered at ATMs deployed by those IADs which specialise in serving the travel and tourist market, including Travelex and ChangeGroup. A large share of ATMs deployed by Cashzone and NoteMachine also offer DCC. Some bank deployers including HSBC and Santander's Bank offer DCC services at some of their ATMs, but this is less widespread among bank terminals.
- ~32% of UK ATMs offer paid-for advertising; this share is the highest of these being deployed by Cashzone.
- Bank ATMs typically offer cardless cash withdrawal, with the exception of a limited number of terminals deployed by NoteMachine.
- Some UK bank deployers offer cardless cash withdrawal as an emergency service; NatWest Group does this nationally; functionality first introduced May 2019.
- UK deployers do not typically offer automated deposit services via ATMs, with automated note deposit available at only ~11% of terminals and automated cheque deposit at just 8% of the total. All ATMs which can accept automated deposits are able to accept banknotes, but cheque acceptance is more limited. Santander and Lloyds Banking Group deploy a large number of branch-based ATMs with automated note/cheque deposit functionality. NoteMachine the only deployer to offer assisted self-service. Bank branch halls are the most common location for automated deposit ATMs due to customer concerns about their personal safety when depositing large volumes of cash outside branches.
- Largest deployers of SATs (stand-alone terminals) in the UK are Lloyds Banking Group and HSBC, with SATs belonging to Lloyds Banking Group accepting both note and cheque deposits, while those belonging to HSBC typically accept one or the other.
