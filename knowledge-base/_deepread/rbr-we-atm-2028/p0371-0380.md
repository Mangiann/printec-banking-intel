Body: ATMs in Ireland typically offer a limited range of additional features to cash withdrawal. Two-thirds of machines allow users to change their PIN, and it is possible to deposit notes at 31% of machines, and cheques at 23%. More and more ATMs in Ireland are accepting deposits in addition to allowing cash withdrawals. At end-2022, 1,240 ATMs allowed automated note deposit and 882 terminals allowed cheque deposit (a number which has risen since end-2021). Note recycling was available at 727 ATMs. By contrast, the number of deposit-enabled SATs was 199 (1.5%) — text notes 1.2% by end of 2022. Other notable features include dynamic currency conversion (DCC), available at 19% of machines, and the option to print a statement, at 17% of machines. A small number of ATMs offer the chance to top up mobile phones (PIN) and just 3% have audio guidance, which can help equipped customers and wheelchair users.

### p.376 (Ireland: Page 10) — 4.5 cont., Value of cash withdrawals
Body: Since 2018, the total number of cash withdrawals made per year in Ireland has fallen by over 50%. Increasing use of cards and electronic payments has contributed to the decline, with contactless payments replacing cash for many instances of low-value transactions. The large drop in cash withdrawals in 2020 can be attributed to the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic and the associated restrictions and lockdowns in the country, with the fall in 2021 being much smaller. From the Central Bank of Ireland has found that ATM activity increased during periods when restrictions were relaxed, as there were fewer constraints on economic activity, affecting cash use in particular.

2022 saw a 6% increase in overall cash withdrawals compared with 2021. Despite increasing card use, cash is still widely used in Ireland; with a Department of Finance survey in 2022 finding that one in five adults in Ireland prefer to use cash when paying in stores; compared with cards; reasons for the appeal of cash included protecting the holder's privacy, permitting money to be held in person rather than in a bank account, and helping with budgeting. Cash withdrawals represented 65% of ATM transactions in Ireland in 2022, virtually unchanged since 2018.

### p.377 (Ireland: Page 11) — Cash-withdrawal value commentary (text only)
Body: Research from the Central Bank of Ireland suggests that the increasing value of cash withdrawals is a response to inflationary developments. Year-on-year inflation rose to 8% in Ireland for 2022, driven by rising energy prices; food prices also saw high growth. The average amount withdrawn per ATM visit in 2022 was EUR 151 (USD 159), representing an increase of 0.2% from 2021. Since 2018, the average value has fluctuated, increasing by an average of 3% each year. Alongside lockdowns and COVID-19 restrictions, 2020 saw a 15% increase in the average cash withdrawal value as people made fewer ATM visits but withdrew a higher amount each time; this fell in 2021, but not to 2019 levels. While the total number of ATMs in Ireland has fallen by 3% since 2018, the average value withdrawn at each ATM has also fallen, at an average of 13% per year; 2020 saw the steepest fall, while 2022 marked a departure from this downward trend (high inflation + total ATM number only fell marginally). Cash withdrawals make up a decreasing proportion of GDP, accounting for just 3% in 2022 vs 7% in 2018.

- ATMs per million people — 2023: 780 / 2024: 778 / 2025: 776 / 2026: 774 / 2027: 772 / 2028: 770 / CAGR: -0.3% (approx)
(Source: RBR analysis.)

Body: The total number of ATMs in Ireland is expected to fall slowly and steadily over the forecast period, with 3,870 terminals projected to be deployed across the country by end-2028. This represents a decline of 2% from end-2022. The IAD market is predicted to continue to grow over the forecast period; meanwhile the number of bank-deployed ATMs is expected to continue to fall, particularly in the early years of the forecast period, alongside ongoing bank deployers' planned withdrawal from the market. Both types of deployer are likely to rationalise their fleets in response to falling cash demand and high uptake of electronic payment methods in the country. The number of ATMs per million people is also expected to fall throughout the forecast period. While the Government of Ireland's 2013 National Payments Plan (NPP) aimed to encourage the transition towards cashless payments, planned legislation is now expected to ensure continued access to cash, helping to keep ATM numbers stable. Bank branches are forecast to decline in number between 2023 and 2028; an estimated 390 branches are expected to operate across Ireland at end-2028, a 33% decrease from end-2022. The steepest fall is anticipated at the start of the forecast period owing to two banks' planned withdrawal from the market, with numbers declining steadily in the years thereafter. Banks are expected to continue rationalising their branch networks as they shift towards digital and remote banking.
