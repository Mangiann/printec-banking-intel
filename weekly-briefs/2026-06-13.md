## Top opportunities this week

**1. Hungary's legally forced ATM rollout — the strongest hardware-plus-service signal in the whole footprint.**
Hungary's regulator (the central bank, MNB) has ordered banks to put a cash machine in every settlement of more than 500 people by 31 December 2026. The statistics agent confirms ATM numbers already grew past 5,500 by December 2025 even as branches shrank 7%, and the country is heading toward roughly 6,000 machines. The tenders agent confirms why we see little of this in public tender portals: Hungarian commercial banks are private and buy privately, so the work goes straight to banks and their vendors rather than appearing online. The jobs agent adds a small corroborating detail — OTP is hiring an in-house ATM service technician. The catch is that these are low-traffic rural sites where a normal ATM loses money, so the banks need cheap deployment, cash-recycling machines (which re-use deposited notes instead of being refilled by van), remote monitoring and ideally someone else to run them. That is precisely Printec's managed-services pitch. *Suggested action:* approach the four big ATM operators (OTP ~1,900 machines, MBH ~1,250, K&H ~630, Erste ~500) with rural-ATM economics — recyclers plus remote management to make loss-making locations viable. There is a published bank-by-settlement allocation list worth obtaining first.

**2. PrivatBank in Ukraine — both a big ATM purchase and an urgent maintenance gap.**
Two agents independently confirm Ukraine's largest bank is renewing its ATM fleet: it told the market it bought 1,200 machines in 2025 and plans ~800 more in 2026 (disclosures agent), and its procurement portal shows a live "prequalification" round for new recycling ATMs that exactly mirrors a 2023–24 cycle which ended in an €18.7m hardware order (tenders agent). A second-stage hardware purchase is likely in the second half of 2026. Separately — and more urgently — the bank has twice failed to find anyone to maintain its existing Diebold Nixdorf recycling ATMs (no bidder turned up), and will probably now approach qualified vendors directly. *Suggested action:* if Printec Ukraine is certified on Diebold Nixdorf machines, contact the bank's ATM team straight away about the maintenance gap (this is a near-term win, not a 12-month one); in parallel, register on the bank's tender portal to be ready for the larger hardware round.

**3. Greece — the busiest ATM market in the region, but the one we are most at risk of losing.**
Four agents point the same way. Cash machines in Greece grew almost 18% in a year while branches fell (statistics). Behind that growth: Euronet poured 75% more into its Greek infrastructure last year — its biggest single-year investment in any country in the footprint — and is about to take over the entire ATM fleet of CrediaBank (Greece's #5 bank); NCR Atleos won an ATM-as-a-service deal with Epirus Bank; and Mellon is rolling out 700 recyclers for the National Bank of Greece (vendors). On top of that, the government wants multi-bank ATMs in ~985 post offices (tenders), and Alpha and Eurobank are both upgrading branch cash machines (disclosures). The opportunity is real but so is the danger: competitors are signing whole banks onto outsourcing deals, which locks Printec out of those estates for years. *Suggested action:* focus on the banks still up for grabs — the mid-tier and cooperative banks below the NBG/Eurobank/CrediaBank tier — before Euronet or Atleos reach them; and clarify with NCR Atleos exactly where the partner boundary sits, since Atleos is now selling directly into Printec's home market.

**4. Alpha Bank and Banca Transilvania — two well-funded transformation programs confirmed by both their results and their hiring.**
Alpha Bank (Greece) has announced and funded a retail overhaul that explicitly includes branch investment and contactless ATMs, and it is simultaneously advertising 39 roles citing a "large-scale Transformation Program" — so the money and the delivery team both exist. Banca Transilvania (Romania's biggest bank) has budgeted about RON 1 billion for 2026, "mainly IT and digital," and is hiring an architect specifically for its cards business — and a cards-platform architect typically lands 6–12 months before the bank picks its vendors. *Suggested action:* for Alpha, get the Q1 investor deck for the capex breakdown and watch for branch-fit-out and ATM tenders; for Banca Transilvania, this is the cleanest "get in early" cards/payments signal of the week — track follow-on cards roles and watch Romanian procurement.

**5. Two calendar-driven windows opening in H2 2026: Bulgaria after the euro, and the EU compliance deadlines.**
Bulgaria adopted the euro on 1 January 2026; free exchange of old leva at banks ends 30 June. Three agents agree the changeover spending is now behind the banks, and after June the conversion workload drops sharply — which is exactly when banks decide to rationalise the extra ATM/cash capacity they built for the switch. Bulgaria also had the EU's sharpest branch cut last year (−12%). Separately, the regulation agent flags that the bloc's new anti-money-laundering rules publish their detailed technical standards on 10 July 2026, which kicks off a wave of spending on identity-checking, customer-onboarding and transaction-monitoring tools through the second half of the year — and the jobs agent already sees banks (Eurobank's financial-crime unit, UBB's security team) hiring for exactly this. Both are core Printec compliance and cash-automation territory. *Suggested action:* line up Bulgarian conversations for July, once banks can see what cash capacity they no longer need; and package DORA/AML-ready compliance and onboarding tooling for the H2 buying window.
